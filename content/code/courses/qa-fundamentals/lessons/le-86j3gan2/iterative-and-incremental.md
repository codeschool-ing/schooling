title: Iterative and incremental are two different words
version: 1

**Iterative and incremental are used as one phrase so often that they sound like synonyms. They are two
ideas, and a team can have either without the other.**

- **Incremental** means building the system **a piece at a time**, each piece finished before the next:
  first the price rule, then orders, then the seat map, then payments. At every step, part of the system
  is complete and part does not exist.
- **Iterative** means building the system **by successive versions of the whole**, each one rougher than
  the next: a crude shop that does everything badly, then a better one, then a good one. At every step,
  everything exists and nothing is finished.

Jeff Patton's well-known illustration uses a painting. Incrementally, you paint the Mona Lisa square by
square, each square perfect, and the face appears last. Iteratively, you sketch the whole portrait,
then block in the colours, then refine. Only the second lets somebody look at it on the first day and say
*the face should be turned the other way*.

## Why the difference matters to a tester

The two produce different testing problems.

**Incremental delivery** gives the tester finished pieces to test thoroughly, one after another. Its risk
is integration: each piece is tested alone, and the first time they all work together is late. At Cine
Aurora, built incrementally, the price rule would be well tested in month one and the refused orders that
inflate the seats report would wait for month three, when orders and the report finally met.

**Iterative delivery** gives the tester the whole system early, in a rough state. Its risk is that nothing
is ever finished enough to test thoroughly, and that defects in rough parts are dismissed as "it is only a
draft". Its strength is that the rough whole can be shown to Célia in week two, and her reaction is
information no amount of testing a perfect fragment would produce.

Most modern teams combine them: **small increments, each delivered iteratively**. A thin slice of the whole
shop, for one kind of ticket, end to end, then a wider slice. The agile models of the next lessons are
built on exactly that combination.

## The Unified Process, in one paragraph

Between the spiral and agile came the **Rational Unified Process**, published by Rational Software in the
late 1990s, which made iterative and incremental development the official approach of a very large number
of companies. It divides a project into four phases, inception, elaboration, construction and transition,
each made of several iterations, and in each iteration all the disciplines, including testing, happen to
some degree. You will meet it in older organisations and in the vocabulary of documents; its central
idea, that testing happens in every iteration rather than in a phase, is the one that survived.
