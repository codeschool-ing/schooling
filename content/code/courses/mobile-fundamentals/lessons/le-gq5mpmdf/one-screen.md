**Take the planner's main screen and design it once for each guide: the content is identical, and
four things move.** The screen is the week of Monday 19 October, the one lesson 1 computed: a list of
sessions, the line that says 120 of 180 minutes are done, a way to add a session, and a way to reach
the planner's other two sections, the list of courses and the settings. Nothing about the data
changes between the two drawings. What changes is where each job sits.

The figure is a diagram of regions, not a picture of either phone. Read it row by row.

**The title.** Material puts "This week" in a **top app bar**, a strip at the top that, in the words of
Android's documentation, "hosts a title, core action items, and certain navigation items". The HIG gives the same words a **large
title**, set big at the top of the list; its toolbar page says the large title "transitions to a
standard title as people begin scrolling the content" and comes back when they scroll to the top.
On iOS the title is part of the content until the list moves.

**The one action that matters most.** Adding a session is what a student does every day, so both
designs give it a place of its own, and the places differ. Material gives it a **floating action
button**, which Android's documentation calls "a high-emphasis button" for "the most common pathway a
user might take", sitting above the content at the bottom right. The HIG has no floating button: the
same action is a **+ in the toolbar**, at the top trailing corner, beside the title. A student who
moves from one phone to the other looks for it in the wrong corner for a day.

**The sections.** Both put the three sections in a bar along the bottom, and both bars are for
moving, not acting. Material calls it a **navigation bar**; the HIG calls it a **tab bar**, and
says to keep it visible in every section: "if you hide the tab bar, people can forget which area of
the app they're in". This is the row that looks most alike and is most alike.

**Going back.** It is not on the main screen, and that is the point. On Android the system's back
gesture leaves a screen; on iOS a screen pushed on top of this one gets a back button at the top
leading corner. Lesson 10 is about both, and neither guide asks the planner to draw a back button here.

## Where the add-session form appears

Pressing the action opens a short form: course, date, minutes. The HIG has a component for exactly
this, the **sheet**, which "helps people perform a scoped task that's closely related to their current
context": it rises over the week, the student fills it in, and it goes away. Material offers a bottom
sheet or a dialog for the same kind of short task. Three fields is a
short form on both, so both designs keep the week visible behind it, which is what tells the student
that cancelling loses nothing.

## The same screen on a tablet

**Width changes Material's answer more than the HIG's.** On a wide screen Android's documentation
moves the sections from the navigation bar to a **navigation rail** down the left edge, "for tablet
or desktop layouts", and the floating action button can sit at the top of the rail. The HIG keeps
the tab bar on iPad and lets it turn into a sidebar where the app has more sections than a bar holds.
Lesson 8 is about a layout that changes with the width; this is what each guide puts in the space
that opens up.

## What stays where it is

**The content area belongs to the planner on both phones.** The list of sessions, the week's total,
the course each session belongs to: neither guide tells the planner how to draw those, beyond the
colours, the type and the spacing the next section turns to. That is the part of the screen the
brand owns, and it is most of the screen.
