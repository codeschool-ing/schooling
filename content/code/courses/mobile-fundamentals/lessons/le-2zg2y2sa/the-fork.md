decision, made by you, for yourself.** Each route is two courses: a language, then the apps built
with it. Then all three come back together, because what follows is the same work whichever way the
app was built.

## The three options

| route | the courses | the approach from lesson 2 | what it needs on your computer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Android with Kotlin | `kotlin`, then `android-apps` | native, one platform | Android Studio; any computer |
| iOS with Swift | `swift`, then `ios-apps` | native, one platform | a Mac with Xcode for the screens |
| Multiplatform with React Native | `javascript`, then `react-native` | multiplatform UI, both platforms | Node.js and Expo; any computer |

**Android with Kotlin** teaches one platform deeply, in the language Google recommends for it. It is
the route the bank of the previous section started on. `kotlin` lesson 20 also looks at
Kotlin beyond Android, multiplatform included.

**iOS with Swift** is the same depth on the other platform, and the one route that needs a
particular computer: Xcode runs on macOS alone, as lesson 1 said, so without a Mac the `ios-apps`
course is read more than practised.

**Multiplatform with React Native** is the one route that reaches both phones, in TypeScript, with
the platform's own controls. It is the route the startup of the previous section took, and the one
where lesson 1's `planner.ts` can be imported as it is. Its last lesson, 22, looks at Flutter and
Kotlin Multiplatform from there.

## Running the matrix on yourself

**The criteria change owner, and most of them still apply.** For a person choosing a route rather
than a team choosing a tool, they read like this:

- *languages*: which language do you already write, or most want to?
- *platforms*: do you want to know one platform deeply, or reach both with one codebase?
- *hiring*: which of the three do the jobs you are aiming at ask for, where you live? Look at real
  listings rather than at a ranking.
- *time*: how soon do you need to show a working app to somebody?
- *your computer*: a criterion no team has, and for the iOS route the first one to check.

You can put those in a copy of `choose.ts`, with the three routes as the approaches, and see which of your own
weights decides it. If one point flips the answer, the two routes are close for you, and either is a
good choice.

## What the fork does not decide

**It does not lock you in.** The three routes meet again at `mobile-offline`, then go on to
`testing-cicd` and `mobile-delivery` together, and the lessons of this course hold on all three:
lifecycle, screens, permissions, network, battery and storage. A developer who knows one platform
well does not start the second from nothing, because everything in lessons 4 to 20 of this course
is already theirs. Lesson 4 starts that list with the rule every platform shares and every
beginner trips over: the app is not the one that decides when it runs.
