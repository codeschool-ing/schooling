"NoSQL" sounds like the name of one kind of database. **It is the name of everything that is not a
relational one**, and the products under it have less in common with each other than any of them has
with Postgres. What separates them is a single decision each family makes differently: **what does
the server keep together, and what can it see inside what it keeps?**

The rest of the course is easier to follow with one example carried through all of them. On 14
September 2026 at 10:22, São Paulo time, Ana Ribeiro orders two USB-C cables at 39.90 each and a
wireless mouse at 189.00. It is order 1001, and it comes to 268.80 reais.

## Five places to put it

The `sql-databases` course stored this order the relational way: a row in `orders`, a row per product
in `order_lines`, and the customer and the products in tables of their own, each fact once. Reading
the order back means a join, and the join is assembled at read time from four tables. The other
families store the same facts in other shapes:

| family | what is stored together | what the server can answer about it | what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| relational | each fact once, in its own table | any question SQL can ask, joins included | a join to rebuild the order on every read |
| document | the whole order, lines inside it | questions about any field, nested ones too | a shape chosen for one way of reading |
| key-value | a value under the key `order:1001` | "give me the value of this key", and little else | every other question is the application's job |
| wide column | the rows of one customer, sorted, in one partition | rows of a partition, in their order | a table per question, planned before the data |
| graph | the people, orders and products, and the links between them | "who is linked to whom, through what" | aggregates over everything are not its strength |

A sixth family, **time series**, does not fit the order at all: it stores measurements taken over
time, like the response time of the shop's checkout every ten seconds. The last section of this
lesson describes it with the graph family.

## The question to carry into each section

For each store, watch two things. **The unit of storage**: what one write puts down and one read
hands back. **The visibility**: whether the server can look inside that unit to answer a question,
or only fetch it whole by name. A store that sees inside can answer questions you did not plan for,
at a price; a store that does not answers the planned ones very fast and the others not at all.

That is not a ranking. The three products installed in lesson 1 sit at three different points on
it, and the next three sections put order 1001 into each one.
