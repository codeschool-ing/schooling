Having a legal basis is necessary and not enough. **Article 6** sets principles that every processing
must respect, whatever its basis, and **article 18** gives the people the data is about, the **data
subjects** (*titulares*), rights they can exercise against the organisation.

### The principles

| principle | what it requires, at the shop |
|---|---|
| **purpose** (*finalidade*) | process for legitimate, specific and explicit purposes, told to the person: "to deliver your order" |
| **adequacy** (*adequação*) | the processing matches the purpose told |
| **necessity** (*necessidade*) | only the minimum data needed: no ID document to sell a book |
| **free access** (*livre acesso*) | people can easily find out what is done with their data |
| **data quality** (*qualidade dos dados*) | data kept accurate and up to date |
| **transparency** (*transparência*) | clear information about the processing and who does it |
| **security** (*segurança*) | technical and administrative measures against unauthorised access and accidents |
| **prevention** (*prevenção*) | measures to prevent harm before it happens |
| **non-discrimination** (*não discriminação*) | no processing for unlawful or abusive discrimination |
| **accountability** (*responsabilização e prestação de contas*) | being able to show, with evidence, that the rules are followed |

Three of those ten are this course under other names. **Security** and **prevention** are lessons 4 to 12.
**Accountability** is lesson 13's evidence: the law does not only require doing the right thing, it
requires being able to prove it. And **necessity** is the cheapest security control there is: data the
shop never collected cannot leak. When lesson 3 had the shop delete its old copies of customers' ID
documents, it was avoiding a risk and obeying this principle at the same time.

### The rights

A customer can ask the shop, free of charge, for:

| right | what the shop must be able to do |
|---|---|
| **confirmation** and **access** | say whether it holds data about them, and provide it |
| **correction** | fix data that is incomplete, inaccurate or out of date |
| **anonymisation, blocking or deletion** | of data that is unnecessary, excessive or processed against the law |
| **portability** | provide the data so it can be taken to another provider, as the ANPD regulates |
| **deletion** of data processed with consent | for example, remove them from the newsletter list entirely |
| **information about sharing** | say with whom the data was shared |
| **information about consent** | explain that they may refuse consent, and what follows |
| **revocation of consent** | withdraw it at any time |

Article 20 adds a right to request **review of decisions taken solely by automated processing** that
affect a person's interests.

### What rights mean for security work

Each right is a **process** somebody has to run, and each process has a security side. Before giving a
customer their data, the shop must be sure the request comes from that customer, or the right of access
becomes a way for a stranger to obtain somebody else's data: lesson 8's authentication, applied to a
letter or an email. And deleting data on request means knowing every place it lives, including backups,
which is why lesson 12's retention periods and lesson 16's inventory matter here too.
