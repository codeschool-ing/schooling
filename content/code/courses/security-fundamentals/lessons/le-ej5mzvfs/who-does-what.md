The LGPD distributes duties among a few roles, and the same organisation can hold different roles for
different processing.

| role | who it is | at the shop |
|---|---|---|
| **data subject** (*titular*) | the natural person the data is about | each customer; each member of staff, for payroll |
| **controller** (*controlador*) | whoever makes the decisions about the processing | the shop, for its customer list and its payroll |
| **operator** (*operador*) | whoever processes data on the controller's behalf | the hosting company, the email service, the payroll accountant |
| **encarregado** | the person who is the channel between the controller, the data subjects and the ANPD; often called the DPO | at the shop, one of the owners |
| **ANPD** | the national authority: regulates, supervises, sanctions | |

### Controller and operator

The distinction is about **who decides**. The shop decides why customer data is collected and what is done
with it, so it is the controller. The company hosting its website stores that data only to provide the
hosting the shop bought, following the shop's instructions, so it is an operator. Operators have duties of
their own, including security, and answer jointly with the controller when they break the law or the
controller's lawful instructions.

This is lesson 3's transfer, seen through the law: the shop can contract an operator to hold its data,
and it **cannot contract away being the controller**. The contract with each operator should say what the
operator may do with the data, require security measures, and require it to report incidents to the shop
quickly, since the shop's three working days start when the shop learns of the incident.

### The encarregado

Article 41 requires the controller to indicate an **encarregado**, whose identity and contact must be
published, clearly, usually on the website. The encarregado accepts complaints and requests from data
subjects, receives communications from the ANPD, and guides staff on data protection practice.

### Small businesses

The ANPD's **Resolution CD/ANPD no. 2 of 2022** created a **simplified regime for small processing agents**:
micro and small businesses, start-ups and others, **unless they carry out high-risk processing**. Among its
simplifications: the small agent need not indicate an encarregado, but must provide a channel for data
subjects to reach it; it may keep a simplified record of its processing; and some deadlines are longer.
The duties of article 46 to keep data secure, and of article 48 to report serious incidents, remain.

The shop qualifies, and still chose to name one of the owners as encarregado. A named person is easier for
customers to reach and easier to hold to the principle of accountability.

### What it adds up to

The law's demands, laid next to this course, are mostly things the course has already built for other
reasons. A risk register, least privilege, logs with names, tested backups, an inventory, evidence kept:
each one was justified by the shop's own risks, and each one turns out to be what the LGPD asks for. That
is the lesson 13 pattern one more time. **Security done for real reasons produces most of what compliance
needs**, and what is left, the encarregado, the record of processing, the incident procedure, is a short
list on top.
