The **Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais (LGPD)**, Law no. 13,709 of 14 August 2018, is Brazil's
general law on personal data. It came into force in September 2020, and its administrative sanctions
from August 2021. It is enforced by the **Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados (ANPD)**, the national
data protection authority. Its structure and many of its ideas are close to the European Union's GDPR,
and somebody who knows one recognises most of the other.

This lesson is a map of the law for somebody who will work in security, not legal advice. When a real
decision depends on the details, the law's text and the ANPD's regulations are the source, and a lawyer
reads them.

### Whom it applies to

The LGPD applies to any **processing** of personal data, by a person or an organisation, public or
private, when the processing happens in Brazil, when its purpose is to offer goods or services to people
in Brazil, or when the data was collected in Brazil. Size does not exempt anybody: a nine-person bookshop
with a customer list is covered as a bank is, although the ANPD has a simplified regime for small
businesses (section 06).

**Processing** (*tratamento*) is defined very broadly: collecting, recording, storing, using, sharing,
transferring, deleting, and almost any other operation on the data. Keeping a spreadsheet of customers is
processing; so is a backup of it.

A few activities are outside the law, among them processing by a person for purely private purposes, and
processing for journalism, art, public security or national defence, which have rules of their own.

### What counts as personal data

| term in the law | what it is | at the shop |
|---|---|---|
| **personal data** (*dado pessoal*) | information related to an identified or identifiable natural person | a customer's name, email, address, order history, the IP address in a log |
| **sensitive personal data** (*dado pessoal sensível*) | racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, political opinion, union membership, data on health or sex life, genetic or biometric data | none, and the shop intends to keep it that way |
| **anonymised data** (*dado anonimizado*) | data that can no longer be linked to a person by reasonable means | "1,200 orders shipped to São Paulo in September" |

Two points in that table catch people out. **Identifiable** is enough: an order number or an IP address is
personal data if the shop can link it to a person, even without the name beside it. And **anonymised data
is outside the law** only if the anonymisation cannot reasonably be reversed. Removing the name and keeping
the street address, the date of birth and the order history is not anonymisation; it is a list that
identifies people with one more step.

**Sensitive data** carries stricter rules and narrower legal grounds. The cheapest way to comply with them
is lesson 3's avoidance: do not collect it.
