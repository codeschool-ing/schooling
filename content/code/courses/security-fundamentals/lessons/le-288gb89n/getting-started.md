Whichever family appeals, the way in shares a few steps, and one rule that this course has kept from its
first lab.

### The rule

**Practise only on systems you own, or on systems whose owner has given you written permission to test.**
Every capture in this course ran in a lab built for it, on one machine, with addresses reserved for
documentation. That was not caution for its own sake. Probing, scanning or attacking somebody else's
system without authorisation is a crime in Brazil and in most countries, whatever the intention, and a
conviction ends a security career before it starts. Lesson 10 said what makes the difference: written
authorisation and agreed rules of engagement.

### Build a lab

The cheapest and most useful thing a beginner can do is build their own lab: a couple of virtual machines,
a vulnerable application made for practice, a firewall between them. Configure the defences from this
course, then try to get past them, then read the logs: lesson 10's purple loop, alone. `virtualization`
and `linux-terminal` give you the tools to build one.

### Practise where practice is legal

There are platforms built for practice, where attacking the targets is the point and is permitted:
capture-the-flag competitions (CTFs) and training environments with deliberately vulnerable machines. They
are legal because the owners built the targets for exactly that, and they publish rules about what is in
scope. Read those rules, as you would a client's.

### Write it down

Keep notes and write up what you did: the lab you built, the detection you tuned, the restore test that
found the missing folder. A short, honest write-up shows an employer what you can do far better than a
list of tools, and writing clearly is a skill every family in this lesson needs. It is also lesson 13's
evidence, applied to yourself.

### Certifications

Certifications exist for every family, from entry level to senior. Some require years of professional
experience before they can be awarded; some are practical exams where you must actually break into a
machine; some are courses in implementing or auditing a standard like ISO 27001. Treat them as a way to
structure study and to get past a CV filter, not as the skill itself. `pentest` lesson 21 surveys the
offensive ones.

### The common thread

Every family in this lesson needs the vocabulary of the first seventeen: what risk is and who owns it,
why one control is never enough, why the network is not a trust boundary, the difference between who you
are and what you may do, how to tell a real alert from noise, and how to prove all of it. That is what
this course was for. The rest of the track builds on it, one family at a time.
