The functions and categories describe the ground. Two more ideas let an organisation describe its own
position on it, and they are the parts of the CSF used most in practice.

### Profiles: where we are, where we want to be

A **profile** is the set of CSF outcomes an organisation has chosen, with how far it has achieved each.
Two profiles side by side do the work:

- a **current profile**: the outcomes achieved today;
- a **target profile**: the outcomes the organisation wants to achieve, chosen from its risks, its
  obligations and its appetite.

The difference between them is the **gap**, and the gap becomes the plan. A slice of the shop's:

| subcategory, in short | current | target | action |
|---|---|---|---|
| GV.RM: risk appetite established | achieved: the owners' one-line appetite (lesson 3) | achieved | none |
| PR.AA: MFA on every account | partly: staff have TOTP, the bank access has SMS | security keys for the bank and admin accounts | buy two keys; enrol |
| DE.CM: sign-in failures monitored | partly: a rule exists; the portal log has no time | rule running on timestamped logs | fix the portal log (lesson 10's finding) |
| PR.DS-11: backups tested | achieved since October: monthly restore test | achieved | keep doing it |

A target profile is not "everything at the top". A nine-person shop that sets itself the target of a
bank will never close the gap and will stop trying. The target comes from the risks of lesson 3, and the
order of the actions from the same ranking.

**Community profiles** are targets written by a sector or a group for its members, as a starting
point: one for small businesses, one for a particular industry. An organisation adopts one and adjusts
it, rather than starting from 106 blank lines.

### Tiers: how mature the practice is

The CSF also describes four **tiers**, which characterise how rigorous an organisation's approach to
cybersecurity risk is:

| tier | name | what it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **partial** | security is handled case by case, reactively, with little awareness of risk at the top |
| 2 | **risk informed** | management is aware of risk and approves practices, but not as an organisation-wide policy |
| 3 | **repeatable** | practices are formally approved as policy, consistent, and updated as risks change |
| 4 | **adaptive** | practices adapt continuously from lessons learned and indicators, as part of the culture |

Tiers are not maturity levels that every organisation must climb to the top of. They describe the
approach, and the right tier is the one that matches the risks. Before this course, the shop was in Tier
1: ana fixed things as they broke. With a written appetite, a register reviewed every six months and
tested backups, it is moving into Tier 2, approaching 3. For a shop its size, Tier 3 is a sensible
target; Tier 4 is for organisations whose risks justify a full-time security function.
