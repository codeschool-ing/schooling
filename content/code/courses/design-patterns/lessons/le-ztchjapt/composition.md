**Composition means an object holds other objects and hands work to them.** Inheritance says *is
a*; composition says *has a*. A member of the library is not a kind of e-mail; a member has a way
of being reached. That sentence, said out loud, is usually enough to tell which of the two a design
needs.

Here is a member who has a channel, built on the classes of the previous section:

Compare the inheritance version: `EmailMember(Member)`, `SmsMember(Member)`, `PrintedSlipMember(Member)`.
Now add a second thing that varies, say whether the member is a student with a longer loan
period. Inheritance needs a class for every combination, `StudentEmailMember` and the rest; with two
choices of status and three channels that is six classes, and a third axis multiplies again.
Composition needs one more field.

## The four ideas, together

Encapsulation draws the boundary of an object. Inheritance and composition are two ways of
building an object out of others: by being a modified copy of a parent, or by holding parts and
delegating to them. Polymorphism is what lets either kind be used without the caller knowing which
class it has. The rest of this course is mostly about the third and fourth working together:
SOLID's principles in lessons 3 and 4, dependency injection in lesson 5 and most of the GoF
patterns in lesson 6 are composition plus polymorphism, applied to a particular problem.
