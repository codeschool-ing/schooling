---
title: Who needs to hear what
version: 1
---

Lesson 11 named a communications role in the CSIRT and gave it to the managing partner. This lesson is what that
role does. The rule underneath it is simple to say: **each audience gets what it needs to act, and nothing it
cannot use.** A board does not need a port number; the person whose account was used does not need the
severity scale.

For Thursday, the audiences inside the company are these. Clients and the regulator are lesson 21's.

| audience | needs to know | decides | hears it from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **management**, the managing partner | what happened, what it may cost, what is being done | the business decisions: shut a service, pay for help, tell clients | the incident lead |
| **HR** | which member of staff is involved, and in what role | how that person is spoken to, and any employment question | the incident lead, with the managing partner |
| **legal**, the external lawyer | the facts so far, and what may have left | notification duties, what is written and how | the incident lead, early |
| **the DPO** | whether personal data is involved, and whose | whether the ANPD and the people affected must be told | the incident lead, as soon as personal data is possible |
| **the account's owner**, bruno | what happened to his account, and what he has to do | nothing about the incident; his own new credentials | his manager, with HR |
| **the rest of the staff** | what changes for them, such as keys-only logins | nothing | the managing partner, once the facts are settled |

Two columns are easy to get wrong. **"Hears it from"**: the investigators are not the right voice for every message,
and bruno in particular should not hear about his account from the people investigating it. And **"decides"**:
a message that asks nobody to decide anything is a report, and a message that needs a decision has to name it,
or it will not be taken.
