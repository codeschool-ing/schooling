title: Legal and compliance

**Legal hears early**, on the first morning, not when the report is written. Three reasons, each enough on its own:

- **Deadlines.** Some duties run from when the company *knew*, and lesson 21's LGPD deadline is one of them. A lawyer
  told on day five has lost most of the time to decide.
- **Words.** What is written during an incident may be read in a dispute later. Counsel can advise how findings are
  phrased and which documents are prepared at their request, which in many jurisdictions changes how they may be used.
  The details depend on the country and the case, and are the lawyer's to judge, not the analyst's.
- **Contracts.** Clients' contracts may require the company to tell them of a security incident within a fixed time,
  and a cyber insurance policy usually requires prompt notice before the insurer pays for anything. Legal and the
  managing partner know which apply.

**The DPO**, the *encarregado* the LGPD asks a controller to name, is the company's point of contact with the ANPD
and with the people whose data it holds. The DPO needs to hear the moment personal data becomes possible, not when
it is confirmed. On Thursday that is the 09:30 message: 612 MB left a file server that holds client data, so the
question is open from the start.

**Compliance** in a small company is mostly the same people wearing another hat. Its question is narrower: which
obligations the company has signed up to, such as a client's security requirements or a standard it is certified
against, and what each one says about incidents. Its answer often adds a recipient to the list in the first section.

What the security team owes all three is the same: **facts, sources, and uncertainty said plainly.** What the team
must not do is decide a legal question by default, for example by telling a client "no data was affected" before the
DPO and the lawyer have seen what lesson 17 recovered from the disk.
