---
title: Updates, and the channel
version: 1
---

After the first message, **updates come on a schedule**, even when nothing has changed. "No change since 12:00;
containment holding; next update 16:00" is a useful message: it tells the reader nothing was forgotten. Silence is
read as either "it is over" or "it is worse", and both readings cause trouble.

Every update keeps the five parts, so the reader always knows where to look, and **leads with what changed**.
Facts that turn out to be wrong are corrected explicitly, with the earlier claim named, in the shape "At 09:30 we
said X; we now know Y, because Z." A correction slipped in silently makes every other sentence doubtful.

Two rules about the channel:

- **Out of band.** Lesson 12 asked for a channel outside systems that may be compromised. On Thursday the company's
  e-mail was never in scope, but the response cannot know that on the first morning. So updates go by phone and a
  messaging group set up for the incident, and only move back to e-mail once it is known to be safe.
- **Written as if it will be read later by somebody hostile**, because it may be: in a dispute, by a regulator, or in
  court. That is not a reason to write less; it is a reason to write facts, with their sources, and to keep
  opinions and jokes out of it.

The cadence slows as the incident does: hourly on the first day, daily during recovery, and one closing message with
the link to lesson 15's postmortem.
