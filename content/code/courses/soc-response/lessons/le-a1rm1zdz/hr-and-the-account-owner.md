Bruno's account was used by somebody else. On everything the investigation found, **bruno is a victim**: his
password was guessed, he was at home, and he logged in normally in the morning. Lesson 11 kept him out of the
response team for exactly this reason, and the way he is spoken to matters as much as anything technical in this
course, for two reasons. It is the right way to treat a colleague. And a member of staff who expects to be blamed
for being phished or guessed will hide it next time, which is the most expensive outcome a SOC can have.

How the conversation goes, on Thursday:

- **Who**: his manager, with HR present. Not ana, not diego: the people investigating his account are not the
  people to explain it to him.
- **When**: before his account is locked, if possible, or the moment after, so he does not find out by being
  refused at the login screen.
- **What**: what happened to his account, in plain words; that nothing suggests he did anything wrong; what he has to
  do now, which is receive a new key in person and choose a new password; and who to ask if he has questions.
- **What he is asked**: whether he used that password anywhere else, because if he did, those accounts are at risk
  too. Asked as a question for his protection, not as an accusation.
- **What is written down**: that the conversation happened, when, and who was present. Not a transcript.

HR's role is broader than this one conversation. If an investigation ever does point at a member of staff, an
employment process begins, with rules of its own, and the security team's job is to hand over facts and step back.
Nothing on Thursday points that way, and saying so out loud, to bruno and to the managing partner, is part of the
message.
