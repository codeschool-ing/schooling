title: Relevant risk, and Thursday
version: 1
---

The first question for Thursday is easy: **personal data was involved, or possibly involved.** Two kinds of it:

- **bruno's credentials.** A password is personal data, and the intruder had his.
- **whatever the 612 MB held.** Lesson 17 recovered an export from the file server with the names and e-mail
  addresses of five client contacts, and lesson 20 left open whether it was among the bytes that left. The
  server also held client documents whose content nobody has yet listed.

The second question is the hard one. The RCIS says an incident **may cause relevant risk or damage** when it could
significantly affect the interests and fundamental rights of the people concerned **and**, at the same time, involves
at least one of these:

| criterion | Thursday |
|---|---|
| sensitive personal data, such as health, religion or biometrics | not known to be on the server |
| data of children, adolescents or elderly people | not known |
| financial data | not known; the contracts hold values, not personal accounts |
| **authentication data in systems** | **yes: bruno's password** |
| data protected by legal, judicial or professional secrecy | not known; it depends on what the client documents are |
| data on a large scale | no: one account, five contacts, one server |

So one criterion is met, and others cannot yet be ruled out. Against that: bruno's password was replaced the same
morning and he was asked whether he used it elsewhere; five business contacts' names and e-mail addresses, on their
own, are a small risk.

On Thursday, the DPO and the lawyer decide **to communicate**: authentication data was compromised, the content of
the transfer cannot be established, and communicating within the deadline with what is known, then complementing it,
is safer than defending later a decision not to. A different company, with the same facts, could reasonably decide
the other way, **and record why**. What would not be defensible is no decision at all, or a decision nobody wrote
down.
