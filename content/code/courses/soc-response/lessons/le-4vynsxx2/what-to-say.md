**To the ANPD**, the communication goes through the electronic form on the authority's website, and article 48 of the
LGPD lists what it must contain. Every item is something this course has already produced:

| the communication contains | from |
|---|---|
| a description of the **nature of the personal data** affected | lessons 17 and 20: credentials; contact names and e-mails; client documents, content not yet established |
| information about the **people involved** | one member of staff; five client contacts; others not yet known |
| the **technical and security measures** that protected the data | lessons 1 to 6: logging, the SIEM, the alert that fired |
| the **risks** related to the incident | the previous section's assessment |
| the **reasons for any delay**, if not communicated at once | none, if it goes by 22 September |
| the **measures taken** to reverse or reduce the effects | lessons 13 and 14, and lesson 15's actions |

**To the people concerned**, the communication is in **clear and simple language**, and individual where they can be
reached directly, as the five contacts can. It says what happened, what of theirs was involved, what the risk is,
what the company has done, what they can do, and who to ask. For the five contacts, it could read:

> We are writing to tell you about a security incident at our company. On the night of 17 September 2026, somebody
> outside the company gained access to one of our file servers. That server held a list with your name and your work
> e-mail address, which we kept as your company's contact with us. We have not been able to establish whether that
> list was among the data taken.
>
> Your name and work e-mail address could be used to send you messages pretending to come from us. Please be careful
> with any message asking you to open an attachment, follow a link, or confirm details, and if in doubt, call us on
> the number you already have.
>
> We closed the access the same morning and have changed how our servers can be reached. If you have any questions,
> our data protection officer can be reached at the address below.

It does not minimise ("a minor technical issue"), it does not alarm ("your data has been stolen", which is not
established), and it gives the reader **something to do**. And it is sent by the company, signed by a person, through
a channel the contacts already know, not from an address they have never seen, which would look like the very
phishing it warns about.

**Clients** may also be owed a communication by contract, on their own timetable; lesson 19's legal section is where
that was flagged. That one is the managing partner's, with the lawyer.
