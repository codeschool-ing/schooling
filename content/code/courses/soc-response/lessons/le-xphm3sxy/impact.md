**Impact** is what the facts mean for the company and for people. It is the section readers go to first, and the
one most often written badly, in one of two directions: too reassuring, or too alarming. Both come from writing
what is feared or hoped instead of what is known.

The honest form separates three levels, and says which each claim is at:

| level | means | for Thursday |
|---|---|---|
| **confirmed** | the evidence shows it directly | 612 MB left the file server for an outside address; two servers were accessed with bruno's account |
| **likely** | the evidence points to it, with a gap | the 612 MB came from the file share bruno's account could read, because that is what the account reached |
| **not established** | possible, and not shown either way | whether the deleted export of client contacts was among the 612 MB |

The third row is the important one. Lesson 17 recovered an export holding names and e-mail addresses of five client
contacts, from the same server. Whether those bytes were in the transfer cannot be shown: the transfer was not
captured, and lesson 18 explained what that costs. The report says exactly that. It does not say "client data was
stolen", which is not shown, and it does not say "no client data was affected", which is not shown either.

Impact also has a business side, stated in the same plain terms: the file server's internet access was restricted
for the rest of the week, bruno could not work for a morning, two staff spent most of three days on the response.
And the **people** side, which is lesson 21's whole subject: the five contacts in that export are people the company
may have to tell.
