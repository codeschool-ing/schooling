---
title: Rehearsing it: the tabletop exercise
version: 1
---

A plan nobody has rehearsed is a hypothesis. The cheapest test is a **tabletop exercise**: the people who
would respond sit around a table, a facilitator describes an incident step by step, and each person says
what they would do, with the plan in front of them. Nothing is touched; the gaps show up in the
conversation.

A tabletop for the lab's company, ninety minutes, built from Thursday:

| time | the facilitator says (the "inject") | the gap it is designed to find |
|---|---|---|
| 0 min | "A critical alert fired at 02:35: a login after many attempts. Who sees it?" | nobody is on call at night |
| 10 min | "It is 09:10. The account is bruno's. Bruno is in a client meeting until noon." | how to confirm with an owner who is unreachable |
| 25 min | "The file server sent 612 MB to an unknown address at 02:41." | who may authorise isolating `files` |
| 45 min | "A client calls: they received an email that seems to quote their own tax file." | who speaks to clients, and with what words |
| 60 min | "The lawyer asks: does this have to be reported, and by when?" | whether anybody knows the LGPD deadline (lesson 21) |
| 80 min | "It is 18:00. Who is still working on this tonight?" | shifts, and fatigue |

The output of a tabletop is a **list of gaps with owners and dates**, exactly like lesson 15's actions. A
good tabletop is mildly uncomfortable; one where every answer is in the plan was written too easily.
Run one at least once a year, and after any large change to the systems or the team.
