A response is a small temporary team, the **CSIRT** (computer security incident response team), assembled
around one incident. In a company the size of the lab's, the same few people wear several hats, and the
plan says which hats exist so that none is forgotten:

| role | does | at the lab's company |
|---|---|---|
| **incident lead** | runs the response, sets priorities, decides within the plan's authority | ana, with diego as deputy |
| **analysts** | investigate, collect evidence, propose containment | ana, diego |
| **scribe** | keeps the timeline and the decision log as things happen | whoever is not typing commands |
| **IT operations** | changes systems: blocks, resets, rebuilds | diego |
| **communications** | drafts what staff, clients and the public hear (lesson 19) | the managing partner, carla |
| **legal and privacy** | judges notification duties, preserves privilege (lesson 21) | the external lawyer, the DPO |
| **executive sponsor** | owns the business decisions: shut a service down, pay for help | the managing partner |

Two roles are the ones small teams skip. The **scribe**: a response without a written log of who decided
what, and when, cannot be reconstructed for lesson 20's report or defended to anybody who asks later. And
the separation between **the lead and the person typing**: whoever is deep in a terminal cannot also keep
track of what everybody else is doing.

**Bruno is not on this team for this incident**, although he is staff. His account is involved, which makes
him a witness and possibly a victim, never an investigator of his own case. Lesson 19 covers how he is
spoken to.
