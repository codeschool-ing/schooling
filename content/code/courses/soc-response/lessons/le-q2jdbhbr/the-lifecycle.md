**Incident response** is the organised handling of an incident from the moment it is suspected to the
moment the organisation has learned from it. Two models describe it, and they describe the same work.

The six-phase version, often attributed to SANS and remembered as **PICERL**, splits the work finely, and
this course follows it: preparation here, identification in lesson 12, containment in 13, eradication and
recovery in 14, lessons learned in 15. **NIST SP 800-61 Revision 2** grouped it into four phases, and for a
decade it was the reference most plans were written against.

In April 2025 NIST published **Revision 3**, which supersedes Revision 2 and drops the four-phase cycle as
its organising idea. It describes incident response as part of the organisation's whole cybersecurity
risk management, mapped onto the functions of the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0**: Govern, Identify,
Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. The practical message is that preparation is not a phase that
happens once before incidents, but governance and protection that run all the time. The phases remain
the clearest way to teach and to run a response, which is why they organise these five lessons.

Two habits apply across every phase. **Phases overlap**: containment often starts before identification is
finished, because a P1 cannot wait for a complete picture. And **the loop is the point**: what lesson 15
learns becomes the next version of what this lesson prepares, or the same incident happens twice.
