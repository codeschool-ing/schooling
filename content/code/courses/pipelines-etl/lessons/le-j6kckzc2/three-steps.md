title: Three steps, and the one question about them

Every pipeline in this course does three things, and the names are older than most of the tools:

- **Extract** — read data out of a system that somebody else owns. The shop's database, an API, a
  file. Extraction is a guest in that system: it should take what it needs and leave nothing
  behind, least of all a slow till.
- **Transform** — turn what was read into what somebody wants to ask. Drop the cancelled orders,
  join each line to its book, add the lines up by day and category. Most of a pipeline's logic,
  and most of its bugs, live here.
- **Load** — write the result into the destination, here the warehouse `wh`, in a way its readers
  never see half-done.

**The one question about them is where the transformation happens**, and the two answers have
names:

- **ETL** transforms *before* loading. The data is extracted, reshaped on the way — in a Python
  program, an ETL tool, a server in the middle — and only the finished result reaches the
  warehouse.
- **ELT** loads *before* transforming. The data is copied into the warehouse as it is, and the
  transformation is SQL that runs inside the warehouse, on the warehouse's processors.

The letters are the same, and so is the answer, as this lesson shows by building it both ways and
comparing every row. **What moves is the cost**: what crosses the network, which machine does the
work, and what you can still do next Thursday when somebody asks a question nobody planned for.

That last one decides it more often than speed. Lessons 4 to 7 take the three steps one at a
time; this lesson is about the order.
