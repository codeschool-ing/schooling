---
title: Facts that point at the right version
version: 1
---

A type 2 dimension is useless unless the facts point at the right version of it, and that is the
job of the **surrogate key**: `customer_key`, generated by the warehouse, one per version.
`customer_id` says *which customer*; `customer_key` says *which customer, living where, at the
moment of the sale*.

The fact load finds it with a join that is about time as much as identity — the version whose
`valid_from` is at or before the order, and whose `valid_to` is after it or open:

```sql
  LEFT JOIN marts.dim_customer d
         ON d.customer_id = o.customer_id
        AND o.ordered_at >= d.valid_from
        AND (o.ordered_at < d.valid_to OR d.valid_to IS NULL)
```

Customer 3145 bought a book on 11 March, before the move:

```
ana@vm:~/etl$ psql -d wh -c "SELECT f.order_date, f.order_id, d.city FROM marts.fact_sales f JOIN marts.dim_customer d USING (customer_key) WHERE d.customer_id = 3145 ORDER BY f.order_date"
 order_date | order_id |   city    
------------+----------+-----------
 2026-03-11 |   119940 | São Paulo
(1 row)
```

The sale is in São Paulo, and it will still be in São Paulo next year, whatever the customer does.
**That is the whole point of type 2, and it only works because the key was looked up on the night
the fact was loaded**, while the version that was true then was the current one. Reloading 11 March
today would find the same version, by the same `valid_from` and `valid_to` — which is why the join is
on time and not on `is_current`.

## The facts with no customer

```
ana@vm:~/etl$ psql -d wh -c "SELECT count(*) FROM marts.dim_customer WHERE customer_id = 1880"
 count 
-------
     0
(1 row)

ana@vm:~/etl$ psql -d wh -c "SELECT customer_key = -1 AS unknown, count(*) FROM marts.fact_sales GROUP BY 1"
 unknown | count 
---------+-------
 f       |  4537
 t       |  1890
(2 rows)
```

Nearly a third of the order lines carry `customer_key = -1`: no customer the warehouse can name.
Two kinds of sale end up there, for different reasons:

- **anonymous sales** — a customer at a till who gave no name. The shop has `NULL`, and the
  warehouse has nobody to point at;
- **sales of customers who asked to be erased.** The shop set their orders' `customer_id` to
  `NULL`, and the dimension load deleted every version of them: customer 1880 has no rows left.

**`-1` is a value, not a `NULL`, on purpose.** A report that joins facts to customers with an inner
join silently drops every fact whose key is `NULL`, and revenue falls by a third. With `-1` and a
row in the dimension to match it — "unknown customer" — the join keeps the sales and labels them
honestly. `warehouse-modeling` calls that row the unknown member; the load is where it earns its
keep.

The erasure is the same decision lesson 2 made for e-mail addresses, one layer up: the shop
forgot the customer, so the warehouse must too. **History in a type 2 dimension is not a reason
to keep a person the law says to delete.** Their sales stay, attached to nobody.
