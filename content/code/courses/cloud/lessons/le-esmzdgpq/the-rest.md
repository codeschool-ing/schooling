Six names in a lesson title leave out most of the market. The providers below are not smaller
versions of the big three; **each one exists because of a reason that a particular kind of customer
has**, and knowing the reason is enough to know when you will meet it.

## Oracle Cloud Infrastructure

Oracle's cloud is strongest where a company already runs **Oracle Database**, which is a great many
banks, insurers and governments. Oracle sells its own database there as a managed service in several
forms, including an Autonomous Database that tunes and patches itself. It sells the ordinary core as well:
virtual machines, block and object storage, networks, Kubernetes. It has a region in São Paulo.

## IBM Cloud

IBM's cloud serves customers who already depend on IBM, and the thread that holds it together is
**Red Hat**, which IBM bought in 2019. Red Hat's OpenShift, a Kubernetes platform, runs on IBM's
cloud and on the others, and IBM sells it as the way to run the same platform in a company's own
data centre and in the cloud. That is the hybrid arrangement of lesson 2, sold as a product.

## Alibaba Cloud

Alibaba Cloud belongs to the Chinese Alibaba group. It sells a catalogue on the scale of the
hyperscalers. Its reason for being on this list is geography: **a company that has to serve customers inside mainland China** meets it first. Operating there brings rules of its own, and a provider based there is built around them.

## OVHcloud

OVHcloud is French, founded in 1999, and is Europe's nearest equivalent of Hetzner at a larger size:
dedicated servers, virtual machines, object storage, and a growing list of managed services, in data
centres it builds itself. Its argument to European buyers is **jurisdiction**. A European company
answers to European law, while the three hyperscalers are American companies, subject to American
law wherever their data centres are. For organisations that treat that as a risk, it is the whole
decision. Lesson 2 discussed why the law of the place matters as much as the place.

## Cloudflare

Cloudflare is a different kind of provider, and it belongs here so that you do not look for the
wrong thing in it. **You do not rent a virtual machine from Cloudflare.** It runs a large network in
front of other people's servers, protecting and caching them. On that network it runs your code at
the edge (Workers, lesson 8), stores objects (R2, which is S3-compatible and charges nothing for
data leaving it), and keeps small databases. Platforms such as Vercel and Netlify sit at the same
level, above the clouds rather than beside them; lesson 8 is theirs too.

## Brazilian providers

Brazil has providers of its own. **Magalu Cloud**, from the retailer Magazine Luiza, is one. The
argument for a national provider is the one Hetzner makes in Germany and OVHcloud makes in France:
a local company, local law, data in the country, and a bill in reais rather than in dollars. That
last point is not small. A bill in dollars moves with the exchange rate, so the same month of the
same machines costs a Brazilian company a different amount in reais every month even when nothing
in the list price changed.

The names matter less than the question behind each paragraph, and it works on any provider you
meet: **what reason does it exist for, and is that reason yours?**
