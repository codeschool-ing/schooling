title: Choosing the threshold from what mistakes cost

**The threshold is a business decision**, taken with the model's probabilities and the costs of each
kind of mistake. The program below uses three cost figures that are invented for the example; in real
use they come from finance and from the results of past campaigns, ideally an A/B test of the offer
itself.

As the threshold falls from 0.6 to 0.1, the model flags more customers, from 217 to 800; **recall
climbs from 43.2% to 79.1% and precision falls from 83.9% to 41.6%**. The campaign's value is highest at
a threshold of 0.2, R$ 27,930, against R$ 22,830 at the default of 0.5.

Two things decide where the peak falls, and both are worth saying out loud.

- **The ratio of the costs.** With a cheap offer and a valuable customer, a low threshold pays: many
  false alarms are worth one extra churner kept. If the offer were an expensive gift, the peak would
  move up.
- **The save rate is an assumption, and the most fragile number here.** One churner in four kept by
  an offer is a guess until an experiment measures it. Running the campaign as an A/B test, offer
  against no offer among flagged customers, is how the guess becomes a number, and lessons 7 to 11 are
  how to read it.

The general lesson: **a model gives probabilities; people choose what to do with them**, and that
choice should be written down with the costs that justified it.
