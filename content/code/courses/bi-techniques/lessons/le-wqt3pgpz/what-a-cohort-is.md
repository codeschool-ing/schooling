---
title: What a cohort is, and why averages mislead
version: 1
---

**A cohort is a group of people who share a starting event in the same period**: everybody who
signed up in January 2024, everybody whose first order came during Black Friday, everybody who
arrived through a referral in the spring. The analysis then follows each group forward from its own
start, so that people are compared at the same age rather than on the same date.

The reason is that most customer numbers mix two things. "62 per cent of our customers ordered this
month" depends on how loyal customers are **and** on how many of them joined recently, because new
customers have not had time to leave. A business that doubles its sign-ups looks more loyal by this
measure while every individual customer behaves exactly as before. Lesson 1 called the same problem
the trend leaking into the season: one number carrying two movements.

## Two choices define the group

**The starting event.** For Panela the natural one is the signup date, and since every customer
places their first order on the day they sign up, signup and first order coincide. In many businesses
they do not: an app install, a free trial and a first payment are three different starts, and each
defines a different cohort with a different story.

**The period.** Monthly cohorts are the common choice: big enough to have a few hundred people each
at Panela's size, small enough to see change from month to month. Weekly cohorts show faster change
with more noise; quarterly ones are smoother and slower to react.

A cohort can also be cut by a property fixed at the start: the channel that brought the customer,
the region, the first plan they chose. Lesson 13 compares Panela's channels that way. **The property
must be fixed at the starting event**: cutting by "customers who ordered more than ten times" builds
groups out of the very behaviour being measured, and it will always find that those customers stayed.
