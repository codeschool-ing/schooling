The standard output of a cohort analysis is a table with **one row per cohort and one column per
age**. Each cell answers: of the people who started in this period, what share was active this many
periods later?

```schooling-example

```

Read it in three directions.

**Along a row**, one cohort ages. The January 2024 cohort of 272 customers had 88 per cent ordering
in month 1, 70 in month 3, 49 in month 6 and 25 a year on. Month 0 is always 100, because
everybody orders in the month they sign up; it is there to anchor the row.

**Down a column**, cohorts are compared at the same age. Month 6 ranges from 46 to 54 across the
twelve cohorts of 2024, without a direction: no cohort is clearly better or worse than the others,
so whatever Panela did during 2024 did not change how long customers stayed. Lesson 13 shows a column
that does move.

**Along a diagonal**, from bottom left to top right, cells share a calendar month. A holiday, an
outage or a price change hits a diagonal, not a column, and that is how calendar effects are told
apart from changes in customer behaviour.
