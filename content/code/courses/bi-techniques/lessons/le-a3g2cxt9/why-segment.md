---
title: What a segment is for
version: 1
---

**A segment is a group of customers who will be treated differently from the others.** That last
clause is the definition, and it is the one most often forgotten. A segmentation that produces seven
elegant groups which the business then emails identically is a chart, not a segmentation.

The groups of lessons 12 to 14 were defined by one property known in advance: the month someone
joined, the channel they came from, the device they used. Segmentation asks a different question:
**which customers behave alike now**, using their own history. The answer changes as they do, so a
segmentation is recomputed regularly, and a customer moves between segments.

Two families of method cover almost every case.

- **Rules**, of which RFM is the classic: compute a few numbers per customer, score them, and name
  groups by the combinations of scores. Every segment can be explained in one sentence, and the
  rules are the same next month.
- **Clustering**: give an algorithm the numbers and let it find groups that are internally similar
  and different from each other. It can find structure nobody thought of; its groups need a name and
  an explanation that the algorithm does not provide.

Whichever is used, three questions decide whether it is any good:

1. **Is each segment big enough to act on?** A segment of twelve customers does not justify a
   campaign.
2. **Can the team do something different for each one?** Two segments that would receive the same
   offer are one segment.
3. **Is it stable?** A customer who changes segment every week cannot be followed by a campaign that
   takes a month to run.
