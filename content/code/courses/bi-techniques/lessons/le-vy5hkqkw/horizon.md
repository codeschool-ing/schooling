title: The further ahead, the less you know

The common picture is that a forecast's quality is a property of the method: a good model forecasts
well. **It is a property of the method and the horizon together.** The same model is better a week
ahead than a year ahead, for two reasons that add up. Small errors in the trend are multiplied by
the number of weeks they are carried forward. And the further ahead you look, the more time there is
for something to happen that the past did not contain.

This forecasts every week of 2025 from the end of 2024, once with the trend from lesson 3 and once
with a **damped trend**, which assumes that growth slowly flattens rather than carrying on in a
straight line forever:

**For six months both forecasts are good**, within about 5 per cent a week on average. Then the error climbs:
9.2% in the third quarter and 21.1% in the fourth, for the straight trend. The cause is not in the
method. Panela raised its prices on 1 September 2025 and orders dropped, and no model fitted on
2023 and 2024 could know that would happen. The longer the horizon, the more such events it
contains.

**The damped trend did better far out**, 14.0% in the last quarter, because its growth was already
slowing when the price rise came. That is a common result rather than luck: a straight trend
extended for a year assumes growth never slows, which is rarely true of a business, and damping is
the cheap insurance against it. Forecasting research has found damped trends to be among the most
reliable simple methods for that reason.

What this means for a report: a forecast for next month and a forecast for next December should
never be printed in the same font with the same confidence. The next section is how to say how
confident.
