**A prediction interval is a range that the future value should fall in with a stated probability.**
"Between 8,528 and 9,341 orders, with 80% probability" is a forecast someone can plan with: the
kitchen can order for the top of the range and accept waste, or for the middle and accept the risk
of running out. "8,934 orders" alone hides the choice.

Two intervals are easily confused, and statsmodels names its method after the wrong one.

- A **confidence interval** is about a quantity that is fixed and unknown, such as the average
  orders on a Monday in 2024. More data shrinks it towards zero width.
- A **prediction interval** is about a single future value, which will carry its own noise
  whatever happens. More data shrinks it only so far: it can never be narrower than the noise
  itself.

`conf_int` on a forecast returns a prediction interval, despite its name. That is what this
program uses:

The interval is about 800 orders wide, about 400 either side of the forecast. On paper, four
weeks in five should land inside it.

## An interval is a claim you can check

**16 of the 26 weeks fell inside the 80% interval, which is 62 per cent, not 80%.** The 95% interval held
21 of 26, 81 per cent. Both are too narrow. With 26 weeks a small shortfall could be chance, but not
one this size in both intervals at once.

The reasons are worth knowing, because they apply to almost every interval software prints:

- **It assumes the model is right.** The interval covers the noise the model expects, not the
  chance that the model itself is wrong, and the Carnival week of lesson 3 was a model error.
- **It assumes the parameters are exact.** They were estimated from two years and carry their own
  uncertainty, which this interval leaves out.
- **It assumes the future behaves like the past.** The price rise of September is the extreme case
  and is not even in these 26 weeks.

So printed intervals are usually optimistic, and the honest practice is to **check their coverage
on weeks the model did not see**, as above, before showing them to anybody. If an 80% interval
covers 60% in a back-test, widen it until it covers 80%, and say that you did.
