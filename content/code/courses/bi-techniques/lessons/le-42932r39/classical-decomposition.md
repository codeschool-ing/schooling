**The classical decomposition is three steps, and each is something you have already met.** First,
a centred moving average whose window is the season's period removes the season and the noise,
and what is left is the trend. Second, the series divided by the trend leaves season and noise;
averaging each position of the season across years leaves the season. Third, the series divided by
trend and season leaves the residual.

statsmodels does all three in one call. Panela's seasons are a week and a year; on weekly totals the
week is gone, as lesson 1 showed for months, and only the year remains to take out:

Three things in that output are worth stopping on.

**The trend stops 26 weeks short of each end.** It runs from 9 July 2023 to 29 June 2025, and the last six months
of 2025, the ones a manager asks about first, have no trend at all. That is the price of a centred
window, paid at both ends. It is also why the trend in the figure misses the price rise of September
2025: the step happens in the half-year the trend cannot see.

**The seasonal index reads as a multiplier.** The first week of the year is 0.812: at the trend's
level, that week takes about 19 per cent fewer orders. The week before Christmas is 1.104, about
10 per cent more. Every year gets the same index for the same week, which is what "the season
repeats" means in arithmetic.

**Only two years of the season could be measured.** With the first and last 26 weeks missing a
trend, each week of the year was seen at most twice, sometimes once. A seasonal index averaged from
two values is a fragile thing, and the residuals of the next section show where it broke.
