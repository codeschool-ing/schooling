**A moving average replaces each value with the mean of a window of values around it.** It is the
oldest smoother there is and still the one every decomposition is built on, because of one
property: average over exactly one period of a season and that season cancels. Seven days hold one
Monday and one Saturday, so a seven-day average has no weekly rhythm left in it.

The common picture is that a moving average is a single thing. It is two, and they answer
different questions. Run this to see both on the days around Black Friday 2024:

**The trailing average looks back.** Each value is the mean of the day and the six before it, so it
can be computed the moment a day closes. That is why every dashboard uses it, and why it lags: the
Black Friday spike of 29 November enters the trailing column that day and stays in it until
5 December, which is not in this table but follows from the window. A trailing average describes
the week that has just finished, and it reaches a turn in the series three or four days late.

**The centred average sits on the day.** Each value is the mean of three days before, the day and
three after. The spike now appears from 26 November to 2 December, three days either side, and the
smoothed line no longer drifts behind the data. The price is that the last three days of any series
have no centred value, because their future has not happened yet.

Use the trailing average for monitoring, where only the past exists. Use the centred one for
analysis, where the point is to say what the level *was* on each day. A decomposition is analysis,
and it centres.

## The window is the period

A seven-day window kills the weekly season because seven is its period. A five-day window would
not: it holds a Monday in some weeks and a Saturday in others, and the rhythm leaks through as a
wobble. **The length of the window is not a smoothing knob, it is the season you are removing.**
Weekly data with a yearly season needs a window of 52 weeks; monthly data needs 12.

An even window cannot be centred on a day: twelve months have no middle month. The standard fix is a
**2×12 moving average**, a twelve-month average followed by a two-month average of the result, which
puts half a weight on each end month. pandas does not do it for you; statsmodels does, inside the
decomposition of the next sections.
