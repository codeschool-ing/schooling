Four readings, in the order a careful analyst makes them.

**The baselines are bad here, and the reason is the trend.** The naive forecast repeats the last
week of 2024, a holiday week; the seasonal naive one repeats the first half of 2024, when the
business was about a fifth smaller. Both biases are large and negative: −1,864 and −1,605 orders, too
low every week. On a series without growth the seasonal naive forecast would be the one to beat;
on this one it is easy to beat, and that is worth knowing before anyone is impressed.

**Simple smoothing is in between**, 10.8 per cent, because it has a level but no season and no
trend: right on average around spring, wrong in the same direction at both ends.

**Holt-Winters and seasonal ARIMA are close, and they disagree about which is better.** ARIMA has
the smaller MAE, 362.0 against 366.0. Holt-Winters has the smaller RMSE, 462.3 against 501.8. Both
can be true at once: ARIMA is a little closer on most weeks and further out on a few, and RMSE
punishes those few. **Which number decides depends on what a miss costs.** If an extra hundred
orders of ingredients is a hundred orders of cost, MAE is the cost. If one badly missed week means
customers without boxes and a news story, RMSE is closer to the cost.

**Both over-forecast**, with biases of about 200 and 270 orders a week. Bias that size is small
beside the MAE, and it is still the first thing to investigate, because it is the only kind of
error you can remove by subtraction.
