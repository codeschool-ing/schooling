**A time series is a measurement taken again and again at regular intervals**: orders per day,
revenue per month, visitors per hour. The order of the rows is part of the data. Shuffle a table
of customers and nothing is lost; shuffle a time series and the thing you came to study is gone.

The common picture of a series is one movement: the number goes up, or down, and the job is to say
which. The figure below is Panela's orders for every day from 2023 to 2025, and it holds at least
four movements at once.

**Trend** is the direction the series takes over a long stretch, once the repetitions are averaged
away. Panela took 351,453 orders in 2023, 450,836 in 2024 and 505,927 in 2025. The second rise is
smaller than the first, so the trend is growth that slows. It is not a straight line, and nothing
says it has to be.

**Seasonality** is a pattern that repeats with a **fixed period tied to the calendar**. Panela has
two. Across the week, a Monday averages 1,401 orders and a Saturday 1,028, because people order on
Monday for the week ahead. Across the year, January is the quiet month of the Brazilian summer
holidays and the months towards December are busier. A season has a length you can name before you
look at the data: seven days, twelve months, twenty-four hours.

**Cycle** is a rise and fall that repeats **without a fixed period**: an economy expanding and
contracting over several years, a market filling up and emptying. Nobody can say when the next
turn comes by looking at a calendar, and that is the whole difference from a season.

**Noise** is what is left once the other three are accounted for. Two Mondays in the same month
differ by a few dozen orders for reasons nobody recorded: rain, a football match, a delivery van
that broke down. Noise is not a mistake in the measurement. It is the part of the series that no
pattern explains, and a good model leaves behind noise that has no pattern of its own.

## Why bother separating them

Each movement answers a different question, and mixing them gives wrong answers to all of them.

- **"Are we growing?"** is a question about the trend. Compare December with July and the season
  answers instead: December is always busier.
- **"Was last Monday good?"** is a question about the noise, once trend and season have been
  removed. Mondays in January 2023 ran from 883 to 938 orders and Mondays in December 2025 from
  1,422 to 1,771, so 1,350 is a poor Monday in one month and a remarkable one in the other.
- **"How many boxes do we pack next week?"** needs all of them, put back together in the right
  proportions.

The rest of the time-series half of the course is that separation done properly: lesson 2 takes
the series apart, lessons 3 to 5 forecast it and measure how wrong the forecast was, and lesson 6
covers the things that break both.
