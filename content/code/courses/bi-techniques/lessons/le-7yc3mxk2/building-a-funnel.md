**A funnel counts how many people reach each step of a journey that has an order.** Panela's has
five steps: a session lands on the site, opens the menu, chooses a box, reaches checkout and pays.
`sessions.csv` holds every visit of March 2025, with the furthest step each one reached. It covers
everybody who visited, existing customers included, so its rates are not the first-order conversion
of the A/B lessons.

Two rates come out of every funnel, and they answer different questions.

- **Of the previous step** is the step's own conversion: of the sessions that chose a box, 71.1 per
  cent reached checkout. It is where to look for a problem, because it isolates one step.
- **Of landing** is the cumulative conversion: 14.4 per cent of all sessions ended in a payment. It
  is the number to report upwards, and it hides where the losses are.

## The biggest drop is not the problem

The steepest step of the whole funnel is from the menu to choosing a box: only 55.2 per cent go on.
**The reflex is to fix the biggest drop.** But many people open a meal-kit menu to see what is on it
this week, decide they are not cooking, and leave. That is a steep step doing its job, and no redesign
will make browsers into buyers.

The device breakdown shows the step that is broken. Desktop and mobile behave the same at every step
**except one**: from checkout to paid, 82.8 per cent of desktop sessions finish and only 48.3 per cent
of mobile ones. People who reach checkout have chosen a box and want it; losing half of them on phones
and one in six on laptops is not a difference in intent, it is a difference in the form.

**A problem is a step that is worse than it should be, not a step that is steep.** Comparisons are
how "should be" is found: one device against another, this month against last, one region against
the others. A step that differs between groups that otherwise behave alike is where to look.
