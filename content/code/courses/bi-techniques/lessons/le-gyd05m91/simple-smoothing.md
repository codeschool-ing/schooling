title: Weighting the recent past
version: 1

Two forecasts need no method at all, and every method has to beat them. **The naive forecast**
repeats the last value: next week will be like this week. **The mean forecast** averages
everything: next week will be like the average week. The first trusts one noisy week entirely; the
second trusts a week from 2023 as much as last week. Each is right about something the other gets
wrong.

**Simple exponential smoothing** keeps a single number, the *level*, and updates it every week:

`level = alpha × this week + (1 − alpha) × previous level`

`alpha` is between 0 and 1. Unroll the update and last week has weight `alpha`, the week before
`alpha × (1 − alpha)`, the one before that `alpha × (1 − alpha)²`, and so on: every week counts, and
each counts a fixed fraction less than the week after it. That geometric fading is the
*exponential* in the name. With `alpha` at 1 it is the naive forecast; close to 0 it approaches the
mean.

**The choice of alpha is a choice of memory.** With 0.9 the level after the last week of 2024 is
8,033, close to that week's 7,743, a holiday week; with 0.1 it is 9,171, an average of many weeks
that barely notices the holidays. statsmodels picked 0.484 by trying values and keeping the one
whose one-week-ahead errors were smallest.

And it forecast **the same number, 8,893, for every week of 2025**. That is not a bug. Simple
smoothing has one component, the level, and no idea that the series grows or that January is quiet;
its forecast for any future week is the level now. On Panela's data it will be wrong in a
predictable way, too low by summer and too high in January. The next section gives it the two
missing pieces.
