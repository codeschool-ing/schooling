Lesson 18 traced the bias to two inputs, recency and the order count, which read a slow delivery
rhythm as a customer fading away. The fix measures each customer against **their own** rhythm: eleven
days of silence from somebody who orders every fourteen is on time, and the same eleven days from
somebody who orders every seven is late.

**The North's false-alarm rate falls from 22.0 per cent to 10.0**, inside the range of the other
regions, 6.2 to 10.3. Its margin, 5.9 points, now overlaps all three of theirs.

And the fix cost nothing overall. At the same threshold of 0.2, lesson 17's model had a precision of
58.9 per cent and a recall of 67.0; this one has 59.2 and 67.9. **The model got slightly better for
everybody** while getting much fairer for the North. That is not a coincidence: the old inputs were
measuring something irrelevant, geography, and that was noise for the question of who leaves. Removing
noise helps a model.

It will not always go this well. Often a fairer model is a little less accurate overall, and then the
trade has to be named and decided by somebody with the authority to decide it, not quietly accepted or
quietly refused by the analyst.

Two more things to notice.

- **Region is still not an input.** The fix did not tell the model who lives in the North; it gave the
  model a measure that means the same thing wherever the customer lives.
- **The North's recall moved from 58.8 to 47.1 per cent**, ten people to eight out of 17. On 17
  people that is two customers, and it cannot be read as a change in either direction. It does mean
  the North is worth watching as more data arrives, which is the subject of the monitoring section.
