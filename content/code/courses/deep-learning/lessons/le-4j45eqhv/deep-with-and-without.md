Lesson 3 trained a network with one hidden layer and lesson 7 a wide one. **Depth is where
normalisation shows**, because without it every layer passes its scale to the next and the effects
multiply through the stack. The program below builds ten hidden layers of 64 units, trains them for
20 epochs at four rates, and takes one word to say whether a normalisation layer goes in each block.
Save as `~/dl/deep.py`:

      "code": "def make(depth=10, width=64):\n    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)\n    layers, n_in = [], 64\n    for _ in range(depth):\n        layers.append(Linear(n_in, width, rng))\n        if Norm:\n            layers.append(Norm(width))\n        layers.append(ReLU())\n        n_in = width\n    return Net(*layers, Linear(n_in, 10, rng))",

**Without normalisation, only the smallest rate trains.** At 0.03 it reaches 0.961. At 0.1 it
climbs to 0.867 by epoch 10, falls to 0.356 by epoch 15 and ends at 0.669, which is the shape
lesson 6 taught you to read as a rate too high. At 0.3 it never passes 0.422. At 1.0 the training
loss is `nan`: the numbers overflowed, and the accuracy never moves from 0.092, no better than a
guess.

**With batch norm, every rate trains.** At 0.3 it is at 0.914 after one epoch and finishes at 0.975,
the best result in either table, and even 1.0 ends at 0.961. At the smallest rate the gain is
smaller, 0.969 against 0.961 at the end, though it is ahead the whole way.

That is the practical meaning of "makes training stable". **Batch norm does not make a good rate
better so much as it makes a large rate safe**, and larger steps are fewer steps. The reason is the
property at the end of the previous section. Without normalisation, a step that enlarges the weights
of layer 3 enlarges the sums of layers 4 to 10 as well. With it, each layer starts from mean 0 and
variance 1 whatever happened before it.

It is not free:

- it adds two parameters per unit, 1,280 here, and three more operations to every layer, forward and
  backward;
- it ties the examples of a batch together, so one image's output depends on which others came with
  it, and batches of a few examples give noisy statistics;
- it behaves differently in training and in evaluation, and the next section is what happens when
  that difference is forgotten.
