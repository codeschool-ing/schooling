title: Sliding windows, in the Kafka Streams sense
version: 1
---

**In Kafka Streams, a sliding window is not anchored to the clock at all: two events share a
window if their times differ by no more than the window's size.** There are no starts at
multiples of anything. The windows that exist are the ones the events themselves define.

The question it answers is the one hopping windows answer approximately: what is the most that
happened in any five minutes? A hopping window of five minutes advancing every minute would get
close, and would still miss a burst from 09:01:20 to 09:06:20, since no window starts at 09:01:20.
Making the advance a second, or a millisecond, gets closer and multiplies the cost. The sliding
window turns the question round: only a moment when an event enters or leaves can change what is
in "the last five minutes", so those are the only windows worth computing.

This program computes the windows that **end** at each sale: for every sale at `t`, the window
from `t − 5 minutes` to `t`, with every sale in it, **both edges included**:

```
ubuntu@stream:~/work$ python windows.py sliding 5
```

Ten windows, one per sale. The busiest hold four sales, and there are three of them: the windows
ending at 09:05:00, at 09:06:20 and at 09:08:50. The one ending at 09:05:00 starts at 09:00:00
and holds sale 1 at 09:00:40 as well as sale 4 on its end: with both edges inside, a sale exactly
five minutes after another shares a window with it, which a half-open tumbling window would not
allow. **Inclusive at both ends is Kafka Streams' definition**, and it is the main thing to check
when comparing its sliding windows with anybody else's.

Kafka Streams goes one step further than this program. A window's contents also change when an
event **leaves** it, five minutes after it happened, so Kafka Streams creates a second window for
each record, starting just after it, and updates both as records arrive. The program keeps to the
windows ending at a sale, which are enough for "the most in any five minutes" and short enough
to read.

## When to use which

| question | window |
|---|---|
| a figure per fixed period, to sum or chart | tumbling |
| a figure over the last N minutes, refreshed on a schedule | hopping |
| the most, or the least, in any N minutes; whether two events came within N of each other | sliding |

The last row is where sliding windows earn their keep. "A card used twice within ten minutes" is
a sliding-window question: two events whose times differ by at most ten minutes, wherever the
clock's ten-minute marks happen to fall. Tumbling ten-minute windows miss a pair at 09:09 and 09:12,
which straddle 09:10. Hopping windows advancing every five minutes catch that pair and miss others:
09:01 and 09:10:30 are nine and a half minutes apart, and neither 09:00 to 09:10 nor 09:05 to 09:15
holds both. Only a sliding window catches every pair.
