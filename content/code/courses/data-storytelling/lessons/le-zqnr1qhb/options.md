A recommendation is stronger when the reader can see **what else was considered and why it lost**. One
option presented alone invites the question "what about…?"; three options compared, with one chosen,
answer it in advance and leave the reader a real decision.

## Faro's options

| option | cost | expected effect | main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **do nothing** | none now | none | about 510 early cancellations a year continue, about R$ 790 thousand of margin |
| **remove the manual check** for addresses the postcode lookup confirms | no new spending | removes the day the check uses | some boxes go to a mistyped address |
| **promise three days** instead of two for first orders | none | late boxes become on-time by definition | a weaker offer at checkout, and the customer still waits |
| **express service** from Ligeiro for every first box | about R$ 110 thousand a year | faster delivery, check still in place | the cost recurs every year, and the cause stays |

Laid out like this, the choice almost makes itself, which is the point. **Removing the check is the only
option that treats the cause** at no new cost; its risk is real and measurable. Promising three days moves
the goalposts: the customer waits as long as before, and only the measure improves, which lesson 12 has a
name for. The express service works, and it pays every year for a step that need not exist.

## Rules for an honest options table

- **Include doing nothing.** It is always an option and usually the default, and its cost is the strongest
  argument for acting.
- **Use the same columns for every option**, and fill every cell. An empty "risk" cell on the option you
  prefer is the first thing a sceptic will notice.
- **Make the alternatives real.** An option nobody would choose, added to make the favourite look good, is
  furniture, as lesson 2 called evidence nobody needs; readers spot it and discount the whole table.
- **Say which one you recommend, and why, in one sentence under the table.** The reader asked for your
  judgement, not only your arithmetic.

## When the reader prefers another option

Sometimes Paulo will look at the table and pick the express service, because he trusts the address check
more than you do. **That is the table working**, not failing: the decision was made with the costs and
risks in view. Your job was to make the choice informed, not to win it.
