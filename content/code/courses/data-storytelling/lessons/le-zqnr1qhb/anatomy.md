---
title: Five parts of a recommendation
version: 1
---

An actionable recommendation answers five questions. **Leave any of them out and the reader has to fill it
in**, and lesson 1 showed what a room does with gaps: fills them with the default, which is nothing.

| part | the question | Faro |
|---|---|---|
| **action** | what changes, exactly? | remove the manual check for addresses the postcode lookup confirms |
| **owner** | who does it? | Sandra's team |
| **time** | from when, until when? | from 1 September, eight weeks, review 27 October |
| **effect** | how much difference should it make? | late first deliveries in the interior from 23.6% to 8% or less |
| **measure** | how will anybody know? | the weekly first-delivery rate, already on the dashboard from lesson 7 |

## Each part, and how it fails

- **Action** fails by being a direction rather than a change: *improve*, *optimise*, *review*. An action
  is something a person could start on Monday.
- **Owner** fails by being a department ("logistics") or plural ("the teams involved"). A named team with
  a named lead is an owner; a function is an alibi.
- **Time** fails by being missing, which is the commonest omission of all, because naming a date feels
  presumptuous. It is the opposite: a date is what tells the owner this is a priority.
- **Effect** fails by being unquantified ("should improve retention") or overstated. The next sections size
  it honestly.
- **Measure** fails by being something nobody collects yet. Faro is lucky: the measure is already on the
  dashboard. When it is not, setting it up is part of the recommendation.

## A sixth part: why

A recommendation also carries **its reason, in one clause**, pointing back at the evidence: *because late
first boxes more than double early cancellations*. In a presentation the reason was the whole argument
before it; on a page or in an email, the clause is what lets a reader agree without scrolling up.

## Rewriting a weak one

Take the line from the start of this lesson: *first deliveries are often late, and this may affect
retention*. Run it through the five questions and it becomes Faro's recommendation. **Do the same now to
the last line of your own analysis**, in `my-analysis.txt`, and keep both versions: the before is as
instructive as the after.
