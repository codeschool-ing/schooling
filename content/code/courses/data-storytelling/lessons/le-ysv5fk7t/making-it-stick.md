The meeting that approves a change is where analysts tend to stop paying attention, and **it is where most
changes begin to fail**. Procedures drift back, the new measure slips off the agenda, the people who drove it
move on. Making a change last is the R of ADKAR, and it is work.

## What the pilot showed

Over the eight weeks, the interior's share of late first deliveries fell from 23.6% before the pilot to
16.5% in the first week, then 10.8% and 9.5%, and **stayed at or under 8% from the fourth week on**, ending
at 7.1%. In the capital, where the check stayed, it moved between 12.2% and 14.0%, around its usual level. The
guardrail held: wrong-address returns stayed between 0.56% and 0.84% of first orders, under the 1% agreed in
advance.

On 27 October, Paulo decided to extend the change to the capital from November. The decision took ten
minutes, because the success criterion, the guardrail and the decision rule had been written before the
pilot started.

## What keeps it

- **The measure stays visible.** The first-delivery rate stays in the top-left corner of the dashboard, with
  its target, and in the board's quarterly measures as lesson 4 asked.
- **It enters somebody's targets.** From the next year, the logistics team's objectives include the
  first-delivery rate, not only the overall one. A measure nobody is accountable for drifts.
- **A check at thirty, sixty and ninety days.** Marina scheduled three short reviews after the roll-out. The
  ninety-day one matters most, because it is the first time the lagging measure, cancellations of customers
  whose first box came after the change, can be read.
- **A name on what to do if it slips.** If the rate rises above 10% for two weeks running, Sandra's team
  checks whether the lookup has stopped confirming addresses, before anybody blames the drivers.

## The analyst's last job

The analyst's last job on a change is to **close the loop**: say, with data, whether the thing that was
supposed to happen did. For Faro, that means the cancellation rate of the autumn cohorts, read in early 2026.
If it fell, the story that started in lesson 1 has an ending. If it did not, the next analysis starts there,
and lesson 12's habit of saying what would change your mind is what makes that result useful rather than
embarrassing.
