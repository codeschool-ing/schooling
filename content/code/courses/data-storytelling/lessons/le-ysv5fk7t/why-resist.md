---
title: Why people resist a number
version: 1
---

An analyst who has just proved something tends to expect agreement. **What arrives instead, often, is
resistance**, and it usually comes dressed as a question about the analysis. Understanding where it comes
from is the difference between answering the question and answering the person.

## The common reasons

- **Loss of standing.** Sandra's team was praised for 94.5% for years. A new number that makes them the
  cause of a problem takes something away, regardless of whether it is fair.
- **Loss of control.** The change will happen in their warehouse, designed by somebody from another
  department. People resist changes done *to* them far more than changes done *with* them.
- **Fear of blame.** If late first boxes cost R$ 790 thousand a year, somebody will ask whose fault that
  was. The easiest defence is to doubt the number.
- **A threat to competence.** The address check exists because somebody built it, years ago, for a good
  reason: mistyped addresses cost money too. Removing it suggests they were wrong.
- **Change fatigue.** If the last three initiatives were announced, half-done and forgotten, the fourth
  is met with a shrug before anybody reads it.
- **Work.** The pilot means new procedures, extra tracking and eight weeks of being watched.

## Resistance carries information

Not all resistance is defensive. **Some of it is the people closest to the work telling you something
you do not know.** The warehouse staff warned that some interior addresses are new streets the postcode
lookup does not recognise. That was not obstruction; it was why the pilot skipped the check only for
addresses the lookup could confirm, which is in the recommendation of lesson 9 because of them.

So the first move with resistance is to listen for the part of it that is right. **A change that ignores the
objections of the people who will carry it out usually meets them again, later and at higher cost.**

## The analyst's part

An analyst cannot remove every reason to resist, and should not try to manage people they do not
manage. What the analyst controls is **how the number arrives**: early, privately, with the person's own
data, and with a role in the fix, as lesson 13 described. The next sections are about the cases where
that is not enough.
