title: Involve the people who will change

---

The single most reliable way to reduce resistance is to **make the people who will change part of designing
the change**. It is slower at the start and much faster overall, because a plan the team helped write
does not need to be sold to them.

## What Faro did

- **The warehouse staff shaped the rule.** Their warning about new streets turned "remove the address
  check" into "skip it for addresses the postcode lookup confirms". The pilot was safer, and the people who
  would run it had already changed it once.
- **Sandra's team ran the pilot and reported it.** Each week, Diego sent the interior and capital rates to
  Paulo, Marina and the warehouse. **The weekly number came from logistics, about logistics**, and nobody
  had to be told how they were doing by an outsider.
- **The first win was shown to the people who made it.** In the first week the interior's late share fell
  from 23.6% to 16.5%. Sandra put that number on the warehouse noticeboard, not Marina in a deck.

## The pilot as a way to involve

A pilot is a technical device for testing a cause, as lesson 9 described. It is also **a social device for
letting people find out for themselves**. A team told that removing a step will help may doubt it; a team
that removes it and watches its own number fall does not need convincing.

That is why the pilot was given to Sandra's team rather than run by a project group: the result belongs to
the people who will have to sustain it.

## What involvement is not

Involvement is not a consultation held after the decision has been taken, in which people are asked for
their views on a plan that will not change. **People can tell the difference**, and a fake consultation
produces more resistance than none. If the decision is made, say so, and involve them in *how*, which is
usually where their knowledge is anyway.
