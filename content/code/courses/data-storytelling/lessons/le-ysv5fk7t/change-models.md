Change management has a large literature and a few models that most organisations have met. Two are
worth knowing by name, because the people you work with will use them, and because **each is a checklist
for a different scale of change**.

## Kotter's eight steps

John Kotter, a professor at Harvard Business School, described in *Leading Change* (1996) eight steps he saw
in organisations that changed successfully, roughly in this order: create a sense of urgency; build a guiding
coalition; form a vision; communicate it; remove obstacles; generate short-term wins; consolidate gains and
produce more change; anchor the new approaches in the culture.

Kotter's model is about **organisation-wide change**, a new strategy or a merger. Most analytical findings are
smaller, and using all eight steps on a change to one warehouse procedure would be heavy. Two of them
carry over well at any scale: **a guiding coalition** (Sandra, Diego and Paulo, rather than Marina alone)
and **short-term wins** (the first pilot weeks, shown to the team that produced them).

## ADKAR

**ADKAR**, from Jeff Hiatt and the firm Prosci, describes change from the side of **the individual who has to
change**, in five stages that must come in order: Awareness of why the change is needed, Desire to take part,
Knowledge of how to change, Ability to do it in practice, and Reinforcement so that it lasts.

Its usefulness is diagnostic. When a change stalls, ADKAR asks which stage the people are stuck at. **A team
that knows exactly what to do and does not want to is not helped by more training**; one that wants to and
cannot is not helped by another presentation about why.

## Using them honestly

Both are frameworks distilled from experience, not laws established by experiment, and both have critics.
Treat them as checklists that help you notice what you forgot, not as recipes that guarantee a result. For an
analyst, the most useful single question in either is ADKAR's first: **does everybody who has to change know
why?** Most resistance to a number starts there.
