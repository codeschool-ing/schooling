---
title: The finding: one sentence with its comparison
version: 1
---

The finding block is the top of lesson 3's pyramid, written for somebody who has not seen any of the
analysis. **It states what is true, how much, and compared with what**, and nothing else.

## Faro's finding

> One new subscriber in six gets their first box late (17.3% in the first half of 2025), and those
> customers cancel within 90 days at 41.5%, against 17.4% for everybody else. The gap holds in the capital
> and in the interior.

Three sentences of work, read in fifteen seconds:

- **The size of the problem**, as a share the reader can picture ("one in six") and as the exact number.
- **The consequence, against its comparison**: 41.5% means nothing alone; against 17.4% it is more than
  double.
- **The strongest objection, answered in a clause**: "the gap holds in the capital and in the interior"
  closes the question lesson 10 deals with at length, before the reader has asked it.

## What stays out

The method stays out: "joining orders to deliveries to cancellations" belongs in the full analysis. So do
the cohort-by-cohort numbers, the definitions and the twenty-four rows. **A reader who needs them will
follow the link at the bottom of the page**; a reader who does not will not miss them.

Hedging stays out too, but caveats do not. The difference matters. A hedge weakens every sentence without
saying why: *it seems that there may be a relationship between*. A caveat names a specific limit and
says how much it matters. If a caveat could change the decision, it goes in the finding as a clause. If it
could not, it goes in the full analysis.

## Precision that fits the reader

The finding uses the precision the decision needs. **17.3% and 41.5% carry one decimal place because the
comparison is close enough that the decimal is honest**, and a reader who checks the deck will find the same
numbers. Writing 17.30% would claim a precision the data does not support; writing "about 20%" would make
the figure on the page disagree with the slide. Pick one precision for each number and use it everywhere
the number appears.
