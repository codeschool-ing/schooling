title: The next step: what, who, when, and how we will know

The next step is the block most summaries leave vague, and **the only one the reader can say yes to**. A
finding and an impact with no next step produce agreement and no action, which is exactly what happened in
Marina's first meeting.

## Faro's next step

> Approve an eight-week pilot in the interior, from 1 September, that removes the manual check for
> addresses the postcode lookup confirms. Owner: Sandra's team. Cost: no new spending; the risk of more
> mis-delivered boxes is measured during the pilot. Success: late first deliveries in the interior down
> from 23.6% to 8% or less. Review: 27 October.

## Its parts

- **The decision, as a verb the reader performs**: *approve*. Not "it is recommended that consideration be
  given", which has no subject and asks nobody for anything.
- **The scope**: eight weeks, the interior, addresses the postcode lookup confirms. Narrow enough that
  saying yes is cheap.
- **The owner**: a team, named. Lesson 2 explained why it is Sandra's.
- **The cost**, including the costs that are not money. Here the honest cost is a risk, and naming it
  before anybody asks is what makes the page trustworthy.
- **The test of success, decided in advance**: from 23.6% to 8% or less. Without it, the pilot will be
  judged afterwards by whoever has the strongest opinion.
- **The date of the review**, so the next conversation is already scheduled.

## Options, briefly, if there are any

When there are real alternatives, the next-step block can name them in a sentence each, with the one you
recommend first. Faro's page could add: *Alternatives: promise three days instead of two for first
orders (no cost, but a weaker offer at checkout); or move first orders to Ligeiro's express service (about
R$ 9 more per first box).* Lesson 9 builds the full comparison; the page carries only enough for the reader
to know the alternatives were considered.

## The checklist

Before sending, read the next-step block and check that a reader could reply with one word, *approved*,
and everybody involved would know what happens on Monday. **If the reply would have to be a question, the
block is not finished.**
