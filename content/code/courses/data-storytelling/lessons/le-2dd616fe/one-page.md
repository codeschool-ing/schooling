An **executive summary** is a single page that lets somebody who will not read anything else understand
what you found and decide what to do about it. It is written last and read first, and **for most of its
readers it is the whole of your work**.

## Who reads it

- **People who were not in the meeting.** Renata, the finance director, missed Marina's presentation and
  will see only what reaches her inbox.
- **People who will decide before they read the deck.** Paulo may approve the pilot from the summary and
  open the slides only if somebody objects.
- **People your work is forwarded to.** Every manager who passes an analysis upwards adds a line on top
  and attaches whatever is shortest. If the shortest thing is your page, the line on top will be
  accurate.

## The three blocks

The page answers the three questions of lesson 1, under three headings that say what each block is:

1. **Finding.** What you found, in one or two sentences, with the key number and what it is compared
   with.
2. **Impact.** What it means in the reader's unit: money, customers, risk. How big, and what happens if
   nothing is done.
3. **Next step.** What you want, from whom, by when, at what cost, and how anyone will know whether it
   worked.

Above them goes a header that a busy reader scans first: **who it is for, who it is from, the date, and the
date by which a decision is needed**. Below them, at most, a line pointing to the full analysis.

## Why one page

One page is a constraint, and constraints force decisions. **Every sentence on the page has displaced
another one**, so each must earn its place, which is the same discipline as lesson 2's evidence and lesson
5's single message, applied to prose. Two pages invite the reader to skim; one page gets read.

It is also the format with the longest life. A deck is presented once. A page is forwarded, printed,
attached to the decision record, and found again a year later when somebody asks why the address check
was removed. **Write it as if it will be the only trace of the work**, because often it is.
