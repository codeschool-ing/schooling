The impact block turns the finding into **what it costs, in a unit the reader already uses to decide**. For
Faro's readers that unit is money and customers, so the impact block speaks in both.

## Faro's impact

> About 510 customers a year cancel early who would otherwise have stayed. The margin they would have
> brought over their lifetime is about R$ 790 thousand a year, and Faro also spends about R$ 78 thousand a
> year acquiring them. The cause is mostly inside our warehouse: a manual address check on first orders
> that uses one of the two promised days.

Each number is built from the finding and from facts finance already holds. Lesson 11 does the
arithmetic: 1,058 late first boxes in six months, cancelling 24.1 points more often than on-time ones,
gives 255 extra cancellations in six months and 510 in a year; each one loses about R$ 1,554 of lifetime
margin; and each was a customer Faro paid R$ 152 to acquire. **The page shows the results and the full
analysis shows the arithmetic**, so a finance director who wants to check can.

## Round, and say so

The calculation gives R$ 792,611.40. The page says "about R$ 790 thousand". **A number with eight
significant figures on a page about a decision claims a precision that its inputs do not have**: the average
lifetime of a customer is an estimate, and so is the margin per box. Rounding to two significant figures,
and saying "about", is the honest form.

That is a different rule from the finding's, and the reason is the source. The cancellation rates are
counted; the money is estimated from counts and assumptions. **Count precisely, estimate roughly, and let
the precision of each number tell the reader which kind it is.**

## What happens if nothing is done

An impact block should say what continues if the reader does nothing, because doing nothing is always one
of the options and usually the default. Here it is easy: **the 510 customers a year keep cancelling**, at a
cost that has not changed all year, as lesson 7's sparkline showed. That sentence is what turns "interesting"
into "worth deciding this week".

Where the impact is uncertain, give a range rather than a point. Lesson 9 does that for the pilot: the gain
if it works is somewhere between R$ 210 and 430 thousand a year, and the range is the honest answer.
