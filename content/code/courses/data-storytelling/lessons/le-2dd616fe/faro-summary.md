## Reading it as its readers will

- **Paulo** reads the header and the next step. He sees *decision by 22 August*, reads *approve an
  eight-week pilot*, checks the owner and the cost, and can reply.
- **Renata** reads the impact. She sees R$ 790 thousand, notices "about", and opens the full analysis to
  check the arithmetic, which is there.
- **Sandra** reads the last sentence of the impact first, because it is about her warehouse, and then the
  next step, because it names her team as owner. **The page does not blame her team; it gives it the fix.**
- **The board**, next quarter, will see the page attached to the pilot's results.

Each reader found what they needed in their own order, which is the test of a summary: **it does not
assume a reading order, because it will not get one**.

## Write yours

Write the page for your own analysis in `my-analysis.txt`, with the three headings and a header, and hold
it to the length of one printed page. Then apply three checks:

1. **Cover everything except the finding.** Does a reader know what you found, how much, compared with what?
2. **Cover everything except the impact.** Is it in the reader's unit, rounded honestly, with what happens
   if nothing is done?
3. **Cover everything except the next step.** Could the reader reply *approved*?

If any answer is no, the page is not finished, and no amount of work on the deck behind it will make up
for that, because for most of its readers the page is the analysis.
