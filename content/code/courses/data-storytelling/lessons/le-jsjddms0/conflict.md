The conflict is the **gap between what the room expects and what the data shows**. Without one, the
analysis is confirmation, and confirmation is rarely worth a slot in a meeting.

## Faro's conflict

Sandra's 94.5% says deliveries are fine. Marina's data says that 17.3% of first deliveries are late. **Both
numbers are correct, and the conflict is that they seem to disagree.**

They do not disagree; they count different things. In the six months of the analysis Faro made 132,513
deliveries, and only 6,113 of them were first deliveries: 4.6% of the total. Renewals to addresses the
carrier already knows arrive on time 95.1% of the time, and they are so many that they set the overall
rate almost on their own.

So the conflict, in one sentence:

> Our on-time rate is 94.5%, but it is made almost entirely of renewals. For first deliveries, the ones
> that decide whether a customer stays, it is 82.7%.

That sentence does three things. It **agrees with Sandra before it disagrees with her**, it explains why
her number could not have shown the problem, and it says why the gap matters. Nobody is wrong in it, and
everybody now has a reason to listen.

## Four shapes a conflict takes

Not every analysis has a contradiction to resolve. The gap comes in four common shapes:

- **A gap against expectation.** The KPI says fine and the customer says otherwise. Faro's is this one.
- **A change.** Something that was stable has moved: the conversion rate fell four points in June.
- **A comparison.** One region, product or team behaves differently from the others for no known reason.
- **A risk.** Nothing is wrong yet, and the trend says it will be: at the current rate the warehouse is
  full in November.

If none of the four fits, ask whether the analysis has a story at all. **"Here is our data for the
quarter" has no conflict**, and it is a perfectly good report to send rather than present.

## Do not manufacture one

A conflict exaggerated to get attention is found out at the evidence stage, and it costs the rest of
the presentation. Faro's late first deliveries are a real gap of real size; calling them "a crisis in
logistics" would hand Sandra an easy and correct rebuttal, because 94.5% of deliveries are on time.
