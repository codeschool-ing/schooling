title: Context: what the room already agrees on
version: 1
---

The context is the **part of the story nobody in the room would dispute**. Its job is not to inform; it
is to put everybody on the same starting line, so that the conflict lands on all of them at once.

## Faro's context

Marina's context is one sentence, and it is somebody else's number:

> Deliveries are on time 94.5% of the time, and the first box is the moment a new subscriber decides
> whether Faro keeps its promise.

The first half is Sandra's measure. Nobody will argue with it, and **quoting it shows Sandra that her
work has been read** before anything in the presentation could be taken as an accusation. The second half
is a premise the room will accept without evidence, because everybody has been a new customer somewhere.

## What does not belong in it

The most common mistake is to put **the history of the analysis** in the context: where the data came
from, how it was joined, which months were excluded. That is method, and it belongs in an appendix or in
the answer to a question (lesson 10). The room does not need to know how you got there before it knows
where you are.

The second mistake is context that is not shared. "As you know, our activation rate has been falling"
is not context if half the room did not know; it is a finding presented as a premise, and **a finding
smuggled into the context is the first thing a sceptic will attack**, before you have shown any evidence
for it.

The third is length. Context earns about a minute. If the audience needs ten minutes of background
before the conflict makes sense, either it is the wrong audience or the presentation is really a
training session.

## Choosing context for a different room

Context depends on who is listening, which is why lesson 4 rewrites it four times. For the board, the
shared starting point is growth and margin. For the logistics team, it is the carrier contract and
the 94.5%. For Ligeiro, the carrier, it is the service-level agreement both companies signed. The
same analysis gets a different first sentence for each, and **the test is always the same: would
anybody in this room disagree with it?** If yes, it is not context yet.
