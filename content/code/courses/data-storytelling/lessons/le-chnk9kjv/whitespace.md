The fourth principle is the one people fight hardest. Williams calls it **proximity: items that relate to
each other should be grouped close together**, and the topic of this lesson calls it whitespace, because the
grouping is done by the empty space around the groups.

## Space is a separator

On the left of the figure, the elements of the slide are spread evenly over it: the title, three numbers,
their three labels and a note, each the same distance from its neighbours. The eye cannot tell which label
belongs to which number. On the right, each number sits right above its label, and the three pairs are
separated by more space than anything inside them. **Nothing was drawn to make the groups**: no boxes, no
lines. The empty space did it.

That is why boxes and divider lines are rarely needed on a slide. A frame around each group adds marks
for the eye to process; space between groups adds nothing and groups just as well.

## The fear of empty space

An empty area on a slide feels like waste, and the instinct is to fill it: a bigger chart, a second chart,
a logo, an extra bullet. **Empty space is not waste; it is what makes the rest legible.** A slide with
generous margins and space between groups looks calmer and is read faster than one where every
centimetre carries something.

Two practical rules follow:

- **Keep margins.** Nothing within about a twentieth of the slide's width from its edge. Projectors crop
  edges, and text against the edge looks as if it is falling off.
- **Space by meaning.** Space between groups is clearly larger than space inside them. If the two are
  similar, the eye cannot see where one group ends.

## Putting the four together

Contrast says what to read first; alignment says the slide was put together deliberately; repetition
says the rules hold from slide to slide; space says what goes with what. **None of the four needs a design
tool beyond what every slide program has**, and each removes a decision the audience would otherwise make
while you are talking. Apply them to one slide of your own analysis now, and keep the before and after in
the course folder.
