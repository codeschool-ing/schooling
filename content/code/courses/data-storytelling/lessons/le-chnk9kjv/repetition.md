title: Repetition: the same thing looks the same

Williams's third principle: **repeat visual elements throughout the piece**. In a deck, repetition is what
lets the audience stop thinking about the form and attend to the content, because after two slides they
know where everything will be.

## What repeats

- **Position.** The title in the same place, the chart area in the same place, the source note always at
  the bottom left.
- **Type.** One family of font, two or three sizes, used for the same jobs every time: title, body, note.
- **Colour, and its meaning.** This is the one that matters most for data. In Faro's deck, **late first
  delivery is drawn in one colour on every slide where it appears**, and on-time in grey. A reader who
  learnt that on slide 4 reads slide 5 without a legend.

## Colour that changes meaning

The top row of the figure is three slides from Marina's first deck. Late deliveries are one colour on the
first, a second colour on the second, and on the third the first colour means the capital. Each slide was made on a different
day and each is fine on its own. **Together they mislead**: a reader carries the meaning of a colour from
one slide to the next, as everybody does, and misreads the third.

The bottom row is the same three slides with one rule applied: one colour means late, everywhere, and
nothing else gets that colour. It costs nothing to do and it is almost never done unless somebody
decides it at the start.

## Templates, and their limits

A company template enforces some repetition for you: the fonts, the logo, the title position. It does
not know what your colours mean. Write down, before you make the first chart, **a small legend for the whole
deck**: which colour means what, which is the neutral grey, and which elements carry no colour at all.
Then every chart in the deck follows it.

Repetition is also what makes the one exception visible. If every slide uses grey and one colour, the
single slide that breaks the pattern, a big number in colour on an empty field, gets attention because of
everything that came before it. That is contrast again, at the scale of the whole deck.
