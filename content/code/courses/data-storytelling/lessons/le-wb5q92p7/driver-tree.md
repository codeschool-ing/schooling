A **driver tree** breaks the number the company cares about into the numbers that produce it, and those
into theirs, until it reaches things somebody can change. **It is the map on which a finding is placed**,
and placing it shows which branch it moves and how far up the effect travels.

## Faro's tree

At the top, **gross margin**. It is the number of boxes sold times the margin per box. Boxes sold depend on
how many subscribers there are and how long they stay. Subscribers depend on how many are acquired and how
many leave early. And the share who leave early depends, among other things, on whether their first box
arrived on time, which is the leaf Marina's analysis found.

Read from the leaf upwards, the tree says what the finding means for the company: **a change in the share
of late first boxes moves early cancellations, which moves the number of subscribers, which moves boxes
sold, which moves margin**. Each link is a multiplication someone in finance already knows how to do.

## What the tree is for

- **Translating.** It turns "17.3% late" into "R$ 790 thousand of margin" by following the links, which
  the next section does.
- **Checking scope.** A finding that moves a small leaf on a long branch moves the top only a little. Faro's
  first box is a small leaf (one delivery in twenty-two) that sits on a short, steep branch: it decides
  whether a customer stays twenty-eight months or under two.
- **Finding the owner.** Every leaf has somebody responsible for it. The first-delivery leaf belongs to
  logistics; the acquisition leaf to marketing; price to the commercial director. **A finding that crosses
  branches crosses departments**, which is what lesson 1 found in Marina's meeting.

## Drawing your own

You do not need finance's model to draw a useful tree. Start with the number your company reports to its
owners, ask "what is this made of?" two or three times, and stop when you reach your finding. Draw it in
`my-analysis.txt` as indented lines. **If you cannot connect your finding to the top in four or five
steps**, either the finding is too small to matter to the company or you have not yet understood how it
matters, and both are worth knowing before the meeting.
