title: The words finance uses

Finance has a precise vocabulary, and analysts lose credibility fastest by using its words loosely.
**Calling margin "profit" in front of a finance director is the business equivalent of calling a median
an average in front of a statistician.** The table holds the words this course has needed and a few that
come up beside them.

| term | what it means | the trap |
|---|---|---|
| **revenue** | money customers pay, before any costs | quoting it as if it were what the company keeps |
| **gross margin** | revenue minus the direct cost of what was sold | confusing it with profit |
| **operating expenses** | the cost of running the company: salaries, rent, marketing | forgetting that margin must pay for them |
| **EBITDA** | earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation | treating it as cash; it is not |
| **cash** | money actually in the bank | assuming profit and cash move together; timing separates them |
| **CAC** | what it costs to win one customer | comparing it with revenue rather than margin |
| **LTV** | margin over a customer's lifetime | presenting it as certain when it rests on a churn assumption |
| **payback** | how long margin takes to repay CAC | ignoring the customers who leave before it |
| **recurring** | revenue or cost that repeats every period | adding a one-off saving to a recurring one as if they were the same |
| **annualised** | a shorter period scaled to a year | annualising a seasonal number |

## Ask for the company's definitions

Every company uses these words slightly differently. One counts discounts in CAC and another does not;
one reports margin after shipping and another before. **Before you put a financial number on a slide, ask
finance which definition the company uses**, and use theirs. A number computed your way and presented next
to theirs will be compared, and the difference will be the conversation.

## The words in Portuguese

In Brazilian companies the same ideas have Portuguese names, often used side by side with the English:
*receita* for revenue, *margem bruta* for gross margin, *despesas operacionais* for operating expenses,
*LAJIDA* for EBITDA, *caixa* for cash. CAC, LTV and *churn* are usually said in English. Match the room:
**use the words the finance team uses in its own reports**.
