title: What goes wrong in analytical projects
version: 1
---

Analytical projects fail in a small number of recurring ways. **Naming them at the start, with what you
will do if they happen, costs ten minutes and saves the project more often than any technique.**

| risk | what it looks like | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| **data access** | the table's owner is on holiday; the export needs approval | ask for access in week one, before you need it |
| **definition dispute** | your number and theirs differ by a day's worth of orders | agree definitions in writing with the data owner first |
| **scope creep** | "while you're at it, could you also…" | the brief's *out of scope* line, and the request log |
| **the moving question** | the requester changes what they want halfway | update the brief and say so the same week |
| **the unwelcome answer** | the finding implicates somebody powerful | the private, early conversation of lesson 13 |
| **a vague success criterion** | the result is in, and nobody agrees whether it passed | write the window and the rule before you look |

## The last row, from Faro's own pilot

Lesson 9's success criterion read *late first deliveries in the interior at 8% or less, measured weekly*. It
did not say **which weeks**. The first three weeks of the pilot were above 8% while the warehouse adapted,
and from the fourth week on every week was at or under it. Paulo read that as a pass; somebody could have
read the eight-week average, 9.3%, as a fail.

Nobody argued, because the trend was clear and the guardrail held. The lesson Marina wrote in her decision
log was still worth writing: **next time, say the window: "the last four weeks of the pilot"**. A criterion
that leaves its measurement open has not finished deciding in advance.

## Keeping the list alive

Write the risk table in the brief, and look at it at each milestone. A risk that has passed comes off; a new
one goes on. **The list is useful only while somebody reads it**, which is why it belongs in a document the
project already uses rather than a separate file nobody opens.
