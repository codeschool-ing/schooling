title: A rough answer in the first week
version: 1
---

An analyst's instinct is to deliver nothing until it is right. **The better habit is to deliver a rough
answer early, label it as rough, and refine it in the open.** It feels exposed, and it is the single change
that did most for Marina's second attempt.

## Why early beats right

- **The question gets corrected while it is cheap.** A rough answer shown to Paulo in week one would have
  revealed in week one that he wanted a ten-minute update, not a thirty-eight-slide tour.
- **The data problems surface early.** The first cut is where you discover that the carrier's timestamps are
  in a different table, or that a month is missing. Better in week one than the night before the meeting.
- **People get used to the finding.** Lesson 13's weekly update depends on having something to say in it.

## What a first cut looks like

Marina's first cut, at the end of week one, was one paragraph and one table: *late first boxes seem to
cancel more than twice as often, 41.5% against 17.4%; not yet checked by region or month; definitions to
confirm with Diego.* It took two days, most of them spent joining the tables, and it already contained the
finding the whole project would rest on.

## Estimates are optimistic

Analysts, like everybody, underestimate how long work will take. Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky named
this the **planning fallacy**: people plan from the best case of the task in front of them, rather than from
how long similar tasks took in the past. Two habits help:

- **Estimate from your own history.** If your last three analyses took twice as long as planned, so will this
  one. Keep a note of how long things actually took.
- **Time-box exploration.** Give the open-ended parts a fixed budget ("two days on alternative explanations")
  and decide at the end whether to spend more, instead of letting them run.

**Data access is the part most often underestimated**: getting permission, finding the owner of a table,
waiting for an export. In Marina's second attempt it took the first two weeks, and the plan said so in
advance because the first attempt had taught her.
