---
title: What a reviewer looks for in an analysis
version: 1
---

A reviewer opening an analyst's portfolio has a few minutes and one question: **would this person turn our
vague questions into decisions?** Charts and tools answer a smaller question, whether the candidate can
operate the software, and most candidates can.

## Four things that separate candidates

- **Judgement about the question.** Did they start from a decision (lesson 15) or from a dataset? A case
  study that opens with "I found this dataset and explored it" reads differently from one that opens with
  "a subscription company wanted to know why new customers leave in the first ninety days".
- **What they ruled out.** Anybody can report the hypothesis that survived. Saying that region was checked
  and explained only a small part of the gap (lesson 10) shows the reviewer how you think.
- **Communication.** The one-page summary of lesson 8, a deck whose titles tell the story (lesson 5),
  numbers rounded honestly. **The reviewer is also the audience**, and how you present to them is a sample of
  how you would present to their board.
- **Honesty about limits.** What the analysis could not show, what the pilot was designed to test, what you
  would do next. A case study with no limits reads as one whose author has not found them yet.

## What reviewers do not need

They do not need every chart you made, the full code of every step, or a history of the tools you tried.
**A portfolio is evidence, not a museum**, as the `portfolio-project` course puts it in its first lesson. The
same discipline as lesson 2's evidence applies: include what answers a question the reviewer has, and link
to the rest.

## Process, including the parts that went wrong

Marina's first meeting failed. In a case study, that failure is an asset: **"my first presentation was
thirty-eight slides and nothing happened; here is what I changed"** is one of the most persuasive sentences
an analyst can write, because it shows the reviewer you can learn from a room's reaction. Leave it in your
own case study too, if you have one like it.
