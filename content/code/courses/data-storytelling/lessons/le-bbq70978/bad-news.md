---
title: Delivering a finding somebody will not like
version: 1
---

Marina's finding was bad news for Sandra before it was good news for Faro. **How bad news is delivered
decides whether its subject becomes an ally or an opponent**, and an opponent with power can stop a
recommendation that every number supports.

## Early, private, first

Three rules, in order of importance:

- **Early.** The longer a person goes without knowing, the more it looks as if they were kept out. Sandra
  heard about the finding the week Marina was sure of it, not the week of the meeting.
- **Private.** Bad news heard alone can be absorbed, questioned and argued with safely. The same news heard
  in front of one's boss has to be defended.
- **First.** The person the news is about hears it before anybody else hears it about them. If Paulo had
  heard first, Sandra's first conversation would have been with her boss, and every later one would have
  been shaped by that.

## With their data, and a role

Marina did not open with "your team is causing churn". She opened with Sandra's own number, 94.5%, and
asked a question: **"Why would first deliveries be slower than renewals?"** Sandra knew the answer, the
address check, before Marina said it, and a cause somebody found themselves is one they defend rather than
dispute.

Then Marina offered the role lesson 2 described: Sandra's team would own the pilot. Bad news with a role in
the fix is a problem to solve; bad news without one is a verdict.

## When the person still disagrees

Sometimes nothing works, and the person disputes the finding to the end. Then **tell them, before the
meeting, that you will present it anyway, and how**: what you will say about their team, and what you will
not. They may still argue in the meeting, but they will not be ambushed, and the room will notice the
difference between a colleague who was warned and one who was not. That difference is lesson 14's subject:
why a department resists a number, and what to do about it beyond a single conversation.
