A useful test for any analysis you are about to present comes from three short questions: **What? So
what? Now what?** They were set down as a model for reflective practice in nursing education, by Gary
Rolfe and colleagues in 2001, and they fit an analysis just as well, because they mark the three steps
between a fact and an action.

- **What?** is the information: what happened, measured. *17.3% of new subscribers got their first box
  late.* Most reports are good at this part, because it is what the analysis produced.
- **So what?** is the insight: why it matters to the people in the room. *Those customers cancel within
  ninety days at 41.5%, against 17.4% for everybody else.* Now the first fact has a cost attached.
- **Now what?** is the decision you are asking for. *Fix the first delivery before anything else,
  starting with a pilot in one region.* Without this, the room has learnt something and has nothing to
  do with it.

## Where analysts stop

The common failure is to stop after *What?*, and it is common for a good reason: **the first answer is
the one the analyst is sure of**. The data says 17.3%; nobody can argue with that. *So what?* requires
a claim about the business, and *Now what?* requires a recommendation that might be wrong, in public.

Stopping early feels safe and is not neutral. A room handed only *What?* fills in the other two itself,
quickly and with whatever it already believed. The head of logistics heard "some first deliveries are
late" and concluded "and we already know deliveries are 94% on time, so this is noise". That was a *So
what?*, supplied by the person with the most reason to supply that one.

## The test, applied to your own slides

Take any presentation you have made and write, for each slide, which of the three questions it answers.
A deck of thirty slides that are all *What?* is a report; it may be a very good report, and it should be
sent rather than presented. A presentation needs at least one slide that answers *So what?* in the
audience's terms, and it should end on *Now what?*.

Lesson 2 turns the three questions into a four-part structure for the whole story, and lesson 9 is
entirely about writing the *Now what?* well.
