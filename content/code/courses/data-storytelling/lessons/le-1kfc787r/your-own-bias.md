Everything in this lesson is easier to apply to somebody else's number. **Your own analysis has an
incentive too: you want it to be right**, and three weeks of work make that want strong.

## Confirmation bias

People tend to look for, notice and remember evidence that supports what they already believe, and to
scrutinise evidence against it more harshly. Psychologists call this **confirmation bias**, and it does not
require dishonesty; it works quietly on careful people. For an analyst, it shows up as a check run twice
when the result is disappointing and once when it is pleasing.

Marina's analysis has a place where it could have worked on her. The cancellation gap was large in the
first cut, and the regional check of lesson 10 was a test that could have shrunk it. **She ran it, and
reported that region explains a small part of the pooled gap**, which is the half of the result she would
not have chosen.

## Three habits that help

- **Write down what would change your mind, before you look.** "If the gap disappears inside each region,
  the finding is about geography, not delivery." Written in advance, it cannot be quietly revised after the
  result comes in. The pilot's success criterion in lesson 9 is the same habit applied to a decision.
- **Look for the evidence against you on purpose.** Ask what you would expect to see if you were wrong, and
  go and look for it. If late customers cancelled more only in one month, or only for one product, the
  finding would be fragile; the monthly lines of lesson 5 were partly this check.
- **Let somebody else try to break it.** The red team of lesson 10 is the outside version of the same habit,
  and it works because the other person does not share your want.

## The cost of being wrong in public

An analyst who presents a wrong finding confidently loses more than the finding: the next one will be
examined for errors rather than for meaning, as lesson 1 put it. **The habits above are cheap insurance**
against that, and they have a second benefit: a finding that has survived your own best attempt to break it
is one you can defend in the room without bluffing.
