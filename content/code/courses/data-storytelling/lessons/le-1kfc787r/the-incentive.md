A number that somebody is rewarded for is a number somebody has a reason to move, and **not always by
changing the thing it was meant to measure**. Questioning the incentive means asking who is paid, promoted
or blamed by a number, and what they could do to it other than improve the reality behind it.

## Goodhart's law

The economist Charles Goodhart observed in 1975 that a statistical regularity tends to collapse once it is
used as a target for control. The anthropologist Marilyn Strathern later put it in the form most people
quote: **when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure**. The psychologist Donald Campbell
made a similar point about social indicators: the more a number is used to make decisions about people, the
more it is distorted by the people it decides about.

## The incentives around Faro's numbers

- **Ligeiro is paid a bonus on its on-time rate**, measured by its own drivers' scans. That gives it a
  reason to scan promptly, which helps, and a reason to code a failed first attempt as something other than
  late, which does not. Neither has been shown to happen; **the incentive is the reason to check**.
- **Sandra's team is measured on the overall on-time rate.** That gave nobody a reason to look at first
  deliveries separately, because the overall number was green.
- **Promising three days instead of two**, the option lesson 9 rejected, is Goodhart's law in miniature:
  the measure would improve overnight while the customer's wait stayed exactly the same.

## What to do with an incentive

An incentive is not an accusation. Most people do not game the numbers they are judged by, and treating
them as if they did poisons the conversation lesson 10 tried to keep clean. **What an incentive tells you is
where to look first**: at the definition the rewarded party controls, and at any change in the number that
arrived without a change in the work.

The defence is to keep a second measure the incentive does not touch beside the one it does. Faro's
first-delivery rate from the carrier's scans is worth reading beside the customer-service tickets about
late first boxes, which nobody is paid on.
