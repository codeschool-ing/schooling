title: When a slide says two things
version: 1
---

The rule is one message per slide, and slides break it all the time, usually for a reason that felt
sensible: the two things were related, there was space, the deck was getting long. **A slide carrying two
messages delivers neither well**, because the room reads the title, looks for evidence of it, finds
evidence of something else, and has to work out which is which while you talk.

## How to recognise one

Four signs, any one of which is enough:

- **The title has "and" in it.** *Late first deliveries double cancellations and the interior is worse.*
  Two claims, joined.
- **There are two charts.** Unless they are the same chart for two groups, side by side to be compared,
  two charts are two pieces of evidence for two messages.
- **You say "also" while presenting it.** "This shows the gap… and also, if you look on the right…"
- **The title cannot be written in two lines** without dropping something the slide shows.

## What to do instead

**Split it.** Two slides with one message each take less time to present than one slide with two,
because each is understood at once. Slide count is not the measure of length; minutes are, and a slide
that needs explaining costs more minutes than two that do not.

When a single chart genuinely holds two steps of an argument, **build it up** instead: show the slide
with only the first part drawn, then the same slide with the second part added. Faro's regional chart is
a good candidate. First the two capital bars, *the gap is there in the capital*; then the interior bars
appear beside them, *and the same in the interior*. Each step has one message, and the build keeps the
comparison on one set of axes.

## The appendix again

Some second messages are not worth a slide at all; they are worth an answer. Cat owners churn slightly
less, cancellations peak on Mondays. **If the room is unlikely to ask, leave it out; if it might ask, put
it in the appendix**, with a title that is a sentence like every other slide, so it can be shown in one
click and understood in one glance.
