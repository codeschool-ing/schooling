Most slide titles are **topics**: a noun phrase that says what the slide is about. *Results.*
*Cancellations by region.* *First-delivery analysis.* They are short, neutral and easy to write, and they
leave the audience to discover the point.

An **assertion title** is a full sentence that states the point. *Late first deliveries more than double
early cancellations.* *The gap holds in the capital and in the interior.* It is longer and harder to
write, and it does the audience's work for them.

## Why the sentence wins

A reader looks at the title first and the chart second. With a topic title, they look at the chart
without knowing what to look for, form their own conclusion, and then listen to you to find out whether
it matches yours. **With an assertion title, they look at the chart to check a claim they have already
read**, which is faster and leaves them listening to you instead of to their own reading.

There is evidence behind this. Michael Alley, an engineering educator at Penn State, calls the
pairing of a sentence headline with visual evidence the **assertion–evidence** approach, and studies he
and colleagues ran with engineering students found that audiences understood and remembered more from
slides built that way than from slides with a topic title and bullet points. The effect was measured on
technical material in a classroom; it is reasonable to expect it in a meeting, and nobody should claim a
precise number for it there.

## Writing one

A good assertion title:

- **is a complete sentence**, with a verb. "Late first deliveries and cancellations" is still a topic;
  "Late first deliveries more than double cancellations" is a claim.
- **is what the chart shows**, no more. If the chart shows two bars, the title cannot claim a trend.
- **fits in two lines**. Past that, it is a paragraph, and the slide is carrying more than one message.
- **carries the number when the number is the point**: "First deliveries are on time only 82.7% of the
  time" beats "First deliveries are often late".

The test from lesson 3 applies directly: **if someone read only the title, would they have the point?**
