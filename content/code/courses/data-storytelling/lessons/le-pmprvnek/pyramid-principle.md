Barbara Minto's *The Pyramid Principle* turns answer-first into a way of organising everything beneath
the answer. **The structure is a tree with one idea at the top**, the governing thought, and every idea
below it is a reason for, or a detail of, the idea above.

## The three rules

- **One idea at the top.** If you have two, you have two presentations, or you have not yet found the
  idea that joins them. Faro's: *fix first deliveries, starting with a pilot*.
- **Each level summarises the level below.** The three reasons under the governing thought are exactly
  what the evidence beneath each one proves, said in one line.
- **The ideas on one level are a group.** They are of the same kind, in a deliberate order, and together
  they cover the point above.

## MECE: no overlaps, no gaps

The last rule is often stated as **MECE, mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive**. Mutually
exclusive: no reason repeats another under a different name. Collectively exhaustive: taken together,
they are enough to support the point above, and nobody can ask "but what about…?" for something that
should have been there.

Faro's three reasons pass the test. *The gap is real*, *it is not the region* and *it is big enough* do not
overlap, and together they answer the three questions a sceptic asks of any finding: is it true, is it
something else, does it matter.

A list that fails looks like this: *late deliveries cause churn*, *customers dislike waiting*, *the
interior is affected*, *we lose money*. The first two are the same reason twice; the third is a detail of
the first, not a sibling of it; and nothing says whether region explains the gap.

## Using it before the slides

The pyramid is most useful **before you open a slide tool**. Write the governing thought, the three
reasons and the evidence under each on one page. If something does not fit anywhere in the tree, it does
not belong in the presentation. If a branch has no evidence under it, you have found the gap in your
analysis before the room finds it for you.

Then the deck follows from the page: one slide for the answer, one or two per reason, the recommendation,
and the appendix for whatever sits below the second level.
