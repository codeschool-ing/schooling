---
title: Answering in the room
version: 1
---

Preparation decides what you know; the answer itself decides whether the room hears it. **A good answer to
a hard question has four moves, and takes less than a minute.**

## The four moves

1. **Acknowledge the question**, briefly and without flattery. "That's the right question" is enough;
   "great question!" sounds like stalling. If the room may not have heard it, repeat it in one sentence,
   which also gives you three seconds.
2. **Answer in one sentence**, first. *Yes, the gap holds in the interior: 43.9% against 20.0%.* The
   inverted pyramid again: the answer before the reasons.
3. **Show the evidence**, if there is a slide for it. Appendix C, two seconds to find, ten to read.
4. **Return to the point.** *So the delay, not the region, is what the pilot targets.* This stops a single
   question turning into a different meeting.

## When you do not know

Sometimes the honest answer is that you do not know. **Say so, say what you would need to find out, and
say when you will come back**: *I didn't separate customers who weren't home from real delays; Diego's
data can tell us, and I'll send it by Thursday.* Then send it by Thursday.

The alternative, a confident guess, is the most expensive mistake in this lesson. If it turns out wrong,
and somebody in the room usually knows, **the guess discredits everything you said before it**, including
the parts you were sure of.

## Questions that are not questions

Some interventions are statements with a question mark: a manager restating their own view, or somebody
showing the room what they know. Treat them as comments. Acknowledge the point, connect it to the finding
if you can, and move on. Arguing with a statement turns it into a debate, and a debate in the middle of
your slot leaves no time for the decision.

## Timing

Take clarifying questions as they come and **hold the long ones for the end of the argument**, with a
promise to return: *I'll come back to cost on the options slide.* Then do. A presentation that answers every
question as it arrives loses its order, and a room that never sees the recommendation cannot approve it.
