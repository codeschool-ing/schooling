The questions in the room are easier to answer if you have heard them before. **Two techniques make that
happen on purpose**: imagining the failure in advance, and asking somebody to attack the work.

## The premortem

Gary Klein, a psychologist who studies how people decide under pressure, described the **premortem** in the
*Harvard Business Review* in 2007. Before a project starts, the team imagines it is a year later and the
project has failed, and each person writes down why. Because the failure is treated as having happened,
people are freed from having to sound supportive, and the reasons come out.

For a presentation, the same move works in five minutes: **imagine the meeting is over and the
recommendation was rejected. Why?** Marina's list:

- Sandra said the 23.6% included boxes held because the customer was not home.
- Paulo asked whether removing the check had been tried before.
- Renata did not believe the R$ 790 thousand.
- Somebody said the exit survey blamed price, not delivery.
- Nobody wanted to own the pilot.

Each reason is a question to answer before the meeting, or a slide to prepare.

## The red team

The second technique is to give the deck to somebody whose job, for an hour, is to break it. **Pick
somebody who will enjoy it and who knows the business**: Diego, Sandra's analyst, is ideal, because his
objections are the ones Sandra will raise. Ask him for the three questions he would ask in the meeting,
and the one he hopes nobody asks.

A red team is uncomfortable, and that is the point. An objection found in a quiet room with a colleague
costs an hour; the same objection found by the board costs the recommendation.

## The appendix, mapped to the objections

Every likely question gets **one appendix slide**, with an assertion title like every other slide, so
that answering it is a click and a sentence.

Faro's appendix has six slides, A to F, and each exists because a question on the premortem list needed
it. Nothing else goes in the appendix, which keeps it short enough that Marina can find the right slide in
two seconds with Paulo watching.
