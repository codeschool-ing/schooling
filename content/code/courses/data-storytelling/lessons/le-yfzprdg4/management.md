Paulo is the audience Marina's first presentation was for, and the one it failed. **Management decides
between options with costs and dates attached**, and it needs those three things set out so it can
choose in the time it has.

## Lead with the choice

Paulo's version of the opening is a choice he can make:

> We can cut late first deliveries from 17.3% toward 8% by removing one step from first orders. I'd like
> to pilot it in the interior for eight weeks, run by Sandra's team, starting on 1 September.

It is the inverted pyramid of lesson 3 with management's content at the top: **the lever, the expected
effect, the owner and the start date**. Paulo can say yes, no, or "not the interior, the capital" to that
sentence, which is a decision either way.

## Two levels deep

Below the opening, management needs to see the reasoning one level down, because Paulo will have to
defend his decision to the people affected:

- **The evidence**, lesson 2's three supports, one slide each.
- **The options**, set side by side. Lesson 9 builds the table: remove the address check, extend the
  delivery promise for first orders from two days to three, or move first orders to a faster carrier
  service. Each with what it costs and what it is likely to do.
- **The risks**, said plainly: removing the check means some deliveries go to a mistyped address. How many
  is unknown, which is one of the things the pilot measures.

## What management does not need

Management does not need the method, except as a sentence ("joined orders to deliveries to
cancellations, checked by Sandra's analyst") that tells Paulo it has been checked. It does not need the
history of the analysis. And it does not need the board's money framing on the first slide: Paulo knows
cancellations cost money, and leading with R$ 793 thousand would read as an attempt to go over his head.

## The question behind every management question

Every question Paulo asks will be a version of **"what happens to my people and my targets if I say
yes?"** Sandra's team will spend time on the pilot. Her on-time rate might dip if fewer addresses are
checked. The warehouse will change a procedure. Answering those before they are asked, in the slide on
risks, is what makes a manager comfortable enough to decide in the room rather than "take it away and
think about it".
