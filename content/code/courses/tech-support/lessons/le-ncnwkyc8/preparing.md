---
title: Preparing without wasting money
version: 1
---

An exam voucher costs real money, and a failed attempt costs it again. Four habits make the first
attempt count:

1. **Start from the official objectives.** Each exam publishes its objectives as a document you can
   download. It is the syllabus: go through it line by line and mark what you can already explain and
   do, what you have only read about, and what you have never seen.
2. **Practise in a lab.** This track's labs, a virtual machine, an old computer: the performance-based
   questions reward having done things, and so does the job afterwards.
3. **Test yourself with practice questions from reputable sources**, and read the explanation of every
   one you got wrong. A practice score tells you which objectives to go back to, not whether you are
   ready in general.
4. **Never use exam dumps**, the collections of real exam questions that circulate online. Using them
   breaks the agreement every candidate accepts, CompTIA can revoke a certification earned with them,
   and they teach answers to questions rather than the subject the questions are about.

Book the exam when the objectives list has no line left in the third group, not before. A date in the
calendar helps some people study; a date set too early is an easy way to pay twice.
