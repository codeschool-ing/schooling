---
title: What a certification proves
version: 1
---

A certification is an exam with a published list of topics, called its **objectives**, set by an
organisation that employers recognise. Passing it says that, on that day, you knew enough of that list.

- **What it gives you**: a line on a CV that a recruiter can check without knowing the subject, and a
  syllabus for studying, written by people who looked at what the job needs.
- **What it does not give you**: experience. An employer hiring for support also wants to see that you
  have diagnosed a real fault and written it down, which is what the next course, the portfolio, is for.

The three in this lesson are **vendor-neutral**: they are not about one company's products, so what they
cover applies wherever you end up working. Vendor certifications, from Microsoft, Cisco, Red Hat and
others, go deeper into one product line, and usually come after a first job rather than before it.

**Everything specific in this lesson has a date on it.** Each exam is replaced by a new version every few
years, with a new code, new objectives and sometimes a new format. The lesson describes their shape, and
the organisation's own site is where the current version is: comptia.org, for all three.
