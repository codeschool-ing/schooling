---
title: An article people can find
version: 1
---

A correct article nobody finds is the same as no article. Search is how a KB is used, so it is written
for search:

- **The title is the problem**, as the person who has it would put it: *the intranet does not open*,
  *documents don't print*, not *intranet configuration* or *CUPS notes*.
- **Error messages are quoted exactly**, character for character. People paste the message they see
  into the search box, and an article that paraphrased it will not come up.
- **One problem per article.** An article that covers five problems is found for all five and read for
  none; five short articles that link to each other are found and read.
- **Say who it is for.** An article for technicians can assume `sudo` and a terminal; one for users
  cannot, and mixing them serves neither. Some teams keep two versions of the same fix.
- **Retire what is no longer true**, rather than leaving it with a warning at the top. A search that
  returns an old article first costs every person who meets it the time to discover that it is old.
