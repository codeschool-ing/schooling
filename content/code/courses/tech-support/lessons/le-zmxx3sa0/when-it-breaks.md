---
title: When the SLA is about to break
version: 1
---

Some tickets will not make their target. What matters is what happens before they miss it:

- **Say so before it breaks, not after.** A user told at three that the fix will be tomorrow can plan;
  one who finds out at six cannot. The ticket records when they were told.
- **Escalate early**, lesson 7. A ticket stuck on something the technician cannot do is not helped by
  more hours at the same desk.
- **Never game the clock.** Closing a ticket to stop the timer and reopening it, marking it *waiting on
  user* when the user is waiting on you, lowering its priority to fit the target: each makes the report
  look better and the service worse, and each is visible to anyone who reads the ticket's history.

An SLA report is useful for exactly one thing: showing where the service does not keep its promises,
so that something changes. A report that is always green because of how tickets are handled, rather
than how problems are solved, has stopped measuring anything.
