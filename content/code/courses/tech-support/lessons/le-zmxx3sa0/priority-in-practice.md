Four tickets arrive in the same half hour. Each is placed by the two questions, not by who sent it:

| ticket | impact | urgency | priority in the example |
|---|---|---|---|
| nobody in the office can print | medium: a team, with no way around | medium: today | P3 |
| the director's second monitor flickers | low: one person, one screen still works | low | P4 |
| Daniel cannot save a report due at five, lesson 3 | low: one person | high: a deadline today | P3 |
| the shop's card machine keeps restarting | high: every sale | high: now | P1 |

Two of these are uncomfortable, and that is the point of writing the rule down:

- **The director's monitor is P4.** Seniority is not impact. If the organisation decides that some
  people's tickets go first, that is a rule too, and it belongs in the matrix where everyone can see
  it, not in a technician's judgement under pressure.
- **Daniel's report is P3 with a deadline.** Priority says which queue it is in; the deadline goes
  in the ticket, lesson 5, and a technician who reads it can still pick it up before four.

**Priority can change**, and should: a P4 that turns out to affect the whole floor is reclassified, and
the ticket says when and why.
