title: Impact and urgency
version: 1
---

Priority is not a feeling about a ticket. It is the answer to **two separate questions**:

- **Impact**: how much is affected. One person with a way around it, a team, a whole office, the
  thing the business sells.
- **Urgency**: how fast it gets worse. Something needed in ten minutes, today, or whenever it is
  fixed.

Asked separately, they stop two common mistakes. **A loud ticket is not an urgent one**: somebody who
writes in capitals may have a low-impact problem that can wait. And **a small-looking ticket can be
high impact**: "the card machine at the front desk keeps restarting" is one device, and it is every
sale of the afternoon.

The numbers in the matrix are an example. What matters is that they are **written before the tickets
arrive**, so that two technicians give the same ticket the same priority, and a manager can see why.
