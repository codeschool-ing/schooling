---
title: What an SLA promises
version: 1
---

A **service level agreement**, SLA, is a written promise between the support team and the people it
serves: for each priority, how soon somebody responds and how soon the problem should be solved. An
example, and the numbers are the contract's, not a standard:

| priority | first response | resolution | counted in |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 15 minutes | 4 hours | every hour of every day |
| P2 | 1 hour | 8 hours | business hours |
| P3 | 4 hours | 3 business days | business hours |
| P4 | 1 business day | 10 business days | business hours |

Three words in it are worth reading exactly:

- **Response is not resolution.** Responding means a person has looked and said so. It is often the
  promise users care about most, because silence is what makes them phone.
- **Business hours** means the clock only runs while the desk is open. A promise of four hours made
  at half past four on a Friday ends on Monday, and the next section computes it.
- **The clock stops while waiting on the user**, lesson 5, and only then. Stopping it for anything
  else is how an SLA report looks good while users wait.
