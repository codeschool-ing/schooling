---
title: What you do not repeat
version: 1
---

Support sees things nobody meant to show it: a message that pops up during a remote session, a file name
on a desktop, a spreadsheet left open, the size of somebody's mailbox. The rule is short: **what you see
while working is not yours to tell**, not to colleagues, not as a funny story at lunch, not "just between
us".

| situation | what the technician does |
|---|---|
| a private message appears on the screen during a remote session | looks away from it, says nothing about it, carries on with the ticket |
| a colleague asks what is on the manager's computer | answers that they do not talk about what is on anyone's computer |
| the ticket needs a file, and the folder has others | opens the one the ticket needs |
| a user asks the technician to look at a colleague's e-mail | refuses; access to another person's data goes through a written request, lesson 12 |
| a user asks the technician to keep a problem quiet from their manager | fixes the problem; what goes in the ticket is the problem, not the person's opinion of their manager |

The last line matters because the ticket is also something people read. **Write the technical facts, not
what you saw on the way**: *the letter to the doctor would not print* is a ticket that repeats something;
*a document would not print from pc1* is enough to fix it.
