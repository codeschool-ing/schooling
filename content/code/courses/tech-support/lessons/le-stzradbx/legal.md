---
title: Legal: data and evidence
version: 1
---

Support meets legal less often, and each time matters more. Two kinds of request are common:

- **About a person's data.** Under the LGPD, a person can ask what data the organisation holds about
  them, or ask for it to be corrected or deleted. Those requests go to the organisation's data
  protection officer, the *encarregado*, and are answered through them. A technician who receives one
  directly passes it on and does not answer it alone, however simple it looks.
- **About evidence.** When something may end up in a dispute or an investigation, such as a suspected
  fraud or a security incident, legal may ask support to **preserve** a computer or an account. That
  means leave it exactly as it is: do not open files, do not "tidy up", do not reinstall. Record who
  touched it and when. A helpful clean-up can destroy the evidence it was meant to protect.

Never hand anyone data informally, **including a manager asking for an employee's e-mails**. Access to
another person's data goes through a written request, approved by whoever the policy names; lesson 13 is
about what a technician sees and does not repeat, and lesson 14 about saying no.
