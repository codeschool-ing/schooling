---
title: Management: a decision, not a report
version: 1
---

A technician writes to management when something needs a decision above their own, lesson 7's
hierarchical escalation. The mistake is to send a report of what happened and hope a decision comes
back. Send the decision:

```localised
Subject: Decision needed: the shared folder fills up again

What happened: on Tuesday the shared folder filled up and nobody in finance could save
files for part of the morning. It was cleared the same day.
Why it will happen again: a program from the sales system writes a log that grows
without limit. The sales team has been told; they have not given a date for a fix.
Options:
  1. Ask the sales team for a fix this month.   Cost: their time. Risk: it fills again first.
  2. Clear the log automatically every night.    Cost: an hour of our time. Risk: none known.
  3. A larger disk.                              Cost: a purchase. Risk: it only fills later.
Recommendation: 2 now, and 1 as the real fix.
I need an answer by Friday to set it up before the month-end reports.
```

Read it as its receiver: **one subject line that says a decision is needed**, the impact in business
terms, why it is not over, options with their cost and risk, a recommendation, and a date. Everything
technical fits in "a log that grows without limit". A manager can answer it in one line, and that is
the test of whether it was written well.
