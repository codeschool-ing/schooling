Ticket systems differ, and the facts they need do not:

| field | what goes in | why |
|---|---|---|
| requester | name, area, how to reach them | who to confirm with, lesson 1 |
| computer | its name, and the account used | lesson 2: whose problem you reproduced |
| symptom | the exact message, copied | lesson 1: a fact, not a summary |
| impact | what cannot be done, and by when it matters | the priority, lesson 6 |
| checked | each check and its result, including the ones that were fine | lesson 3: the layers crossed off |
| cause | what was wrong, in one sentence | the next technician starts here |
| done | what was changed, including what failed | so it can be undone, or not repeated |
| confirmed | who saw it working, and when | lesson 1's last step |
| pending | what is still open, and with whom | lesson 7 |
| times | opened, started, resolved | lesson 6 |

Two rules cover the rest. **Write as you go**, not at the end: at the end, what failed is forgotten
first, and it is the most useful part. And **copy, do not describe**: a message, a path, a command's
answer, pasted as it was.
