---
title: After escalating
version: 1
---

Escalating moves the work. It does not move **the promise to the user**, and that is where handovers
usually fail:

- **Somebody still talks to the user.** In most service desks that stays with level 1, who opened the
  ticket and knows the person. The people in sales were told the system is down and when they will hear
  more; that next update has to happen even if the answer is *still being worked on*.
- **Follow up.** A ticket passed on and never looked at again is how a P2 becomes a week. Check at the
  time you promised, and escalate hierarchically if the functional escalation is not moving, lesson 6.
- **Learn from the answer.** When the owners find the cause, read it. The next time a service fails on
  a permission, level 1 may recognise it in the first minute, and a runbook, lesson 8, may let them
  handle it without escalating at all.

**Throwing it over the wall** is the name for the opposite: passing a ticket with *"not mine"* and no
evidence, and forgetting it. It costs the receiver the work you already did, and costs the user a day.
