---
title: Keeping it true
version: 1
---

A runbook starts going wrong the day it is written, because the systems it describes change: a new
printer model reports a different reason, a queue is renamed, a command gains an option. **A runbook that
is wrong is worse than none**, because it is followed with confidence.

Three habits keep it true:

- **Every use is a review.** If a step showed something the runbook did not expect, the person who
  followed it corrects the runbook the same day, while the difference is on the screen in front of them.
- **It has an owner and a date.** The owner is who gets asked when it is unclear; the date says how
  much to trust it.
- **It is retired when the problem goes away.** A runbook for a printer the office no longer has is
  clutter that makes the useful ones harder to find.

When a runbook is followed often enough that every step is always the same, it is also a candidate for
a script: the steps that are commands become a program, and the steps that are conversations stay with
a person.
