---
title: What a runbook is, and when to write one
version: 1
---

A **runbook** is a written procedure for a problem that comes back: what to check, what to do, what the
result should look like at each step, and when to stop and hand it on. It turns a fix that lives in one
person's head into one the whole team can apply the same way.

Write one when:

- **the same fix has been done three times**, by anyone. The third time is the moment the pattern is
  clear and the details are still fresh.
- **getting it wrong is costly**: a step order that matters, a command that deletes something.
- **somebody will do it alone**, at night, on call, or on their first week.

A runbook is **not a knowledge base article**, lesson 9. An article explains a subject so that someone
understands it; a runbook tells someone what to do next, and is written to be followed under pressure
by a person who may not understand the system at all.
