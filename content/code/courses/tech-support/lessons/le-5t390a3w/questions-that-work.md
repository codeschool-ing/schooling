---
title: Questions that get facts
version: 1
---

The questions that work ask about **actions, times and places**, things a person remembers and can
check, rather than about causes, which they cannot:

| instead of | ask | what it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| "Did you change anything?" | "What were you doing just before it stopped?" | a timeline, without blame |
| "Is the printer on?" | "When did a document last come out?" | the moment it changed |
| "What's the error?" | "Can you show me what you see?" | the exact screen, lesson 1 |
| "Is it broken for everyone?" | "Does it happen from another computer, or to a colleague?" | where it is, lesson 1's isolate |
| "Did you install something?" | "Did anything look different this week, a new window, a question you answered?" | the change they did not count |

Two things about how they are asked matter as much as the words:

- **Closed questions come last.** "Yes or no" questions check a detail once you know where to look.
  Asked first, they get the answer the person thinks you want.
- **"Did you change anything?" is the question that fails most**, because it sounds like an accusation,
  and the honest answer from where they sit is "no". Asking what they were doing gets the same
  information without asking them to confess to it.

Asked of Bruno: *what were you doing just before?* He had saved a report as a PDF, and a window asked
him something about a printer, and he clicked the button to make it go away.
