Bruno, in sales, opens a ticket:

> *The printer is broken. Nothing I send comes out. I didn't change anything.*

Three sentences, and they are three different kinds of thing:

- **A fact he saw**: nothing comes out. That is the part to reproduce, lesson 1.
- **An explanation**: the printer is broken. It may be right, and it is a guess until something tests
  it. Often the user's explanation names the last thing they could see, and the fault is one step
  before it.
- **A belief about himself**: he changed nothing. He is not lying. People do not count as a change what
  they did as part of their work, such as a choice in a dialog box while saving a file. It is the
  sentence most worth a question.

**Take the fact, hold the explanation, and ask about the belief.** Arguing with the explanation starts a
debate; asking about what they did gets you a timeline.
