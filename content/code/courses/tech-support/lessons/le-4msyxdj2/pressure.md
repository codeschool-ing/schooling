---
title: When the person insists
version: 1
---

The hard cases are the ones with pressure behind them: a director who says *do it, I'll take
responsibility*, a user whose deadline is in an hour, a colleague who says *everyone else does it*.

- **Rank does not change the policy.** A senior person can ask for an exception, and exceptions exist,
  but they go through whoever owns the rule, in writing. A technician who makes one quietly, because the
  person was important, owns the consequences alone.
- **Urgency changes the order, not the answer.** A deadline is a reason to find the alternative fast,
  or to escalate at once. It is not a reason for the refused thing to become allowed.
- **Stay polite, and do not argue the rule.** The technician's job is to explain it, not to defend it
  or to agree that it is silly. If the rule really is wrong, that is a conversation for the person who
  owns it, lesson 12, and it can be raised after the call.

And when it is not clear whether something is allowed, the answer is not yes or no but *let me check*,
followed by checking.
