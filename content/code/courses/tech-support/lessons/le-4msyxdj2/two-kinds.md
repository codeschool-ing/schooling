---
title: Two kinds of limit
version: 1
---

The limits of the role come from two places:

- **The account cannot.** The organisation gave the support account the rights its work needs and no more:
  it can restart the print service but cannot create users, or it can reset passwords in one department
  and not another. The computer refuses, and the question ends there.
- **The account can, and the role cannot.** A support account with full `sudo`, like the one this course
  uses, can do almost anything on a computer. What it is *allowed* to do is written in the organisation's
  policy, and only the technician is between the two.

Requests that fall outside the role, and come up in every service desk:

| request | why it is outside the role |
|---|---|
| make me an administrator of my computer | it removes every other limit on that computer, for that person, from then on |
| install this program I found | nobody checked its licence or what it does; approved software goes through a list |
| turn off the antivirus, the computer is slow | it removes a protection the organisation decided to have; the slowness is a ticket |
| tell me my colleague's password, she is away | nobody's password is told to anyone; access is requested, lesson 12 |
| fix my laptop from home | personal equipment is outside the contract, and support has no records for it |
| unblock this website for me | blocks are decided by whoever owns the rule, not by the desk |

None of these are unreasonable things to *ask*. People ask for what solves their problem today, and
it is the technician who knows why the rule exists.
