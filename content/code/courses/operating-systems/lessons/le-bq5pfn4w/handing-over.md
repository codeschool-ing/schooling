## Reinstall macOS

From Recovery, **Reinstall macOS** puts a fresh copy of the system in place and **leaves the Data
volume alone**. Files, apps and settings are all still there afterwards. It is the Mac's version of
lesson 2's in-place upgrade: the right choice when macOS itself is misbehaving and the person is
keeping the Mac.

## Erase All Content and Settings

On Apple silicon, and on Intel Macs with a T2 chip, *System Settings > General > Transfer or Reset*
has **Erase All Content and Settings**. It is the Mac's equivalent of a phone's factory reset. It
destroys the encryption key of the Data volume, so every file, app, account and setting goes in
seconds; it keeps macOS as it is; and it **signs out of the Apple Account and turns off Activation
Lock** on the way. The Mac restarts into Setup Assistant, ready for the next person.

**This is the one for a Mac changing hands.** It asks for the administrator's password and, when the
Mac is signed into an Apple Account, for that account's password too. That second password is why
section 01 asked whose it is.

## Activation Lock, and why the third way fails

When a Mac with Apple silicon or a T2 chip is signed into an Apple Account with **Find My** turned on,
Apple's servers record it as belonging to that account. This is **Activation Lock**, and it is there
so a stolen Mac is worthless.

It lives at Apple, **not on the disk**. Erasing the disk in Disk Utility and reinstalling gives a Mac
that looks new and then, in Setup Assistant, asks for the old owner's Apple Account password before it
goes any further. In an office that is often a person who has left, and it can take days of proof of
purchase with Apple to undo.

So the rule, in order:

1. **Sign out of the Apple Account** (*System Settings >* the name at the top *> Sign Out*), or use
   *Erase All Content and Settings*, which does it for you.
2. Only then erase.
3. A Mac bought second-hand is checked for Activation Lock **before** it is paid for.

The same lock exists on phones, and it is why a support desk asks people to sign out before they hand a
device back, not after.
