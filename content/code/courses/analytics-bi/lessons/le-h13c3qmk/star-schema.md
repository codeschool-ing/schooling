---
title: Facts, dimensions and the star between them
version: 1

Lesson 2 split every question into **measures** — what is added up — and **dimensions** — what it
is broken down by. A semantic layer is laid out along the same split, in a shape that data
modelling calls a **star schema**.

- A **fact** table holds events, one row per event at a stated grain, and carries the measures:
  an order and its net revenue, a line and its value.
- A **dimension** table holds the things the events are about, one row per thing, and carries the
  attributes people group by: a customer with her region and segment, a product with its category,
  a day with its month and weekday.
- Each fact row points at the dimensions it involves by their keys: an order points at a customer
  and a day; a line points at an order and a product.

Drawn with the fact in the middle, the dimensions sit around it like the points of a star, which
is all the name means. The shape is popular for a reason that has nothing to do with drawing: **a
question is a join from the fact to the dimensions it needs, followed by a `GROUP BY`**, and with
this layout that join is always the same short one. "Net revenue by region and month" is orders,
joined to customers for the region and to the calendar for the month.

Lantern's layer has two facts, because it records two grains: `orders`, one row per order, and
`order_lines`, one row per product in an order. Revenue lives on the first, quantity and product
on the second. Which fact a question starts from depends on its grain, and getting that wrong is
the subject of the section after next.

The modelling of stars — choosing grains, building dimensions that keep their history, surrogate
keys — is the whole of `warehouse-modeling`. This course takes the shape as given and asks what it
does for the people reading it.
