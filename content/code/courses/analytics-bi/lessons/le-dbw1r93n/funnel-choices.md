---
title: Four decisions inside every funnel
version: 1
---

The funnel of the last section looks like a measurement. It is also four decisions, each of which
changes the numbers, and a funnel in any tool has made them whether or not the screen says so.

**What is counted: sessions or people.** Lantern's funnel counts visits. A customer who looks at a lamp
on Monday and buys it on Thursday is two sessions — one that stopped at the product page and one that
went straight to the cart — and counted that way, Monday is a loss. Counted by person, it is one
conversion. Session funnels are right for questions about the website itself (does this page work?);
person funnels for questions about customers (do people who look at lamps buy them?). Person funnels
need the identity of lesson 8, which Lantern's sessions do not carry.

**Whether order matters.** Here a session that reached step 4 reached steps 1 to 3 as well, by
construction. Real event data has sessions that reach checkout from a saved link without a product view.
A strict funnel counts only steps taken in order; a loose one counts any session that ever did each
thing. The strict one is smaller, and closer to "the path we designed".

**The time window.** A funnel of people needs a deadline: purchase within the same session, the same
day, seven days? A longer window converts more people and reports later. Two teams using different
windows will report different conversion rates for the same customers, both correct.

**What each step is divided by.** The previous step (as here) says where people leave; the first step
says what share of everybody got that far. Both are useful and they are easy to confuse on a chart.
Label which one it is.

And one decision that is not a choice but a check: **the steps' definitions must not change under the
funnel**. Lesson 8's renamed event turned a working cart into a step with zero sessions. A funnel is
only comparable over time while its events mean the same thing over time.
