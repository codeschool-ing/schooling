---
title: A dashboard has an owner and an end
version: 1
---

Dashboards accumulate. Every question somebody asked once becomes a page, and three years later the
company has four hundred of them, two thirds unopened for months, a dozen showing revenue by slightly
different definitions — lesson 3's problem, grown in a tool rather than in queries.

Three habits keep the number down:

- **Every dashboard has a named owner**, written on the page: the person who answers when a number
  looks wrong and who decides when the page changes. A page with no owner is a page nobody will fix.
- **Usage is measured.** BI tools record who opened what; Metabase's commercial editions include usage
  analytics, and the open-source edition's activity can be read from its own application database. A
  page no one has opened in three months is a candidate for retirement, and so is a page opened only
  by its author.
- **Retiring is normal.** Archive the page, keep its questions if they are used elsewhere, and say
  where its readers should look instead. A dashboard built for a campaign should have its end date
  written on it the day it is built.

A page that survives this is one people trust, because it answers their questions, its numbers have
context, and somebody is responsible for it. That is the whole of this lesson in one sentence, and the
next two lessons take the same numbers somewhere a dashboard cannot reach: into the tools where the
work is done.
