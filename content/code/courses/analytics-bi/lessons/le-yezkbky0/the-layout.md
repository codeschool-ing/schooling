---
title: The layout follows the questions
version: 1
---

Readers of a page in a left-to-right language start at the top left and scan down. The layout uses
that: **the most important answer goes where the eye lands first**, and detail goes where only the
interested arrive.

A tactical dashboard settles into the same four bands almost every time:

1. **The headline numbers**, a row of tiles across the top: this month's net revenue, orders, active
   customers. Each tile is one number with its context, which is the next section.
2. **The trend** below them: the same measures over time, so the reader sees whether the headline is
   normal.
3. **The breakdowns**: by region, by segment, by channel — where the number comes from.
4. **The detail** at the bottom, or behind a click: the table of orders somebody will want once in a
   while.

The figure is Lantern's Monday page, with the numbers it shows for May 2026; every number on it
comes from a query in this lesson. Three things about it are deliberate. There are three tiles, not
eight, because there are three questions. The trend shows a full year rather than six weeks, because
the question is whether the month is unusual and that needs a baseline. And the text card at the top
right says when the data ends and links the definitions, because a reader who does not know that
June is partial will draw the wrong conclusion from the trend.

**Group what is compared**: tiles that are read together sit together, in the same order everywhere
on the page, with the same colours meaning the same things. If net revenue is the first tile, it is
the first line of the trend and the first column of the breakdown.
