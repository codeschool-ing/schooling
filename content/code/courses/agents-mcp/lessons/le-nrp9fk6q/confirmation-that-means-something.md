---
title: A confirmation that means something
version: 2
---

The refunds agent's run asked a person twice: the host, because `refunds__refund` is in `CONFIRM`, and the server, because `refund_mcp.py` will not refund without an approval of its own (lesson 15 section 06). The person said yes both times, to a refund of 0 cents. A confirmation is only a boundary if the person can actually decide, and four habits make the difference.

- **Show what will happen, exactly.** The host's question carried the tool and all its arguments: order `M-1047`, `"0"` cents, the reason. That is the question that can be read, and it was answered without being read, which no wording fixes. A question like *"allow the refund tool?"* asks the person to approve a category, which they will, every time. Lessons 8 and 9 made the same point: approve this call, with these arguments.
- **Ask only where the answer matters.** Reading an order needed nobody. A host that asks before every call trains its people to press `y` without reading, which is the state in which the one question that mattered is answered like the others.
- **Put rules with a right answer in code, before the person.** Lesson 9's hook refused refunds over 50.00 without asking anybody. A person should be asked to judge, not to enforce a limit a program can enforce.
- **Let a refusal be safe.** When the person said no in lesson 15, the model was told plainly that nothing was done, and it sent the customer to customer service instead of pretending. A host whose refusals break the conversation teaches people to approve to keep things moving.

The server's own question is the other half. It does not know or care what the host asked: its owner decided that this system will not refund without an approval, and it asks every host the same way. **Neither owner has to trust the other to have asked**, which is what makes the two together stronger than either.
