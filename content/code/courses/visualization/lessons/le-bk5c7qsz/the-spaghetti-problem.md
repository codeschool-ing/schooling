A chart like this is called a **spaghetti chart**, and it fails in a predictable order:

- **The top line is fine.** Southeast is far above the others and nothing crosses it.
- **Crossings cost the most.** South and Northeast trade places during 2025, and a reader following
  one of them has to guess which is which at every crossing, especially when the colours are close.
- **Small series get squashed.** Centre-West and North share the bottom tenth of the chart, and their
  shape, which may be the most interesting thing about them, is a few pixels tall.
- **The legend becomes a lookup table.** Five colours to match, and a reader who cannot tell two of
  them apart (lesson 14) cannot use the chart at all.

Adding a sixth or seventh region makes all four worse at once.

## What the reader actually wants

Usually one of two things: **the shape of each region on its own** (is it growing, is it seasonal,
did something happen in March?), and **a comparison between regions** (which is biggest, which grew
fastest?). One chart with five lines does the second badly and the first worse. The next section
shows a layout that does both.
