title: Ordering the panels
version: 1
---

Panels are categories, and lesson 3 settled how to order categories: **by the question, unless they
have an order of their own**.

- **Panels that are steps in time keep their order.** Twelve monthly maps run January to December;
  four quarterly panels run Q1 to Q4.
- **Panels that are categories are sorted by something meaningful.** The grids in this lesson run from
  the region with the most orders in December 2025 to the one with the fewest, so the reader meets
  them in order of size.
- **Alphabetical order is the last resort**, useful only when the reader will look up one panel by
  name and there are many of them, such as 27 states. Even then, grouping them by region first, and
  alphabetically inside each region, keeps neighbours together.

## Sort by what the reader will look for

The same five panels could be sorted by size, by growth, or by how seasonal each region is, and each
order tells a different story before a single line is read:

| sorted by | first panel | the story it starts |
|---|---|---|
| December 2025 orders | Southeast | where the volume is |
| growth since January 2024 | North (index 325) | where the business is moving |
| order of the file | Southeast, then South | nothing |

Pick the one that matches the question the grid answers, and **say in the title what the order is**:
"Regions, largest first" costs four words and saves the reader from guessing.

## The position of the panels is a channel too

Panels arranged on a map's rough geography, North at the top and South at the bottom, as the tile map
in lesson 8 did, let the reader find a region by where it is. It is worth doing when the audience
knows the geography and the panels are many.
