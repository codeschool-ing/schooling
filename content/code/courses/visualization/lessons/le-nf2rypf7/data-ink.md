title: The data-ink ratio
version: 1

Edward Tufte, in *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983), divided the ink on a chart
into two kinds. **Data ink** is the ink that would change if the data changed: the bars, the line, the
dots. Everything else is **non-data ink**: frames, backgrounds, gridlines, borders, shading, legends.

The **data-ink ratio** is the first divided by the total:

```localised
data-ink ratio = data ink / total ink used to print the chart
```

A ratio of 1 means every mark on the chart is data. Tufte's advice was to push it up, within reason,
in two moves: **erase non-data ink**, and **erase redundant data ink**, such as a value shown by a
bar's length, its label and a gridline all at once.

Each panel keeps everything the reader needs to answer "which category earns most, and how much?".
What goes is what they had to look past.

## Why it matters

Non-data ink is not neutral. Every line on a chart asks to be read, and the eye cannot ignore a mark
until it has looked at it. A heavy grid, a dark frame and a grey background are three things the reader
inspects and rejects before reaching the bars. On a dashboard with eight charts, that is twenty-four.

The ratio is a way of seeing that cost, not a score to maximise. The last section of this lesson is
about where to stop.
