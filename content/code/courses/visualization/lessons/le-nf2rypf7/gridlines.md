A gridline is a lookup aid. It earns its place when the reader needs to read a value off the chart
with more precision than the position alone gives. The question is never "grid or no grid" but how
heavy, how many, and in which direction.

## Rules of thumb

- **Faint, not absent.** A light grey a step away from the background is visible when the eye looks
  for it and invisible otherwise. If the grid is the first thing you notice, it is too dark.
- **Few.** Four to six lines on an axis. Gridlines at every tick of a fine scale form a mesh the data
  has to be read through.
- **One direction.** A bar chart needs lines across the bars' length, not along them. A line chart over
  time rarely needs vertical lines; the month labels do that job.
- **Behind the data.** In matplotlib, `ax.set_axisbelow(True)` draws the grid under the bars instead of
  over them.
- **Or none, with values.** When only a few values matter, such as the first, the last and the peak,
  writing them beside the points is more precise than any grid and uses less ink.

## Ticks and axis lines

Tick marks are a grid in miniature and follow the same rules. A bar chart with values on every bar
does not need its value axis at all, which is why the clean chart in the last section has no numbers
along the bottom. A line chart usually keeps its value axis, because the reader wants to know the
scale of the whole line, not only of the labelled points.
