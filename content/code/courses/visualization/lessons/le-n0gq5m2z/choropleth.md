---
title: Shading by rate, not by count
version: 1
---

A **choropleth map** shades each area by a value. It is the map everyone draws first, and it has one
rule that most of them break: **shade by a rate, not by a count**.

The figures in this lesson draw Brazil as **one square per state**, placed roughly where the state
is. That is called a tile map, the last section of this lesson says why it is useful in its own
right, and here it lets the course draw every map from the same 27 numbers without a geographic file.

## Which denominator

The rate needs a denominator that matches the question:

- **per person**, for how well a product reaches a population;
- **per household or per customer**, when the unit that buys is not a person;
- **per area**, for density, such as stores per thousand square kilometres;
- **as a share**, such as the percentage of orders delivered late in each state.

The rule has one exception: **a count belongs on a map as a symbol**, a circle sized by the number,
drawn on top of the areas. That is two sections from here.
