A choropleth can shade each state continuously, every value its own shade, or it can group the
values into **classes**, typically four to seven, each with one shade. Classes are easier to read,
since a reader can match five shades to a legend and cannot match twenty-seven, and they hide a
choice that changes the whole map: **where the breaks between classes go**.

Both rows show the same 27 rates, cut into four classes.

- **Equal intervals** split the range from 0.23 to 1.96 into four equal widths, with breaks at 0.66,
  1.09 and 1.52. The breaks are easy to explain, but 21 of the 27 states crowd into the two lowest
  classes, so most of the map is pale, and the top class holds a single state, the Federal District.
- **Quantiles** put about the same number of states in each class, with breaks at 0.44, 0.68 and
  1.01. Every shade is used about equally, so the map has more contrast, and two states with very
  different rates can share a class while two almost identical ones are split.

A third method, **natural breaks** (also called Jenks), puts the breaks in the largest gaps between
values, so that each class holds states that are close together. It often fits the data best and is
the hardest to explain.

**There is no neutral choice.** The same data drawn with two methods can make the North look like a
uniform failure or like a gradual slope. So the method is part of what the chart says:

- **state the method and the breaks in the legend**, so the reader can see what each shade covers;
- **use round numbers** for the breaks when you choose them by hand: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 orders per
  thousand;
- **never change the breaks between two maps meant to be compared**, such as 2024 against 2025, or
  the comparison is between methods and not between years.

## Shades, not hues

The classes are ordered, so the shades must be ordered too: **one hue from light to strong**, as in
lesson 1. A choropleth in a rainbow of hues makes the reader consult the legend for every state, and
lesson 12 is about better palettes for maps that have a meaningful middle.
