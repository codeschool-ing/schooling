---
title: When the points hide each other
version: 1
---

A scatterplot draws every point in full, and that is its weakness as much as its strength. When many
points land in the same place, they are drawn on top of each other, and **a hundred points in one spot
look exactly like one**. That is called **overplotting**.

Items in a basket are whole numbers, so all the deliveries with 8 items sit on one vertical line, and
there are 46 of them. On the left they are 46 dots drawn over each other, and the column for 8 items
looks no busier than the column for 17, which has 6. The chart has lost the one thing it should show:
where the data is.

## Four fixes, from lightest to heaviest

1. **Transparency.** Draw each point half or a third opaque. Where points pile up, they add up to a
   darker spot, and density becomes visible. In matplotlib, `alpha=0.3`.
2. **Jitter.** Move each point a small random distance along the axis that has repeated values. It
   is honest as long as the movement is smaller than the gap between real values, so no point is
   moved into a neighbour's column, and the reader is told.
3. **Smaller marks, or hollow ones.** A small circle overlaps less than a large one, and an outline
   lets the points behind it show through.
4. **Bin the plane.** With tens of thousands of points, count them in a grid of small squares or
