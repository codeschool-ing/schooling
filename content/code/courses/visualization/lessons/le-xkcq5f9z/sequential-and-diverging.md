## Sequential

A sequential palette maps low to high onto **one steady change in lightness**, usually with a hue or
two along the way for richness. Its rules come straight from lesson 11.

- **Lightness changes in one direction only.** Every step lighter, or every step darker, never back.
- **The steps are even to the eye**, which needs a perceptual space; a ramp built in HSL can bunch up,
  as lesson 11's yellow ramp did.
- **Which end is "more"** is a convention, and either works if it is kept. On a light page, dark
  usually means more, because dark ink is what the eye notices. On a dark background the opposite
  reads more naturally.

**viridis**, matplotlib's default colour map since version 2.0 in 2017, is the standard example: dark purple through
blue and green to yellow, getting lighter at every step. **cividis** is a variant tuned for the
commonest forms of colour blindness. Lesson 7's heatmap and lesson 8's choropleth both used a
single-hue sequential scale.

## Diverging

A diverging palette joins **two sequential scales back to back** at a light middle: one hue gets
stronger below the middle and the other gets stronger above it.

The map colours each state by how its region's growth from 2024 to 2025 compares with Horta's overall
growth of 25.9%. The middle, **the company's own growth**, means something: a region on it is keeping
pace. North and Northeast are blue, growing faster; Southeast is red, growing slower, though it is
still the largest region by far; South is almost exactly on the line.

Three rules make a diverging palette honest.

- **The middle sits on the meaningful value**, zero, a target or an average, and not on the midpoint
  of the data's range. Centred on the wrong value, the palette paints half the map red for no reason.
- **The two arms are symmetric**: the same number of steps and the same lightness at the same distance
  from the middle, so +10 and −10 look equally strong.
- **The two hues are not red and green.** Red against blue, or orange against purple, survives colour
  blindness; red against green does not.
