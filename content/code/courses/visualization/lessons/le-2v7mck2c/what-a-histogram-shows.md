A bar chart compares categories. A **histogram** shows how the values of one quantity are spread
out: which values are common, which are rare, where the bulk sits and how far the extremes reach.
That spread is called a **distribution**, and seeing its shape is often the first thing to do with a
column of numbers.

It is built in three steps:

1. cut the range of the values into **bins**, intervals of equal width;
2. **count** how many values fall in each bin;
3. draw one bar per bin, as tall as its count, **with no gaps between the bars**.

Horta's 400 deliveries, in bins of 5 minutes, show three things the average of 34.7 minutes cannot:

- **the bulk**: 232 of the 400 deliveries, 58%, took between 25 and 40 minutes;
- **the peak**: the busiest bin is 30 to 35 minutes, with 87 deliveries;
- **the tail**: the bars thin out to the right and do not stop at 60. Sixteen took an hour or more,
  and one took 101 minutes. A customer who got that delivery does not care about the average.

## Why the bars touch

In lesson 3 the gaps between bars said the categories were separate. Here **the bins are not
separate**: 30 to 35 ends exactly where 35 to 40 begins, and a delivery of 34.9 minutes and one of
35.0 are neighbours. The touching bars say the axis is one continuous quantity cut into pieces.

## Reading the shape

Some shapes have names, and naming them is what a histogram is for:

- **symmetric**: the two sides mirror each other, like heights of adults;
- **skewed right**: a long tail to the right, like these delivery times, incomes and waiting times.
  Anything with a floor and no ceiling tends to look like this;
- **bimodal**: two peaks, which almost always means two groups mixed together, such as deliveries by
  bicycle and by van in one column;
- **uniform**: flat, every value about as common as any other.

The shape decides what can be said honestly about the data. With a long right tail, the mean is
pulled towards the tail, so the median, 33.05 minutes here, describes the typical delivery better.
The `statistics` course treats that in full; this course is about seeing it.
