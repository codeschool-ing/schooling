An **annotation** is text or a mark placed on the chart to explain one part of it: an arrow with a
note, a reference line, a shaded period, a value written beside the point that matters. Titles and
labels describe the whole chart; annotations explain moments in it.

The chart carries three kinds:

- **a reference line**, dashed at 15,000 orders a month, so the reader measures the line against
  something. The first month above it that is not a December, June 2025, is marked;
- **values on the points that matter**: the two December peaks, 16,663 and 20,586;
- **a short note** where the reader would otherwise wonder: the spikes fall back every January.

The output says something the chart alone could mislead about: **Northeast was first ahead in July
2024**, fell back in August and September, and stayed ahead only from October. A title saying
"Northeast overtook South in July 2024" would be true of one month and false of the story. The program
computes the month it puts in the annotation, so the words and the lines cannot disagree.

## Rules for annotating

- **Annotate the point, not everything.** Two or three notes on a chart; more and they compete like a
  rainbow of colours.
- **Put the note near what it explains**, with a thin line or arrow if it cannot sit right beside it.
- **Write the note as a finding**: "first month above 15,000", not "note 1".
- **Explain the strange things.** A dip, a spike or a gap with no explanation makes the reader invent
  one. If you know why, say so; if you do not, say that too: "data missing for March".
