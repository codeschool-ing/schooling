On the desktop the reader's eye goes from the top left across and down. On the phone it goes down. If
the blocks were written in importance order, numbering them left to right and top to bottom gives the
same sequence in both layouts, and the phone version needs no rearranging at all.

## What to do with each block

- **The cards** stack one per row, full width. Two short cards can share a row if both numbers still
  fit at a readable size; three cannot.
- **The main chart** goes straight after the cards and takes the full width. It may need a different
  shape: taller than it is wide, rather than the wide strip that suits a laptop.
- **Supporting charts** follow, in the order they explain the main one.
- **Detail tables** are the hardest. A table of six columns does not fit 360 pixels at any readable
  size. Cut it to the two or three columns that matter, or replace it with a sorted bar chart, or move
  it behind a link.

## What to leave out

A phone screen is a chance to ask lesson 18's question again: does the reader need this? A tile that
survived on the desktop because there was room for it often has no place on the phone. If the phone
version is clearer without it, the desktop version probably is too.
