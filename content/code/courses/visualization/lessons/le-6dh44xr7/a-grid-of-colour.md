title: A grid of colour
version: 1

A **heatmap** is a table whose cells are coloured by their values. It keeps the table's structure,
rows and columns of categories or steps, and lets colour do the first reading. The reader sees where
the large values are before reading any of them.

A manager planning shifts needs exactly those two facts, and the heatmap delivers them in a glance.

## When a heatmap is the right chart

**Both axes are categories or ordered steps, and the value is a quantity.** Days by hours, regions by
months, products by stores. If one axis is a continuous quantity, a line chart per row usually reads
better; if both are continuous, the data is a scatterplot.

**The pattern matters more than the exact values.** Lesson 2 put colour at the bottom of the ladder:
nobody can tell 147 from 150 by shade. A heatmap is for finding the hot spots and the shapes, and the
exact numbers belong in the cells or a table beside it (the last section of this lesson).

**conditional formatting** with a **colour scale**. Choose a scale that runs from white or pale to one
strong colour. The three-colour red, yellow and green scale that spreadsheets offer first is the
wrong choice here, and lesson 12 explains why.
