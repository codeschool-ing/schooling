title: The five-second test
version: 1
---

A dashboard is finished when somebody who has never seen it can say what it shows after a glance.
The cheapest way to find out is to ask.

**Show the screen to a colleague for five seconds, then hide it**, and ask:

1. What was the most important number?
2. Is it going up or down?
3. What would you look at next?

If they name the number you meant, the hierarchy works. If they say "there was a pie chart", the
pie was the loudest thing on the screen. If they cannot say what to look at next, the supporting
charts are not grouped or not ordered.

## A checklist for the slower look

After the glance, the same colleague can go through the screen with this list:

- **Every number has a label with its period and its unit.**
- **Every number has a comparison**, and the comparison says what it is against.
- **The main chart is the biggest thing on the screen**, and it is the one the first question needs.
- **Related things are close together**, and the gaps between groups are wider than the gaps inside
  them.
- **Colours mean the same thing everywhere**, and nothing depends on colour alone.
- **Nothing is there by default**: every tile answers one of the questions written down at the start.
- **Charts that will be compared share a scale.**

The test takes a minute and catches most of what this lesson is about. It is worth running again
every time a tile is added, because dashboards rarely get worse by design. They get worse one
reasonable request at a time.
