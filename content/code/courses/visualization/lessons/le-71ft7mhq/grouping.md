The eye does not read a screen tile by tile. It sees **groups** first, and the groups it sees are
decided by a few rules that psychologists describe as the Gestalt principles of perception. Three
of them do most of the work on a dashboard:

- **Proximity.** Things that are close together are read as belonging together.
- **Common region.** Things inside the same box or on the same background are read as a group.
- **Similarity.** Things that look alike, in colour, size or shape, are read as the same kind.

The six tiles on the left are spaced evenly, so the eye sees six unrelated numbers and has to read
each label to sort them. On the right the same tiles sit in two clusters, sales and delivery, with a
wider gap between the clusters than inside them and a heading over each. The reader sees two
subjects before reading a single label.

## Using the rules deliberately

- **Gaps carry meaning.** Use a small gap inside a group and a larger one between groups. Equal
  gaps everywhere say there are no groups.
- **One subject per region.** If a box holds a chart, its title and its filter, it should hold
  nothing else.
- **Similar means the same.** If Southeast is blue in one chart it is blue in every chart (lesson
  13). A colour that changes meaning between tiles is read wrongly with confidence.
- **Align everything.** Edges that line up form invisible lines the eye follows; a tile a few
  pixels out of line looks like a mistake, because it is one.
- **Share scales where charts are compared.** Two bar charts side by side on different axes invite a
  comparison they cannot support, the small-multiples rule from lesson 10.
