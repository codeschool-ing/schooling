title: Must, should, could, won't
version: 1
---

With the skeleton running and the one thing identified, everything else still has to be sorted. A
common way to do it is **MoSCoW**, four buckets named by their initials: must, should, could, won't
(this time). Here is loanbook's whole list, as it stood after the brief:

| bucket | loanbook | the test for the bucket |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | list, lend, take back; refuse a second loan; show what is late | without it, the brief's *done when* fails |
| **should** | clear error messages; a page that works on a phone; labels a screen reader can use | without it, it works and a reviewer notices the gap |
| **could** | a nicer layout; seed data for a demo; a search box | nice, and nobody would miss it in the first version |
| **won't** (this time) | e-mail reminders; reports; the library; accounts | named in *out of scope*, kept for later |

Two rules make the buckets honest. **The must bucket is defined by the brief, not by taste**: an item
is a must only if a *done when* line fails without it. And **won't is a bucket, not a bin**: things in
it are written down, which is what lets you say no to them today without losing them.

The should bucket is where portfolio projects differ from work projects. Error messages, a phone
layout and accessible labels are *should* for Marta, who would use it without them. For a reviewer,
who is checking what you notice, they are close to *must*, and lessons 11 and 13 treat them that way.
