---
title: Where good problems come from
version: 1
---

The shape comes from your track; the problem itself has to come from somewhere, and the best ones
come from places where **a real person has a real inconvenience**. Four places to look:

- **Your previous work, in any field.** A shop, a clinic, a school office, a warehouse: every one has
  a sheet of paper, a spreadsheet or a routine that somebody hates. loanbook is exactly this.
- **A small organisation you know.** A sports club, a church, a community project, a relative's
  business. Small organisations have real problems and no budget, and you can ask them what they need.
- **A public dataset or public API** with a question nobody has answered in the way you would. Open
  data from a city or a government agency is usually messier and more interesting than a teaching
  dataset.
- **A tool you wished existed** while studying this track. If you needed it, others at your level
  did too, and you know exactly what it has to do.

Two sources to avoid. **A clone of a famous product**, because it invites a comparison with a team of
hundreds that you cannot win, and it is already in lesson 2's pile of tutorials. And **an idea that
needs users to be worth anything**, such as a marketplace or a social network: with no users it is an
empty screen, and you cannot get them in six weeks.

A real person has one more advantage: **they give you a brief**, which is lesson 4.
