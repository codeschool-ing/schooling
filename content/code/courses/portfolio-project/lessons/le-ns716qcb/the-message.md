---
title: The message that started it
version: 1
---

Real briefs rarely arrive as briefs. This is the message that started loanbook, written for this
course the way the coordinator of a school's IT room would write it: quickly, between two other
things, to somebody she knows can program.

```localised
Hi! Remember the sheet on the IT room door for the projectors? It's a mess again.
Yesterday two teachers turned up for the same projector at 8am, each sure she'd
booked it, and one class had no projector. Half the time people forget to write
when they bring things back, so the sheet says things are out that are on the shelf.
Could you make something on the computer for this? Nothing fancy. It would be great
if it sent reminders too, and if the head could see reports at the end of term.
And maybe the students could use it for the library books? Thanks!!
Marta
```

Read it as a brief and sort what is there. **The problem** is clear and real: the sheet is wrong in
two directions, things marked out that are in, and nothing stopping a double booking. **A must not**
is hiding in the first story: two people cannot have the same projector at once. **Users** are
teachers and Marta, and nothing says how many.

Then notice what the message asks for without meaning to. Reminders, reports and the library are
**three more projects**, each with its own users and rules. Marta is not wrong to want them; she is
listing everything she can imagine, which is what everyone does before somebody asks them what
matters first. And *nothing fancy* is not a constraint, it is a hope.

The message leaves out the thing you most need: **how she will know it worked.** That is the first
question to ask back.
