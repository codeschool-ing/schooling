With the message and the answers, the brief writes itself in about ten minutes. This is the one the
rest of the course builds against:

```localised
loanbook — brief

Problem   The loan sheet on the IT room door is wrong in both directions: items
          marked out are on the shelf, and nothing stops two teachers taking the
          same projector at the same time.
Users     About 40 teachers, on the staffroom PC or a phone. Marta, in the IT room.
Must      Show every item, and for each one on loan, who has it and until when.
          Lend an item to a named person. Take it back.
          Show which loans are late.
Must not  Lend an item that is already out, even if two people try at the same moment.
          Lose a loan that has been recorded.
Out of    Reminders by e-mail. Reports for the head. The library. Accounts and
scope     passwords: the borrower is a name typed in.
Done when A second loan of an item that is out is refused, with who has it.
          A returned item shows as available straight away.
          A loan is marked late the day after it is due.
          Marta can open it from the IT room and a teacher from a phone.
```

Every line of *done when* can be checked by doing something and looking at the result. That is the
difference between a criterion and a wish: *easy to use* cannot be checked; *a teacher can lend an
item from a phone* can. Three of the four lines become tests in lesson 12, and the fourth is checked
by hand in lesson 13.

**Out of scope** names three of Marta's ideas and keeps them. They are not rejected, only not first,
and the README's *not yet* section in lesson 16 is this line, rewritten for strangers.
