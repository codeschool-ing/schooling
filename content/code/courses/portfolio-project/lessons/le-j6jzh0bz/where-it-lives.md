**In the repository**, as `RETROSPECTIVE.md` beside the README, with one line in the README pointing to it.
A retrospective in a blog post or a private notebook is fine for you and invisible to a reviewer, and the
reviewer is half of the point. loanbook's history ends at `v1.0.0`, so its retrospective would be the next
commit; the course shows the text rather than adding a step to the history.

**Written soon after**, within a week of the last milestone, while the weekly notes of lesson 7 still make
sense and the reasons are still in your head. A retrospective written six months later is lesson 7's
warning in reverse: what you would like to have happened.

**And then the project is finished.** Lesson 2's five checks all pass; the README explains it in sixty
seconds; the two minutes are rehearsed; the questions are expected; the retrospective says what you would do
next. There will always be a next feature, and it belongs to *not yet*, not to *not finished*. Decide
whether you will keep improving it or archive it, lesson 18, and say so.

The course ends here, and the next one begins where this one leaves the project: **first-job** takes the
finished work to the people who will read it, through the CV, the applications and the interviews that
this course has been preparing for since its first lesson.
