---
title: What a retrospective is for
version: 1
---

A retrospective is a short, written look back at a piece of work, and it has three readers.

**You, on the next project.** Lesson 5 asked you to write estimates before starting and keep them;
lesson 7 asked for a weekly note. This is where they are compared with what happened. Without the
comparison, the next estimate is as wrong as this one was, for the same reason.

**A reviewer.** Lesson 1's three proofs were *you can finish*, *you can decide* and *you can explain*. A
retrospective adds a fourth that no other file in the repository can show: **you can learn from your own
work.** A candidate who writes *the rules took five days where I estimated three and a half, because I
had not counted the error handling* has shown a habit teams value more than any single technology.

**The interview.** *What would you do differently?* is asked in almost every one, lesson 20. A written
retrospective means the answer exists, is specific, and has been thought through, rather than improvised
into something vague about *better planning*.

What it is not: a list of apologies, a defence of every choice, or a changelog. It is a page of facts about
how the work went, with the conclusions you drew from them.
