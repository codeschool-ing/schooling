---
title: What a tutorial proves
version: 1
---

A tutorial is a good way to learn. It is a weak way to be judged, and the reason is what it leaves
you to do.

In a tutorial somebody else made every decision that matters. They chose the problem, the data, the
libraries and the structure. They met the errors before you did and wrote the steps that avoid them.
What is left for the person following it is typing, and making it run on their machine. That proves
you can follow instructions carefully, which is a real skill and the one everybody else applying can
show too.

There is a second problem, and the reader sees it immediately: **the same tutorial is in hundreds of
portfolios**. A to-do list, a weather app, a clone of a well-known site, a movie recommender on the
same public dataset. A reviewer who has read twenty of them recognises the twenty-first from its
file names, and at that point reads nothing else in it. It does not count against you. It simply
does not count.

So the question to ask of anything in a portfolio is: **what did I decide here that the instructions
did not?** For a tutorial followed to the letter the honest answer is nothing, and that is what the
reader concludes too.
