title: The tutorials you already have
version: 1
---

None of this means deleting what you learned from. The tutorials you followed are a record of how
you got here, and some readers like seeing it. What matters is where they sit.

- **Leave them public, and do not pin them.** Lesson 1's museum problem is about what is seen first,
  not about what exists.
- **Say what they are.** A one-line description, *following the official tutorial for X*, turns a
  thing that looks like a weak project into an honest note of what you studied.
- **Archive the ones you will not touch again.** GitHub marks an archived repository as read-only,
  which tells a reader it is finished in the sense of *left*, not in the sense of lesson 2.
- **Never present one as your own work.** A reviewer who recognises it stops trusting the rest, and
  the rest may have been good.

Then spend the time you would have spent on a sixth tutorial on the one project. The effort that
went into five small things, put into one, is the difference between five first lines ticked and
one of each.
