---
title: A milestone is something you can show
version: 1
---

A milestone is not a date, and it is not a percentage. It is **a state of the project you could
demonstrate to somebody**, with a date attached. *80% done* cannot be demonstrated; *a teacher can lend
a projector and the second loan is refused* can.

loanbook had four:

| milestone | what can be shown | the brief's lines it closes |
|---|---|---|
| `v0.1.0` the skeleton | a page that lists, lends and takes back, end to end | none yet, and that is expected |
| `v0.2.0` the rules | a second loan refused with who has it; late loans marked | the first and third *done when* lines |
| `v0.3.0` deployed | the page at an address, over HTTPS, surviving a restart | the fourth: Marta and a phone can open it |
| `v1.0.0` worth showing | README, screenshot, seed data, licence | the whole of lesson 2's *finished* |

Three properties make these good milestones. **Each one is demonstrable**, which means each ends in a
thing that runs rather than in a document. **Each one is useful on its own**: if the project had stopped
at `v0.2.0`, what existed would still have been the interesting part. And **they are few**: four
milestones for five weeks. A milestone every two days is a task list; one for the whole project is a
wish.

Notice that the second milestone is the one thing from lesson 5. Putting the point of the project in
the first milestone after the skeleton is deliberate: whatever happens later, the part that proves
something is already done.
