---
title: Rehearsing until it fits
version: 1
---

Two minutes written is not two minutes spoken. The first time a script is read aloud with a clock, it
usually runs long, and the way to fix it is not to speak faster. It is to cut.

**Time it, aloud, three times.** Silent reading is faster than speech and hides the problem. Record
yourself on a phone if you can; listening back finds the sentences that are hard to say.

**Cut from the middle.** The problem and the result are short already. What grows is the decision, where
every detail feels essential to the person who made it. Keep the choice and its reason; cut the second
reason, the history of how you got there and the alternatives you considered, which are exactly what the
questions of lesson 20 will ask for.

**Use the verbs from lesson 3.** The job adverts you collected say what the team needs done: *design and
maintain APIs*, *investigate incidents*, *document procedures*. Where your story honestly does one of those
things, say it with their verb. It translates your story into the listener's language.

**Say it to a person.** A friend, a mentor, anybody who will tell you where they stopped following. The
point where their attention drifts is the sentence to cut or rewrite.

A rehearsed two minutes sounds less rehearsed than an improvised one, because the words have stopped
being something you are looking for.
