---
title: Showing it live
version: 1
---

Sometimes the two minutes come with a screen: *can you show me?* A live demo is the strongest evidence
there is, and the most fragile, so prepare for both.

**Have the page open before you are asked.** Seeded, lesson 17, on the screen that proves the point:
the list with one loan overdue. The first ten seconds of a demo spent typing an address and waiting for a
free tier to wake up are ten seconds of nothing.

**Show one thing happening.** The same beat as the video's best moment: lend an item, then try to lend it
again and let the refusal speak. One action that shows the rule is worth more than a tour of every
screen.

**Have the recording ready.** Networks fail, laptops update themselves, free tiers go to sleep. The
ninety-second video of lesson 17, or the transcript of lesson 15's deploy, is the fallback: *the network
here is not cooperating, here is the recording*. Said calmly, that sentence is itself evidence of
preparation.

**Never debug live.** If something breaks during a demo, say what broke in one sentence, switch to the
recording, and move on. Five minutes of an interview spent watching you search for a typo is five minutes
the interviewer will remember instead of the project.
