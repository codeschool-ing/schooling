A portfolio has readers, and they are not the same person. It usually meets three, sometimes one
person wearing all three hats, and each one reads to a different depth.

**Screening** comes first. It may be a recruiter or a person on the team, and the question is narrow:
is there anything finished here? They look at your profile, at the repositories you chose to pin, and
at the first screen of one README. If that first screen says what the project is and shows it
working, the portfolio has done its job at this level. If it opens on a list of installation steps
for something they cannot picture, it has not.

**The hiring manager** reads further. They want to know whether you would solve the kind of problem
their team has. They read the README, open the live link or watch the demo, and look for the part
where you explain a decision: why this database, why no login, what you left out.

**The technical interviewer** reads the code, usually in preparation for talking to you. They look
at the commits, at the tests, at the one part of the project that was hard, and then they ask you
about it. What they are checking is less the code than whether you can explain it.

Two things follow. **Most readers stop at the first level**, so the first screen carries more weight
than everything below it. And **each level has to hold up on its own**: a strong README over code
nobody can explain fails at the third reader, and good code behind a README that says nothing never
reaches the second.
