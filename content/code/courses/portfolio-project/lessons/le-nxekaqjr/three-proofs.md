---
title: What one project has to prove
version: 1
---

Strip away the technology and a portfolio project proves three things about the person who made it.
Each maps to one of the three readers.

**That you can finish.** A project that runs, that somebody else can start from the README, that has
an address or a recording. Every reader has seen plenty of projects that stopped short of the end,
which is why a finished one stands out before anybody reads a line of code. This is what screening sees.

**That you can decide.** Real work is a sequence of choices with costs: this database and not that
one, this feature cut so the rest could ship. A project that records two or three of those choices,
with what each one cost, shows judgement, and judgement is what the hiring manager is trying to
hire.

**That you can explain.** Someone will ask *why did you do it that way*, and the answer is the
interview. A project you understand end to end, including the part you would do differently now,
is worth more than a larger one you assembled from pieces you cannot account for. This is the
technical interviewer's whole question.

Notice what is missing from the list. **Size is not on it**, and neither is novelty. A small service
that does one thing properly, with its decisions written down, proves all three. The course's example
is chosen to be exactly that small, so the three proofs are visible rather than buried.
