---
title: Why a museum works against you
version: 1
---

The instinct is to show everything. Every exercise, every tutorial, every weekend experiment, on the
reasoning that more is more. It is not, for a reason that has nothing to do with quality: **the
reader samples**. They open one or two things, and they judge you by what they opened.

If your profile has twenty repositories, the reader does not see twenty. They see whichever one
caught their eye: the one with the most recent commit, or the funniest name, or simply the first in
the list. If that happens to be a half-finished experiment from two years ago, that is your portfolio
for that reader, and the good project three places down might as well not exist.

So a museum is not neutral. **Every weak item is a chance to be judged by it.** The fix is not to
delete your history; it is to decide what gets shown first:

- **Pin one to three projects.** GitHub and GitLab both let you choose what appears at the top of
  your profile. Those are the only ones most readers will see.
- **Put one sentence on your profile** that names the project you want read first.
- **Leave the rest where it is.** Old exercises are not a liability once they are not the first
  thing anybody meets. An empty profile with one pinned project is stronger than a full one with
  none.

And **one finished project beats several that are nearly there**, which is lesson 2.
