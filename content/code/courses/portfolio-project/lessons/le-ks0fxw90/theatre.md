---
title: What is theatre for one person
version: 1
---

Most of what project tools offer exists to coordinate people. Done by one person, alone, each piece
answers a question nobody is asking. Dropping it gives that
time back to the project.

| practice | the question it answers in a team | alone |
|---|---|---|
| story points and velocity | how much can this team take on in a sprint? | you already know what you did last week |
| two-week sprints with planning and review | when do we all stop and agree on the next batch? | the milestones of lesson 7 already do this |
| burndown charts | will the team finish the sprint's work in time? | the board's *to do* column is the chart |
| a dozen labels and priorities | which of these many things matters most, and to whom? | you have one milestone and one card in doing |
| a daily stand-up note | what is everybody doing today? | nobody else is there |

The test for anything not in this lesson is the same: **does it change what you do next?** If filling
it in does not change the next task, it is a ritual, and a portfolio project has no time for rituals.

There is one exception worth making. If you want a job on a team that works in sprints, **knowing the
vocabulary is useful**. It belongs in the interview rather than on the board: *I tracked it with a
simple board and a work-in-progress limit of one, because sprint ceremonies need more than one person*
is itself a decision, with its reason.
