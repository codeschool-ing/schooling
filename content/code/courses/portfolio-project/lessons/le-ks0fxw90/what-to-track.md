---
title: What is worth tracking
version: 1
---

A board for one person has a single job: **to remember what you would otherwise forget**. That gives a
simple test for whether something goes on it.

**Track:**

- **The tasks of the current milestone**, from lesson 5's list. One card per task of a day or less.
- **What you notice while doing something else.** You are fixing the page and see that an unknown item
  number crashes the server. Do not fix it now and do not trust yourself to remember: one card, one
  line, back to what you were doing.
- **What you cut.** The *won't* and *could* buckets live here as cards, so that *not now* is written
  somewhere other than your memory.

**Do not track:**

- **The steps of the task you are doing right now.** They belong in your head or in the commit, not
  on a card you will move twenty minutes later.
- **Every idea.** An idea that does not belong to a milestone or to the cut list is a note, not a
  card. A board with sixty cards is a list nobody reads, including you.

On GitHub or GitLab, each card is naturally an **issue** and the board is the project view that
arranges issues in columns. A text file with three headings works just as well; what matters is
that the cards have numbers, for the last section of this lesson.
