---
title: Ninety seconds of video
version: 1
---

A video is optional for a web project with a live address and close to required for one without, and for
a mobile app. It is watched once, often without sound, so it has to work in about ninety seconds and make
sense muted. Plan it as a script before recording anything:

```localised
0:00  the page, seeded: "loanbook replaces the paper sheet on the IT room door"
0:10  lend Projector 1 to a teacher; the status line announces it
0:25  a second teacher tries the same projector: refused, and told who has it
0:40  Laptop 03 marked overdue; return it; it becomes available at once
0:55  the page at 320 pixels wide: the same list as cards
1:05  the terminal: seven tests passing, then the health check over HTTPS
1:20  one sentence on screen: what I would build next
```

Every beat shows **something happening**, not something existing: a loan, a refusal, a return. The
refusal gets the most time because it is the project's point, lesson 5. The terminal gets fifteen seconds,
enough to show evidence and not enough to lose the viewer.

Three practical rules. **Record with the seed data**, so the video is reproducible and shows nobody real.
**Add captions**, or at least on-screen text, since many viewers will not turn the sound on. And **no dead
air**: a five-second pause while a page loads is five seconds in which the viewer decides to stop. Cut it
in editing, or slow nothing down in the first place by recording on the lab rather than a free tier that
sleeps. Any screen recorder works; OBS Studio is a free, open-source one available on every desktop system.
