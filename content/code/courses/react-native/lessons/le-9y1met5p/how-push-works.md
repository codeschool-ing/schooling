title: Local notifications and push notifications
version: 1
---

**A notification reaches a phone by one of two roads, and they share nothing but the way they look.**

- **A local notification** is scheduled by the app, on the phone, for a time or an interval. The system
  keeps the schedule and shows it when the time comes, whether the app is open, in the background or
  closed. No server and no network are involved, and nothing outside the phone knows it exists.
- **A push notification** is sent by a server, at a moment the server chooses. It cannot go to the phone
  directly: on iOS it goes through Apple's **APNs**, on Android through Google's **FCM**, and those
  services hold the connection every phone keeps open to its maker.

For a push, the server needs an address for this app on this phone, a **token**. The app asks the system
for one and sends it to its server, which keeps it with the account. The token changes now and then, and
it stops working when the app is uninstalled; a server that keeps sending to a dead token is told so.

**The planner's reminders are local.** Every session has a date, the plan is already on the phone, and a
reminder on the evening of a session needs nothing a server knows. A push is right when the news starts
somewhere else: a friend joined your course, a teacher moved a session. The last two sections of this
lesson are about push, and why *this course could not deliver one*.

Both roads end in the same place in the app, and that is most of this lesson: asking permission, choosing
how a notification shows while the app is open, and what a tap does.
