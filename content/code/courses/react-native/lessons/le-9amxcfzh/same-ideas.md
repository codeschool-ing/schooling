**Most of what this course taught is not React Native's.** A list that makes only the rows near the
screen, state with one source of truth, a permission asked at the moment it is needed, a key that signs
every release: each platform has its own words for the same idea, and the catalogue has a course for each.
Here is where each idea lives in this course, in *Android apps* and in *iOS apps*, by lesson number:

| the idea | React Native | Android apps | iOS apps |
|---|---|---|---|
| components that describe a screen | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| layout | 5 | 6 | 4, 5 |
| state, and where it lives | 9 | 4, 10 | 6, 7 |
| what renders again, and why | 19 | 5 | 3 |
| lists that make only the rows on screen | 7 | 8 | 8 |
| navigation and deep links | 8 | 9 | 9 |
| the network, a cache, and offline | 10 | 13, 16 | 12, 14 |
| forms and the keyboard | 11 | — | 10 |
| animation | 12 | — | 11 |
| permissions, and the answer no | 13, 15 | 20 | 18 |
| images and their memory | 13, 19 | 17 | 15 |
| a database, and its migrations | 14 | 15 | 13 |
| work while the app is closed | 15 | 18 | 16 |
| notifications | 16 | 19 | 17 |
| tests | 3 onwards | 21 | 20 |
| signing and the release | 20 | 22 | 21, 22 |

A dash means that course has no lesson for the idea, not that the platform lacks it.

**Read the other two courses' version of an idea you found hard here**, and the idea gets easier: the same
problem described a second time, with different names, is usually the explanation that was missing.
Recomposition in Compose and a SwiftUI `body` that runs again are this course's lesson 19 in other words;
Room's migrations are lesson 14's `user_version`; WorkManager is lesson 15's task that survives the app
closing.
