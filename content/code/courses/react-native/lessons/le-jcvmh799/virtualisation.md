## The settings that size the window

| prop | default | what it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `initialNumToRender` | 10 | how many items exist on the first frame |
| `windowSize` | 21 | how much is kept around the screen, in screen heights: 10 above, the screen, 10 below |
| `maxToRenderPerBatch` | 10 | how many new items are rendered at a time while scrolling |
| `updateCellsBatchingPeriod` | 50 ms | how often a new batch may be rendered |

**The defaults are a balance**, and most lists never need them changed. A smaller `windowSize` saves
memory and risks blank space when the person flings the list faster than items can be rendered; a larger
one does the reverse. Change one when a measurement says to, which is lesson 19's subject.

## What it means for your rows

Because items are created and destroyed as they enter and leave the window, **a row's state does not
survive scrolling far away and back**. A row that was expanded, then scrolled a few screens off, comes
back closed, because it is a new component. State that must survive belongs above the list, in the data
or in the screen, keyed by the item's id; lesson 9 is about where state lives.

The same churn is why `renderItem` must be cheap. Every item entering the window runs it. A row that
computes something expensive, or filters the whole plan to count its sessions, does that work again for
every row, every time it is rendered. The planner's `CourseRow` counts sessions with `filter`, which is
fine for three courses and is the first thing lesson 19 measures on three hundred.
