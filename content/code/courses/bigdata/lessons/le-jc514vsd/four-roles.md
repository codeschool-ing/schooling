title: Four roles, and who does what
version: 1

**A Spark cluster has four roles, and two of them are easy to confuse.** The confusion is worth
clearing first, because every failure in this lesson is a failure of one role, and what survives it
depends on which.

- **The driver** is your program. `spark-submit generate.py` starts it, it builds the plan of the
  job, cuts it into tasks and decides where each task runs. It holds the only complete picture of
  the job, and at the end it holds the answer.
- **The cluster manager** owns the machines. In this lab it is Spark's own *standalone* master; on
  other clusters it is YARN (lesson 12) or Kubernetes (lesson 13). It knows which workers exist and
  how many cores and how much memory each has free, and it hands them out to applications. It
  knows nothing about your job.
- **A worker** is a machine's offer to the cluster: "I have one core and 1 GB." It does no work
  for your job itself. When the master assigns some of its resources to an application, the
  worker starts a process for it.
- **An executor** is that process. It belongs to one application, lives as long as the
  application does, and runs the application's tasks one per core, keeping data in its memory
  between them.

The two easily confused are **the master and the driver**. Both sound like the boss. The master is
the landlord: it rents out cores and does not care what you do with them. The driver is the
foreman: it decides what every task does and where, and talks directly to its executors. Once an
application has its executors, the master is out of the conversation.

**The words change between systems, and the roles do not.** YARN calls its master the
*ResourceManager* and its workers *NodeManagers*; Kubernetes has a control plane and nodes. The
driver and its executors are Spark's own and keep their names everywhere.
