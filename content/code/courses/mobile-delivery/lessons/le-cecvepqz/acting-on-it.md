title: Acting on retention without annoying people
version: 1
---

**The cohort table says when people leave; the work is to change what they meet at that moment.**
For the planner, the moment is the first week. Three kinds of change move week 1, and one kind
moves it the wrong way.

## Changes that help

- **Make the first session happen in the first visit.** Lesson 16's funnel lost the most people
  between adding a course and planning a session. A first screen that suggests a session for this
  evening, already filled in, turns a course into a plan in one tap.
- **A reminder the person asked for.** Notifications switched on at the moment somebody plans a
  session, lesson 5, remind them of something they chose. Compare retention with and without them
  carefully, because the cause runs both ways: keen people are the ones who switch them on.
- **Fewer reasons to delete it.** A small app is deleted last, lesson 18; an app that loses no data,
  `mobile-offline`'s subject, is trusted with more.

## The change that hurts

**Notifications nobody asked for.** "We miss you!" and "You haven't studied in a while" move DAU
for a day and teach people to switch notifications off, after which nothing reaches them. Both
platforms make that switch a few taps away, and it is rarely switched back on.

## Measuring a change

A change to onboarding affects **new** cohorts, so it is measured by comparing the cohorts after it
with the cohorts before it, at the same week: week-1 retention of the October cohorts against the
September ones. Releasing it to half of the new installs, with lesson 19's feature flag, makes the
comparison fair: same weeks, same season, the only difference being the change.
