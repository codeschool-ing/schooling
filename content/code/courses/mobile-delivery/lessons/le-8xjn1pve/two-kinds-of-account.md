**Both stores ask the same first question, and the answer is hard to change later: is the
account a person's or an organisation's?** It decides whose name the store shows as the seller,
what the store needs to verify, and what happens when the person who opened it leaves. *Neither
console was opened for this course. The Apple half is read from Apple's enrolment pages in October
2026; Google's Play Console Help could not be reached from the recording machine, so the Google
half follows that help as Google published it in 2025, and the console is where to check it.*

| | a person | an organisation |
|---|---|---|
| the seller name the store shows | the person's legal name | the company's legal name |
| what is verified | the person's identity | the company's identity, legal status and address |
| what it needs | a government ID | a **D-U-N-S number**, and somebody with authority to sign for the company |
| when somebody leaves | the account leaves with them | the account stays; its users change |

**The seller name is public.** A personal account publishes your full legal name on every listing,
and in the European Union, whose rules for traders apply to both stores, an address and a phone
number as well. People
who build a side project under their own name find that out on the first day it goes live.

## The D-U-N-S number

Apple and Google both identify an organisation by its **D-U-N-S number**, a nine-digit
identifier that Dun & Bradstreet assigns to businesses. A company that does not have one requests
it, free, and Apple's page warns that the request can take days. **Start it before you need it**:
it is the step of enrolment that nobody can hurry, and a launch date set before it arrives is a
launch date that moves. Government entities are the one exception Apple names.

## Which one to open

An app that will ever carry a company's name, take payment for a company, or outlive one
developer's interest belongs in an organisation account from the first day. **Moving an app from
one account to another is possible in both stores, and slow in both**: it is a transfer that the
receiving account must accept, with agreements and tax details already in place on both sides.
A personal account is right for a personal project, and for practising the steps of this course
on a live store once you decide to pay for one.

## The same choice on the other two

There is no third store for React Native: its Android build goes to Google Play and its iOS build
to the App Store, through the same two accounts. Expo's services need an Expo account as well,
which is free to open and is not a store.
