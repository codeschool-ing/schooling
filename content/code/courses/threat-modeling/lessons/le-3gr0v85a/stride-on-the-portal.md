### What the list shows

**Every fact from lesson 1 became a threat.** The webhook is T01, the owner's password is T13, the
console on the internet is T12. That is the drawing working: the facts were known, and the
structure put each one in front of a question.

**Most threats sit on flows that cross a boundary.** Ten of the fourteen are on a flow, and every
one of those flows crosses a line drawn in lesson 2. The two processes with threats of their own,
the console and the worker, are there because of a privilege they should not have.

**Some letters found more than others, and that is information.** Only one tampering threat
survived the afternoon. Either Vereda's integrity is in good shape, or tampering is the letter
the team understands least. carla's note on the list says the second, and lesson 5's attack trees
come back to it.

**Two threats are about privacy rather than security.** T08 and T09 harm patients without
anybody breaking in. STRIDE found them by asking about disclosure; lesson 5's LINDDUN is built for
exactly that kind and finds more.

### Kept as a file

The list lives in the model's repository as `threats.csv`, one line per threat, with the same
columns as the table. Later lessons add to it: lesson 8 gives each threat a requirement, lessons 9
to 11 a risk, and lesson 12 a decision. The whole file, and then a count by letter, a one-line
check that the afternoon did not skip a category:
