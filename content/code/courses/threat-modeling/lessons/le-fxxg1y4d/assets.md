An asset is **anything the organisation would be worse off for losing, having changed, or having
seen by the wrong person.** The usual first list is data, and data is most of it. It is not all of
it: the portal being up is an asset, because a clinic that cannot see its agenda cannot work; and a
staff password is an asset, because it is the key to the other assets.

A good asset list does two things a list of tables does not. It **names each asset at the level a
person cares about**, "clinical notes" rather than "the `notes` table", and it **says which of the
three properties matters for each**, because that decides which threats are serious.

Three things the matrix says that a list would not:

- **Clinical notes and exam PDFs are high on both confidentiality and integrity.** Read by the
  wrong person, they expose somebody's health; changed, they lead a physiotherapist to treat the
  wrong condition. That second half is the objective PASTA's stage 1 found missing in lesson 4.
- **The agenda matters most for availability.** Few people want to read it, and a wrong time is an
  annoyance, but a morning without it is four clinics turning patients away.
- **Payment status is about integrity alone.** Nobody steals it; somebody changes it. That is
  exactly T01, and it is why T01 never looked like a confidentiality problem.

### Where the assets live

Every asset in the matrix sits somewhere in the DFD of lesson 2: in a data store, travelling on a
flow, or held by a process. Writing that down joins the asset list to the threat list:

| asset | stored in | travels on | threatened by |
|---|---|---|---|
| clinical notes | records database | flow 9 | T05, T09, T12, T13 |
| exam PDFs | exam files | flows 3, 4, 10, 2 | T04, T07, T10, T14 |
| payment status | records database | flows 5, 7 | T01 |
| patient contact data | records database | flows 11, 12, 6 | T02, T08, T11 |
| staff credentials | the console's account table | flow 8 | T03, T12 |

An asset with no threat beside it is a question rather than an answer: either it is well
protected, or nobody has looked. Lesson 6's spreadsheet of clinics was an asset nobody had listed,
which is why the input it created had not been noticed either.

### Assets that are not data

**Reputation** belongs on the list for a clinic, and it is the hardest to value. A leak of health
data is news; the same leak at a shoe shop is not. Lessons 9 and 10 put numbers on losses, and the
reputational part is where the estimates are widest.
