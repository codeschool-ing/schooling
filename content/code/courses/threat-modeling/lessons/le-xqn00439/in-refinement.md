The cheapest moment to model a change is before it is built, while it is still a story on a
board. The team is already in a room discussing what the story means and how big it is. Four
questions added to that conversation cost a few minutes, and they are where most new threats
first become visible.

### What should reopen the model

Not every change touches the model. A new colour on a button does not; a new page that takes
input does. The changes that matter are the ones that **change what the model draws**, and they
fall into a short list:

The top row can be seen in a story before anybody writes code. The bottom row arrives from outside,
on its own schedule: an incident, an audit finding, a vendor's report like the one that produced
T18 and T19, a review date coming due, a flaw published in a library the portal uses. Those need an
owner whose job is to notice them, and at Vereda that is ana, with the weekly check of the next
sections doing the remembering.

### Four questions for a story

For the top row, the questions go into refinement itself. They are the DFD's questions in a form
anybody in the room can answer:

1. **Does it add a way in?** A page, an API, an upload, a webhook.
2. **Does it handle a new kind of data?** Above all personal data, and in a clinic, health data.
3. **Does it depend on something new?** A vendor, a service, a library.
4. **Does it move a trust boundary?** Something that was internal becomes reachable, or the
   reverse.

Four noes and the model does not change; that is the common answer and it takes thirty seconds.
Any yes means the story touches the model, and the yes says where.

Three yeses. **A way in**: a download, which is the shape of T07, an exam downloaded by somebody
who is not the patient. That threat has a requirement, R10, and the story inherits it as an
acceptance criterion: only the patient who paid gets the invoice. **New data**: the patient's tax
number, the CPF, which is personal data under the LGPD and needs the same care as the rest.
**A new dependency**: the city's electronic invoice service, a new external entity with a flow
across the vendors' boundary.

The first yes was settled in the room, by reusing a requirement. The other two need more than a
minute, so the story gets a short design review before it starts. That is the next section.

### What refinement must not become

The questions are for noticing, not for modelling. A refinement session that turns into an hour of
STRIDE on one story will be skipped the next time. **The rule is that refinement finds the yes and
the design review deals with it**, so the four questions stay short enough to be asked every time.
