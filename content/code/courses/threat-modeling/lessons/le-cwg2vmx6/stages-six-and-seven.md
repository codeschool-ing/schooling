### Stage 6: attack modelling

Stage 6 puts the threats and weaknesses together into **paths**: sequences of steps from a
starting position to a goal that matters to the business. The goal comes from stage 1. The steps
come from stages 4 and 5. The usual notation is the attack tree, which lesson 5 teaches properly;
here a simplified drawing is enough.

The goal is the one stage 1 said Vereda could least afford: every patient's clinical record. Three
paths reach it, and each step is a threat already on the list. Under each step is the control that
would break it. Two things are worth noticing:

- **A path is broken by breaking any one step.** The phishing path dies if staff have a second
  factor, or if the console only answers the clinic network, or if receptionists cannot see notes.
  Stage 7 can choose the cheapest of the three, and it does not need all three to break the path.
- **Some paths reach less than the goal.** The patient-password path reaches exams through T07 but
  not clinical notes, which only the console shows. Drawing it shows that its impact is smaller
  than it first looks.

Stage 6 is where PASTA is most often done badly, in two opposite ways: as a pentest by another
name, where somebody tries the paths on the live system, or as a page of generic attack patterns
copied from a catalogue. Both miss its purpose, which is **a map of where defences would cut**,
drawn from facts about this system.

### Stage 7: risk and impact analysis

The last stage returns to the business. For each path, and each threat on it, it asks **how likely
it is given stage 4, what it would cost given stage 1, and what risk remains after the controls
proposed in stage 6**. The output is a ranked list of risks, the countermeasures recommended for
each, and the **residual risk**: what is left after they are in place.

At Vereda, stage 7's ranking put the phishing path first: carla's reports said it is being tried,
stage 1 said clinical data is the objective the clinics can least afford to fail, and a second
factor for staff is cheap. The forged webhook came second, because 60% of bookings depend on the
payment state. The SMS text (T08) came near the bottom: real, but low in cost and easy to fix
whenever the template is next edited.

How stage 7 puts numbers on likelihood and impact is the subject of lessons 9 to 11, and the
ranking above was done qualitatively. What PASTA contributes is the **reasoning that connects
each number to a business objective**, so the ranking can be explained to daniel in his own terms.
