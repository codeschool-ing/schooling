Most organisations do not simulate anything. They use a **risk matrix**: likelihood in five bands
across, impact in five bands up, each cell a colour, each risk placed in a cell and given a score,
usually likelihood times impact. It fits on a slide, needs no software, and is required by many
frameworks in some form. `security-fundamentals` (lesson 3) introduced it, and it is worth seeing
exactly what it does to the same estimates.

`matrix.py` puts lesson 9's best guesses through bands. The bands are typical of a small company's
matrix: likelihood from *rare*, under once in ten years, to *almost certain*, more than three times
a year; impact from under R$ 2,000 to over R$ 200,000:

A value equal to an edge falls in the lower band, which is what bisect_left gives."}, {"code": "rows = []\nfor r in csv.DictReader(open(\"risks.csv\")):\n    per_year, loss = float(r[\"per_year\"]), float(r[\"loss\"])\n    l = bisect.bisect_left(LIKELIHOOD, per_year) + 1\n    i = bisect.bisect_left(IMPACT, loss) + 1\n    rows.append((l * i, per_year * loss, r[\"id\"], l, i))", "note": "For each risk, its likelihood band and impact band, from 1 to 5, and the usual score: one times the other. The expected loss is kept beside it for comparison."}, {"code": "\nprint(f\"{'':4} {'L':>2} {'I':>2} {'score':>6} {'R$ per year':>12}\")\nfor score, expected, rid, l, i in sorted(rows, reverse=True):\n    print(f\"{rid:4} {l:2} {i:2} {score:6} {expected:12,.0f}\")", "note": "Sorted by score, as a matrix would rank them, with the expected loss in the last column."}]}

### What the matrix got right

**T03 is still first.** Its score of 10 is the highest, and lesson 9's table put it first too. At
the top, for the risk that dominates everything else, the two methods agree, and that is common:
a matrix tends to get the most extreme risk right.

### And what it did with the rest

Read the score column against the expected loss beside it:

- **T01, the forged webhook, is now second**, with a score of 9, ahead of T07 and T13, which each
  cost more than twice as much per year. It landed in the middle of both scales, and 3 × 3 beats
  2 × 4 and 1 × 5.
- **T10, uploads filling the storage, at 6, is above T13 at 5.** R$ 3,000 a year above R$ 15,000.
- **T13 and T02 tie at 5**, and so do T14, T08 and T11 at 4, though the expected losses inside
  each group differ by up to a factor of nearly four.

None of this is a fault in the estimates. The same numbers went in. The matrix lost the information
when it put each number into a band, and lost more when it multiplied two band positions as if
they were quantities. The next section names each loss.
