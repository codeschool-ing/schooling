**Kanban** does without the sprint. There is no fixed period and no planning of a batch: the board is the
whole method, and work flows across it one card at a time. When somebody finishes something, they **pull**
the next card from the top of *to do*. Nobody pushes work at them.

What stops that from becoming chaos is the WIP limit from the first section. It is not optional in kanban; it
is the mechanism. Without a limit, a board with no sprint is just a list that grows.

## What it measures instead

Scrum asks *how much did we finish this sprint?*. Kanban asks **how long does one card take?**

- **Lead time**: from the moment a ticket is written to the moment it is done. What the person who asked
  experiences.
- **Cycle time**: from the moment somebody starts it to the moment it is done. What the team controls.

If cycle time is short and lead time is long, work is waiting in *to do*, and the question is priorities or
people. If cycle time itself is long, work is waiting inside the board, usually in review, and the answer is
often to lower a WIP limit.

## Which, when

- **Scrum** fits work that can be planned two weeks ahead and benefits from a rhythm: building a product in
  steps, with people outside the team who want to see progress at regular intervals.
- **Kanban** fits work that arrives unpredictably and must be handled as it comes: support, operations, a
  team whose week is decided by what breaks.

## The mixture

Most teams you will join use neither by the book. A common shape keeps Scrum's two-week rhythm, planning and
retro, and adds kanban's WIP limits to the board. Some call that *scrumban*; most just call it "how we work".
**Your first week's job is to find out which parts they kept.** Ask:

1. How long is a sprint, if there is one? What happens at its start and end?
2. What do the columns mean, and is there a limit on any of them?
3. What does *done* mean here?
4. Who decides what is next when I finish something?

In Brazil, as in most places, teams use the English words: *sprint*, *backlog*, *daily*, *card*, *board*,
*WIP*. You will hear *quadro* and *pronto* as often as *board* and *done*, sometimes in the same sentence.
