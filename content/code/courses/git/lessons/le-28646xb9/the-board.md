Lesson 12 moved ticket #23 to *in progress* and later to *done*. The place where it moved is the **board**:
a set of columns, one per state, with a card for every ticket. Jira, GitHub Projects, GitLab's boards and
Trello all draw the same thing, and plenty of teams still use a wall with sticky notes.

## A column is a state with a rule

The columns look like labels, but each one is an agreement about **what must be true for a card to be
there**:

- **To do**: the ticket is written and ready to start. Lesson 16 is about the tickets that are not.
- **In progress**: somebody has taken it and is working on it now. Their initials are on the card.
- **In review**: a pull request is open and waiting for a reviewer. On most tools this column moves by itself
  when the pull request is opened, because it is linked by the ticket's number.
- **Done**: what "done" means is the team's **definition of done**, lesson 17. On this board it means
  released.

Teams add columns (*ready for QA*, *blocked*, *deployed*) and the names vary, but the idea does not: a card's
column should say something true, and anybody should be able to look at the board and know what is
happening without asking.

## Who moves a card

**The person doing the work moves their own card**, at the moment it changes state. A board updated once a
week in a meeting is a picture of last week, and people stop looking at it. The card on #36 says *blocked*:
Bruno cannot continue until the hosting company answers. Saying so on the board is not a confession. It
tells the team this card is stuck and somebody may be able to help.

## Why there is a limit

*In progress* and *in review* say **limit 2**. That is a **WIP limit**, for *work in progress*. When a column is
full, nobody starts anything new in it; instead, they help finish what is already there. Right now both are
full, so the useful thing for Ana to do after #34 is not to start #35. It is to review #30 or #32, which moves
cards to the right and frees a slot.

The limit feels backwards at first: surely starting more work gets more done? It does not. Five things half
done are worth nothing to a customer, and each one switched between costs time. A team that finishes one
thing before starting the next delivers **sooner**, even though each person is busy for less of the day.
