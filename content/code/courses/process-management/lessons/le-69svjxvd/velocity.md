**Velocity** is the number of story points a team finishes in a Sprint — finished meaning that the items met the Definition of Done of lesson 2. Partly done items count for nothing, which is the same rule the burndown followed.

## The Agenda team's history

Over its last eight Sprints the Agenda team finished **21, 28, 24, 31, 19, 26, 27 and 24** points.

The mean is **25**, and so is the median. Over the last six Sprints the slowest was **19** and the fastest **31**. That spread is not a sign of anything wrong. Holidays, an illness, a story that turned out bigger than its points, an incident that took two days: an ordinary team's velocity moves by this much from Sprint to Sprint.

## A forecast is a range

The backlog for the clinic network's next release adds up to **150 points**. How many Sprints will it take?

Dividing by the mean gives **6 Sprints**. Dividing by the fastest of the last six gives 4.8, so **5 Sprints**; dividing by the slowest gives 7.9, so **8 Sprints**. The honest forecast is the range: **between 5 and 8 Sprints, most likely 6**.

The range is wide, and that is the information. A team that told the director "6 Sprints" would have hidden that 7 or 8 is entirely ordinary for this team. The workbook from lesson 3 computes it in five cells, and the values below are what LibreOffice returned with the eight Sprints in B2 to B9:

```localised
=AVERAGE(B2:B9)                 25
=MIN(B4:B9)                     19
=MAX(B4:B9)                     31
=ROUNDUP(150/MAX(B4:B9),0)      5
=ROUNDUP(150/MIN(B4:B9),0)      8
```

In Portuguese, `AVERAGE`, `MIN`, `MAX` and `ROUNDUP` are `MÉDIA`, `MÍNIMO`, `MÁXIMO` and `ARREDONDAR.PARA.CIMA`, with a semicolon between arguments.

## What the forecast assumes

The forecast assumes the backlog is 150 points and stays 150. Backlogs grow: new stories arrive, and existing ones split into more than their original points. A forecast that ignores growth is optimistic in the same way as the sum of most likely values in lesson 9. The `delivery-metrics` course takes this further in its lesson 10, with a simulation that draws from the team's history instead of three fixed rates.
