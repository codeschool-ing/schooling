## PMP

The **Project Management Professional** certification from PMI, the body behind the PMBOK Guide of lesson 6, is the most widely recognised project-management credential in the world, and the one recruiters in Brazil and the United States most often name.

It is an **experience** certificate. To apply you need 35 hours of project-management education and documented experience leading projects: **36 months** with a four-year degree, or **60 months** without one, within the last ten years. PMI can audit the application and ask your managers to confirm it.

The exam was updated on **9 July 2026** to a new content outline. It still covers three domains — people, process and business environment — and gives business environment a far larger share than the version before it. Predictive, agile and hybrid approaches appear throughout, so lessons 1 to 6 of this course are all relevant to it. The certificate is kept by earning **60 professional development units every three years**.

## CAPM

The **Certified Associate in Project Management**, also from PMI, is the entry-level version. It requires **23 hours of project-management education** and no experience, which makes it the PMI certificate somebody early in their career can take. It proves vocabulary, not experience, and is valued accordingly.

## PRINCE2

The **PRINCE2** certificates, owned by PeopleCert since it acquired the method's previous owner, follow the method of lesson 7. The **Foundation** exam tests knowledge of the principles, practices and processes and has no prerequisite. The **Practitioner** exam tests applying them to a scenario and requires Foundation or one of a short list of other project-management certificates. The current edition is **PRINCE2 7**, from 2023.

PeopleCert certificates must be **renewed within three years**, either by taking another exam from the same family or by retaking the same one.

## Which one

For a career in Brazil or in the Americas, the PMP carries the most weight, and the CAPM is its stepping stone. PRINCE2 is the stronger choice for work in the United Kingdom, much of Europe, Australia and organisations funded by international bodies — the places lesson 7 named. Neither says anything about whether you can lead the project in front of you; both open the door to being asked.
