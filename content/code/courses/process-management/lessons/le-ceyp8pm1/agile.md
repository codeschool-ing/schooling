Agile certification is a crowded market, and the certificates differ mainly in who issues them and what you have to do to get one.

## Scrum

Two organisations dominate. **Scrum Alliance** was founded in the early 2000s, with Ken Schwaber among its founders; he left it and founded **Scrum.org** in 2009.

- **Scrum.org** issues the **Professional Scrum Master** (PSM) certificates. PSM I is an online assessment that can be taken **without attending a course**, and the certificate **does not expire**. Higher levels, PSM II and PSM III, test applying Scrum to harder situations. There are parallel tracks for Product Owners (PSPO) and Developers.
- **Scrum Alliance** issues the **Certified ScrumMaster** (CSM). It **requires a course** with a certified trainer, followed by an online test, and it must be **renewed every two years** by collecting 20 Scrum Education Units and paying a fee. A Product Owner version, the CSPO, also exists.

The practical difference is that the PSM shows you passed a demanding exam and the CSM shows you sat through a course with an exam at the end. Employers in Brazil tend to recognise both; the knowledge tested is lesson 2's.

## PMI-ACP

PMI's **Agile Certified Practitioner** covers agile approaches in general — Scrum, Kanban, XP, lean — rather than one framework, and, like the PMP, asks for training and experience before the exam. It suits people whose employer already values PMI credentials and who want an agile one from the same body.

## SAFe

Scaled Agile, the company behind the framework of lesson 5, issues a family of certificates tied to roles: **SAFe Agilist** for leaders, from the *Leading SAFe* course; **SAFe Scrum Master**; **SAFe Release Train Engineer**; **SAFe Architect** for architects. Each requires the corresponding course and is renewed **annually**, with a fee. They are worth having in an organisation that runs SAFe, and of little use elsewhere, because they certify a specific framework.

## A caution

Lesson 2 noted that Scrum's rules fit in thirteen pages. A two-day course and an online test can teach them; they cannot teach how to rescue a Sprint whose goal has become obsolete. Agile certificates are better read as **vocabulary certificates** than as proof of practice, which is fine as long as nobody, including the holder, reads them as more.
