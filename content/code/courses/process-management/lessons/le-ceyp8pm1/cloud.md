For an architect, cloud certificates are often worth more in the job market than any certificate in the rest of this lesson, because they are specific, the exams are hard to pass without hands-on experience, and employers can map them to the platform they use. Each of the three large providers has an architecture path.

| provider | the architecture certificate | the one before it | how long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon Web Services | Solutions Architect – Professional | Solutions Architect – Associate | three years, renewed by passing the exam again |
| Microsoft Azure | Azure Solutions Architect Expert | Azure Administrator Associate | one year, renewed with a free online assessment |
| Google Cloud | Professional Cloud Architect | — | two years, renewed by passing the exam again |

## What they test

Unlike the frameworks in the rest of this lesson, the cloud exams test **design decisions on a specific platform**: which storage service fits a workload, how to make a system survive the loss of a zone, how to control cost, how to secure access. They are scenario questions, and they are hard to pass from reading alone. That is what gives them their value, and it also means they age quickly: each provider updates its exams as its services change, and the renewal cycles in the table exist for that reason.

## How they relate to this course

None of the cloud exams is about process. Their link to this course is through the decisions it described: the irreversible ones of lesson 2, the technical risks of lesson 11, the availability targets of lesson 8. A cloud architecture certificate shows that somebody knows the building blocks; this course is about how the decisions to use them get made, estimated and argued for. The `cloud` course and the provider-specific courses of the catalogue prepare for the exams themselves.

## Choosing one

Choose the provider your employer, or the employer you want, actually uses. A certificate for a platform nobody around you runs is a signal of effort and little else. If you have no preference, the market share of the provider in the region where you want to work is a reasonable tiebreaker, and the associate-level exam of that provider is the usual first step.
