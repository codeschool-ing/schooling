The certificates in this lesson cost money, weeks of study and, for most of them, a renewal every one to three years. Choosing among them is itself a prioritisation problem of the kind lesson 12 described: value, cost and timing.

## Start from the door, not the content

Every certificate's knowledge is available without it: the Scrum Guide is free, the PMBOK Guide and ITIL books can be read, the cloud providers publish their documentation. What a certificate adds is a **signal to somebody who does not know you**. So the first question is not *what do I want to learn?* but **who needs to see this, and will they?**

- A recruiter filtering applications for a project-manager role in a bank: PMP, or CAPM early in a career.
- A public tender that scores the qualifications of the proposed team: whatever the tender lists, often PMP, ITIL or PRINCE2.
- A company that runs SAFe: the SAFe certificate for your role.
- An architecture role on a cloud platform: that provider's architect certificate.
- Your current team, who already know your work: probably none.

## A sensible order for this course's audience

For somebody on the Tech Lead or Software Architecture track, a path that matches the doors most likely to matter:

1. **A cloud certificate** for the platform you work on, because it is the most specific signal for an architect.
2. **A Scrum certificate**, PSM I being the one with no renewal, if your organisation or the ones you apply to run Scrum.
3. **ITIL 4 Foundation**, if you work with an operations organisation that uses ITIL's vocabulary.
4. **PMP**, once you have the experience it requires, if your career is moving towards leading programmes, or if the market you work in asks for it.

## When not to bother

A certificate is a poor investment when nobody you want to impress asks for it, when its renewal costs more each year than the doors it opens, or when it is being used as a substitute for experience you could get instead. Two years leading the delivery of a real system, with its estimates, its risks and its debt argued in the terms of lessons 9 to 14, is worth more than any certificate in this lesson to the people who will hire you to do it again.

## Keeping it current

Write down, beside each certificate you hold, its renewal date and what renewing requires. A lapsed certificate on a CV is a small problem; discovering in an interview that it lapsed a year ago is a larger one. And re-read the certifying body's requirements before each renewal, because, as this lesson's first section said, they change.
