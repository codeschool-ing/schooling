**PRINCE2** — *PRojects IN Controlled Environments* — is the project method of the British government, and it travelled with British public administration and the organisations that work with it. Its predecessor, PRINCE, was published by a UK government agency in 1989; PRINCE2 followed in 1996 and has been revised several times since, most recently in 2023. The method is now owned by PeopleCert, which also runs its certifications.

Where the PMBOK Guide of lesson 6 describes good practice in general, **PRINCE2 is a method**: it says who does what, in which process, producing which document. Its structure is three sets of seven.

## Seven principles

The principles are what make a project a PRINCE2 project; the guide says a project that does not follow all seven is not using PRINCE2, whatever its documents are called.

- **Continued business justification** — there is a documented reason to run the project, and it is checked at every stage. A project whose business case stops making sense is stopped.
- **Learn from experience** — lessons from earlier projects are sought at the start and recorded throughout.
- **Defined roles and responsibilities** — everybody knows who decides, who manages and who delivers.
- **Manage by stages** — the project is planned, monitored and controlled stage by stage, and the next stage is authorised only at the end of the current one.
- **Manage by exception** — each level of management works within limits set by the level above, and escalates only when those limits are forecast to be broken. The next section is about this one.
- **Focus on products** — the plan is built around what will be produced, each described by a product description with quality criteria.
- **Tailor to suit the project** — the method is adapted to the project's size, risk and environment.

## Seven themes and seven processes

The **themes** are the aspects of the project that must be managed continuously, much like PMBOK's knowledge areas: the business case, organisation, quality, plans, risk, change and progress. The 2023 edition calls them *practices* and adjusts some names, and the idea is unchanged.

The **processes** are the sequence of activities from start to finish: starting up a project, directing a project, initiating a project, controlling a stage, managing product delivery, managing a stage boundary and closing a project. Each process names its outputs, which is why PRINCE2 projects are recognisable from their documents: a **business case**, a **project initiation documentation**, an **end stage report** at each boundary.

## The Project Board

PRINCE2's most distinctive structure is the **Project Board**, the group that directs the project on behalf of the organisation. It has three roles. The **Executive** owns the business case and has the final say. The **Senior User** represents the people who will use what the project produces. The **Senior Supplier** represents those who will build it, which for an architect's project often means the head of the development or infrastructure teams. The **Project Manager** runs the project day to day and reports to the board, but is not a member of it.
