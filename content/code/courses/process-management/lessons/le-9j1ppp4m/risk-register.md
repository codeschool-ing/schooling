Risks that are identified and not written down are forgotten by the next meeting. The **risk register** is the list where they are kept, and its value depends almost entirely on whether anybody looks at it again.

## What each entry holds

| field | example for the Agenda team |
|---|---|
| identifier | R3 |
| statement, as cause, event, effect | because clinic records were kept by hand for years, the data may need cleaning before it can be migrated, which would add about six days |
| category | data |
| probability | 50% |
| impact | 6 working days |
| expected value | 3 days |
| owner | Davi, who owns the migration |
| response | mitigate: sample and clean the data of two clinics in the first Sprint |
| trigger | more than 5% of a sample fails validation |
| status | open, reviewed on 2 March |

Three fields deserve particular attention.

**The owner** is a person, not a team or a role. A risk owned by "the team" is owned by nobody, and nobody watches it.

**The trigger** is the observable sign that the risk is starting to happen, so that the response is started on time rather than after the impact has arrived. A trigger turns a risk from a worry into something that can be checked.

**The status** includes when the risk was last reviewed. A register reviewed at the start of the project and never again is a record of what people feared once, not a management tool.

## Keeping it alive

The register works when reviewing it is part of a regular event the team already holds — Sprint planning, a stage boundary in PRINCE2's terms, a PI planning in SAFe's — and when each review asks three questions: which risks have become more or less likely, which have happened and become issues, and what has appeared that is not on the list. Risks that can no longer happen are **closed**, with a note; a register that only grows becomes too long to read.

## Keep it small

A register with two hundred entries is no better than one with none, because nobody can hold it in mind. Most projects are threatened by a handful of risks, and the register should make those few prominent. The next section shows how to decide which few.
