title: Three artefacts, three commitments
version: 1
---

Each of Scrum's three artefacts is paired with a **commitment**, an addition of the 2020 guide that says what the artefact is for. The pairing is the part most teams have never heard of, and it is what keeps each artefact from turning into a list.

| artefact | its commitment | what the commitment does |
|---|---|---|
| Product Backlog | Product Goal | says what the product is trying to become, so the backlog has a direction |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal | says why this Sprint matters, so the team can trade items and still succeed |
| Increment | Definition of Done | says when work is finished, so "done" means the same to everybody |

## The Product Backlog and the Product Goal

The Product Backlog is **an emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product**, and the single source of work for the Scrum Team. It is ordered, not merely prioritised: there is a first item, a second and a third, and two items cannot share a place. Items near the top are small and clear enough to be done in one Sprint; items further down are larger and vaguer, because detail far ahead is wasted.

The **Product Goal** describes a future state of the product. The team works towards one Product Goal at a time, and the backlog is everything that might get it there.

## The Sprint Backlog and the Sprint Goal

The Sprint Backlog is the **Sprint Goal, the items selected for the Sprint and the plan for delivering them**. It belongs to the Developers, who update it through the Sprint as they learn.

The Sprint Goal is **the single objective for the Sprint**. It is what makes the selected items a coherent piece of work rather than a heap, and it gives the team room to move: if an item turns out bigger than expected, the Developers and the Product Owner can renegotiate the scope while keeping the goal. A Sprint Backlog with no goal is a to-do list, and a team with only a to-do list can only succeed by finishing every item.

## The Increment and the Definition of Done

An Increment is **a concrete step towards the Product Goal**, added to every previous one and verified so that they work together. It must be usable. Several Increments can be produced in one Sprint, and the guide is explicit that releasing one does not have to wait for the review.

Work becomes part of an Increment only when it meets the **Definition of Done**, which the next section takes on its own because it is where quality is decided.
