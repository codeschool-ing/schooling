## The Sprint

**The Sprint is a fixed period of one month or less** in which the team turns backlog items into a usable Increment. A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one ends. Two weeks is the most common choice in practice, and the guide does not recommend any length; it only caps it, because a longer Sprint lets the goal go stale and increases the risk.

During a Sprint, no change is made that would endanger the Sprint Goal, quality does not decrease, and the Product Backlog is refined as needed. **Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint**, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

## Sprint Planning — at most eight hours

Planning answers three questions. *Why* is this Sprint valuable? The answer is the Sprint Goal. *What* can be done this Sprint? The Developers select it from the Product Backlog. *How* will the chosen work get done? The Developers plan that, often by breaking items into tasks of a day or less.

## Daily Scrum — fifteen minutes

The Daily Scrum is **for the Developers**, every working day, at the same time and place. Its purpose is to inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan for the next day. Earlier editions of the guide suggested three questions (what did I do yesterday, what will I do today, what is in my way); the 2020 edition dropped them and lets the team choose any structure that serves the goal.

## Sprint Review — at most four hours

The team shows what it accomplished to the stakeholders, and together they discuss what to do next; the Product Backlog may be adjusted as a result. **It is a working session, not a presentation**, and it is not a gate: an Increment may be released to users before the review, as many times as the team likes.

## Sprint Retrospective — at most three hours

The team inspects how the Sprint went — people, interactions, processes, tools, its Definition of Done — and picks the most helpful changes. The guide allows those improvements to be added to the next Sprint Backlog, which is how a retrospective stops being a conversation and becomes work.

## And refinement, which is not an event

**Product Backlog refinement** is the ongoing activity of breaking items down, adding detail, estimates and order. It has no time box and no box in the figure. Teams commonly spend a regular slot on it each week, but the guide treats it as an activity because it happens whenever it is needed.
