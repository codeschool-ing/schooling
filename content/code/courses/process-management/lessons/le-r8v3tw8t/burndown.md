A burndown is not one of Scrum's artefacts. The 2020 guide mentions burn-downs only as one of several practices for forecasting progress, beside burn-ups and cumulative flow. It is still the chart most Scrum teams keep, and it is worth reading properly because it is often read wrong.

## What it plots

The horizontal axis is the days of the Sprint. The vertical axis is **the work still open**, usually in story points (lesson 10 explains them) or in hours of remaining task estimates. A straight **ideal line** runs from the total committed on day 0 to zero on the last day. The actual line is redrawn each day.

The Agenda team committed **34 points** for a ten-day Sprint. The ideal line therefore drops 3.4 points a day.

## What this one says

Three things are visible that no status report would have said.

**The line is flat on days 1, 3 and 6.** On those days the team was working, and nothing reached Done. A burndown counts finished items, so a team with five items each 90% complete shows no progress at all — which is the correct reading, because 90% complete is not usable.

**Most of the drop happens late.** From day 6 to day 9 the line falls 15 points, from 23 to 8. Items that finish together at the end are a sign of work started too early and in parallel; lesson 3 has a name for the cure, which is limiting work in progress.

**It ends at 5, not at 0.** Five points of the commitment did not meet the Definition of Done. Those items go back to the Product Backlog, and the Product Owner decides whether they come into the next Sprint. They are not "carried over" automatically, and they are certainly not counted as partly done.

## What it cannot say

A burndown shows the quantity of work left; it says nothing about whether the right work is being done. A team can burn its line to zero by finishing every item and still miss the Sprint Goal, if the items it finished were the easy ones. And when scope is added in the middle of a Sprint, a burndown hides it: the line simply falls more slowly. A **burn-up**, which plots work completed against a separate line for the total scope, shows the same Sprint with the scope change visible.
