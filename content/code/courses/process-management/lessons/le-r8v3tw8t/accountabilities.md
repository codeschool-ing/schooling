Scrum is the most used of the agile frameworks, and the most misdescribed. Its rules fit in a document of thirteen pages, the **Scrum Guide**, kept by its two authors, Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland. They first presented Scrum at a conference in 1995 and published the first guide in 2010; the current edition is from **November 2020**. The name comes from rugby, by way of a 1986 *Harvard Business Review* article by Hirotaka Takeuchi and Ikujiro Nonaka about product teams that moved "as a unit" instead of passing work along a relay.

The guide is short on purpose. It defines a small set of rules and leaves the practices to the team: it says there is a Sprint Backlog, not which tool holds it, and it says the Developers forecast the work, not how they estimate it.

## The Scrum Team

A Scrum Team is **one Product Owner, one Scrum Master and the Developers**, typically ten people or fewer. There are no sub-teams and no hierarchy inside it. The 2020 guide calls the three parts *accountabilities* rather than roles, which is a deliberate word: a role is a hat somebody wears, an accountability is something somebody answers for.

**The Product Owner is accountable for maximising the value of the product.** In practice that means ordering the Product Backlog, writing or approving its items and deciding what the product is for. It is one person, not a committee. Other people can influence the order, but they do it by convincing the Product Owner, and a Product Owner whose decisions are overruled by somebody else is not the Product Owner.

**The Scrum Master is accountable for the team's effectiveness**, by establishing Scrum as the guide defines it and helping everybody understand it. The 2020 edition describes them as "true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organization". They coach, remove impediments and make sure the events happen and are useful; they do not assign work.

**The Developers are everybody who builds the Increment** — programmers, testers, designers, analysts. The guide makes them accountable for creating the plan for the Sprint, for keeping to the Definition of Done and for adapting their plan each day.

## Who is missing

There is no project manager, no team lead and no architect in that list. The work a project manager used to do is split: the Product Owner decides what and in which order, the Developers decide how and how much, and the Scrum Master looks after the process. An architect in a Scrum organisation is either one of the Developers on a team or somebody outside the teams who serves several of them, and this lesson's eighth section says what that means in practice.
