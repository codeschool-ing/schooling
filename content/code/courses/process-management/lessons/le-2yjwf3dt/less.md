LeSS starts from the opposite instinct. Its authors, Craig Larman and Bas Vodde, developed it from about 2005 with large product groups, and its central claim is in its name and its slogan, *more with LeSS*: **scaling Scrum should mean less process, not more.** Where SAFe adds roles and events to coordinate teams, LeSS removes what makes coordination necessary.

## One product, one of everything

LeSS applies to **two to eight teams** working on one product. Its rules keep Scrum's single-team structure and stretch it:

- **one Product Owner** for the whole product, not one per team;
- **one Product Backlog**, from which every team takes work;
- **one Definition of Done** shared by all the teams;
- **one Sprint**, starting and ending on the same days for every team, producing **one shippable increment** of the whole product.

There is no release train engineer, no product management layer and no system architect role. The Scrum Masters, typically one for one to three teams, look after the process; the teams coordinate with each other directly.

## What changes in the events

**Sprint Planning has two parts.** In *Sprint Planning One*, representatives of all the teams meet the Product Owner and decide which backlog items each team will take. In *Sprint Planning Two*, each team plans on its own how it will do its items, and teams whose items are closely related may plan together.

**The Sprint Review is one review for the whole product**, often run as a bazaar where stakeholders walk between teams' stations. And there are two retrospectives: each team's own, and an **Overall Retrospective** where team representatives, Scrum Masters, the Product Owner and managers look at the system the teams work in — the organisation, the tools, the policies.

## Beyond eight teams

Past about eight teams, one Product Owner cannot know the whole backlog. **LeSS Huge** divides the product into **requirement areas**, each with an Area Product Owner and four to eight teams, while keeping one Product Backlog and one Sprint for everybody. It is meant for products of hundreds of people, and the LeSS authors are explicit that it is hard to adopt.

## The demands it makes

LeSS asks for things that SAFe does not, and they are organisational rather than procedural. Teams must be **feature teams**, able to finish items end to end, which the next section explains. Managers are expected to change the organisation's structure rather than add a coordination layer over it. And one person must genuinely own the product. An organisation unwilling to change its structure will find LeSS impossible; one willing to change it usually finds it lighter than the alternatives.
