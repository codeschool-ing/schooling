A team that measures nothing argues from impressions: "we have been slow lately", "releases feel risky". A team that measures everything drowns in dashboards nobody reads. Between the two is a short list of numbers chosen because each one **answers a question somebody needs to decide**. That is the test this lesson applies: a measure is worth keeping when it changes a decision, and only then.

## Three uses, and one to refuse

Delivery measures serve three legitimate purposes:

- **Forecasting**: when will this be ready? Lesson 3's cycle time and lesson 10's velocity were measures used this way.
- **Improving**: is the change we made to how we work helping? A team that limits work in progress should see its cycle time fall; if it does not, the change was not the answer.
- **Noticing**: is something going wrong that we have not felt yet? A lead time creeping up over three months is visible in a chart long before anybody complains.

The fourth use, which this lesson's sixth section refuses, is **judging people**. It is the most common use in practice, and it destroys the other three.

## Goodhart's law

Charles Goodhart, an economist advising the Bank of England, observed in 1975 that any statistical regularity tends to collapse once pressure is placed on it for control purposes. The anthropologist Marilyn Strathern later put it in the form everybody quotes: **when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.**

Lesson 10 met it with velocity, which rises when it is made a target without anything else changing. It applies to every number in this lesson. A team rewarded for deployment frequency can deploy empty changes; a team rewarded for a low change failure rate can stop deploying. The numbers stay useful only while nobody is trying to move them directly, which means they belong first to the team that produces them.

## Measure the system, not the people

The measures in this lesson describe **a team and its process** — how work flows, how often changes reach users, how often they break. They say nothing about any individual's contribution, and they are not meant to. That is a feature: a team that owns its numbers can look at a bad month honestly, because the number points at the system the team can change rather than at a person who will defend themselves.
