---
title: Kanban or Scrum
version: 1
---

The question is asked constantly and usually framed as a choice between two religions. The two methods answer different parts of the same problem, and many teams use pieces of both.

| | Scrum | Kanban |
|---|---|---|
| rhythm | fixed Sprints of a month or less | continuous flow, no iterations required |
| how work in progress is limited | by what fits in a Sprint | by an explicit limit per column or per board |
| roles | Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers | none required; start with the roles you have |
| when to change the plan | at the next Sprint Planning | whenever the top of Ready changes |
| main measures | the Sprint Goal met; velocity, often | cycle time, throughput, work item age |
| how it starts | adopt the framework | visualise what you do now |

## Where each fits

**Scrum fits work that can be planned a few weeks ahead** and benefits from a regular review with stakeholders: a product team building features against a goal. The fixed rhythm gives stakeholders a predictable moment to see progress and change direction.

**Kanban fits work that arrives unpredictably** and has to be answered in days: support, operations, maintenance, a platform team serving requests from other teams. Forcing that work into Sprints means either leaving urgent requests for two weeks or breaking the Sprint every few days, and Kanban's continuous flow avoids both.

## Using both

Many teams run Sprints and keep a Kanban board with limits inside them, which Scrum allows, since it prescribes no particular board. The Scrum.org community even published a short guide for that combination. The opposite direction also happens: a Kanban team that keeps a fortnightly review with stakeholders and a retrospective, without calling either a Sprint event.

## What does not work

What does not work is a Scrum team that starts every item on the first day of the Sprint and calls its task board Kanban, or a Kanban team with no limits that calls its absence of planning flow. **The limit is what makes Kanban Kanban**, and the goal is what makes a Sprint a Sprint; keeping the vocabulary and dropping either one keeps the cost of the method and loses what it was for.
