title: Negotiating time to pay it down
version: 1
---

Paying down debt takes time that could be spent on features, so it has to be agreed with whoever decides priorities. Most teams find this conversation difficult, and most of the difficulty comes from having it in the wrong terms.

## Speak in the other side's terms

"The code is a mess and we need to clean it up" asks for trust and offers nothing in return. The Product Owner hears a request to stop delivering for the developers' comfort, and says no, reasonably.

"The test suite costs us 25 hours every Sprint, about 6% of the team's 400. Fixing it takes 60 hours and pays back in under three Sprints. After that those 25 hours go into features every Sprint" is a business proposal with a return, and it competes with features on equal terms. It is the same request, translated.

The translation has three parts, and each comes from this course:

- **the interest**, in hours or in risk, from this lesson's fifth section;
- **the principal**, estimated as a range, as lesson 9 taught;
- **the payback**, and what is gained after it, in the units the other side cares about — features delivered, incidents avoided, dates protected.

The `architect-communication` course gives the general skill a lesson of its own, its thirteenth: negotiating deadline, scope, quality and debt.

## Offer options, not a demand

A negotiation goes better with choices. For the test suite: fix it fully in one Sprint for 60 hours; fix the three worst tests for 20 hours now and the rest next quarter; or accept the interest and say so in writing. The Product Owner can choose, and whichever choice is made is now a decision with a cost attached rather than an argument somebody lost.

## Tie it to what the business already wants

Debt repayment is easiest to agree when it is part of delivering something wanted. "Online booking needs changes to the payment module; we will refactor it as part of that work, which adds about a week but makes the next three payment features faster" ties the repayment to a feature the Product Owner is already asking for. Many teams pay down most of their debt this way, a piece at a time, in the code they are changing anyway.

## When the answer is no

Sometimes the answer is no: a contract date, a launch, a crisis. That is legitimate, and the right response is to **record the decision and the interest** — "accepted for this quarter, 25 hours a Sprint" — and to bring it back at the next planning point with the accumulated cost. A debt refused once with its interest recorded is a much stronger case the second time.
