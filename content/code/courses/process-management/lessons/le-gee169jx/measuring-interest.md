The most useful thing a team can do for its technical debt is **measure the interest**, because the interest is what makes a debt worth paying and what lets it compete with features. It does not need to be precise; it needs to be honest and in the same unit as everything else, which is usually hours.

## Two debts, priced

The Agenda team's two largest debts, as the team recorded them:

**The flaky test suite.** Five developers each lose about half an hour a day to re-running tests that fail at random. Over a ten-day Sprint that is:

```localised
5 developers × 0.5 hours × 10 days = 25 hours a Sprint
```

Fixing it is estimated at **60 hours**. The fix pays for itself in 60 / 25 = **2.4 Sprints**, and after that every Sprint gives back 25 hours.

**The manual deployment.** Each deployment takes three hours of manual steps, and the team deploys twice a Sprint:

```localised
3 hours × 2 deployments = 6 hours a Sprint
```

Automating it is estimated at 40 hours, which pays back in 40 / 6 = **6.7 Sprints**. Still worth doing, and much less urgent than the test suite. Over ten Sprints the two debts cost **250** and **60** hours respectively, which is the comparison to put in front of a Product Owner.

## The interest that is not hours

Some interest is not time spent but **risk carried**, and it is better expressed in the terms of lesson 11. The database leaving support has almost no weekly cost today; its interest is the probability of an unpatchable security flaw, times its impact on a system holding health data. A module only one person understands costs little while that person is there; its interest is risk B on the register.

For those, use the risk register's numbers rather than inventing hours: probability, impact, expected value, and the date after which the probability changes.

## Measuring rather than guessing

The first estimate of interest is usually a guess from the developers, and that is a reasonable start. It becomes credible when it is measured for a couple of Sprints: count the test re-runs from the CI logs, time the deployments, note how long changes to the tangled module take compared with similar changes elsewhere. **A measured interest of 25 hours a Sprint ends arguments that an estimated one starts.** It also guards against the opposite error: the debt everybody complains about that turns out, measured, to cost very little.
