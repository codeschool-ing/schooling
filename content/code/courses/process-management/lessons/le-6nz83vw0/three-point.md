Analogy and parametric estimates produce one number. **Three-point estimating** asks for three for each task, and uses them to describe the range directly:

- **O**, the **optimistic** duration, if everything goes well;
- **M**, the **most likely** duration, the one the person would bet on;
- **P**, the **pessimistic** duration, if the things that usually go wrong go wrong — not if the building burns down.

## The PERT formulas

The method comes from **PERT**, the *Program Evaluation and Review Technique*, developed for the US Navy's Polaris missile programme in 1958, in the same years as the critical path method of lesson 6. It treats each task's duration as a distribution and gives two formulas:

```localised
mean               = (O + 4M + P) / 6
standard deviation = (P − O) / 6
```

The mean gives the most likely value four times the weight of either extreme. The standard deviation is a sixth of the range, a rule of thumb that treats the optimistic and pessimistic values as roughly the edges of the distribution.

A simpler alternative, the **triangular** mean, weights the three equally: (O + M + P) / 3. It is more cautious, because the pessimistic value pulls harder.

## The Agenda team's four tasks

| task | O | M | P | PERT mean | standard deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slots API | 3 | 5 | 13 | 6.00 | 1.67 |
| booking screen | 2 | 4 | 8 | 4.33 | 1.00 |
| payment integration | 3 | 6 | 20 | 7.83 | 2.83 |
| notifications | 1 | 2 | 5 | 2.33 | 0.67 |

The payment integration is the one to look at. Its most likely value is 6 days, and its pessimistic value is 20, because a payment provider's sandbox, documentation and approval process are outside the team's control. That long right tail is what pulls its mean up to **7.83**.

## The asymmetry is the information

Most tasks' three figures are lopsided like this: a little room to go faster than expected, a lot of room to go slower. **Whenever they are, the mean sits above the most likely value**, and a plan built from most likely values is built from numbers that are each more optimistic than the average outcome. The next section shows what that does when the tasks are added up.
