---
title: Giving a range, and defending it
version: 1
---

The Agenda team now has several estimates for online booking: 21.6 days by analogy, 22.4 days for the screen work by parametric rate, a PERT mean of 20.5 days with an 85th percentile of 24.1. What should it tell the clinic network's director?

## Say a range and a confidence

The answer that serves the director is a **range with a confidence attached**: *"between 20 and 25 working days; we are about 85% confident of finishing within 25"*. It says three things a single number cannot: the most likely outcome, how much it could vary, and how sure the team is.

A single number — "21 days" — will be heard as a commitment. When the work takes 24 days, as it well may, the team will have missed a commitment it never made. The range protects both sides: the director can plan around the upper end, and the team is not punished for an outcome that was always inside its estimate.

## Different estimates are evidence, not a contradiction

The analogy, the parametric rate and the PERT mean landed within two days of each other. That agreement is worth saying out loud, because **independent methods agreeing is the strongest evidence an estimate can have**. If they had disagreed — analogy saying 15 days and PERT saying 30 — the disagreement would itself be the finding, and the right next step would be to find out which assumption differs, not to average them.

## When the target does not fit

Suppose the director needs booking in 18 working days. The team's estimate says that is below the most likely outcome. The honest conversation is not a negotiation over the estimate, which would only change the word and not the work. It is about the three things that can change:

- **Scope**: which part of booking can wait? Notifications, at 2.3 days, could follow a week later.
- **Resources**: can the work be split so more people help, without the communication costs of lesson 5 eating the gain?
- **Risk**: can the payment integration's long tail be cut, for example by starting the provider's approval process this week?

## Re-estimate as you learn

An estimate is a snapshot. The cone of uncertainty from lesson 1 narrows as decisions are made, and the estimate should narrow with it. When the payment provider's sandbox works on the first day, the pessimistic 20 for that task is no longer credible, and the range for the whole feature tightens. **Updating an estimate as information arrives is not a failure of the first estimate**; refusing to update is.
