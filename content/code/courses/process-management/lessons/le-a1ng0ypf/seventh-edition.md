title: The seventh edition, principles and domains
version: 1
---

In 2021 PMI published a seventh edition of the guide that looks almost nothing like the sixth. The 49 processes and their grid are gone. In their place are **twelve principles** of project management and **eight performance domains** — areas of activity that every project has to look after, whatever approach it uses.

## Twelve principles

The principles are stated as short imperatives. Paraphrased, they ask a project to be a diligent and respectful **steward**; to build a collaborative **team**; to engage **stakeholders**; to focus on **value**; to recognise how the parts of the project interact, as a **system**; to show **leadership**; to **tailor** the approach to the context; to build **quality** into processes and deliverables; to navigate **complexity**; to optimise its responses to **risk**; to embrace **adaptability and resiliency**; and to enable **change** in order to reach the future it was set up for.

Several of them would sit comfortably in the agile manifesto. That is deliberate: the seventh edition was written for a profession in which a large share of projects are run with agile or hybrid approaches.

## Eight performance domains

| domain | what it looks after |
|---|---|
| Stakeholders | the people and groups the project affects |
| Team | the people doing the work |
| Development approach and life cycle | predictive, adaptive or hybrid, and the phases |
| Planning | organising the work, at whatever level of detail fits |
| Project work | the processes and the day-to-day flow |
| Delivery | delivering value and meeting the requirements |
| Measurement | knowing how the project is doing |
| Uncertainty | risk, ambiguity and complexity |

## Tailoring is the centre

The idea that ties the seventh edition together is **tailoring**: deliberately adapting the approach, the governance and the processes to the project in front of you. A sixth-edition reader could treat the grid as a checklist to complete; the seventh edition says the checklist was never the point. A small internal project with users close at hand may run adaptively with almost no formal planning documents; a regulated infrastructure project may need a predictive plan with baselines and change control. Both can be good project management.

## Why the change matters to you

The change means that "following PMBOK" no longer tells you how a project is run. One organisation may mean the sixth edition's processes, with charters, baselines and earned value; another may mean the seventh edition's principles over a Scrum team. **Ask which.** The vocabulary of this lesson's earlier sections remains the one you will hear most, because it is concrete and because so many organisations were built on it, and lesson 15 notes that the main PMI certification now tests predictive, agile and hybrid approaches together.
