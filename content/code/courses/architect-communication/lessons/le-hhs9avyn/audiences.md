**An incident has several audiences at once, and each needs a different message on a different
channel at a different rhythm.** Lesson 3's rule still holds: the facts are the same in every message.
What changes is the unit, the detail, and what the reader can do with it.

## Who needs what

| audience | needs | channel | rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the responders** | everything, as it happens | the incident channel | continuous |
| **leadership** | impact in business terms, whether they need to act | leadership channel | every 15 minutes |
| **support** | what to tell customers, right now | support's own channel | at every change |
| **customers** | whether it affects them, and when to try again | status page, app banner | every 15 to 30 minutes |
| **clients** like Boa Praça | effect on their stores, and a person to call | a direct message from the account owner | at start and end |

## Support needs words, not explanations

At 19:24, Sofia's support team was receiving three complaints a minute and had nothing to say. The
most useful message of the whole evening was not the status page; it was Lívia's line to support at
19:26:

> For customers who can't pay: "We know about it and are fixing it. Your basket is saved; nothing has
> been charged. Please try again in about half an hour." Status page link: …

**A ready sentence for support is worth more than any explanation of the cause.** Support agents do
not need to understand connection pools; they need something true to say to the next person, and
permission to say it.

## Leadership needs to know whether to act

The messages to Otávio and Caio had one extra line the others did not: **"You do not need to do
anything right now"**, or, if they did, what. Lesson 1's note to Caio used the same line for the same
reason: a director with no instruction starts asking questions, and the questions land on the
incident commander.

## Clients hear from a person

Boa Praça's stores were affected too: their customers order through Marola's app. At 19:30 the account
owner messaged Tânia directly, in the form lesson 3 described: what was happening, what it meant for
her stores, that nothing was needed from her, and a number to call. At 20:05 a second message said it
was resolved. **A client should never learn about an incident affecting them from their own
customers.**
