---
title: What each audience decides
version: 1
---

**Adapting a message is not simplifying it. It is changing the unit, the length and the ask to fit
what the reader decides.** The common picture is a technical truth that has to be "dumbed down" for
people who are not engineers. That picture insults the reader and produces the wrong document: a
finance director does not need fewer ideas, he needs the ideas measured in the unit he works in.

Lívia writes to four kinds of reader in a normal month. Each one decides something different, and
the decision is what tells her what to put in.

| | the board and the CEO | product | the engineering team | an external client |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| decides | money and direction, a few times a year | what is built, and in what order | how it is built | whether to keep trusting and paying |
| their unit | reais, customers, quarters | scope, dates, customer impact | latency, connections, engineer-weeks | orders, hours of service, their own staff's work |
| reads for | the outcome and the risk | the trade-off | the constraints and the reason | what changes for them, and what they must do |
| time they give it | a paragraph | a page | as long as it takes | one message |

## The board decides in money and risk

Otávio, Marola's CEO, and the board he reports to do not choose databases. They choose where money
goes and which risks the company carries. A message to them succeeds if it states **the outcome in
reais or customers, the risk in plain words, and the decision they are being asked for**, in that
order. Everything about *how* is a sign that the writer has not decided which of their questions
they are answering.

## Product decides between things that all matter

Renata, the head of product, owns the order in which things get built. Every engineer-week Lívia
asks for is an engineer-week taken from something Renata had promised somebody else. So a message to
her has to **name what it displaces**, or she will work it out herself, usually with a less
flattering answer. "Six engineer-weeks in April" means nothing to her until it becomes "the new
substitution flow moves from April to May".

## The team decides how

Engineers want the constraints, the reasoning and the detail, and they will check it. The failure
here is the opposite of the board's: **leaving out the reason** because it was obvious to the
writer. A team told "use the replica" with no why will remove it the first time the replica is
inconvenient.

## A client decides whether to trust you

A client reads every sentence as a commitment, because in a contract it may be one. They want to
know what changes for *them* and what they must do, and they do not want to know which team inside
Marola was at fault. The last section of this lesson is about them.

## The facts never change between audiences

**Four versions of a message may differ in everything except the facts.** The unit, the length,
the order and the detail all move; the number of failed checkouts, the cost and the date do not.
Audiences talk to each other. When the board hears "a small risk" and the team hears "this could
take checkout down on any Friday", somebody eventually puts the two messages side by side, and
from that day nothing the writer says is taken at face value.
