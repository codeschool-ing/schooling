title: Credibility without selling
version: 1
---

**The difference between building a reputation and selling yourself is whether the audience leaves
knowing more about the subject or more about you.** Engineers distrust self-promotion for good reason,
and some conclude that any visibility is self-promotion. It is not. Teaching in public is visibility,
and it is the kind that lasts.

## Teaching against advertising

| advertising | teaching |
|---|---|
| "how we built a world-class platform" | "how a batch job took down checkout, and what we changed" |
| only what went right | what went wrong as well, and what is still unknown |
| "I designed…" for the team's work | "we", with names |
| the technology as the hero | the problem as the subject |
| the talk ends with a hiring slide | the talk ends with what the audience can do on Monday |

Both columns make the speaker visible. Only the right-hand one makes other engineers trust them, because
**honesty about failure is the most credible signal a speaker can send**: it costs something, and nobody
who was only selling would pay it.

## Do not overclaim

Credibility is spent much faster than it is earned. A single number inflated in a talk, a result
described as more general than it was ("this will work for any company"), or a technology praised
without its costs, and an audience of engineers stops believing the rest. **Say what worked, where, and
how sure you are**, the same precision lesson 1 asked of a status note, with the same reason behind it.

## Declare interests

If Lívia's talk praised a product whose maker sponsored the meetup, or a tool her company sells, the
audience should hear that from her first. **An undeclared interest, discovered later, turns a good talk
into an advertisement in retrospect.** One sentence at the start is enough.

## Say no to the wrong invitations

Lesson 8 applies here too. A visible engineer gets asked to speak, write and review more than anybody
could do well. Saying no to a talk on a subject you do not know well, or to a panel whose purpose is to
fill a stage, protects the reputation the talks you do give are building. The alternative to offer is
often a colleague who knows the subject better, which is sponsorship from lesson 10, given outside the
company.

## Where this course ends

Every lesson in this course has been about one idea from a different side: **the work is not finished
until the people who need it understand it.** A decision nobody understood was not made; a risk in an
adjective was not reported; a no without an alternative ended a conversation instead of moving it; a
postmortem that found someone to blame learnt nothing. A reputation is what accumulates when that idea
is practised for long enough that other people start to count on it.
