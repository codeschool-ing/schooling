---
title: Five kinds of alternative
version: 1
---

**A no without an alternative ends the conversation; a no with one moves it to a better question.**
Most requests can be met in some form, and finding that form is the part of saying no that takes
skill. There are five shapes it usually takes, and it is worth running through all five before
answering.

| alternative | what it says | for the flash deals |
|---|---|---|
| **not now** | yes, at a later date | full flash deals for the Christmas campaign |
| **not all of it** | a smaller version now | a countdown banner on existing promotions, without reserved stock |
| **not like this** | a different way to meet the same need | a "Black Friday early access" list for returning customers, which needs no new checkout logic |
| **not by us** | somebody else, or something already built | the marketing team's existing email tool can send a timed offer |
| **yes, if** | yes, in exchange for something else | yes by the 27th, if the substitution flow moves from December to February |

The last row is not a refusal at all, and it is often the most useful: **it hands the trade-off to
the person entitled to make it.** Otávio can decide that flash deals matter more than the
substitution flow. Lívia cannot decide that for him, and she should not decide it by default by
quietly overloading the team.

## Finding the smaller version

"Not all of it" is the alternative most often available and most often missed, because the request
arrives as a bundle. Otávio's flash deals were three things at once:

1. a **countdown** on the home screen, which is what customers see;
2. a **one-hour discount**, which the promotions engine already supports;
3. **reserved stock** for whoever adds the item first, which is new logic in checkout.

The first two carry most of the visible effect and take about three days. The third carries most
of the risk and most of the cost. **Splitting a request into its parts and pricing each one** is how
the smaller version is found, and lesson 13 uses the same move when the negotiation is about scope
and deadlines.

## An alternative has to be real

An alternative offered only to soften the no, which the team does not actually intend to deliver,
is worse than a plain no. The asker accepts it, plans around it, and discovers later that it was a
courtesy. **Offer only what you would be glad to be held to.** Lívia checked the banner with Bruna
before offering it, and it went into the team's plan the same day.
