**A good no has five parts: it shows the need was understood, says no clearly, gives the reason in
the asker's unit, offers an alternative, and says what would change the answer.** Leave out the
first and the asker feels dismissed. Leave out the second and they hear a yes. Leave out the last
two and the conversation ends where it should have started.

## Lívia's answer to Otávio

She did not answer in the corridor. "Give me until tomorrow at 10:00 and I'll come back with what we
can do" is itself a good first answer, because a considered no is worth more than an instant one.
At 10:00 on Wednesday she said:

> **The need.** You want customers to have a reason to choose us over the competitor on Black
> Friday, and to see it the moment they open the app.
>
> **The no.** We can't build flash deals with reserved stock by the 27th without putting Black
> Friday itself at risk.
>
> **The reason.** Reserving stock is new logic in checkout, and it would need about 30 of the team's
> 60 days in a month where the load test and the payment provider's deadline already take most of
> them. If it fails on the day, checkout fails for everybody, as it did on 6 March.
>
> **The alternative.** A countdown banner on the home screen for one-hour discounts, using the
> promotions engine we already have, without reserved stock: the discount applies while stock lasts.
> About three days. It gets most of what customers see.
>
> **What would change the answer.** If flash deals with reservation matter more to you than the
> substitution flow in December, we can build them properly for the Christmas campaign instead.

## What each part does

- **The need** first, said back in his terms (lesson 6's paraphrase). It tells Otávio he was heard
  before he hears the word no, which makes the no easier to hear.
- **The no** in one sentence, early, without "unfortunately", "I'm afraid" or "it might be
  difficult". Softening words do not make a no kinder; they make it unclear, and an unclear no is
  heard as a yes.
- **The reason** in his unit: risk to Black Friday, and a reference to an outage he remembers. Not
  "the reservation logic needs distributed locking".
- **The alternative** that serves the need, not a consolation prize. Lívia's banner gets customers
  most of the visible effect for a tenth of the cost.
- **The door**: the condition under which the answer becomes yes. It turns the refusal into a
  trade-off he can make, and it is honest, because the feature is not impossible; it is expensive
  at this moment.

## The tone

Neutral and direct. **A no is not an apology and not a lecture.** Apologising for a correct
decision invites the asker to argue it away; explaining at length why the asker should not have
asked makes them defensive. Say it the way you would report any other fact about capacity: plainly,
with the evidence, and with the next step.
