**A written no is read without your tone of voice, more than once, and sometimes by people you did
not write it for, so it has to be clearer and kinder on the page than it would need to be in
person.** The five parts from earlier in this lesson still apply; what changes is that every
softening word, every hedge and every missing reason is now permanent.

## Three examples from one week

**To a team asking Lívia to review their design by Friday:**

> Thanks for sending it. I can't do a proper review before Friday: Black Friday preparation has
> my week. I can do it on Tuesday the 1st, or Bruna has reviewed two similar designs and could look
> at it this week, if you'd rather not wait. Which suits you?

Not now, or not me, both offered. The reason is one clause. The reader chooses.

**To Renata, asking whether a client request could "just be added" to the next release:**

> I'd rather not add it to this release: it changes how substitutions are charged, and that needs
> the finance team's review, which can't happen before the 27th. If the client needs it before
> Christmas, it can go in the release on 8 December with that review done. Shall I ask finance now
> so the date holds?

The reason is a risk Renata would care about, not an engineering preference. The alternative comes
with an offer to make it happen.

**To Tânia at Boa Praça, through the account owner, about a custom report:**

> Tânia, we won't be able to build a separate report for Boa Praça this quarter; our reporting work
> is committed to changes every client will get. Two things that may cover most of what you need:
> the standard weekly report now includes deliveries by store, and the data export can be filtered
> by your stores in a spreadsheet. If neither covers it, let's talk about what the report is for,
> and I'll see whether it fits into next quarter's plan.

To a client, the no is about the company's plan, not about one team. And it ends with lesson 6: ask
what the report is for, because the real need may already be met.

## Words that weaken a written no

| instead of | write |
|---|---|
| "Unfortunately, I'm afraid it might be difficult to…" | "I can't…" |
| "We'll try to fit it in" | a date you will keep, or no |
| "It's not really a priority right now" | what *is* the priority, so the reader understands the trade |
| "As I said in my last message…" | the point again, once, without the reminder |
| "Sorry for the inconvenience" | what you can do instead |

**A written no should be as long as it needs to be to carry its five parts, and no longer.** The
three examples above are each eighty words or fewer.
