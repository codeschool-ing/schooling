title: Pairing to teach
version: 1
---

**When a senior engineer pairs with a junior, the senior's job is to keep their hands off the keyboard
and their answers to themselves for longer than feels natural.** The junior learns by driving, deciding
and occasionally getting it wrong with somebody beside them. The senior who grabs the keyboard "just to
show" turns the session into a demonstration.

## Diego and Lívia

When Diego started the delivery-slot change from lesson 10, he and Lívia paired on the first
afternoon. The rules they agreed at the start:

1. **Diego drives most of the time.** Lívia navigates, and when she has a specific idea, she says it
   and Diego types it: strong style, so the idea goes through his hands.
2. **When Diego is stuck, Lívia asks before she tells.** "What are the options?" first; "have you
   looked at the opening-hours table?" second; the answer only if both fail.
3. **Mistakes are allowed to run for a minute.** If Diego writes a loop that will not work, Lívia waits
   until the test shows it, unless it is costing more than a few minutes. Finding it himself teaches
   him; being told teaches him that she found it.
4. **Lívia drives once, at the end**, to show one thing she does differently, and says why.

## What the senior learns

Pairing with a junior is also the fastest way for a senior engineer to see where the codebase is hard
to understand. Every "why is it like this?" from Diego was either a good question with a good answer,
or a sign that the code needed a comment, a rename or a better test. Lívia kept a list; four of the
items became small pull requests the following week.

## Signs it is going wrong

- **The junior stops asking.** They are either lost and embarrassed, or the senior has been answering
  questions they did not ask. Swap, take a break, and ask what they are thinking.
- **The senior is typing.** Hand the keyboard back.
- **Neither is talking.** Somebody is working alone while the other one watches. Say what you are doing
  or what you think is next, or stop the session.

**A pairing session that leaves the junior able to do the next one alone succeeded, even if it was
slower than the senior working by themselves.**
