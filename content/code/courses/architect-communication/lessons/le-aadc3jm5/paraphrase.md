**A paraphrase is your understanding of what the other person said, put in your words and offered
back to them so they can correct it.** It is the cheapest test in any conversation: a wrong
understanding found in ten seconds at the coffee machine costs nothing, and the same one found after
a quarter of work costs a quarter of work.

## What a paraphrase sounds like

After Renata's corridor request, Lívia's first instinct was to answer with an estimate. What she
said instead:

> "Let me check I've got this. The average basket has been falling, leadership is asking you why,
> and you think recommendations would push it back up. Is that right?"

Renata's answer changed the conversation: "Not exactly. It's falling for *returning* customers.
New customers are fine." That one correction, which Lívia would never have heard without the
paraphrase, pointed away from recommendations and towards something that had changed for people
who already used the app.

## Three rules

1. **Your words, not theirs.** Repeating "you need a recommendations service" back proves only that
   you can repeat. Restating it as "you want returning customers to buy more" tests whether you
   understood the purpose, and gives the speaker something to correct.
2. **Short.** One or two sentences. A long paraphrase becomes a speech of your own.
3. **End with a real question.** "Is that right?", "What did I miss?", "Is that the main thing?" The
   question is the point; without it, a paraphrase is a statement the other person has to interrupt
   to correct, and most will not.

## Summarising a longer conversation

In a meeting, the paraphrase grows into a summary at natural breaks: "So far I've heard three
things: the basket is down 8% for returning customers since January, the stores think it's the
app, and leadership wants an answer by the quarterly review. Anything else before we talk about
causes?" A summary like that does three jobs. It checks understanding, it gives quieter people a
moment to add what was missed, and it marks the move from describing the problem to solving it,
which is the move engineers make too early.

## Naming what is not said

Sometimes the most important thing in the conversation is a feeling or a pressure nobody named.
"Everybody upstairs is asking me why" is not a requirement, and it is the most important sentence
Renata said: she is under pressure and needs an answer she can give. **Reflecting it carefully,
as an observation and not a diagnosis**, shows you heard it: "It sounds like you need something
you can tell the board by the review." That is a statement about her situation, which she can
confirm or correct. "You seem stressed" is a statement about her, and most people at work resent it.
