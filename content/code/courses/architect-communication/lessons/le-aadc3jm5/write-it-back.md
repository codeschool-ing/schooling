---
title: Writing back what you heard
version: 1
---

**After a conversation about a need, send the other person a short written summary and ask them to
correct it.** Memory of a conversation drifts within days, and it drifts differently for each
person in it. A written summary sent the same day fixes one version that both sides have agreed to,
and it is the version both will refer to when the work is delivered.

## The shape

Five parts, each one or two lines:

1. **What I heard**: the situation, in the speaker's terms.
2. **The need**, as you understood it, separated from the solution originally requested.
3. **What we agreed**, including what you will do and by when.
4. **Open questions**, with who will answer each.
5. **The ask**: "Please correct anything I got wrong by Friday."

Lívia's note to Renata after the second conversation:

> Renata, here's what I took from today, so we're working from the same page.
>
> **What I heard.** The average basket for returning customers is down about 8% since January (from
> about R$ 150 to R$ 138); new customers are unaffected. Leadership wants an explanation and a plan
> by the quarterly review on 14 May.
>
> **The need.** Get returning customers buying what they used to. A recommendations service is one
> way; the data suggests the "buy again" list leaving the home screen in January is a cheaper
> place to start.
>
> **Agreed.** Bruna's team puts the list back on the home screen next week (about two days). The
> data team measures returning-customer baskets for three weeks after.
>
> **Open.** Whether recommendations are still worth scoping if the basket recovers: we decide on 12
> May with the numbers.
>
> Please correct anything I've got wrong by Friday.

## Why it matters more than it seems

- **It catches the misunderstanding the conversation hid.** Renata replied with one correction: the
  review was on the 14th, not the 15th, as Lívia had first written. Small, and exactly the kind of
  error that becomes a missed deadline.
- **It protects the person who listened.** If the basket does not recover, the note shows that the
  recommendations question was left open on purpose, with a date, rather than dismissed.
- **It is the start of the next document.** If recommendations do come back, the note's "need"
  paragraph is the first paragraph of the one-pager from lesson 2.

## Listening in meetings

The same applies to a meeting: **the person who writes the summary decides what the meeting
agreed.** That is a responsibility, not a privilege, and the summary has to be what was agreed and
not what the writer wished had been. Send it within the day, to everybody who was there, with the
same final line: correct anything I got wrong.
