**People rarely bring a problem to an architect. They bring a solution they have already chosen,
and the problem is underneath it.** "We need a recommendations service" is a solution. "Returning
customers are buying less" is the problem. "Leadership needs an answer by the review" is the
pressure that made the request urgent. A good response serves all three, and the first is the one
most likely to be wrong.

This is not a criticism of the people asking. Renata knows her customers better than Lívia does,
and she has heard that recommendations raise basket size at other companies. Proposing a solution
is how most people describe a problem they cannot yet name. **The listener's job is to keep the
solution as one option while finding the problem it was meant to solve.**

## What Lívia found

Following the open questions from the last section, over two conversations and an afternoon with
the data team:

- The basket for **returning** customers fell from about R$ 150 to about R$ 138 between January and
  April, a fall of 8%. New customers' baskets did not change.
- In January the app was redesigned, and the "buy again" list, which showed a customer the items
  they ordered most, moved from the home screen into a menu.
- Customers who still opened that list bought as much as before. Fewer customers opened it.

The real need was not recommendations. It was getting returning customers back to the list of
things they already buy. Putting the list back on the home screen took Bruna's team **two days**.
The recommendations service had been estimated at a quarter for two teams.

## Jobs to be done

Clayton Christensen, who studied how companies decide what to build, described the same idea as
*jobs to be done*: customers do not want a product, they "hire" it to get a job done in their
lives, and understanding the job tells you what to build. His best-known example is a fast-food
chain trying to sell more milkshakes, which learned that many were bought early in the morning by
commuters who wanted something to make a long drive less boring and to keep them full until lunch.
That job, not flavour, was what the milkshake was competing on.

The idea works inside a company as well. **Ask what job the requested thing is being hired to do**,
and the request becomes one candidate among several.

## When the stated request is right

Sometimes the person asking has done the diagnosis and the solution is correct. The test is not to
reject every request; it is to understand the need well enough to know. If Lívia had found that
returning customers were opening the list and still buying less, a recommendations service might
have been exactly right, and she would have proposed it with a measure attached: the basket back to
R$ 150. **Listening for the real need is how you find out whether the requested solution is the
right one, not a way of refusing it.**
