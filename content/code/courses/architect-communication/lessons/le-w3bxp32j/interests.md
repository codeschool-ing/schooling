**The first move in a deadline negotiation is the same as in a conflict: find what each side needs,
not what each side is asking for.** Lesson 9 did it for database connections; here it is a date
and a feature, and the move is the same.

## Behind "1 December"

Lívia, who was asked by Henrique to help, asked Renata the lesson 6 question: "What happens if it
launches on the 14th instead?" The answer had two parts:

- **Boa Praça's Christmas campaign** starts on 1 December, and its stores will be telling customers
  they can order ahead. If the feature is not there, Boa Praça's own staff take those orders by phone,
  which they did last year and hated.
- **Marola's own campaign** does not depend on the date at all. Its email goes out on the 10th.

So the date that matters is Boa Praça's 1 December, and what Boa Praça needs on that date is narrower
than the whole feature: **customers ordering ahead for delivery within the week, at their stores.**

## Behind "ten weeks"

Henrique's team broke the estimate down: four weeks for the ordering flow itself, two for
recurring orders, one for editing an order after confirmation, and **three for untangling the order
state machine**, the code that decides what state an order is in, which had grown over five years
and which every change to checkout or logistics now had to work around.

The three weeks of untangling were not part of the feature. They were **debt being paid inside the
feature**, because doing the feature without it would take longer and break more. That changes the
conversation, and the fourth section returns to it.

## BATNA

Fisher and Ury, whose interests-and-positions idea appeared in lesson 9, named the other half of any
negotiation: the **best alternative to a negotiated agreement**, or BATNA. It is what each side does
if they do not agree, and it sets how much either side should give.

| | if there is no agreement |
|---|---|
| Renata | Boa Praça takes advance orders by phone again, and Marola loses some of them |
| Henrique's team | the team works to 1 December anyway, skipping things, and carries the consequences |

Neither alternative is good, which is why both sides had a reason to find a better trade. **Knowing
your own BATNA stops you agreeing to something worse than it**; knowing the other side's tells you
what they will accept. Henrique's team's real BATNA was the bad one in the table, and saying it out
loud, "if we just push for the date, this is what you will get", was what made Renata take the
quality lever off the table herself.
