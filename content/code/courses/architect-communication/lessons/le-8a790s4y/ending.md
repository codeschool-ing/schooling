---
title: Ending well, and handing on
version: 1
---

**A mentoring relationship should end on purpose, when its goal is met or when it has stopped being
useful, and not by fading away.** Relationships that fade leave both people unsure whether it ended
or is only paused, and the junior unsure whether they did something wrong.

## The review

On 30 September Lívia, Diego and Bruna met for the review the plan had set. The evidence row was the
agenda: had Diego taken a feature from idea to production without Bruna stepping in? He had, with the
delivery-slot change, apart from the midnight bug, which he had found, fixed and written up himself.

Three outcomes are possible at a review like that, and all three are good ones if they are chosen:

1. **The goal is met; end, or set a new goal.** Diego chose a new one: leading a design review for
   somebody else's feature.
2. **The goal is not met, and the plan is wrong**; change the activities.
3. **The relationship is not the right one**: a different mentor would serve better, or none is
   needed. Saying so is a kindness, not a failure.

## Handing on

As Diego's new goal was about leading reviews, Lívia suggested that Bruna, who reviews more of the
team's code than anybody, become his mentor for the next phase. Lívia stayed in a lighter role:
**sponsor rather than mentor**, putting his name forward when design reviews needed a second
reviewer.

**Mentoring ends; sponsorship can last a career**, and what it costs the
sponsor is a little of their own standing, staked each time they say a name in a room.

## What the mentor gets

Mentoring is usually described as a gift from the senior person to the junior, and it is also
work the senior person benefits from. Lívia learned how checkout's newest engineers see the system,
which was not how she saw it; she found two places where Marola's onboarding assumed knowledge
nobody gives a new hire; and she practised, every two weeks, the listening from lesson 6 and the
patience coaching takes. **A senior engineer who is not mentoring anybody is losing a view of their own
organisation they cannot get any other way.**
