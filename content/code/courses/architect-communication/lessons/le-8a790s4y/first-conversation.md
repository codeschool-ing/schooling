**The first mentoring conversation is not about advice. It is about finding out what the other
person wants, where they actually are, and what they could do next, in their words.** Advice given
before that is advice to an imagined person, and it is usually the advice the mentor needed at the
same age.

## GROW

The structure most coaches use for a conversation like this is **GROW**, popularised by John
Whitmore in *Coaching for Performance*: Goal, Reality, Options, Will. It is not a script; it is an
order that keeps the conversation from jumping to solutions.

**Goal: what do you want?** Lívia asked Diego where he wanted to be in a year. His first answer was
"senior", which is a title, not a goal. The follow-up was lesson 6's question in another form: "What
would you be doing then that you don't do now?" The second answer was a goal: "Taking a feature from
an idea to production myself, without Bruna having to check every step."

**Reality: where are you now?** Facts, not judgements. Diego writes good code for tasks somebody else
has broken down. He has never written a design document, never led a review of somebody else's
work, and he waits until he is stuck for a day before asking for help. That last one came from Diego
himself, after Lívia asked "What happens when you get stuck?", and it was the most useful sentence of
the hour.

**Options: what could you do?** Diego listed them, with Lívia adding one or two only after he
stopped: write the design document for his next feature; review two pull requests a week from other
people; pair with Bruna on breaking a feature down; set himself a rule to ask after two hours stuck,
not a day.

**Will: what will you do, and when?** One or two things, with dates, chosen by Diego. He chose the
design document for the delivery-slot change, by the end of the month, and the two-hour rule, starting
the next day.

## The mentor talks less than half the time

In a good GROW conversation the person being mentored does most of the talking, and the mentor's job
is mostly questions and summaries (lesson 6's paraphrase). Lívia's rough check is that if she has
spoken for more than a third of the hour, she has been mentoring when she meant to coach.

## What to agree at the end

Before leaving, agree the practical shape of the relationship:

- **how often**: every two weeks, thirty minutes, on Diego's calendar, which he books;
- **whose agenda**: his. He brings what he wants to talk about; Lívia brings nothing unless asked;
- **what is confidential**: what Diego says stays between them, unless it is something Bruna must
  know for safety or legal reasons, and Lívia said that exception out loud;
- **how it ends**: a review in three months, where either side can end it or change it.

The agenda point matters more than it seems. **A mentoring relationship where the mentor sets the
agenda becomes a second line manager**, and Diego already has one.
