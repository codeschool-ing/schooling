Twenty-one lessons ago, business intelligence was defined as turning the records a company already
keeps into answers to the decisions it has to make, the same way every time. **Everything since has
been one of a few habits applied to that sentence**, and they fit on a page.

| the habit | what it sounds like | where it was learnt |
|---|---|---|
| the question first | "what will you decide with this?" | lessons 1, 2 and 4 |
| the definition, written down | "which sales, counted when, and who owns the formula?" | lessons 1 and 10 |
| the denominator | "per what? and did the denominator move?" | lessons 2, 12, 17 and 19 |
| the comparison | "against last year, the target, the stores that were open both years?" | lessons 6, 15 and 18 |
| the right kind of analysis | "what happened, why, what next, what to do?" | lessons 6 to 9 |
| few indicators, each with a guard | "which five, and what stops each one being gamed?" | lesson 11 |
| the person who reads it | "who decides, and what do they need to see?" | lesson 13 |
| the level of the decision | "today, this month or this year?" | lessons 14 to 16 |
| the four questions of an industry | "decisions, indicators, data, trap" | lesson 17, used in 18 to 21 |
| a number that can be produced again | "which snapshot, which version, who approved?" | this lesson |

Each habit answers one of the ways a number goes wrong without anything on the screen saying so,
which was the warning at the end of lesson 1. None of them needs a tool, and every one of them
survives a change of tool.

## What one habit looks like at work

Take Lívia's first year at Varanda as the test. The Monday sales figure became one written
definition computed in one place. Helena's question about enlarging Contagem was answered with sales
per m² and like-for-like growth, not with total sales. The spring campaign was measured against a
holdout, and the next one was cheaper. The garden buyer opens a page that leads with the empty shelf.
**None of those needed anything beyond a spreadsheet and the habits in the table**, and all of them
changed a decision.

## What the rest of the track adds

This course used a spreadsheet and a handful of functions, on purpose. The other courses of the `bi`
track give the same habits better tools and deeper methods:

- `excel-analytics` is the spreadsheet in earnest: lookups, `SUMIFS`, pivot tables and dashboards
  built in Excel;
- `statistics` is the mathematics this course pointed at and did not teach: mean and median
  properly, variation, sampling, correlation and regression, and when a difference is real;
- `sql-databases` and `data-cleaning` are how the records are reached and made fit to use, at sizes
  no spreadsheet holds;
- `visualization` and `data-storytelling` are the answer shown well, and to the right person;
- `analytics-bi`, `warehouse-modeling`, `pipelines-etl` and `bi-techniques` are the machinery that
  computes a definition in one place for everybody, every day;
- `data-governance` is the law, quality, lineage and audit this lesson touched, done properly;
- `portfolio-project` and `first-job` turn the work into evidence that somebody will hire you for.

The tools in those courses will change several times during a career. The question at the top of
the table will not: **before any of them, ask what somebody will decide with the answer.**
