After lesson 11, Caio asked Lívia for a delivery dashboard: the five indicators, refreshed every
day, for everybody who deals with deliveries. The obvious way to start is to ask Caio what he wants
and build it. **That treats the person who asked as the only person who matters, and on a project
like this one he is one of about ten.** Everybody whose decisions, work or pay the dashboard touches
is a stakeholder, and the ones left off the list are the ones who stop a project at the end, when
changing it is most expensive.

## Four ways to have a stake

A useful list asks four questions about the project, and each one finds different people.

**Who decides?** The people who approve, pay for or sign off the work. At Varanda that is Caio, who
asked for it and owns the KPI, and Helena, who has to agree to the hours Lívia and Tiago will spend.

**Who influences?** People who cannot stop the project on their own but whose opinion the deciders
listen to. Otávio is one: the carriers' contracts are his, and part of what the dashboard will show
is whether they earn their price.

**Who is affected?** The people whose work changes because the dashboard exists. Marcos, who
coordinates deliveries at the warehouse, will open it every morning. The carriers will have their
performance shown to people who renegotiate their contracts.

**Who provides the data?** Tiago builds the pipelines that copy orders and delivery records out of
the ERP and the carriers' files. Without him there is no dashboard, and he is the stakeholder most
often forgotten, because he is not a user and not a decider. **A project that discovers in its last
week that the carriers' file arrives only on Fridays was planned without its data stakeholder.**

## The one nobody listed

Varanda's store managers were not on Lívia's first list. The dashboard is about home deliveries
from the warehouse, and most of them never touch a van. Then Lívia read the 2026 bonus plan: **a
part of each store manager's bonus depends on the on-time rate of the deliveries their store
sells.** From that day the delivery KPI was no longer something they read; it was their pay.

That changes what the project has to get right. Bruno Teixeira, at Contagem, sells most of the
furniture, and lesson 12 showed what a bare total does to a store with that mix: Contagem was better
than Savassi at both kinds of delivery and seven points worse overall. A dashboard that ranks the
stores on the total would cut Bruno's bonus for the wrong reason, and he would be right to fight it.
**He is a stakeholder whether or not anybody invites him**, and it is cheaper to invite him at the
start than to meet him after launch.

## How to find the missing names

Three checks find most of them:

- **Follow the number.** For each indicator, ask who is judged, paid or blamed by it. That is how the
  store managers turn up.
- **Follow the data.** For each source, ask who produces it, who maintains it and who fixes it when it
  breaks. That is how Tiago, and the carriers' own systems, turn up.
- **Ask the people already on the list who else should be.** Marcos named the two drivers' team
  leaders, who know which addresses are always late and why.

The list is not a formality. The next section sorts it, and the sort decides how much of Lívia's
time each person gets.
