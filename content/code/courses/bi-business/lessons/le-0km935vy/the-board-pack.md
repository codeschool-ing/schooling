The board pack is a document of thirty pages, and the board reads one of them closely: the first. Lívia
and Otávio built it for the meeting of March 2026, which looked back at 2025. **Five numbers, five
years each, and the plan the board approved in 2022 beside every one.** Everything else in the pack is
there to answer the questions this page raises.

## The five numbers, and why these five

**Sales, in millions of reais**: R$ 72.4 million in 2021 and R$ 98.0 million in 2025, against a plan of
R$ 100 million. It is the number everybody knows, which is exactly why it cannot stand alone.

**Real growth, per cent a year**: the year's growth once price rises are taken out, using a price index
the next section explains. It fell from 4.4% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2025, against a plan of 4% a
year. This is the row that changes how the first one reads: **Varanda's sales grew every year, and in
2025 most of that growth was prices.**

**Store sales per square metre**: what each square metre of shop floor sold, in reais. Lesson 2 used
it to compare stores; here it measures whether the floor Varanda pays for is earning more over time. It
went from R$ 3,459 in 2021 to R$ 4,011 in 2023, and then stopped: R$ 4,018 in 2024 and R$ 4,019 in 2025,
against a plan of R$ 4,300. Part of the reason is in a footnote: Ipatinga, the ninth store, opened in
July 2024 and is still young, which lesson 18 takes apart.

**Online share of sales**: the online shop's sales as a share of the total. It rose from 12.6% to 15.9%
by 2024 and stayed at 15.9% in 2025, against a plan of 18%. That is the row behind the second question
on the board's table: whether to keep investing in the online shop.

**Sales per employee, in thousands of reais**: a rough measure of productivity, R$ 219 thousand in 2021
and R$ 239 thousand in 2025, against a plan of R$ 250 thousand. Rough, because a part-time employee
counts as one, and a pack that changes that definition between years has to say so.

## How to read the page

**Every row is below plan, and no row is alarming on its own.** Sales were 2.0% short of the plan; the
online share is 2.1 points short; sales per employee is 4.4% short. Each, presented alone, would be
explained in a sentence and moved past. Together they say something none of them says: the company is
growing in reais and barely in real terms, its stores are not getting more productive, and the channel
that was supposed to carry the growth has stalled. **The page is designed so that the five rows are read
together**, and that is why it is one page.

Three details make the reading possible:

- **The trend is drawn small, beside the numbers.** A sparkline per row, with the plan as a dashed line,
  shows the shape of five years in the space of a word. The numbers stay, because a board member who
  doubts the shape wants the value.
- **The plan is the one approved in 2022, not a newer one.** It would be easy to show the plan as it was revised
  last year, which the company was closer to. The board approved the 2022 plan, and judging the company
  against a target that moved towards it is how a pack slowly stops telling the board anything.
- **The footnotes are part of the page.** One says the price index is illustrative; the other says
  when the ninth store opened. Both change how a number reads, and both would be the first question at
  the meeting if they were missing.

## What is not on it

No monthly figures, no store-by-store table, no campaign results. They are in the pack's later pages,
and in Renata's and Caio's monthly reviews. **What the first page leaves out is a decision as much as
what it shows**, and the same discipline as lesson 11's: each number on it is there because a question
on the board's table depends on it. A number nobody would decide anything about, such as the number of
visits to the website, has a place in a monthly review and none here.
