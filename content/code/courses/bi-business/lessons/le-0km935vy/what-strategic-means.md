**Strategic BI serves the decisions a company takes about years: where to grow, what to stop, how
much to invest and in what.** They are taken by the owners and the board, a few times a year, and the
screen that serves them is the opposite of Marcos's board in lesson 14: few numbers, long series, and
a good deal of the world outside the company. This lesson is the last of the three levels.

## The decisions, and who takes them

At Varanda the strategic decisions sit with Helena Prado, the CEO, and the board, which meets every
quarter. The ones on the table for 2026 give the flavour:

- whether to open a tenth store, and whether it should be the first outside Minas Gerais;
- whether to keep investing in the online shop, whose share of sales has not moved since 2024;
- whether the warehouse in Contagem can serve both channels for five more years or needs to grow;
- whether to close a store whose sales per square metre have fallen behind the others for years.

They share the shape lesson 9 gave the decisions that should never be automated: **rare, expensive
and hard to reverse.** A store, once opened, is a lease of ten years and a team of forty people.
Closing one costs the same team and a reputation in a town. Nobody takes these decisions on one month's
numbers, and nobody should be shown one month's numbers when they do.

## The wrong picture: everything, in more detail

The common mistake is to treat the board as the people who deserve the most information, and give
them every department's dashboard on one page. **The board needs less, over longer.** Marcos needs
every route now; Renata needs every channel this month; the board needs five numbers over five years,
because the questions above are about direction, and direction only shows in a long series.

A number in a strategic pack therefore has three properties:

- **It is annual, closed and checked.** The board decides on the year's figures as finance closed them
  and the auditors reviewed them, not on a monthly estimate. A strategic number that is later restated
  costs the board's trust in every number next to it.
- **It is compared over years, and against the plan.** The plan the board approved is the strategic
  version of Renata's budget: it says what the company meant to become, and the pack says how far it
  got.
- **It is put next to the world outside.** A company growing 6% a year while prices rise 4% and its
  market grows 8% is losing ground three ways at once, and nothing inside its own records says so.

## The outside

That last property is what most separates strategic BI from the other two levels. Operational and
tactical BI read the company's own records. **Strategic BI also reads other people's.**

- **Prices.** A real of 2025 buys less than a real of 2021, so sales in reais of each year overstate
  growth. Brazil's official consumer price index is the IPCA, published by IBGE, and a pack that shows
  growth without saying how much of it was prices is answering a question nobody asked. The next
  section but one does that sum.
- **The market.** Whether Varanda grew faster or slower than home-and-garden retail in Minas Gerais
  changes the meaning of its growth. IBGE publishes a monthly survey of retail trade, and trade
  associations publish their own figures; neither breaks the market down the way Varanda would like,
  and the pack has to say which one it used.
- **Competitors.** A listed competitor publishes results every quarter. A private one publishes
  nothing, and what is known about it comes from the store managers who walk past its windows.

Each of these is data the company does not own and cannot define. **It arrives late, in someone
else's categories, and sometimes revised**, and the analyst's job is to say so on the page rather than
to make it look as solid as the company's own sales.

## How often, and who prepares it

The board meets every quarter, and between meetings nothing in its pack changes much: that is the point
of a strategic number. Otávio and Lívia prepare the pack together. Otávio owns the financial figures
and the plan; Lívia owns the definitions, the long series and the external data, and she makes sure
that a number defined one way in 2021 is still defined the same way in 2025. A series whose definition
changed in the middle is two series with one line drawn through them.
