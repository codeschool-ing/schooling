On 5 December, the morning Otávio closed November, Renata Sá met her team to review the month: the
paid-media analyst, the email lead, Lívia, and Otávio himself for the budget. The screen on the wall
was the one Lívia had built with Renata in October. **Every number on it sits beside the number it is
judged against**, and nothing on it is there to be admired.

## The top row: three numbers, each with its comparison

**Online sales of R$ 2,060 thousand**, 8.4% above budget and 24.1% above November 2024. The asterisk
leads to the footnote, which says the yearly campaign moved from late October into November, and that
October and November together grew 3.6%. Without that line, the 24.1% would have opened the meeting as
the headline. With it, the room knows the honest growth figure before anybody quotes the flattering
one.

**Orders: 5,150**, 7.3% above the target of 4,800. Sales above budget and orders above target are
consistent with each other, which is worth checking: if sales had beaten the budget on fewer orders,
the story would be about prices or the size of a basket, not about the campaign.

**Media spend: R$ 189 thousand** against a budget of R$ 170 thousand, 11.2% over. This tile is the one
outlined in red, because it is the one off plan in the wrong direction. Spending more than planned is
not a sin when it buys more than planned, and the table underneath is where the room finds out whether
it did.

## The table: what each channel bought

The rows are the online shop's traffic channels, and the column that matters is the last: **cost per
order against its target**, as a bar with a white mark where the target sits.

| channel | spend | orders | cost per order | target | conversion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paid search | R$ 96 thousand | 1,860 | R$ 51.6 | R$ 55 | 1.58% |
| Social ads | R$ 84 thousand | 1,090 | R$ 77.1 | R$ 65 | 0.77% |
| Email | R$ 9 thousand | 930 | R$ 9.7 | R$ 12 | 2.02% |
| Organic and direct | no media | 1,270 | — | — | 0.76% |

Paid search came in 6.2% under its target and email well under. **Social ads cost R$ 77.1 an order
against a target of R$ 65, 18.6% over**, and their visits converted at 0.77%, half the rate of paid
search. Social ads brought the most visits of any paid channel, 142,000 of the month's 474,000, and the
fewest orders. Lesson 10 warned about exactly this shape: a channel can raise visits and leave sales
where they were, because the visitors it brings are less ready to buy.

## Why cost per order, and why not one cost per order

The agency's own report for social ads leads with visits and cost per visit, and by that measure the
channel had its best month of the year. **A visit is what the agency sells; an order is what Varanda
wants.** The review measures channels by the thing the business is paying for, which is lesson 12's
test for a vanity metric turned into a column.

There is a second trap in the same column, and it is a hidden denominator. Divide all of November's
media spend by all of November's orders and the cost per order is **R$ 36.7**, comfortably under every
target. Divide it by the orders that came through paid channels — search, social and email — and it is
**R$ 48.7**. The first figure counts 1,270 organic and direct orders that no media paid for, and it
flatters the media by the size of them. The review shows each channel on its own, and when it needs a
blended figure it uses the second.

## What is not on the screen

No daily chart of visits. No list of every ad and its clicks. Both exist, inside the ad platforms and
in Lívia's working files, and the paid-media analyst opens them after the meeting to find out which ad
sets dragged social ads over target. **A tactical screen carries the questions the meeting has to
answer, and the detail lives one step behind it.** Putting it all on the wall turns a review into a
reading, which the last section of this lesson is about.

The same idea, at a different level, is `data-storytelling` lesson 7: a screen designed for the
thirty seconds before somebody asks the next question. The craft of laying out the grid is
`visualization` lesson 18, and building this kind of report in a spreadsheet, with slicers and a
navigable layout, is `excel-analytics` lesson 17.
