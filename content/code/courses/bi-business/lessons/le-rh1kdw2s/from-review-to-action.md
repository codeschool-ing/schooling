The worst monthly review is easy to recognise. Somebody shares the screen and reads each number
aloud — sales up, orders up, spend over, social ads expensive — and everyone nods. The meeting ends on
time, and next month the same screen is read aloud with different numbers. **A review that only reads
the numbers decides nothing**, and a tactical screen exists for one reason: to change what the company
does next month.

## The shape of a review that decides

Renata's review runs in a fixed order, and the order is the point.

1. **Last month's actions, first.** Each one: was it done, and did the number it was meant to move
   move? A review that opens with the new month's numbers lets last month's promises quietly expire.
2. **Three findings, at most.** Each says what the number shows, why if it is known, and what follows.
   Three is a working limit rather than a law, the same kind lesson 11 gave for indicators: a fourth
   finding gets less attention than the first three, and a tenth gets none.
3. **The action list.** Each action has an owner, a date and the number that will show whether it
   worked. **An action with no owner belongs to nobody, and one with no date is never late**, so it
   is never done either.

## November's three findings

Lívia drafts the findings with Renata the day before, from the screen in this lesson's second
section and the variance table of the third. In the meeting they become this:

**Social ads bought orders at R$ 77.1 against a target of R$ 65**, with the most visits of any paid
channel and the lowest conversion. The paid-media analyst found two of the five ad sets responsible for
most of the gap, both aimed at a broad audience that clicked and did not buy.

**November's +24.1% on last year is the campaign that moved.** October and November together grew
3.6%, and that is the figure for any conversation about growth. The 2026 budget still spreads the
campaign's sales the 2024 way, across October.

**Content production is under budget by timing, not saving.** December will carry the R$ 8 thousand
back. Nothing needs deciding, but the December review has to expect it.

## The action list

| action | owner | by | how we will know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop the two weakest social ad sets; test R$ 15 thousand of December's social budget in paid search | paid-media analyst | 10 Dec | December's cost per order, social and search |
| Show October and November together in every growth comparison until the 2026 calendar settles | Lívia | 15 Dec | the December pack carries the two-month line |
| Move the campaign's sales in the 2026 budget from October to November | Otávio, with Renata | 19 Dec | the 2026 budget by month |
| Expect R$ 8 thousand over on content in December | Otávio | — | December's variance, marked as timing |

Two details are deliberate. The first action is a **test**, not a transfer. Paid search delivered
orders at R$ 51.6 in November, but that is the average over the money already spent, and the next
R$ 15 thousand will buy the searches that were not worth buying before. Nobody knows what those orders
will cost, so the action is phrased as something to measure. And the last row has no date because it is
not a decision; it is written down so that December's review does not discover it as a surprise.

## The next review opens here

On 8 January, Renata's December review starts with this table. The analyst reports that the two ad sets
were stopped on the 9th and what December's cost per order was in each channel; Lívia shows the
two-month line; Otávio shows the re-phased budget. **A finding becomes an action, and an action becomes
the first item of the next review.** That loop is what makes a monthly screen tactical rather than
decorative, and it is the BI cycle of lesson 1 turning once a month.

Lívia's own part in it is easy to underrate. She prepares the screen before the closed numbers reach
the meeting, drafts the findings, and keeps the action list from one month to the next. Turning a
finding into a recommendation somebody can act on is `data-storytelling` lesson 9, and keeping the
people around the table with you while the numbers say uncomfortable things is its lesson 13. Lesson 16
moves up the last level, to the board, which reads its screen once a quarter and decides about years.
