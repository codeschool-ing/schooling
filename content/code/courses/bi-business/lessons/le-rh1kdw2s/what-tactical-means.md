title: What tactical means
version: 1

**Tactical BI serves the decisions a company takes over weeks and months, by managers who move
budget, people and stock.** Its question is "are we on plan, and where do we move resources next
month?", and its screen puts each number beside the number it is judged against. Lesson 14 was the
person on the floor deciding in minutes; this lesson is the manager deciding in a meeting, with
days to think and a month of data to think about.

## The decisions, and who takes them

At Varanda, the tactical decisions belong to the directors and their teams, and each department
holds a review at the start of the month:

| who | what they move | the question at the review |
|---|---|---|
| Renata Sá, marketing | media spend between channels; next month's campaigns | which channels bought orders at the planned cost? |
| Caio Barreto, operations | staff rosters per store; stock between stores and the warehouse | where did we run short, and where did we carry too much? |
| Otávio Lins, finance | costs against budget; cash for the coming weeks | which lines are off budget, and is it timing or real? |
| Sônia Freitas, people | hiring; overtime; holidays | where are we under-staffed for next month's demand? |

These decisions are fewer and bigger than Marcos's. Moving R$ 15 thousand of media from one channel
to another is reversible, but only a month later, and nobody wants to find out after four weeks that
it was a mistake. **That is why a tactical screen is read in a meeting, not at a glance**: the people
around the table have time to ask why, and the screen has to have the answer ready.

## The wrong picture: the month's total is the answer

A tactical screen that shows "online sales in November: R$ 2,060 thousand" has said nothing. Is that
good? It depends on what was expected. **Every tactical number needs two comparisons, and each one
catches what the other misses.**

- **Against the budget or the target**, which is what the company planned. November's online sales
  were 8.4% above the R$ 1,900 thousand in the budget. That says whether the plan held, and it is
  only as good as the plan.
- **Against the same month last year**, which carries the season. November 2025 was 24.1% above
  November 2024. That says how the business moved, and it is only fair if the two months are alike.

This November they were not. Lesson 7 found that the online shop's yearly campaign ran in late
October in 2024 and in early November in 2025, so November 2025 carries a campaign that November 2024
did not. Taken together, October and November grew 3.6%. **A comparison with last year has to be read
with the calendar beside it**, and the review screen in the next section says so in a footnote.

A third comparison is the previous month. It is the least useful of the three for a retailer, because
a December always beats a November and nobody learns anything from it. Lesson 6 made the same point
with month-on-month and year-on-year growth.

## The closed month

Operational data is minutes old. Tactical data waits for the month to close. Returns arrive after a
sale, an order is cancelled a week later, a supplier's invoice for November lands on 3 December.
Otávio closes Varanda's books on the fifth working day of the next month, and **the monthly review
uses the closed figures, the same ones finance reports**.

That is a decision about definitions as much as timing. On 1 December, marketing's count of online
sales for November included orders that were cancelled before delivery; finance's did not. If Renata
reviewed her month on her own count, her November would be bigger than the one in the company's
accounts, and the meeting would spend its first twenty minutes on why. Lesson 1's two directors with
two totals is the same problem at the monthly scale, and `analytics-bi` lesson 2 is about keeping one
definition across departments.

## Inside the month

A month is a long time to wait for bad news. So tactical BI often adds a weekly check: the month so
far against the part of the target that should have been reached by now. **The catch is that the
target does not arrive evenly.** At Varanda, November's campaign ran from the 3rd to the 13th, so a large part of the
month's online orders was always going to arrive in its first half. A check that compared the
15th against half of the target would have reported a triumph in the second week and a collapse in
the fourth, with nothing changing in between. The part of the target expected by a given day has to
follow the shape of the month, which usually means last year's daily pattern with the campaign days
moved to where they fall this year.
