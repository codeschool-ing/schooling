The usual picture of a decision made without data is a careless one: somebody who did not bother to
look. Most of them are not like that. **They are made by experienced people, from what they have
seen, and what they have seen is a biased sample of what happened.** The problem is not effort or
intelligence. It is that memory and a company's records keep different things.

## Helena's Saturday

In February 2026 Helena Prado, Varanda's CEO, proposed enlarging the Contagem store. Her reason was
one she had watched with her own eyes: she visits a store most Saturdays, Contagem is the one she
visits most, and on Saturday afternoon its car park is full and the tills have queues. It is also, as
everybody at Varanda knows, the store that sells the most.

Both things are true. Neither answers the question she was asking. A full car park on Saturday says
how busy Contagem is at its busiest hour, not how much it sells for the space it has; and the store
that sells the most may simply be the largest. This lesson follows the afternoon Lívia spent finding
out which, and the section on the store ranking has the numbers.

## Three ways experience misleads

**Memory keeps the vivid, not the frequent.** A customer who shouted at a till in Betim is
remembered for a year. The thousands who paid without a word are not remembered at all, so a
director asked "how are the Betim tills?" answers from one afternoon. Caio Barreto, the operations
director, was sure deliveries to Juiz de Fora were always late because the two worst complaints of
2025 came from there. The delivery records said 6.8% of that town's deliveries arrived late, against
6.5% across every route.

**The loudest source wins.** Store managers who phone the head office every week get their problems
fixed first, whatever the size of the problem. A sales director who visits the large stores hears the
large stores. The quiet store with a slow decline is the one nobody visits.

**The last month stands in for the year.** A good November makes everybody optimistic about the
online shop; a weak October makes the same people worried. One month is a small sample of a year
with a Christmas in it, and lesson 6 shows how far one month can sit from the trend.

## The HiPPO

Analysts have a name for the most common way a meeting decides: **the HiPPO, the highest-paid
person's opinion.** The joke is not that senior people are wrong. They are right often, which is
how they became senior. The trouble is that their view ends the discussion before anybody looks, and
it carries the same weight on the days when it is a guess.

Varanda's CEO is not a villain in this lesson. Helena asked for the analysis herself, and she
changed her mind when she saw it. **The point of BI here is not to overrule the experienced
person; it is to give that person the part of the picture memory cannot hold** — every store, every
week, counted the same way.

## Why the numbers are not the enemy of experience

Experience tells you which questions matter, where the data is likely to be wrong and what a number
leaves out. Lívia knows none of that in her first month, and the best thing she can do is ask the
people who do. Data tells you how often something happens and how big it is, which no amount of
walking around a store can give you. **The decisions that go wrong are the ones that use only one of
the two**, and the next section is about when the second one is worth fetching at all.
