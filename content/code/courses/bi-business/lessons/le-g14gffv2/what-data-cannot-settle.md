title: What the data cannot settle
version: 1
---

The opposite mistake to deciding by anecdote is believing that a good enough table decides on its
own. **It does not, and pretending it does is how a company ends up doing what is easy to measure
instead of what is right.** The store ranking is a good example, because it is correct, useful and
still leaves the decision to Helena.

## Data-informed, not data-driven

"Data-driven" is the phrase on every job advert. Taken literally it means the numbers decide and
people carry out the result. **Data-informed is the more honest description of what works: the
numbers are one input, weighed by somebody who is accountable for the choice.** The difference shows
up as soon as the data runs out, and in the store decision it runs out in four places.

## The average metre is not the next metre

Sales per square metre is an average over the whole store. Enlarging a store adds metres at the
edge — a new wing, an extra floor — and those sell what the extra space attracts, not what the best
corner by the entrance sells. **Nova Lima sells R$ 6,300 per square metre on 1,500 of them; nothing
in the table says what metre 1,501 would sell.** It might be close to that, if customers are being
turned away for lack of space. It might be far below it, if Nova Lima is small because the town is.
The table measures what happened, and the question is about something that has not.

## New things have no history

Varanda has never enlarged a store, so there is no record of what an enlargement did to sales.
Every number Lívia can show is about stores as they are. When a decision is about something new — a
first store in São Paulo, a new product line, a first enlargement — the data covers the neighbourhood
of the decision and not the decision itself.
A forecast built on it is a guess with arithmetic around it. Lesson 8 is about forecasts and where they fail.

## The easy measure and the right one

Sales per square metre was chosen because Varanda records both numbers. **What Helena actually cares
about is profit, and the table does not have it.** Savassi is in one of Belo Horizonte's most expensive
neighbourhoods and pays the highest rent per square metre in the chain. The store with the best sales
per metre may not be the one with the best profit per metre. Lívia asked Otávio Lins, the CFO,
for each store's rent; until it arrives, her note says the ranking is by sales and that profit could
reorder it.

## The stores that are not in the table

The table has nine stores because Varanda has nine stores today. In 2021 it closed a 3,000-square-metre
store in Venda Nova that never covered its costs, and **a closed store leaves no row in this year's
data.** Look only at the survivors and large stores seem to do fine; look at every store Varanda has
ever opened, and one of the largest is the one it had to shut. This is survivorship:
the data only contains what lasted long enough to be recorded.

## What is left for judgement

Helena still has to weigh things no table holds: whether Savassi's landlord would allow building
work, whether Bruno Teixeira, who manages Contagem, would read a project elsewhere as a verdict on
his store, what the board believes about the economy in 2026. **BI narrows the argument and makes it honest; it does
not end it.** A good analyst says where the data stops as plainly as what it shows, and Lívia's note
had a line for each of the four limits above.
