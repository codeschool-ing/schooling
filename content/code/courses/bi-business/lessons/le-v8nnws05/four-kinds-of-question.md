title: Four kinds of question
version: 1

Every question a company asks of its data falls into one of four kinds, and the kind decides the
method, the effort and what the answer can be trusted for. **Descriptive analysis asks what happened.
Diagnostic asks why. Predictive asks what is likely to happen next. Prescriptive asks what to do
about it.** This lesson is the first kind, and lessons 7, 8 and 9 take the other three in order.

The four names are usually credited to the research firm Gartner, which drew them as a staircase:
each step worth more than the one below and harder to climb. The staircase carries a wrong idea with
it, and it is worth naming before the right one.

## The wrong idea: descriptive is the step you leave behind

Drawn as a staircase, descriptive analysis looks like the ground floor, the thing a company does
until it is mature enough to predict. **It is the opposite: every other kind stands on it.** A
diagnosis explains a number somebody first described. A forecast extends a series somebody first
counted correctly. A recommendation is judged by what happened afterwards, which is a description
again. A company whose description is wrong gets the other three wrong with it, and more
expensively, because a forecast built on a miscounted year looks more authoritative than the miscount
did.

Most of the questions a company asks every Monday are descriptive: what sold, where, against what.
A BI analyst who spends most of the week on them is not working at the bottom of a staircase. They
are doing the part everything else leans on.

## One question of each kind, at Varanda

Lívia started at Varanda Casa & Jardim in January 2026, with 2025 just closed. Each kind of question
was on her desk in her first month:

| kind | the question | what answers it |
|---|---|---|
| descriptive | how much did we sell in 2025, and against 2024? | counting and comparing: this lesson |
| diagnostic | why did October fall when no other month did? | breaking the number apart and testing causes: lesson 7 |
| predictive | how much will we sell from January to March? | a history and a method, checked against the past: lesson 8 |
| prescriptive | when should the warehouse reorder garden-hose reels? | options, constraints and a rule: lesson 9 |

**Two of the four look back, one looks forward and one decides.** Descriptive and diagnostic both
work on 2025, which is finished: the data is complete and the answer can be checked. Predictive works
on 2026, which has not happened, so its answer is a range and it can only be checked later.
Prescriptive sits at today: it takes a forecast and turns it into an action, and the action changes
the future the forecast was about.

## What grows from one kind to the next

**Value grows, because each kind is closer to a decision.** "Sales rose 5.7%" informs; "reorder
hose reels when the warehouse is down to 198" acts. **Difficulty grows too, and so does the room for being
wrong without noticing.** A total can be checked against the accounts. A cause can only be made more
or less likely. A forecast is wrong by some amount every month. A recommendation that was followed
cannot be compared with the one that was not.

That is why the four are taught in this order. Each needs the one before it to be right, and each
asks for a little more honesty about how sure the answer is.
