---
title: The descriptive report
version: 1
---

A descriptive report is the most common thing a BI analyst writes, and the easiest to write badly.
The bad version is not wrong. It is a number with nothing around it, so the reader supplies the
period, the definition and the comparison from memory, and two readers supply different ones. **A
good descriptive report carries five things, and the fifth is a sentence.**

## The five parts

| part | what it says | at Varanda, for 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| period | exactly which days are counted | 1 January to 31 December 2025, closed on 12 January 2026 |
| definition | what is counted, and when it counts | sales net of returns, stores and online, an online order counted on the day it was paid |
| comparison | against what | the same months of 2024, counted the same way |
| source | where the numbers came from | the ERP's sales table and the online shop's orders, loaded on 12 January |
| finding | the one sentence somebody will repeat | below |

The definition row is the one Varanda learnt the hard way. In lesson 1, two directors brought two
totals for the same week to one meeting, because one counted online orders when placed and the other
when paid. **A report that states its definition cannot start that argument**, and one that leaves
it out can start it every week.

The source row looks like bureaucracy and is the first thing anybody checks when a number surprises
them. "The ERP, loaded on 12 January" tells Otávio Lins, the CFO, which figure to compare it with in
the accounts and whether late returns could still move it.

## The finding

Somebody will read the report and repeat one sentence of it in a meeting. **If the analyst does not
write that sentence, the reader writes their own**, usually from the biggest number on the page.
Lívia's for 2025:

> Varanda sold R$ 98.0 million in 2025, 5.7% more than in 2024. Every month grew against the same
> month of 2024 except October, which fell 0.7%; the last quarter was 30.9% of the year, the same
> share as in 2024.

Read it against the five parts. It names the period and the comparison. Its numbers are the ones
the sheet in this lesson computed. It is specific about the one month that broke the pattern.

## What the report does not say

**It does not say why October fell.** Lívia had a suspicion, and so did everybody else at Varanda:
the weather, a competitor's sale, a problem at the online shop. None of it was checked when the
report went out. A descriptive report that adds "probably because of the rain" has done two things
at once, and the second one was not done properly. The reader cannot tell which half of the sentence
was counted and which was guessed, and the guess travels with the authority of the count.

So October goes into the report as a fact with no explanation, and onto Lívia's list as a question:
**why did October fall when no other month did?** Answering it is a different kind of work, with a
method of its own, and it is lesson 7.

## Two versions of the same month

The difference shows best side by side:

| | what it says |
|---|---|
| weak | "October was weak, probably the weather. Sales are up overall." |
| descriptive | "October 2025: R$ 7.96 million, 0.7% below October 2024 and the only month of the year below its 2024 value. Cause not yet investigated." |

The weak version is shorter and sounds more confident. **The descriptive version is the one a
director can act on**, because it says what is known, how it was measured and what is still open.
