title: Backpressure, saying so upstream

Every stage of a system has a queue in front of it, whether or not anybody wrote one: the kernel's
backlog of connections waiting to be accepted, nginx's connections to the box office, the threads
waiting for the CPU, a message queue between two services. **Backpressure** is what happens when a
full queue makes the stage before it slow down, instead of letting the work pile up.

It takes a few forms, and the box office has met most of them:

- **A bounded queue that blocks.** A producer that finds the queue full waits. The waiting travels
  back one stage at a time, until it reaches something that can slow down, usually a person. The
  box office's listen backlog is one: `request_queue_size = 128`, past which the kernel lets new
  connections wait or drops them.
- **A refusal that says when to come back.** The `429` and `503` of the last two sections, with
  `Retry-After`. It only works if the caller honours it.
- **A closed loop.** `load.py`'s workers wait for each answer before asking again, so a slower box
  office automatically receives fewer requests a second. Section 02 is that: the rate stayed near 105
  a second however many workers there were, because they could ask no faster than they were
  answered.
- **Flow control in the protocol.** TCP's receive window tells the sender how much more it may send;
  HTTP/2 does the same per stream.

**The opposite of backpressure is an unbounded queue**, and it is the most common way systems fall
over. A queue with no limit accepts everything, so nothing upstream learns that anything is wrong.
The latency grows with the queue, memory grows with it, and by the time somebody notices, the queue
holds minutes of work that every caller has already given up on, and that the system will still
dutifully do. Every request in such a queue is a request somebody is no longer waiting for.

## The caller's half

`load.py` ignores `Retry-After`: a refused worker asks again at once, which is why the refusals of
section 05 did not lower the load. A browser or an app does the same if nobody wrote it otherwise,
and a few thousand of them refreshing an on-sale page are the bot traffic of section 04 with better
intentions. **The refusal is half of backpressure; the caller that waits is the other half**, and
the caller that waits sensibly, with backoff and jitter, is lesson 10.
