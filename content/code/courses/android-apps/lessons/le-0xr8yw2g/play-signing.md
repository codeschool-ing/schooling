title: Two keys on Google Play, one key everywhere else

**On Google Play the key you made is not the one phones see.** Google's documentation on app signing describes the arrangement it calls Play App Signing. You sign the bundle with your **upload key**, and Google Play checks that signature to know the upload came from you. Then it builds the APKs and signs them with the **app signing key**, which Google generates or receives from you and keeps in its own key management service. Configuring it is required to distribute through Google Play, according to the
same page, except for apps created before August 2021.

The split exists for the worst day. **If you lose your upload key, or somebody else obtains it, you
can request an upload key reset in the Play Console**, and because the app signing key never left
Google, the next update still installs over the old version on every phone. The documentation states
the comparison as well: for an app not using Play App Signing, losing the signing key means losing
the ability to update the app.

## The `direct` flavour has no such safety net

The `direct` APK of section 03 is installed from a file, and the key it is signed with is the only
thing every future update will be checked against. **For that flavour your key is the app.** Lose it,
and the planner on every student's phone can never be updated; the only way forward is a new app,
under a new package name, that each student has to find and install while the old one sits there
untouched. Keeping a copy of the keystore and its password away from the computer, which section 04
asked for, is the whole defence.

## What the store sees, and what it asks for

Google Play also uses the certificate fingerprints: the app signing key's SHA-256, shown in the Play
Console, is the fingerprint a service such as a sign-in provider or a maps API asks for to restrict
an API key to your app. The upload key's fingerprint, the one `keytool` printed in section 04, is the
one you give Google when you register the upload key.
