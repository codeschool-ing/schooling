first time `setContent` runs its lambda, Compose records each composable call as a node, with the
parameters it received; that record is the **composition**. The calls in `PlannerCardPreview` give
this one:

Two things in that picture are worth fixing in mind before lesson 5 builds on them.

**The tree mirrors the calls, not the files.** `CourseRow` appears twice because it was called twice,
with different parameters each time; each call is its own node with its own position in the tree.
Lesson 4's `remember` stores a value in a node, which is why two `CourseRow`s each get their own
remembered values even though they are the same function.

**Only a few nodes draw.** `PlannerCard`, `CourseRow` and the `Column`s draw nothing themselves;
they arrange and pass data down. The pixels come from the `Text` nodes at the bottom and from the
`Card`, which paints its background. Compose measures and places the tree in a second pass, called **layout**, and
paints it in a third, **drawing**; lesson 6 is about the middle one.

## Composition, then recomposition

When a value a composable read changes, Compose does not rebuild the tree from the top. It calls
again only the composables that read that value, compares what they emit with what is in the tree,
and changes the nodes that differ. That is **recomposition**, and it is the subject of lesson 5. What
this lesson needs from it is one fact, which section 07 measures: **a composable runs many times**,
once at first and again every time something it reads changes. Every rule about what a composable
may do follows from that fact.
