## The parts

- **The box** runs from Q1 to Q3. It holds the middle half of the data, and its length is the IQR.
- **The line inside the box** is the median.
- **The whiskers** run from the box out to the most extreme values that lie within **1.5 IQR** of it. Here the left whisker reaches the fastest delivery, 27.5, because it is well within range; the right whisker stops at 52.5.
- **The points beyond the whiskers** are values more than 1.5 IQR from the box. Here there is one, the 61-minute delivery, beyond the upper fence at 55.44 minutes.

The fences themselves are usually not drawn. They are the rule that decides where a whisker stops.

## What the shape says

A boxplot answers three questions at a glance.

**Where is the centre?** The median line.

**How spread out is the middle?** The length of the box.

**Is the data symmetric?** Look at where the median sits inside the box and at the two whiskers. A median off-centre, or one whisker much longer than the other, means a tail on that side.

## What it hides

A boxplot shows five numbers and the stragglers, and nothing else. Two peaks inside the box are invisible, and so is a gap in the middle of the data. Lesson 7 shows a bimodal distribution whose boxplot looks perfectly ordinary. When there is room, draw the histogram too; when there is not, remember that the boxplot summarises the shape, and a summary can hide some of it.

The separate points are also not a verdict. The 1.5 IQR rule flags values worth looking at. It does not say they are errors, and in a sample of 400 skewed baskets it flags seventeen real orders. Lesson 9 is about telling the two apart.
