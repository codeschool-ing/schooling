The **five-number summary** is the minimum, Q1, the median, Q3 and the maximum. Five numbers, and between them they describe the centre, the spread of the middle half, and how far the extremes reach.

For the twelve delivery times:

| minimum | Q1 | median | Q3 | maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27.5 | 32.625 | 37.25 | 41.75 | 61.0 |

For the 400 baskets, in reais:

| minimum | Q1 | median | Q3 | maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14.18 | 42.56 | 66.73 | 106.38 | 421.78 |

## Reading shape from five numbers

The gaps between the five numbers already describe the shape.

For the baskets, the gap from Q1 to the median is R$ 24 and from the median to Q3 is R$ 40. The gap from the minimum to Q1 is R$ 28 and from Q3 to the maximum is R$ 315. **Every gap on the right is wider than its partner on the left.** That is the long right tail again, read without drawing anything: the upper half of the baskets is much more spread out than the lower half.

For symmetric data the gaps would be roughly equal in pairs. For data with a tail on the left, the left-hand gaps would be the wide ones.

## Each quarter holds the same count

The five numbers cut the data into four groups of equal size, each holding a quarter of the values. A narrow gap means the quarter is crowded into a small range; a wide gap means the same number of values are spread thinly. For the baskets, a hundred orders fall between R$ 42.56 and R$ 66.73, and another hundred are spread across the R$ 315 from R$ 106.38 to the maximum.

That is the idea the boxplot draws. Each part of the picture holds the same number of values, and **its width shows how spread out they are**.
