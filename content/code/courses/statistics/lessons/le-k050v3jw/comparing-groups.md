## What the chart says without a single number

**The centres climb with distance.** Centro and Cambuí, close to the warehouse, have medians around half an hour; Taquaral is about seven minutes slower; Barão Geraldo, on the far side of the city, is slowest by a long way, at 53.5 minutes.

**The spreads differ too.** Barão Geraldo's box is the tallest, with an IQR of 9.5 minutes against Centro's 5.75. Longer routes have more to go wrong: more traffic lights, more junctions, more chances of a jam.

**Centro has one straggler**, a 45.5-minute delivery above its whisker. In Horta's records it was a fourteen-item order on a rainy evening. A boxplot cannot tell you that; it can only tell you where to look.

## Medians and boxes, not just means

A bar chart of the four mean delivery times would show the climb, and nothing else. It would hide that Barão Geraldo is not only slower but less predictable, and it would hide Centro's straggler. **When groups are compared, compare their distributions**, and the boxplot is the cheapest honest way to put four distributions side by side.

## A first look at a question for later

Is the difference between Centro and Cambuí real, or could thirty more deliveries in each reverse it? Their medians differ by under two minutes and their boxes overlap almost entirely, so the chart suggests chance could explain it. Barão Geraldo against the rest is a different matter: its box does not overlap any other. The boxplot shows these differences; lesson 16 tests them, with the analysis of variance and its rank-based cousin.
