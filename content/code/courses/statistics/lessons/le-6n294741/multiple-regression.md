Delivery time depends on more than distance. **Multiple regression** puts several predictors in one model:

**predicted minutes = a + b₁ × km + b₂ × items + b₃ × rain**

where rain is 1 for a rainy delivery and 0 for a dry one. Least squares again chooses the coefficients that make the squared residuals as small as possible.

## Horta's model

For the 120 deliveries:

| | coefficient | standard error | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| intercept | 21.25 | 0.75 | 28.4 | < 0.001 |
| km | **2.36** | 0.077 | 30.6 | < 0.001 |
| items | **0.44** | 0.071 | 6.2 | < 0.001 |
| rain | **6.34** | 0.81 | 7.8 | < 0.001 |

R² rises from 0.836 with distance alone to **0.910**, and the residual standard deviation falls from 4.28 to **3.20** minutes.

## Reading a coefficient: the others held fixed

Each coefficient is the change in predicted minutes for one unit more of **that** predictor, **with the other predictors held fixed**. One more item adds 0.44 minutes, for deliveries of the same distance in the same weather. Rain adds 6.34 minutes, for deliveries of the same distance and size.

A six-kilometre, eight-item delivery is predicted at 21.25 + 2.36 × 6 + 0.44 × 8 = **38.9 minutes** dry, and 6.34 more, **45.2 minutes**, in the rain.

## Why holding fixed matters

On its own, rain goes with deliveries **10.68** minutes longer. In the model, its coefficient is 6.34. The difference has a reason: in this month the rainy deliveries happened to be longer trips, 7.1 km on average against 5.2 km for the dry ones. The simple comparison credits rain with some of distance's minutes; the multiple regression separates them.

This is the adjustment for confounders promised in lesson 18. The coupons of that lesson went with deliveries 9.8 minutes longer. With distance in the model, the coupon's coefficient is **0.82** minutes, with a standard error of 1.00 and p = 0.41: no evidence of any effect once distance is held fixed.

## In a spreadsheet

`LINEST` fits several predictors at once and returns an array. With the three predictors in A2:C121, `INDEX` picks out single values. The coefficients come back in the **reverse** order of the columns, with the intercept last:

```localised
=INDEX(LINEST(D2:D121, A2:C121, TRUE, TRUE), 1, 3)    2.35727805506795
=INDEX(LINEST(D2:D121, A2:C121, TRUE, TRUE), 1, 1)    6.34110466951963
=INDEX(LINEST(D2:D121, A2:C121, TRUE, TRUE), 3, 1)    0.909818185713742
```

The first row holds the coefficients, the second their standard errors, and the first cell of the third row is R².

## The limit of adjustment

"Held fixed" means held fixed **statistically**, for the variables in the model. A confounder that was never measured is not held fixed by anything, and a coefficient can carry its effect without any warning. Multiple regression adjusts for what you thought of and measured; the randomised experiment of lesson 18 is still the only design that handles the rest.
