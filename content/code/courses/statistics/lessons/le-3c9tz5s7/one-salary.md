## A mean that describes nobody

Eight of the nine people earn less than the mean. The nearest salary to it is R$ 3,400, more than R$ 1,900 away. Somebody told "the average salary at Horta is about R$ 5,300" would picture a company that does not exist.

The mean is not wrong. It is exactly what it claims to be: the payroll shared out equally. The problem is the question it was used to answer. "What does a typical person here earn?" is the median's question, and the median answers it with R$ 2,400, a salary one of the nine actually receives, with four people below it and four above.

## One value did all of it

Remove the founder and recompute. The eight remaining salaries have a mean of R$ 2,506.25 and a median of R$ 2,350. The two now differ by about R$ 150, where before they differed by nearly R$ 3,000.

So one value in nine moved the mean by more than R$ 2,800 and the median by R$ 50. That is the whole lesson in two numbers: **the mean follows every value, so an extreme value carries it away; the median follows the order, so it barely notices.**

## Why this keeps happening

Salaries, house prices, company sizes, followers on social media, the money customers spend: wherever a quantity has a floor but no ceiling, a few very large values are normal. Pay cannot go below zero, and in practice not below the minimum wage, but it can go a long way up. Data like that has a long tail on one side, and a long tail always drags the mean towards itself.
