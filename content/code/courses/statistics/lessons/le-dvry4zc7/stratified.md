title: The stratified sample
version: 1

A simple random sample of 20 of the 120 deliveries might, by chance, contain 9 from Centro and 2 from Barão Geraldo. Since Barão Geraldo's deliveries take far longer, that sample would underestimate the typical delivery time. Nothing is wrong with the method; it is just unlucky.

A **stratified sample** removes that kind of bad luck. Split the population into groups called **strata**, chosen so that members of each stratum are alike, then draw a simple random sample from **each** stratum.

## Strata by neighbourhood

The 120 deliveries split into four strata of 30, one per neighbourhood, and delivery times within a neighbourhood are much more alike than across them. A stratified sample of 20 takes 5 from each.

**Stratifying halved the wobble**, at no extra cost: the same 20 deliveries, chosen with a little more thought. The reason is that the biggest source of variation, which neighbourhood a delivery went to, is now fixed by design instead of left to chance.

## Proportional and otherwise

Here the four strata were the same size, so 5 from each was **proportional**. When strata differ in size, proportional allocation takes from each in proportion to its share of the population: if Barão Geraldo held 10% of deliveries, it would get 10% of the sample.

Sometimes a small stratum is deliberately **oversampled**, to say something reliable about it on its own: a survey that wants to compare business customers with households, when business customers are 2% of the list, might take 100 of each. The overall results then have to be **weighted** back to the true proportions, which is lesson 3's weighted mean doing a new job.

## What makes a good stratum

Strata help when members of a stratum are alike in what is being measured and different from the other strata. Neighbourhood is a good stratum for delivery time; it would be a poor one for, say, the share of customers paying by pix, if pix use is the same everywhere. Stratifying on something unrelated does no harm, but it does not help either.
