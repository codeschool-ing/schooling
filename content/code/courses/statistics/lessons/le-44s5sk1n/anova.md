Lesson 6's boxplots showed four neighbourhoods with different delivery times. A test of whether they differ could compare them two at a time, six comparisons in all, but lesson 14 showed what running many tests does to the false-alarm rate. The **analysis of variance**, or **ANOVA**, asks the question once:

- H₀: all four neighbourhoods have the same true mean delivery time.
- H₁: at least one differs.

## Two spreads compared

ANOVA's idea is in its name: it compares two measures of variation.

**Between groups**: how far the group means sit from the overall mean. If the neighbourhoods really differ, their means are spread out.

**Within groups**: how far the individual deliveries sit from their own neighbourhood's mean. This is the ordinary noise.

The test statistic is the ratio of the two, each divided by its degrees of freedom, and it is called **F**, after Ronald Fisher:

```localised
F = (between-group variance) ÷ (within-group variance)
```

If the null is true, the group means differ only by chance, and F is near 1. If the groups really differ, the between-group variance is much larger than the within-group one, and F is large.

|---|---|---|---|
| between neighbourhoods | 10,197.2 | 3 | 3,399.1 |
| within neighbourhoods | 2,977.0 | 116 | 25.7 |

F = 3,399.1 ÷ 25.7 = **132.4**, with 3 and 116 degrees of freedom. The p-value is about 3 × 10⁻³⁷: as close to zero as makes no difference. The neighbourhoods differ.

Spreadsheets compute ANOVA through an add-on rather than a function, but the p-value for a given F is one function:

```localised
=F.DIST.RT(132.448364988499, 3, 116)      2.61353269781692E-037
```

## What ANOVA does not say

A rejected ANOVA says that **at least one** neighbourhood differs, not which. The boxplots make it obvious here — Barão Geraldo is far above the rest — but in general the next step is a set of comparisons between pairs, using a method that adjusts for making several of them. The best known is **Tukey's honestly significant difference**, which statistical software provides.

With only two groups, ANOVA and the equal-spread t test are the same test: for Centro and Cambuí alone, F = 1.19 and p = 0.28, matching the t test's 0.28.
