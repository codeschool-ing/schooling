---
title: Why 27 °C is not twice 13.5 °C
version: 1
---

On a cool morning in Campinas the thermometer reads 13.5 °C. By the afternoon it reads 27 °C. Is it
twice as warm?

The numbers say 27 ÷ 13.5 = 2. But write the same two temperatures in Fahrenheit, which is just a
different scale for the same air, and they become 56.3 °F and 80.6 °F. Now the ratio is 1.43. In
kelvin, 286.65 K and 300.15 K, it is 1.05.

The air did not change. Only the scale did, and the ratio changed with it. **A ratio that depends on
the choice of scale describes the scale, not the thing measured.**

## The zero is the culprit

Celsius puts its zero where water freezes. Fahrenheit puts it at the temperature of a brine mixture
used in the eighteenth century. Both zeros are conventions. Kelvin puts its zero at absolute zero,
where there is no heat left to remove, and that zero is a fact about the world.

A ratio compares two distances from zero. If the zero is a convention, so is the ratio. Only on
the kelvin scale is 27 °C compared with 13.5 °C as it physically is, and there the ratio is 1.05:
the afternoon is about 5% further from absolute zero than the morning, not twice as far.

## Differences survive

Here is what does survive. The rise from morning to afternoon is 13.5 °C, or 24.3 °F, or 13.5 K.
Those are the same physical rise written in different units, and converting one into another is a
fixed multiplication. A **difference** means the same thing whichever scale you use. That is what
makes Celsius an interval scale: differences are meaningful, ratios are not.

## The same test for any column

To decide whether a quantity is interval or ratio, ask: **does zero mean none of it?**

- A basket of R$ 0: no money. Ratio.
- 0 minutes of delivery: no time. Ratio.
- 0 items: no items. Ratio.
- The year 0: not the absence of time, just a starting point somebody chose. Interval.
- 0 °C: not the absence of heat. Interval.

The answer decides one practical thing. On a ratio scale "twice as much", "half as long" and "a 20%
increase" all mean what they say. On an interval scale, report differences instead: "13.5 degrees
warmer", not "twice as warm".
