Suppose the delivery times have a standard deviation of about 6 minutes, so the standard error of a mean of 25 is 6 ÷ √25 = **1.2 minutes**.

- If the routing changed nothing, sample means centre on **40**.
- If it really saves **2 minutes**, sample means centre on **38**.

The one-sided test at α = 0.05 rejects when the sample mean falls below **37.95 minutes**, the critical value from lesson 13 turned back into minutes.

## Reading the picture

**Under the null** (the curve centred on 40), the area left of 37.95 is 5%: that is **α**, the false alarms.

**Under the improvement** (the curve centred on 38), the area left of 37.95 is about **48%**: those are the trials that correctly detect the improvement. That share is the **power**. The rest, about 52%, falls to the right of the line, where the test does not reject: that is **β**, the missed effects.

The two curves overlap heavily because 2 minutes is not much bigger than the 1.2-minute standard error. Most sample means that a 2-minute improvement produces could also have been produced by no improvement at all.

## Moving the line

- Move the critical value **left**, to α = 0.01, and false alarms become rarer, but the improvement curve has even less area beyond the line: the power falls to about **22%**.
- Move it **right**, to α = 0.10, and the power rises, at the price of twice as many false alarms.

There is no position of the line that makes both errors small at once, because the curves overlap. The only way to separate them is to make both curves **narrower**, which means a smaller standard error, which means **more data**.
