The four distributions answer four kinds of question. Choosing one starts from the random variable, not from the data.

| the variable | the model | its parameters |
|---|---|---|
| any value in a range, none favoured | uniform | the two ends |
| successes in a fixed number of independent trials | binomial | *n* and *p* |
| events in an interval, at random, at a steady rate | Poisson | the rate λ |
| a measurement built from many small independent effects | normal | μ and σ |

Late deliveries out of tonight's ten: a count with a fixed number of trials, so binomial. Complaints tomorrow: a count with no fixed number of trials, so Poisson. The weight of the next bag: a measurement with many small influences, so normal.

## Checking the fit

A model is a claim about the data, and claims get checked. The simplest check is to put the model's predictions beside what happened.

| complaints | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| observed days | 5 | 17 | 16 | 8 | 12 | 2 | 0 |
| expected days | 5.4 | 13.1 | 15.7 | 12.5 | 7.5 | 3.6 | 2.1 |

The two do not match bar for bar, and they should not: 60 days is a sample, and samples wobble. Days with one complaint came out about four more than expected, days with four about four and a half more, and days with three about four and a half fewer. What matters is that the shape agrees — a peak at 1 and 2, a fall-off after 4 — and that the mean and the variance, 2.18 and 1.85, are close, as a Poisson's should be.

Whether gaps like these are larger than chance would produce is a question with a precise answer. Lesson 16's chi-square test gives it, using exactly this table of observed and expected counts.

## Warning signs

- **A model that predicts the impossible**: negative weights, more late deliveries than deliveries.
- **A variance far from what the model implies**: a Poisson whose variance is three times its mean, a binomial with much more spread than *np*(1 − *p*).
- **Two peaks**, which none of these four models produces.
- **A broken condition**: trials that are not independent, a rate that changes through the day.

When a warning sign appears, the model may still be useful for the middle of the distribution, but its tails are not to be trusted.
