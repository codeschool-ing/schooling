---
title: Reporting a test honestly
version: 1
---

A test is worth what its report lets a reader understand. Here is a checklist, applied to Horta's two tests.

## Report the estimate and its interval first

The question was about a quantity, so lead with the quantity: "deliveries under the new system averaged 38.9 minutes (95% interval 36.6 to 41.2), against 40 before". A reader learns the size of the change and its uncertainty before any test is mentioned.

## Give the test statistic, its degrees of freedom and the exact p

"t = −1.00, 24 degrees of freedom, one-sided p = 0.16". Not "p > 0.05", and not "n.s.". The exact value lets a reader judge the strength of the evidence and combine it with other studies.

## Say what the hypotheses and the level were, and when they were chosen

"The test was one-sided, as the supplier's claim was that deliveries would be faster; the hypotheses, the 5% level and the sample of 25 were set before the trial." That sentence is what separates a test from a description of whatever turned up.

## Say how many tests were run

If the routing trial also tested evening deliveries, weekend deliveries and deliveries to Barão Geraldo separately, say so, and say which results are being highlighted and why.

## Interpret without overclaiming

- Rejected: "the data is strong evidence that the machine overfills, by between 2.3 and 6.9 g per bag".
- Not rejected: "the trial did not show the new system to be faster; it could not rule out an improvement of up to about three minutes, nor no improvement at all".

Neither sentence uses the word "proves", and neither claims more than the interval allows.

## Two phrasings to avoid

**"The result was significant, so the effect is important."** Significance is about whether the effect is distinguishable from zero, not about whether it matters. Lesson 22 is devoted to the difference.

**"The result was not significant, so there is no effect."** Lesson 13's courtroom: not guilty is not innocent. Lesson 15 measures how likely a study was to detect an effect if there was one, which is what tells you how much a non-significant result is worth.
