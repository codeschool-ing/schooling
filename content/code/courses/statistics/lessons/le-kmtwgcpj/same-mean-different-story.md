They are not. A customer waiting for Lia can count on 33 to 36 minutes. A customer waiting for Davi might get the shopping in 22 minutes or in 44, and cannot plan around either. If Horta promised "delivery in 40 minutes", Lia would keep the promise every time and Davi would break it twice in eight.

## Centre and spread are different questions

A **measure of centre** answers "where are the values?". A **measure of spread**, or dispersion, answers "how far apart are they?". One cannot stand in for the other, and a summary that gives only the first leaves out what a customer of Davi's cares about most.

This matters well beyond couriers. Two suppliers whose bags of rice both average 5 kg can differ in how many bags come in under weight. Two investments with the same average return can differ in how often they lose money. Two schools with the same average grade can differ in how many students fail. In each case **the spread is where the risk is**.

## Three measures, in order of sophistication

The rest of the lesson builds three measures of spread, each fixing a weakness of the one before.

1. The **range**: the distance from the smallest value to the largest. Easy, and fooled by a single value.
2. The **variance**: the average squared distance from the mean. Uses every value, and comes out in squared units.
3. The **standard deviation**: the square root of the variance. Back in the original units, and the measure everybody reports.

Lesson 6 adds a fourth, the interquartile range, that the median and the boxplot are built on.
