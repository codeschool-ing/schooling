**This course needs no software.** Every calculation in it can be done with a pencil, and most of
them are done that way on the page, one step at a time, because the steps are where the
understanding is. A function that returns the standard deviation tells you the answer and hides
the idea.

Where a calculation is long — a standard deviation over a hundred values, a p-value — the course
shows the arithmetic once by hand and then gives the spreadsheet formula that does the same. Any
spreadsheet will do: Excel, LibreOffice Calc or Google Sheets. The formulas are written the way an
English-language spreadsheet spells them.

## Every number has a source

The orders, the salaries, the delivery times and the tests in this course belong to Horta, an
invented grocer. The numbers were produced by a script that keeps the course's data and repeats
every calculation, so a number quoted in one lesson is the same number when a later lesson uses it.
When a figure is rounded on the page it says so, and the questions accept the rounding.

## What the questions ask of you

Each lesson ends with questions, and many of them ask for a number. Type it with a point or a comma
as your keyboard prefers, and to the precision the question names. When a question says "to two
decimal places", an answer within a small margin of the exact value is accepted, so a rounding in
the last digit will not count against you.

None of the questions in a lesson carry a mark. A wrong answer shows you why it was wrong, and that
explanation is the most useful thing the question has to give.
