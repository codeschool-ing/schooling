Lesson 17 showed that one point can create a correlation. In a regression, that point has a name, and a number that finds it.

## Leverage and influence

A point's **leverage** measures how far its predictor values are from the rest. A point far out along the horizontal axis has high leverage: the line has to pass near it, because nothing else constrains the line out there. Leverage depends only on the predictors, not on the response.

A point is **influential** when removing it would change the fitted line substantially. That needs two things together: high leverage, and a response that does not follow the pattern of the other points.

With all eleven points the slope is **+2.66**; without the lone point at 12 km it is **−0.64**. One observation decides not only the size of the slope but its sign. Its leverage is **0.98**, nearly the maximum of 1, meaning the line passes almost exactly through it.

## Cook's distance

**Cook's distance** combines leverage and residual size into one number per observation: how much all the fitted values would change if that observation were left out. Two common rules flag a point for a closer look: a Cook's distance above **1**, or above **4 ÷ n**.

The lone point's Cook's distance is **91.8**. The largest among the other ten is 0.20.

For Horta's 120 deliveries and the three-predictor model, the largest Cook's distance is **0.078**, for a 13.8 km delivery to Barão Geraldo that took 48.5 minutes, quicker than its distance predicts. Five deliveries exceed 4 ÷ 120 = 0.033, and none comes near 1: no single delivery is steering the model.

## A typo with low leverage

Influence does not need an extreme predictor. Suppose delivery 11, which took 24.0 minutes, had been typed as **240**. Its predictors are ordinary, so its leverage is only 0.027, but its residual is enormous. Its Cook's distance is **0.77**, far above every genuine delivery; the rain coefficient jumps from 6.34 to 4.67 minutes; and R² collapses from 0.91 to **0.15**.

## What to do with an influential point

Lesson 9's rule applies: **find out what it is before deciding**. A typo is corrected. A case from a different process, a delivery beyond the area Horta normally serves, may belong in a separate analysis. A genuine case of the same kind stays in, and the report shows the results with and without it, so that a reader can see how much one observation is carrying.
