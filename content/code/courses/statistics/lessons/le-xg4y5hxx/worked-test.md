Here is the whole procedure on Horta's two questions.

## Did the new routing make deliveries faster?

**1. Hypotheses.** H₀: μ = 40. H₁: μ < 40. One-sided, because the claim was "faster".

**2. Significance level.** α = 0.05, chosen before the 25 deliveries were collected.

**3. Test statistic.** Mean 38.9, standard deviation 5.52, *n* = 25, standard error 1.10. So t = (38.9 − 40) ÷ 1.10 = **−1.00**, with 24 degrees of freedom.

**4. Decision.** The critical value is −1.71. The statistic, −1.00, is not below it. **The null hypothesis is not rejected.**

**In plain words:** the 25 deliveries averaged 1.1 minutes faster than before, and a difference that small is well within what 25 deliveries under the old system could produce by chance. The data does not show that the new system is faster.

What it does **not** show is that the new system makes no difference. One minute might be real; 25 deliveries are simply not enough to tell. Lesson 15 calculates how many deliveries it would take to detect a real improvement of a minute or two, and the answer is uncomfortable for anyone who expected 25 to be plenty.

## Is the filling machine on target?

A second example, two-sided this time. The supplier's machine should fill bags with a mean of 1000 g. Horta weighs 15 bags.

**1. Hypotheses.** H₀: μ = 1000. H₁: μ ≠ 1000.

**2. Significance level.** α = 0.05.

**3. Test statistic.** The 15 bags have a mean of **1004.6 g** and a standard deviation of **4.23 g**. Standard error 4.23 ÷ √15 = 1.09. So t = (1004.6 − 1000) ÷ 1.09 = **4.21**, with 14 degrees of freedom.

**4. Decision.** The two-sided critical values are ±2.14. The statistic, 4.21, is far beyond the upper one. **The null hypothesis is rejected.**

**In plain words:** the machine is overfilling. Fifteen bags averaging 4.6 g over target, with a spread of about 4 g, would almost never happen if the machine were really centred on 1000 g. Horta's supplier is giving away rice.

## What to report

A test result is more than "rejected" or "not rejected". A useful report gives the estimate, its interval, the test statistic and the decision: "the bags averaged 1004.6 g (95% interval 1002.3 to 1006.9 g); t = 4.21 with 14 degrees of freedom, so the machine is off its 1000 g target". The interval says how far off; the test only says whether. Lesson 14 adds the last piece a report needs, the p-value.
