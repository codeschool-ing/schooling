title: A checklist for every analysis
version: 1
---

This course has built a set of habits. Here they are in the order an analysis uses them, with the lesson that made each case.

## Before the data

1. **State the question and the outcome.** What is being measured, in what units, on whom? (Lessons 1 and 2.)
2. **Decide the smallest effect that would matter**, from costs or what people notice. (Lessons 15 and 22.)
3. **Plan the sample size** for adequate power to detect that effect. (Lesson 15.)
4. **Randomise if you can.** Otherwise, list the confounders and measure them. (Lesson 18.)
5. **Write down the hypotheses, the test and the analysis** before looking. (Lesson 14.)

## With the data

6. **Look before you summarise.** Histograms, boxplots, scatter plots. (Lessons 4, 6, 7 and 17.)
7. **Choose summaries that fit the shape**: the median and IQR for skewed data, the mean and standard deviation for symmetric. (Lessons 3 to 6.)
8. **Find out what any extreme value is** before deciding what to do with it. (Lesson 9.)
9. **Choose the test from the outcome and the design**: paired or independent, numbers or counts. (Lesson 16.)
10. **Check the model's assumptions** with residual plots. (Lesson 20.)

## In the report

11. **Lead with the estimate and its confidence interval**, in natural units. (Lessons 12 and 22.)
12. **Give the exact p-value**, not "p < 0.05" or "n.s." (Lesson 14.)
13. **Say how many tests were run** and which were planned. (Lesson 14.)
14. **Compare the interval with the smallest effect that matters**, not only with zero. (Lesson 22.)
15. **Use causal language only when the design supports it.** (Lesson 18.)
16. **Say what the data cannot tell you.** (Every lesson.)

## The point of all of it

Statistics does not remove uncertainty. It measures it, so that decisions can be made in proportion to what the data actually supports. Horta's managers do not need certainty to act. They need to know how big an effect probably is, how sure they can be, and whether it is large enough to matter. Every tool in this course serves one of those three questions.
