## The options

**Correct it.** When the true value is known — the payment record says R$ 212.60 — fix the value and record that it was fixed.

**Remove it.** When the value is wrong and cannot be recovered, or belongs to a population outside the analysis, remove it and record which observations were removed and why. A removal that is not written down is indistinguishable from a removal made because the result looked better without the value.

**Analyse it separately.** When it belongs to a different population — business customers, say — describe that group on its own. It may turn out to be the most interesting part of the data.

**Keep it.** When it is genuine, it stays, and the analysis uses methods that cope with it: the median and the IQR from lesson 6, a trimmed mean from lesson 4, or the approach in the next paragraph.

## Report with and without

When a genuine extreme changes the conclusion, the honest move is to **report both versions** and say which values make the difference. For the 400 baskets:

| | mean basket |
|---|---|
| all 400 baskets | R$ 82.78 |
| without the 17 beyond the fence | R$ 74.38 |
| winsorised at the 95th percentile | R$ 79.34 |

The third line is **winsorising**, named after the biostatistician Charles Winsor: instead of removing extreme values, cap them at a chosen percentile. Here every basket above the 95th percentile, R$ 192.25, is treated as R$ 192.25. It keeps the count of observations and pulls in the tail without pretending the big baskets do not exist.

If the three versions lead to the same decision, the extremes do not matter for that decision, and the report can say so in a sentence. If they lead to different decisions, the reader needs to know that the conclusion depends on a handful of orders. That is called a **sensitivity analysis**, and it is the most honest way to handle values you cannot explain away.

## The rule that never changes

Whatever is done with an outlier, it is **written down**: what was flagged, by which rule, what was found, and what was done. That record is what lets somebody else trust the result. It is also what protects the analyst from the temptation every analyst feels at some point: removing the values that spoil a result and keeping the ones that do not. A decision made before seeing the result, and written down, cannot drift.
