Both curves have mean 0 and standard deviation 1. The blue one is the **normal** shape that lesson 8 introduces. The red one has **heavy tails**: it is taller in the centre, thinner on the shoulders, and — the part that matters — it stays above the normal curve beyond about 2.5 standard deviations. Values that the normal curve treats as almost impossible are, under the red curve, merely rare.

## The number

Kurtosis is computed like skewness but with the **fourth** power of the standardised deviations, which makes the far values dominate completely. It is usually reported as **excess kurtosis**: the kurtosis minus 3, so that the normal shape scores 0.

- **Excess kurtosis above 0**: heavier tails than normal. Extreme values are more common than the normal shape would suggest.
- **Excess kurtosis below 0**: lighter tails. The values are bunched more evenly, with fewer extremes.

The spreadsheet's `KURT` returns the excess kurtosis, with a sample-size correction:

```localised
=KURT(A2:A401)      6.43443080916449
```

The baskets score 6.43: heavy tails, from the long run of large orders. The bags of rice score 0.48, close to normal. The test scores score 1.80.

## Not peakedness

Older textbooks describe kurtosis as how "peaked" a distribution is. That description is misleading: a distribution can be sharply peaked and have light tails, or flat-topped with heavy ones. What the fourth power measures is almost entirely the tails, because a value two standard deviations out contributes 16 times as much as one at one standard deviation, and one at four contributes 256 times as much.

## Why anybody cares

Heavy tails are where surprises live. A risk model that assumes the normal shape for losses that are really heavy-tailed will call a large loss a once-in-a-century event, and then see one every few years. For Horta, heavy-tailed baskets mean that a week's revenue can be moved noticeably by a handful of very large orders, and that a sample of a few dozen orders may contain none of them and underestimate the mean.

Like skewness, kurtosis needs a lot of data to estimate. From fifty values it is little more than a hint.
