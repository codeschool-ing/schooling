Most distributions have one peak, and the first thing to say about one is whether it is **symmetric** or **skewed**: whether the values fall away from the peak evenly on both sides, or stretch out further on one side than the other.

## The three shapes

**Symmetric.** The 200 bags of rice from Horta's supplier pile up around 1003 g and thin out at about the same rate below and above. The mean is 1002.61 g and the median 1002.90 g: almost the same. A machine aiming at a weight and missing by small random amounts produces this shape.

**Right-skewed**, or positively skewed. The 400 baskets have their peak on the left and a long tail running right. The mean, R$ 82.78, is pulled into the tail, well above the median of R$ 66.74. Quantities with a floor and no ceiling — money, waiting times, file sizes, the number of followers an account has — are right-skewed.

**Left-skewed**, or negatively skewed. Scores on an easy internal test pile up near the top, at 90 and above, with a tail of a few low scores running left. The mean, 86.4, is pulled below the median, 89. Quantities with a ceiling that most values approach — scores on an easy test, the age at death in a country with good health care — are left-skewed.

## The name follows the tail

The direction of a skew is the direction of the **tail**, not of the peak. A right-skewed distribution has its peak on the left. People get this backwards, and the cure is to remember that the tail is where the unusual values are, and the unusual values are what the skew describes.

## The mean and the median as a test

Lesson 4's rule of thumb is the quickest diagnosis there is:

| shape | the mean is | the median is |
|---|---|---|
| right-skewed | pulled right | to its left |
| symmetric | in the middle | at the same place |
| left-skewed | pulled left | to its right |

It is a rule of thumb, not a law. With two peaks, or with a tail on each side of different lengths, the mean and the median can sit in any order. But for the common one-peaked shapes it works, and it needs no drawing at all.
