The scatter plot shows an association; **Pearson's correlation coefficient**, written *r*, measures how close it is to a straight line. Its name comes from Karl Pearson, who formalised it around 1900.

## The idea: products of deviations

Take each point's distance from the mean on both axes. When a point is above average on both, or below average on both, the product of its two deviations is positive. When it is above on one and below on the other, the product is negative.

Add up the products. If most points sit in the two positive quarters, the total is large and positive; if most sit in the negative quarters, it is large and negative; if they are spread over all four, the positive and negative products cancel out and the total is near zero.

## Horta's twelve orders

For the twelve orders of lesson 1, items have mean 6.25 and baskets have mean R$ 79.17. The products of deviations are all positive, from 1.27 for an order close to both means up to **1,167.51** for H-1048, fifteen items and R$ 212.60. They add up to **2,736.49**.

Divided by n − 1 = 11, that total is the **covariance**: **248.77**. Its unit is items times reais, which nobody can interpret, and its size depends on the units: measure baskets in cents and it becomes a hundred times larger.

## From covariance to r

Dividing by the two standard deviations removes the units. The standard deviations are 4.20 items and R$ 59.42, so

**r = covariance ÷ (s of x × s of y) = 248.77 ÷ (4.20 × 59.42) = 0.996**

The result is a pure number, always between −1 and 1, and it does not change if either variable is rescaled: baskets in cents, or minutes in hours, give the same *r*.

## In a spreadsheet

With items in A2:A13 and baskets in B2:B13:

```localised
=CORREL(A2:A13, B2:B13)          0.996237865635769
=PEARSON(A2:A13, B2:B13)         0.996237865635769
=COVARIANCE.S(A2:A13, B2:B13)    248.771590909091
```

`CORREL` and `PEARSON` are the same calculation under two names. The order of the two ranges does not matter: the correlation of items with baskets is the correlation of baskets with items.
