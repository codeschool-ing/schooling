## What to read in it

Four questions, in this order.

**Direction.** Do the points rise from left to right, fall, or neither? Here they rise: longer deliveries take longer. A rising cloud is a **positive association**; a falling one, such as the price of a product against the number sold, is a **negative association**.

**Form.** Do the points follow a straight band, a curve, or clusters? Here the band is straight. A curve needs a different summary, as later sections show.

**Strength.** How tightly do the points hug the pattern? Here, fairly tightly: at any one distance, most deliveries fall within a band about thirteen minutes wide, while across all distances they spread over forty-five.

**Exceptions.** Does any point sit far from the rest? Here, none does.

## Which variable goes where

When one variable might plausibly influence the other, it goes on the horizontal axis: distance across, minutes up, because distance can affect duration and not the reverse. The habit makes the picture easier to read. It does not prove anything about cause, and lesson 18 is entirely about that.

## When the points pile up

With many points, or with values rounded to a few levels, dots land on top of each other and the plot hides how many there are. The 120 deliveries were rounded to the half minute and the tenth of a kilometre, which is fine at this size. With thousands of points, make the dots small and partly transparent, or add a little random jitter so that stacked points separate.
