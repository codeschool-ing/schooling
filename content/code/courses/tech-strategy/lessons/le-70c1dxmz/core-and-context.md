Before any sum, one question settles a good share of build-or-buy decisions: **is this the thing
customers choose you for?** Geoffrey Moore gave the question its vocabulary. **Core** is the work
that differentiates a company — what wins customers from its competitors. **Context** is
everything else the company has to do, often has to do well, and wins nobody over by doing well.

## Important is the wrong axis

The common rule is "build what matters, buy what doesn't", and it fails on its first example.
Payroll matters: a company that pays its people late has a crisis by Friday. Nobody builds
payroll, because no customer has ever chosen a supplier for the quality of its payroll. **The
axis is differentiation, not importance.** A good deal of context is critical — it has to work,
every day — and it is still context.

Ask the question at Coreto and the answer is already written down. Lesson 1's diagnosis says
Coreto earns its reputation in about twelve big on-sales a year. What would make a theatre leave
for a competitor? An on-sale that collapses at 10:00. A Box Office app that cannot scan tickets at
the door. Those are core: the seat-hold path, the door, the payouts a venue lives on.

Search is different. A buyer who cannot find a show is a lost sale, and a venue whose festival
does not come up will complain loudly. But no venue chooses a ticketing platform for its search
box, and a hosted search service will sell the same search to every competitor Coreto has.
**Search is context — critical context.** It has to work well, and it does not have to be
Coreto's own.

## The rule, and where it bites

Moore's rule is short: put your own engineers on core, and get context done as cheaply and
reliably as you can, which usually means buying or adopting it. The reason is that engineers are
the scarcest thing a company has, and **every hour spent building context is an hour core does
not get.**

At Coreto that has a figure on it. The build option's 960 hours are more than half an
engineer-year, out of a company whose guiding policy is "protect the on-sale first". Those hours
would come from engineers who could be on the reservation path. Lesson 13 prices that kind of
loss as opportunity cost; for now it is enough to see that the sheet in the next section does not
include it, and that core-and-context is how it enters the decision.

Four questions sort a candidate in a few minutes:

| question | it points to core if | it points to context if |
|---|---|---|
| Would a venue move to a competitor over this? | yes | no |
| Can a company like Coreto buy it, ready-made? | no | yes |
| Could our version be better than what is for sale, three years from now? | yes, and somebody can say why | nobody can say why |
| Does it carry knowledge only we have? | yes — the seat maps and holding rules of Brazilian venues | no |

Search answers "context" in every row. The holds on a venue's seat map answer "core" in every row,
which is why lesson 1 gave them a team.

## Three traps

**Everything is core to the team that wants to build it.** A team proposing to build will find
reasons its version would be special, and some of them will be true. Ask the venue question
rather than the team question: would the venue notice, and would it leave? If the honest answer
is "it would notice if it broke", that describes context that must not break.

**Context is not careless.** Buying search does not mean caring less about search. It still
needs an owner at Coreto, a target for how fast and how available it must be, and somebody who
reads the vendor's status page on the day of a big on-sale. The difference is what the owner
spends time on: the contract and the integration, rather than the index.

**Core moves.** What differentiates a company now becomes a commodity when the market catches up,
and something that was context becomes core when the strategy changes. If Coreto decided to win
venues on discovery — recommending the next show to each buyer, filling seats the venue could not
fill alone — then search and ranking would move towards core, and building would become a
serious option again. **The classification belongs to the strategy**, so it is revisited when the
strategy is, and written down beside it.

For search, today, Coreto's answer is context. That makes buying or adopting the default, and it
puts the burden on the build option: building has to win on money by enough to justify the hours.
