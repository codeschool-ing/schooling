**The Architecture Development Method, the ADM, is TOGAF's cycle for developing and changing an
enterprise architecture.** It is drawn as a ring of phases around a centre, and the centre is the
part people forget: requirements management, which every phase feeds and draws from.

## The phases

| phase | what it does | its main output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preliminary** | prepares the organisation to do architecture: who, how, with which principles and tools | the tailored framework, the architecture principles |
| **A. Architecture Vision** | scopes one change, identifies stakeholders, sets out the vision and gets approval to proceed | the Statement of Architecture Work, the vision |
| **B. Business Architecture** | describes the baseline and target business architecture and the gaps between them | business part of the Architecture Definition Document |
| **C. Information Systems Architectures** | the same for data and applications | data and application parts of the definition |
| **D. Technology Architecture** | the same for technology | technology part of the definition |
| **E. Opportunities and Solutions** | groups the gaps into work packages and identifies the projects that will deliver them | the first version of the Architecture Roadmap |
| **F. Migration Planning** | orders and costs the work into transition architectures and a plan | the Implementation and Migration Plan |
| **G. Implementation Governance** | oversees the projects as they build, checking they conform | Architecture Contracts, compliance reviews |
| **H. Architecture Change Management** | watches for changes in the business or technology that require a new cycle | change requests, a new A |
| **Requirements Management** (centre) | holds the requirements, which every phase adds to and draws from | the Architecture Requirements Specification |

## A cycle, with loops inside it

Three things about the drawing are easy to misread:

- **It is a cycle, not a waterfall.** Phase H feeds a new Phase A. An enterprise is never finished,
  and the method assumes the next change is coming.
- **There are loops inside it.** The standard describes iterating within B to D until the
  architecture is good enough, and between phases when something found later changes something
  decided earlier. A team that runs A to H once, in order, has not followed the ADM.
- **Requirements management is continuous.** A requirement found in Phase D, such as *the station
  internet links cannot carry video*, goes into the centre and can send Phase B back to change a
  business process.

## How big a turn is

One turn of the cycle can take a fortnight or a year, and can be about a whole company or one
capability. The standard calls the decision about scope and depth **tailoring**, and it is done in
the Preliminary phase and again in Phase A for each change. The next section takes Carcará round the
cycle once, for its digital boarding programme, in about three months.
