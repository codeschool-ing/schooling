**TOGAF, The Open Group Architecture Framework, is a framework for enterprise architecture: a method
for developing an architecture, a vocabulary, a set of recommended deliverables and guidance on
governing the whole effort.** The Open Group publishes it; the current version is the TOGAF
Standard, 10th Edition, released in 2022. It is the framework most often named in corporate job
descriptions for architects, and the one with the largest certified community.

## Enterprise architecture, again

`architecture-role` lesson 4 separated three levels: application architecture, the inside of one
system; solution architecture, one outcome across several systems; and **enterprise architecture,
the whole portfolio**. It covers how the organisation's business, information, applications and technology
fit together, and how they should change over years to serve its strategy.

Everything in lessons 1 to 7 of this course worked at the first two levels. TOGAF works at the
third. Its unit is not a system but **a change to the enterprise**, such as Carcará's board deciding
that every passenger boards with a digital ticket by the end of 2027.

## What is in it

The 10th edition separates two kinds of content:

- **the Fundamental Content**: the stable core, which includes the **Architecture Development Method
  (ADM)**, the techniques it uses, the content framework that says what deliverables and artefacts
  exist, and the material on the enterprise continuum and architecture capability;
- **the Series Guides**: practical guidance on particular topics, such as using TOGAF with agile
  delivery, for digital transformation, or for security, published and revised separately.

The split matters to a reader: the core changes rarely, while the guides are where TOGAF responds to
how architecture is actually practised now.

## What it is not

- **It is not a notation.** TOGAF says what views and artefacts to produce, not how to draw them.
  Its sister standard for drawing is **ArchiMate**, which lesson 9 shows.
- **It is not a methodology you run end to end.** The standard itself says the ADM should be
  **tailored**: adapted to the organisation, with steps merged, skipped or made lighter. The common
  failure is the opposite, a team producing every deliverable because the book lists it.
- **It is not specific to software.** A change in TOGAF's sense may be a reorganisation, a merger or
  a new supplier, with no code in it.

## Why it comes up in interviews

A corporate architect role, especially in banking, insurance, telecoms and government, will ask
whether you know TOGAF. What a good interviewer wants to hear is not the list of phases. It is
whether you can say **what problem each part solves and which parts you would leave out** for a
given organisation. The last section of this lesson argues that case for Carcará.

Certification exists at two levels, Foundation and Practitioner. Like BABOK's in lesson 7, it is a
signal to employers, not a requirement of this course.
