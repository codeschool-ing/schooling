**Architecture governance is how an organisation makes sure the architecture it agreed is the one
that gets built, and that departures from it are decisions rather than accidents.** TOGAF describes
several mechanisms; three are worth knowing because they come up in any large company.

## The architecture board

An **architecture board** is the group that owns the architecture's decisions across the enterprise:
it approves principles and standards, resolves conflicts between projects, and grants exceptions. In
a bank it may meet monthly with a formal agenda. At Carcará it is Tainá, the five team leads and
Otávio, for one hour every other week, with the decision log open on the screen.

What makes a board useful rather than a gate is the same as lesson 5's decision records: **its
decisions are written down with their reasons**, and anybody can read them.

## Architecture contracts

An **architecture contract** is an agreement between the architecture function and a project about
what the project will deliver and which architecture it must conform to. It sounds bureaucratic, and
in its full form it is. Its light form is a short page per work package: the target building blocks,
the principles that apply, the decision records it must follow, and the review it will go through.

## Compliance reviews and dispensations

A **compliance review** checks a project's design or implementation against the architecture.
TOGAF grades the result on a scale from *irrelevant* through *consistent*, *compliant*,
*conformant* and *fully conformant* to *non-conformant*, and the distinction between the middle
grades is finer than most organisations need. What matters is the outcome: **conformant, or not, and
if not, why**.

When a project cannot or should not conform, the board can grant a **dispensation**: a recorded,
time-limited exception. At Carcará, the pilot stations kept their old PCs for one quarter because
the tablets were late; the dispensation said so, had an end date, and was closed when the tablets
arrived. **An exception with an end date is governance; one without is a new architecture nobody
decided.**

## Governance that helps

Lesson 13 is about architecture reviews in depth. The principle it develops starts here: governance
earns its keep when it **finds problems while they are cheap**, early in a design, and when the
people being governed can predict its answers from the written principles. A board that surprises
teams at the end is a gate; a board whose answers teams already know is a standard.
