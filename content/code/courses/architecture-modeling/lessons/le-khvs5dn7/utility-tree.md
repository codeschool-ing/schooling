**A utility tree arranges the quality scenarios under the attributes they refine, and rates each one
on two scales: how important it is to the business, and how hard it is to achieve.** It comes from
ATAM, the architecture evaluation method lesson 13 walks through, and it is useful on its own as
soon as a system has more than a handful of scenarios.

## The shape

The root is *utility*, the overall goodness of the system. Under it come the quality attributes,
under each attribute its refinements, and the scenarios are the leaves:

Each leaf carries a pair of ratings, **(importance, difficulty)**, each **H**, **M** or **L**:

- **importance** is the business's call: how much does it matter if this scenario is not met?
- **difficulty** is the architect's call: how hard is it to meet with the current design, or any
  plausible one?

## Reading the ratings

The pairs sort the scenarios into four groups, and the groups decide where design effort goes:

| rating | what it means | Carcará's |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **(H, H)** | important and hard: these drive the architecture | QS-01 peak purchases, QS-02 offline boarding |
| **(H, M)** or **(H, L)** | important and manageable: make sure they are designed for, then move on | QS-03 database failover, QS-05 refund audit |
| **(M or L, H)** | hard but not very important: question whether to do them at all | multi-region active-active, asked for once |
| **(L, L)** | cheap and minor: let the teams handle them | the ticket e-mail rendering in old mail clients |

**The (H, H) leaves are where the architecture is decided.** At Carcará there are two, and almost
every major decision record in the log traces back to one of them: seat holds in Redis to QS-01,
the local ticket list to QS-02.

## Why a tree and not a list

A list of thirty scenarios has no shape, and in a list every scenario looks equally urgent. The
tree does three things a list does not:

- it shows **which attributes have no scenarios at all**, which is a gap, usually a forgotten
  conversation;
- it makes the **ranking explicit**, so a disagreement about priorities happens on paper, before
  the design, rather than in production;
- it limits the number of **(H, H)** leaves. If everything is rated (H, H), the business has not
  ranked anything, and the tree is sent back with that question.
