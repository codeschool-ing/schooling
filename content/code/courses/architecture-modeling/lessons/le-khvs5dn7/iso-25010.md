**ISO/IEC 25010 is the international standard's model of software product quality: a list of
quality characteristics, each divided into sub-characteristics.** Nobody designs from it directly.
Its use for an architect is as a **checklist**: walk the list, ask whether each characteristic matters
for this system, and you find the qualities nobody mentioned until the night they failed.

| characteristic | the question it asks of Carcará |
| --- | --- |
| **functional suitability** | does it do what passengers, agents and drivers need, completely and correctly? |
| **performance efficiency** | how fast, and with how much hardware, at the São João peak? |
| **compatibility** | does it work alongside Frota, the gateway and the station printers? |
| **interaction capability** | can a passenger with a slow phone, or with low vision, buy a ticket? |
| **reliability** | is it available when buses leave, and does it recover after a failure? |
| **security** | who can read a passenger's CPF, and is every refund traceable to a person? |
| **maintainability** | can a team change the refund policy in a week without breaking boarding? |
| **flexibility** | can it run in another cloud region, or grow to a partner company's routes? |
| **safety** | can it put anybody in danger? |

The 2011 edition had eight characteristics and called two of these *usability* and *portability*;
you will meet the old names in older documents and job descriptions. The 2023 revision renamed them
*interaction capability* and *flexibility*, and added **safety**.

## Using it as a checklist, not a target

Two mistakes are common:

- **Treating every characteristic as a requirement.** *The system shall be maintainable, portable and
  secure* is the list copied into a document. It constrains nothing. The checklist's output should be
  a handful of characteristics that matter here, each turned into scenarios, as the next section
  does.
- **Stopping at the characteristic.** *Reliability* is a heading, not a requirement. Its
  sub-characteristics, such as **availability**, **fault tolerance** and **recoverability**, are
  closer to something you can measure, and even they need a number and a situation.

For Carcará, walking the list took Tainá and Joana an hour and found two qualities nobody had
written: **interaction capability**, because 30% of ticket sales happen on phones more than five years old, and **safety**. The team first dismissed safety and then kept it for one case: a driver
must never be pressed into boarding more passengers than seats, so the app refuses a scan once the
bus is full, whatever the list says.

## What the standard does not do

It gives names, not numbers. It does not say how fast is fast enough, and it cannot, because that
depends on the business. The numbers come from the people who own the business problem, through the
questions `architecture-role` lesson 7 practised, and they go into scenarios.
