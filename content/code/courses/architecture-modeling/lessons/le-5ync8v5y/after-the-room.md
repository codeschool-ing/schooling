**A presentation lasts an hour; what is left behind lasts as long as somebody keeps it.** The
decision Celeste took in the review, and the budget the directors approved on Monday, were acted on for months, by people who were not in the room and who
had only what was sent afterwards.

## The pack

After each of the three meetings, Tainá sent a short pack, the same shape every time:

| part | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **one paragraph** | what was decided, by whom, on which date |
| **the views shown** | exported from the model, with their version and date |
| **the records** | links to the decision records behind it, ADR-0022 among them |
| **the findings** | the review's findings, with owners and dates, in the team tracker |
| **the open questions** | what was asked and not answered, with who answers and when |

None of it was new work. Every part already existed somewhere in what the course built: the model of
lesson 12, the records of lesson 5, the findings of lesson 13, the owners and dates of lesson 11.
**The pack is where those things are pointed at together**, and it is what a new engineer like Bento
reads in October to understand why the stations have two links.

## What this course gave you

Fourteen lessons ago, Carcará had a wiki of 140 pages that nobody trusted and a system nobody could
draw from memory. Look at what now exists, and which lesson made it:

- **notations to draw with**: UML (lesson 2), C4 (lesson 3), flows, states and context maps
  (lesson 4), ArchiMate (lesson 9);
- **records of why**: decision records (lesson 5), requirements and quality scenarios (lesson 6),
  the analyst's view of a business need (lesson 7);
- **frameworks to place the work in an organisation**: TOGAF (lesson 8), IAF (lesson 9), Zachman
  (lesson 10);
- **ways to keep it true**: living documentation and fitness functions (lesson 11), diagrams as code
  (lesson 12), reviews (lesson 13);
- **and a way to put it in front of people**, this lesson.

None of these is the job on its own. The job is choosing which one a question needs, and that
choice is the thread through all fourteen lessons: **a diagram answers a question, for a reader,
and stays true only if somebody keeps it so.**

## Where the track goes next

The `software-architecture` track goes on from here into the ground the models stand on: the
terminal, networks, the cloud, infrastructure as code, observability, data, enterprise software
and processes. It closes with
`architect-communication`, which takes the last step of this lesson and makes it a whole
course: writing, proposals and RFCs, adapting a message, translating risk, the
story of a decision and the questions after it. This course gave you the model; that one is about
the people the model is for.
