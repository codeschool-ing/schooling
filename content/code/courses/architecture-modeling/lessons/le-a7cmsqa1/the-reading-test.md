**A diagram is ready when a reader who was not there can answer its question from the page alone.**
Everything in this section is a way of finding out, before you hand it over, whether that is true.

Five parts carry the contract, and a reader looks for each of them, in roughly this order:

1. **A title that says what is shown and at what scope.** *Carcará ticket sales: containers*, not
   *Architecture*. If the diagram belongs to a set, the title says which one.
2. **A type on every element**, as well as a name. The reader should never have to infer from the
   shape whether something is a person, a service or a database.
3. **A label on every line**, a verb phrase in the direction of the arrow, with the protocol when the
   reader is technical.
4. **A legend** for every shape, colour and line style that carries meaning.
5. **A date or a version**, and ideally an owner. Lesson 11 is about why a diagram without a date
   cannot be trusted a year later.

## Eight questions, asked of every diagram

Read your own diagram as if you were the reader named at the top. These are the questions; any
"no" is something to fix.

| | question | the failure it catches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Does the title say what is shown? | a picture whose scope the reader has to guess |
| 2 | Could the question at the top be answered from this page alone? | the diagram drifted to a different question while it was drawn |
| 3 | Does every element have a name and a type? | the box called `Booking` that might be five things |
| 4 | Does every line have a label and a direction that agree? | arrows that mean calls in one place and data in another |
| 5 | Is every shape, colour and line style in the legend? | colour the reader decodes into a meaning nobody intended |
| 6 | Is there one kind of boundary, and is it named? | a box around things that are deployed together, owned together and trusted together, which are three different claims |
| 7 | Is anything on the page that the question does not need? | the boarding app on a picture about buying |
| 8 | Is the date on it, and is it still true? | the printout taped to the wall in 2023 |

The checklist is short on purpose. C4, in lesson 3, has a longer one of its own, and lesson 13
turns this kind of question into a review of a whole design.

## The test that no checklist replaces

**Give the diagram to one person like the intended reader and ask them to explain it back to you**,
without your help. Do not explain it first, and do not correct them while they talk. Every place
where their explanation differs from what you meant is a mark on the page that made a different
claim from the one you intended.

When Tainá tried this with the right-hand diagram from earlier in this lesson, Bento read the arrow
from the ticketing API to the payment gateway as *the gateway calls us back when the payment
clears*. That is also true, through a webhook, and it was not on the diagram at all. The fix was a
second, dashed line in the other direction labelled *sends payment confirmations to (webhook)*, and
a legend entry for dashed lines. One reader, five minutes, one real defect.
