**BABOK, the *Business Analysis Body of Knowledge*, is the International Institute of Business
Analysis's description of the discipline: what business analysis is for, the tasks it involves and
the techniques used to perform them.** The current guide is the third edition, published by the
IIBA in 2015. It is the reference behind the IIBA's certifications, and when a job description asks
for "BABOK", it means a person who works in its vocabulary.

## Business analysis, in one sentence

The guide defines business analysis as **the practice of enabling change in an enterprise by
defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders**. Two things in that
sentence are easy to miss and matter most:

- **It starts from a need, not a solution.** *Automate the reconciliation* is a solution somebody has
  already chosen. The need underneath is something like *Finance cannot close the month without two
  days of manual matching, and errors reach the auditors*. A business analyst's first job is to get
  from the first sentence to the second.
- **It is about change in general, not software.** A process, a policy, a contract or an
  organisational change are all solutions in BABOK's sense. Some needs are met without writing any
  code at all.

## A body of knowledge, not a method

**BABOK does not prescribe an order of work.** It lists knowledge areas, tasks and techniques, and
says that an analyst chooses among them according to the situation: a small change in an agile team
uses a handful of techniques lightly; a regulated change in a bank uses many of them formally. In
that respect it is like ISO/IEC 25010 from lesson 6: a vocabulary and a checklist, not a recipe.

That is also the most common misunderstanding about it. A team that tries to *apply BABOK* as a
process, every task in sequence, produces documents nobody reads. A team that uses it to ask *have
we identified the stakeholders? have we confirmed what we elicited? who approves a change to this
requirement?* catches the gaps that sink projects.

## Why an architect should know it

Three reasons, and the third is the strongest:

1. **You will work with analysts**, and their words, *business requirement*, *transition
   requirement*, *elicitation*, *traceability*, are BABOK's. Lesson 6's requirements came out of
   exactly this kind of work.
2. **Corporate job descriptions ask for it**, alongside TOGAF, which is lessons 8 to 10.
3. **On most teams there is no analyst**, and the architect does the work without the name. Knowing
   the discipline means doing it on purpose rather than by accident.

The certifications, for the record, are the ECBA for beginners, the CCBA for practitioners with some
experience and the CBAP for senior analysts. This lesson does not prepare you for them; it gives you
the parts of the discipline an architect uses.
