**Putting an app on the App Store, or on anybody's iPhone but your own, needs a paid membership of
the Apple Developer Program, and it costs 99 US dollars a year.** That is the figure Apple's
programs page gave on 30 October 2026, "$99 annual membership", and its help page on fee waivers
names the same fee, "99 USD (or in local currency where available)". Nothing in this course has
asked you to pay, and nothing in this lesson or the next asks you to. The two lessons describe what
the membership unlocks so that you know what you would be paying for, and **none of it was run for
the course**: no membership was bought, and no signing identity was made.

## What a free account already does

You do not need the membership to learn, to build, or to run the app on your own phone. Apple's
developer account overview, read the same day, says that an Apple Account signed in to Xcode with no
membership is a **Personal Team**, with these limits:

| with a free Personal Team | as Apple's page puts it |
|---|---|
| App IDs | "up to 10 App IDs, which expire after 7 days" |
| devices | "up to 3 devices, which expire after 7 days" |
| apps per device | "up to 3 apps per device" |
| how long an install lasts | profiles "expire 7 days from issuance", and you rebuild and reinstall |

**So every lesson before this one, including the simulator and your own iPhone, works with a free
account.** What the free account does not have, in the same page's comparison table, is App Store
Connect, TestFlight and Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles on the website, which lessons 21 and 22
are about.

## What the membership adds, and what it asks of you

- **Distribution.** App Store Connect, TestFlight and a distribution certificate. Lesson 22.
- **Longer-lived signing.** Certificates and profiles you manage on the website, for up to 100
  registered devices of each type, according to Apple's page on renewal.
- **Enrolment as a person or as an organisation.** As an individual, Apple's enrolment page says,
  "your personal legal name will be listed as the seller on the App Store". An organisation needs to
  be a legal entity, a **D-U-N-S number**, which Dun & Bradstreet assigns and which the page says is
  free in most jurisdictions, a website on its own domain and a person with the authority to sign
  for it.
- **Renewal every year.** If the membership expires, the renewal page says, "your apps will no longer
  be available for download", though copies already installed keep working.

**There is a fee waiver**, and it is for organisations, not people. The waiver page lists nonprofit
organisations, accredited educational institutions and government entities that distribute only free
apps, and says plainly that an individual or a single-person business is not eligible. A school
publishing a free app for its own students may qualify; a student publishing their own app does not.

If you decide not to pay, this lesson and the next are still worth reading: every iOS job asks about
signing, and section 05 runs the one piece of it that runs on any computer.
