**Every build that goes to the store is reviewed by people at Apple against one public document, the
App Review Guidelines.** The copy read for this course was last updated on 16 October 2026 and read
on 30 October; Apple changes it several times a year, and **the version on the day you submit is the
one that counts**. Nothing here was run. The guidelines are long and readable, and five numbered
sections organise them: 1 Safety, 2 Performance, 3 Business, 4 Design and 5 Legal.

## The ones a study planner meets

Most of the document is about apps the planner is not: games of chance, medical devices, children's
apps with ads. The ones it does meet:

- **2.1, App Completeness.** No crashes, no placeholder text, no broken links, and a demo account if
  any feature needs signing in. Apple's App Review page says that "over 40% of unresolved issues are
  related to guideline 2.1", which makes it the commonest reason by far.
- **2.3, Accurate Metadata.** The listing of section 04 describes the app that was submitted.
- **4.2, Minimum Functionality.** The common-issues page warns that an app that "doesn't offer much
  functionality or content" may not be approved. A planner with one screen and a sum is close to that
  line; the four screens, offline sync and reminders of this course are what take it past.
- **5.1.1, Data Collection and Storage.** A privacy policy, permission before collecting, purpose
  strings that "clearly and completely describe your use of the data", and, in 5.1.1(v), "if your
  app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app".

**The last one is the planner's real risk.** If the sync of lesson 12 asks people to create an
account, the app has to let them delete it from inside the app, not by email. A course that adds
accounts adds that screen.

## How long it takes, and the two ways to ask for more

Apple's App Review page said that day that review works "around the clock, typically reviewing at
least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours". Two requests change
the course of a review:

- an **expedited review**, for "a critical bug" or an event the app is "directly associated with",
  with the steps to reproduce the bug;
- an **appeal** to the App Review Board, when you believe the reviewer "misunderstood your app's
  concept and functionality". The page asks for "only one appeal per submission".

Section 07 is what to do in between: read the rejection, reply in App Store Connect, fix, resubmit.
