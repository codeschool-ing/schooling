AWS sells three services that produce **findings**, records that say *something here may be wrong*, and
people confuse them because their findings end up in the same place. Each answers a different question:

| service | the question | what it reads |
|---|---|---|
| **GuardDuty** | is somebody doing something bad here, now? | CloudTrail events, VPC Flow Logs, DNS queries, and optionally S3, EKS, RDS and runtime activity |
| **Inspector** | which of our software has known vulnerabilities? | packages in EC2 instances, images in ECR, Lambda functions |
| **Security Hub** | what is wrong with our configuration, and where is everything else? | the account's settings, against standards; and the findings of the other two |

GuardDuty is **threat detection**: it looks for behaviour, credentials of an instance used from an
address outside AWS, an instance talking to a known mining pool, an unusual burst of `ListBuckets` from a
new place. It needs no agent and nothing to configure beyond turning it on, because it reads logs AWS
already has.

Inspector is **vulnerability management**: it lists the packages in each image and instance, compares
them against the published vulnerabilities, the CVEs, and scores each one, raising the score when the
vulnerable thing is reachable from the internet and an exploit is publicly available. Lesson 2's
`scan-on-push` was the basic form of this for one registry; Inspector is the continuous one.

Security Hub is two things. A **checker**: it runs controls from standards such as AWS's Foundational
Security Best Practices and the CIS benchmark against the account's configuration, and writes a finding
for each failure, a bucket open to the public, a trail without validation. And an **aggregator**: the
findings of GuardDuty, Inspector and other products arrive there in one format, the **AWS Security
Finding Format**, ASFF, so there is one queue to read instead of three.

## What the lab can show

**None of these services finds anything in moto**, because there is nothing to find: no traffic, no
packages, no real configuration. The lab enables them and sees what they keep, puts six made-up
findings into Security Hub in the real format, and spends the rest of the lesson on the part that is
the same everywhere, the question this lesson's title asks: **with a queue of findings, which one first?**
