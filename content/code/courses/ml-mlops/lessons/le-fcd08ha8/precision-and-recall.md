title: Precision and recall

Two ratios read the confusion matrix from the side that matters for a rare outcome.

**Precision: of the members the model flagged, how many lapsed?** 170 of 248, so 0.685. It is the
share of vouchers that reach somebody who needed one. Low precision means marketing pays for
vouchers that change nothing.

**Recall: of the members who lapsed, how many did the model flag?** 170 of 530, so 0.321. It is the
share of the problem the model reaches. Low recall means most of the lapsing members never get a
voucher at all.

**They pull against each other, and the threshold is the rope.** Lower the threshold and more
members are flagged: recall rises because more of the lapsing members are caught, and precision
falls because more of the flagged ones were staying anyway. Raise it and the opposite happens. A
model does not have *a* precision; it has one at every threshold.

Two more names, which modellers use and dashboards print:

- **F1** is a single number from the two, their harmonic mean, which is high only when both are.
  It is convenient for comparing models and it hides the trade, so it says nothing about where to
  put the threshold;
- **specificity** is recall for the other class: of the members who stayed, how many the model left
  alone, 2,522 of 2,600 here.

Ask which of precision and recall the people using the model care about more, and you have asked
what a false alarm costs against a miss. **There is always an answer to that, and it is never
"both equally"**, because a voucher and a lost member are not the same amount of money.
