---
title: Splitting by time, and the gap a label needs
version: 1
---

For a model that predicts the future, **the test has to be in the training data's future**, because
that is where every prediction the model makes in production will be. So the split is a date, not
a fraction of the rows, and choosing the dates has one rule that is easy to get wrong.

**A training row is only usable once its label is known.** A member's row as of 31 August is
labelled by their visits up to 29 November. If the model is deployed on 30 November, every training
row with a cutoff after 31 August has a label window that was still open on deployment night. In a
backtest, where all the data is already in the database, nothing stops you using those rows; in
production they would not have existed yet.

That is why `splits.py` trained on cutoffs up to 31 August and tested on 30 November: the newest
training label closes on 29 November, the day before the test cutoff. **The gap between the last
training cutoff and the test cutoff is the label window, 90 days, and it cannot be shorter.**

In general, for a model with a label window of *w* days:

| | the dates |
| --- | --- |
| the moment the model is used | *T* |
| the newest training cutoff it may learn from | *T* − *w* or earlier |
| the oldest feature it needs | *T* − 180 days, for this model's `_180d` features |

**This is a constraint the platform has to supply, not one a modeller can work around.** A model
retrained every night on "all the data" includes rows whose labels are still being written, and the
next section shows how far off those labels are.
