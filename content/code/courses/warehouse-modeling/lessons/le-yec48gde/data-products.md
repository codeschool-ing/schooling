title: What makes data a product
version: 1

"Data as a product" is easy to say and easy to mistake for "data with a nice name". The difference is that a
product has **users who did not build it**, and so it has to be usable without a conversation with its authors.
In practice a data product is a bundle of four things:

- **The data**, in one or more forms its users need: a table in the warehouse, files on the lake, an API.
- **The code** that produces it, from its sources to what it publishes, owned and run by the domain.
- **The metadata**: what each column means, the grain, how fresh it is, who owns it, who may read it. Lesson 12
  is about this part.
- **The guarantees**, written down: what will not change without notice, how late it may be, what quality checks it
  passes before it is published.

Dehghani's book lists the qualities a product should have, among them discoverable, addressable, understandable,
trustworthy, interoperable and secure. Each of them is a familiar idea from earlier lessons with an owner attached:
discoverable is the catalogue, interoperable is the conformed dimension, trustworthy is the reconciled number.

Ana's `fact_sales` is close to a product already. It has a grain, written in a comment; its dimensions are the
conformed ones; its December figure has been reconciled against finance's. What it lacks is the last of the four:
**a written promise**, which its users can rely on and its authors can be held to. That promise is called a
**data contract**, and the next section writes one and checks it by program.
