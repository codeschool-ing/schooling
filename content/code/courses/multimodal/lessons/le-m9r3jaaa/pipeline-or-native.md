There are two ways to build anything that crosses modalities, and the choice comes up in almost every lesson of this course.

A **cascade** is a chain of specialised models joined by text. A voice assistant built as a cascade runs speech-to-text on what the caller said, hands the transcript to a language model, and hands its answer to a text-to-speech voice. A **native** multimodal model takes the audio itself and gives audio back, with no text in between that anyone can read.

## What each one gives you

**A cascade shows its working.** Between every pair of stages there is text, and text can be logged, searched, corrected and measured. When the assistant gives a wrong answer you can tell whether it misheard (the transcript is wrong), misunderstood (the transcript is right and the reply is wrong) or mispronounced (the reply is right and the audio is wrong). Each stage can be replaced on its own: a better transcriber next year changes one line. And each stage can be measured against its own truth, which is exactly what lessons 5 to 7 do with Whisper and Piper in the lab.

**A native model hears what the text throws away.** A transcript does not carry tone, hesitation, a laugh or the fact that the caller is shouting. A native model takes all of that in, and it answers faster, because there is one model to wait for instead of three.

What a native model costs you is the window into it. There is no intermediate text to check, so "the assistant misunderstood" and "the assistant misheard" look the same from outside, and fixing one stage means changing the whole model.

## How to choose

Ask three questions, in this order.

1. **Does anyone need the text?** Call centres keep transcripts for quality checks and complaints; a caption file is text by definition. If somebody needs it, you are going to produce it anyway, and a cascade gives it to you for free.
2. **Is the delay a problem?** Three models in a row add their waits together. Lesson 6 measures how long the lab's voice takes before it says its first word, and lesson 13 adds up a whole chain.
3. **Is the meaning in the words or in the way they are said?** A refund request is in the words. Whether a customer is angry is partly in the voice.

Most products in this course end up as cascades, and the reason is the first question: the shop needs the text. That is a decision about your product rather than about which approach is better, and it should be made on purpose.
