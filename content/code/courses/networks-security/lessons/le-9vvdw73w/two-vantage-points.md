---
title: A sensor on the network and a sensor on the host
version: 1
---

The sensors of lessons 14 and 15 are **network intrusion detection systems** (NIDS): they watch
traffic on a segment and know nothing about what happens inside the machines. A **host intrusion
detection system** (HIDS) runs on a machine and watches the machine: its files, its processes, its logs,
its open ports. They answer different questions, and each is blind exactly where the other sees.

| | NIDS | HIDS |
|---|---|---|
| sees | every conversation crossing the segment, from every machine, managed or not | one machine's files, processes, users and logs |
| cannot see | inside encryption; traffic between two processes on one host | anything on another machine; traffic content before or after its own processes handle it |
| survives a compromise of the machine it watches | yes: it runs elsewhere | not necessarily: an intruder with root can tamper with it |
| costs | one sensor per segment, and a copy of the traffic | an agent on every host, and its upkeep |
| examples | Suricata, Snort, Zeek | AIDE, OSSEC and Wazuh, auditd, osquery |

The two rows in the middle are the reason to run both. **Encryption moved the interesting content of
network traffic out of the NIDS's reach**, and only the host that decrypts it sees the request. And an
intruder who controls a host controls what the host says about itself, so the network's independent
record of what that host did is the one that cannot be edited from the host. The next sections take
each blind spot on the lab.
