---
title: The conversation: short, clear, and not a debate
version: 1
---

The conversation in which a manager tells somebody their employment is ending is short. **The decision
comes in the first minute, it is not open for discussion, and the rest of the time belongs to the
person.** Every instinct a manager has, to soften it, to explain at length, to justify, makes it worse.

## Before the room

Renata met Sônia an hour before and went through what she would say, out loud, twice. That sounds
excessive and is not: under stress people say what they have practised, and what they have not
practised comes out as something they regret. They agreed that Sônia would be in the room, which is
Caju's practice, so that questions about terms could be answered straight away and so that there was a
second person who had heard the same words.

Renata had told Marcos the day before that she and Sônia wanted to meet him at ten the next morning
about the end of the plan. She did not tell him the outcome by message, and she did not leave him
unaware there would be a meeting.

## The first minute

What Renata said, after Marcos sat down:

> Marcos, thank you for coming in. I'm going to tell you the decision first. We have decided to end your
> employment at Caju. The plan asked for four of six tickets within the range we agreed, and it was
> three. Sônia is here to go through what this means for you, your notice and your payments, and to
> answer any questions about that.

**The decision is in the second sentence.** Not after five minutes about the plan, not after
reassurances, not phrased as a question. The reason is one sentence, and it refers to the plan Marcos had
read and corrected every week, so nothing in it was new to him.

## What not to do

Three things tend to happen in these conversations, and each causes harm.

**Debating the decision.** Marcos said the last ticket had been late because of a dependency on
Payments. Renata said: "I understand. The decision has been made, and I'm not able to change it in this
meeting." She did not argue about the ticket, because arguing implies the decision is open, and it is
not. If he believed the process had been unfair, Sônia explained how to raise it.

**Over-explaining.** A long account of everything that went wrong is for the manager's comfort, not the
person's. Marcos knew; the summaries had told him every week.

**False comfort.** "This might be the best thing for you" or "I'm sure you'll find something better
soon" is said to make the manager feel better and is heard as dismissive. What Renata said instead was
true and specific: that his work on documenting the notification library had been good, and that she
would give a factual reference about the parts of his work that had gone well, if he wanted one.

## The rest of the time

After the decision, the meeting belongs to the person. Some people want to leave at once. Some want to
ask questions. Some are angry, and that is legitimate. Marcos was quiet, asked Sônia two questions about
his notice period, and asked whether he could say goodbye to the team himself. Renata said yes, and
asked how he would like it done, which is the next section.

The whole meeting took twenty minutes. Sônia gave him the letter with the terms. Renata thanked him for
the three years. **A conversation like this cannot be made painless, and trying to make it painless is
usually how it becomes cruel**: by being slow, unclear, or full of hope that is not real.
