---
title: When and where, and the review that surprises nobody
version: 1
---

The same feedback, said at a different moment or in a different place, is a different message.
**Feedback works best soon after the event, in private when it is corrective, and in conversation
rather than in writing.** Each of those rules has a reason, and each has an exception worth
knowing.

## Soon

Feedback given within a few days of the event can be acted on while the person still remembers it.
Feedback saved for a quarterly review is about something the person can barely picture, and it
arrives with the implicit message that the manager noticed months ago and said nothing.

**The rule that follows is that nothing in a performance review should be a surprise.** Everything
in it should already have been said, in a one-to-one, close to when it happened. A review is a
summary of conversations that took place, not the place where they take place for the first time.
Renata's reviews at the end of her first cycle quoted her own one-to-one notes, and nobody read
anything in them they had not heard before.

The exception is the moment itself. Feedback given in the heat of an incident, or when either
person is angry, is rarely heard well. "Soon" means the next calm opportunity, which might be an
hour later or the next morning.

## Private, when it is corrective

Corrective feedback in front of other people adds a second message to the first: that the person
was corrected publicly. Whatever the feedback was about, the person will remember the audience
more than the content, and so will the audience. Renata gave Diego the feedback about the review in
their one-to-one, not in the team channel and not in the review thread.

Positive feedback is different. Recognition in front of the team can be worth more than in private,
because it tells everybody what the team values. Lesson 9 covers when public praise helps and when
it embarrasses the person it is meant for.

## In conversation, not first in writing

Written feedback looks efficient and loses most of what makes feedback work. The person cannot ask
what you meant, you cannot see how it lands, and a sentence that would have been gentle out loud
reads as cold on a screen, especially in chat. **Say it first, write it after.** The written version
goes in the one-to-one notes from lesson 5, as a record that the conversation happened.

The exception is a code review, which is written by nature. Review comments are feedback on the
work, and the SBI habit still helps there: point at the line, say what is wrong with it and why it
matters. "This will break when the clinic has no time zone set, because line 42 assumes one" is
feedback. "This is wrong" was the comment that started this lesson.

## Small and often

The commonest feedback failure is not bad feedback. It is too little: managers save things up until
there is enough to justify a conversation, and by then there is too much for one. Small, frequent
feedback, positive and corrective, keeps each piece light. A sentence at the end of a one-to-one,
"the way you summarised the incident for Helena was exactly right", costs nothing and builds the
habit that makes the harder conversations normal.

## Feedback upwards

Everything here applies in the other direction too. Renata asks her reports for feedback on herself,
as lesson 6 described, and when she gets it she treats it with the same care: thanks, one question,
what she will do. She also gives Otávio feedback, using the same structure, privately and soon.
**A manager who never gives feedback upwards is teaching their own team that feedback only travels
down.**
