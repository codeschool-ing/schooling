A hiring process is a funnel: many people at the top, one at the bottom, and a stage between each
that removes most of those who reach it. **Drawing the funnel with real numbers is the fastest way to
see where a process wastes candidates' time, interviewers' time, or good people.**

One hire from 240 applications. Two things in the table are worth looking at closely.

**The last row.** Of two offers, one was declined, because the candidate accepted another offer
during the week Caju took to decide. A process that loses half its offers at the end has spent all
the earlier effort on somebody it did not get.

**The technical interview.** Fifteen people reached it and nine were turned down. That is the most
expensive stage that removes the most people, which makes it worth asking whether the earlier stages
are filtering for the right things. Lesson 16 asks what that stage actually measures.

## Where candidates come from

Caju's applications came from three sources, and each behaved differently. Most of the 240 came from
the public advert. A handful came from referrals by current employees, and those passed the CV
screen at a much higher rate, which is the usual pattern and has a cost: referrals bring people who
resemble the existing team. **A funnel that relies on referrals hires the same team again**, which
is efficient and narrows the pool. Renata kept referrals, and made sure the advert went to two
communities of Brazilian engineers outside Caju's usual networks.

## What the funnel does not show

The table counts people. It does not count the experience of the 239 who were not hired, and every
one of them now has an opinion of Caju. Two habits matter here:

- **Every candidate who spoke to somebody gets an answer.** Not silence. Caju sends a short message
  within a week of each stage, and a candidate rejected after the final interviews gets a call.
- **Nobody waits more than a week between stages.** Candidates who are good are interviewing
  elsewhere, and a slow process loses them, as the declined offer showed.

## Your task

For a hiring process you know, as candidate or interviewer, draw the funnel with whatever numbers you
have or can estimate. Then answer in your notebook:

- Which stage removes the most people?
- Which stage costs the most hours per candidate?
- Is the expensive stage removing people the cheap stages could have removed?
- How long did a candidate wait between stages?
