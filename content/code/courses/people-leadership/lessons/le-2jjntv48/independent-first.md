title: Write it down before you hear anybody else
version: 1
---

Lesson 15 listed four parts of a structured interview, and the fourth was that each interviewer scores
alone before any discussion. **It is the part people most want to skip, because talking is faster and
feels more collaborative, and it is the part that protects the other three.** Without it, a debrief
turns four independent pieces of evidence into one opinion held four times.

## Anchoring

When people estimate something uncertain, the first number they hear pulls their estimate towards it,
even when they know the number is arbitrary. Psychologists call it anchoring, and Amos Tversky and
Daniel Kahneman described it in a famous 1974 paper on judgement under uncertainty.

A debrief is a room full of uncertain estimates. If the first person to speak says "strong hire", the
next person's "I had some doubts" softens into "a few small things", and by the fourth interviewer the
room has converged on the first opinion. **Nobody changed their mind on new evidence. They adjusted
towards the anchor**, which is the opposite of what four interviews were for.

## The loudest and the most senior

Two kinds of voice anchor a room harder than others. The most confident speaker, because confidence
is easily mistaken for evidence. And the most senior person, because disagreeing with them has a cost.
Some teams call this the HiPPO effect, after the highest-paid person's opinion.

Before Renata arrived, Agenda's debriefs opened with Diego, who was the most senior engineer and had
strong opinions quickly. Paula told Renata in her listening tour that she had stopped saying what she
thought in debriefs, because by the time it was her turn the decision felt made.

## How Caju does it

Caju's hiring tool hides every interviewer's feedback from the others until they have submitted their
own. Each interviewer submits within a day of the interview, before the debrief, with a score for each
row of their rubric and the evidence behind it, the way lesson 15 described. In the debrief, everybody
has already read everybody else's written feedback, and nobody has had a chance to be anchored by it
before writing their own.

Renata added two rules of her own:

- **The hiring manager speaks last.** Her opinion is the one most likely to anchor, because she makes
  the decision.
- **Nobody may change their written score in the debrief.** They can say they now see it differently,
  and why, and that goes in the notes. The written score stays as the record of what they thought
  before they heard anybody else.

## Why the record matters later

Keeping the original scores has a use beyond the decision. Over a few hiring rounds, Renata can see
how each interviewer's scores compare with the others', and with how the people hired actually
performed. That is the calibration in the title of this lesson, and the last section of it. It is
only possible if the scores were independent to begin with: scores written after a discussion measure
the discussion.
