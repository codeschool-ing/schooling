A manager sits between the company and their team, and some of what passes through them cannot be
shared or promised. **The hardest moments in the middle are when somebody on the team asks for a
promise that is not the manager's to give**: a promotion, a raise, that nobody will be laid off, that a
decision will be reversed. The temptation is to give it. The cost of giving it arrives later and lands on
the person who believed it.

## Three requests Renata could not grant

**Yara asked whether she would definitely be promoted if she led the booking migration.** Renata
believed she probably would. But the decision was the panel's, as lesson 12 described, and a promise
from the manager would have been a promise about somebody else's decision.

**Thiago asked, after rumours of cost-cutting at Caju, whether his job was safe.** Renata knew nothing
about any plans, and said so. She did not say "nobody on this team will be laid off", because she had no
authority to promise it and could not know it was true.

**Fábio asked for a raise to match an offer a friend had received elsewhere.** Pay bands at Caju were set
by the company, not by managers.

## What can be said instead

In each case, there was something honest Renata could say, and it had the same shape: what she knew,
what she did not, what she would do, and when she would come back.

| request | what Renata said |
|---|---|
| the promotion | "It's the panel's decision, not mine. Leading the migration is exactly the kind of evidence the staff row asks for, and I'll make sure it's in the case. I can't promise the outcome." |
| job security | "I don't know of any plans affecting this team. If I learn something I can share, I'll tell you as soon as I'm allowed to. I'm not able to promise anything about decisions I'm not part of." |
| the raise | "Pay bands are set by the company. I'll check where you sit in yours and whether there's a case for review, and tell you what I find by the end of the month." |

**Each answer promises the manager's own action, which can be kept, and refuses to promise somebody
else's decision, which cannot.** It is the distinction from lesson 7 between "I'll find out and tell you"
and "I'll get you the budget", applied to the questions that matter most to people.

## Information you cannot share

The same position applies to information. A manager sometimes knows about a reorganisation, a layoff, or
a colleague's departure before the team does, and is asked not to share it. Two rules help.

**Do not lie.** If somebody asks directly about something you cannot discuss, "I can't talk about that"
is honest, and people respect it more than they expect. "There's nothing going on" when there is will be
remembered, and every future reassurance will be discounted.

**Push for the information to be shared as early as possible.** Part of a manager's job upwards is to
argue that the team should hear things soon and directly, rather than through rumour. Renata did this
when Otávio's group was reorganised: she asked for the plan to be announced to the teams on the day it
was final, and it was.

## Carrying decisions you argued against

Lessons 7, 12 and 22 all came back to this: when a decision is made above, the manager carries it to the
team as the team's decision, even if they argued against it. **That is not dishonesty about the
disagreement**, which the manager can acknowledge if asked: "I argued for something different, the
decision went the other way, and here's what we're going to do." It is refusing to turn every decision
from above into a grievance the team can hold, which would leave the manager with a team that trusts
nobody above them and a manager above who cannot rely on them.

## The year

Renata's first year ended a few weeks after the reorganisation. Agenda had shipped the no-show work and
confirmations, hired Lucas, promoted Yara, let Marcos go, lost Camila, and had a hiring debrief, an
onboarding plan and one-to-ones that belonged to the people in them, none of which had existed twelve
months earlier. On the decisions page of her notebook, the line she had written on the first day,
"manage for at least two years, then decide again on purpose", had a year's worth of entries beneath it.
She left it where it was.
