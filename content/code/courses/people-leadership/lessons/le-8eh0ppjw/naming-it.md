---
title: The first conversation: naming the pattern plainly
version: 1
---

The first performance conversation has one job: **the person should leave it knowing, without any
doubt, that there is a concern about their performance, what it is, and what happens next.** Almost
every way it goes wrong is a version of the manager softening it until that knowledge does not
survive the meeting.

## What Renata prepared

Renata booked thirty minutes with Marcos, separate from their one-to-one, and told him the day
before what it was for: "I'd like to talk tomorrow about how the last couple of months have gone.
It's a serious conversation, and I'd rather you weren't surprised by that." Telling him in advance
was deliberate. A performance conversation sprung inside a routine one-to-one teaches the person to
dread every one-to-one afterwards.

She wrote down, before the meeting:

- **the pattern**, as observations: the upgrade, the two estimates, the forty minutes on call, with
  dates;
- **the feedback already given**, with the date of the conversation about the upgrade;
- **what the role requires** in those areas, from Caju's level description for a mid-level engineer,
  which lesson 11 looks at;
- **what she did not know**, which was why any of it was happening.

## What she said

She opened with the point, not with small talk and not with praise:

> I want to talk about a pattern I've seen over the last two months, because I'm concerned about
> it. There have been three things. The notification upgrade, where you were stuck for two weeks
> before I found out. The waiting-list and reschedule tickets, each estimated at three days and each
> taking more than two weeks. And the page on the 14th, which you acknowledged and didn't act on for
> forty minutes. We talked about the first one in March. Taken together, they're a concern about
> whether things are going well in the role, and I'd like to understand what's happening.

Then she stopped and listened, which is the subject of lesson 19.

Notice the words she used. "A pattern", "concerned", "a concern about whether things are going well
in the role". She did not say "performance improvement plan", because she was nowhere near one and
saying it would have been a threat. She also did not say "I'm sure it's nothing" or "just something
to keep an eye on", because it was something, and saying otherwise would have erased the purpose of
the meeting.

## Three ways the message gets lost

**Burying it.** Ten minutes of how the team is doing, a compliment, and then the concern in the last
five minutes, phrased as a question. The person remembers the compliment.

**Softening it into a suggestion.** "It might be worth thinking about your estimates a bit." That is
lesson 8's feedback, and the person has already had feedback. This conversation is about the fact
that feedback did not change the pattern.

**Arguing about each instance.** The person disputes the first example, and the manager spends the
meeting on whether the upgrade was really his fault. **The point is the pattern, not any one
example.** Renata's line, if it came to that, was going to be: "We might see that one differently.
What I want us to look at is the three together."

## What the person should leave with

Before the meeting ended, Renata checked: "Just so we're both clear about where we are: I'm concerned
about the pattern, I'd like us to meet again on Thursday once you've had time to think about it, and
in the meantime I'll write a short summary of what we talked about and send it to you." Marcos
repeated it back, more or less. **If the person cannot say what the meeting was about, the meeting
did not happen**, whatever the manager believes was said.
