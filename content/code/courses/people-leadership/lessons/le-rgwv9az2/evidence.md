---
title: Evidence: working at the next level, for long enough
version: 1
---

A promotion is a statement that somebody already works at the next level, not a reward for hoping
they will. **The evidence for it is a set of specific pieces of work, spread over enough time to show
a pattern, each mapped to a row of the ladder.** Most promotion cases that fail do so because they
offer effort, tenure or potential instead.

## What counts and what does not

When Yara told Renata she wanted to be considered for staff engineer, E4, Renata's first move was
to sort what they had into two piles.

| counts as evidence | does not, on its own |
|---|---|
| she designed the waiting list from an ambiguous problem, and three people built from her design | she works very hard |
| she led the migration of booking data across three teams, including the rollback plan | she has been at Caju for six years |
| two other teams adopted her pattern for idempotent reminders, and say so in writing | she is clearly ready |
| she mentored Thiago through his first design document | everybody likes working with her |

The left column is specific: a piece of work, a date, an outcome somebody else could check. The right
column is true and irrelevant. Effort and tenure are not on the ladder, for the reasons lesson 11
gave. "Ready" is the conclusion the case has to reach, not a piece of evidence for it. And being
liked is pleasant and is not what any row describes.

## Long enough to be a pattern

One excellent project shows that somebody can work at a level once. A promotion says they do so
reliably. **Most panels want to see the next level's work sustained over something like six months
to a year**, across more than one kind of situation, before they believe it is the person's normal
rather than a good season.

That is why the one-to-one notes from lesson 5 matter here. Renata had a year of them, with dates.
The waiting-list design was in April, the booking migration ran from June to September, the reminder
pattern spread in October, the mentoring of Thiago ran throughout. Four pieces across seven months,
each against a different row, is a pattern. **Without the notes, Renata would have remembered the
migration vividly and the rest vaguely**, which is the recency and vividness trap of lesson 7 aimed at
a promotion.

## Mapping to the rows

Each piece of evidence goes against the row it supports. For staff at Caju the rows are scope
beyond one team, autonomy over problems nobody has framed, influence on how other teams work, and
craft whose decisions hold up across teams. The migration supported scope; the waiting-list design
supported autonomy; the reminder pattern supported influence; and the rollback plan, which was used
once and worked, supported craft.

Mapping does two useful things. It shows where the case is thin: Renata noticed that most of the
influence evidence was about one pattern, which a panel might call a single success. And it keeps the
case honest about what the ladder actually asks, instead of what the person happens to be best at.

## When the evidence is not there yet

Sometimes, sorting the piles shows that the case cannot be made yet. That is not a failure of the
conversation; it is its most useful outcome. "Here is what's there, here is the row that's thin,
and here is the kind of work that would fill it" gives the person a plan, which lesson 13 is about.
What a manager must not do is put a thin case forward to avoid that conversation, because the panel
will have it instead, with less care, and the person will hear "no" from strangers.
