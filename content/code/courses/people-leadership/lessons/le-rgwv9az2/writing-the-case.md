A promotion case is a document read by people who do not know the person. **Each paragraph makes one
claim against one row of the ladder and backs it with evidence a stranger could check.** Written
that way, it can survive a panel. Written as a warm description of a great colleague, it reads as
advocacy, and panels discount advocacy.

## The structure

Caju's template, which is a common shape, has four parts.

1. **The summary.** Two or three sentences: the level asked for, and why in one line. "Yara has worked
   at staff scope for the past seven months, leading work across three teams and changing how two of
   them build reminders."
2. **One section per row.** Scope, autonomy, influence, craft. Each opens with the claim, then the
   evidence: what, when, the outcome, and where somebody can check it.
3. **The gaps.** What is thinner, said plainly, with what is being done about it.
4. **Other voices.** Short written statements from people outside the team who saw the work.

Each part has a reason. The summary is for the panel member who reads only the first paragraph,
which is most of them on a long day. The rows keep the case organised by what the ladder asks. The
gaps section, counter-intuitively, makes the case stronger: **a panel that finds a weakness the
manager did not mention starts doubting everything else**, and one that reads it in the manager's own
words sees a manager who knows the person.

## A paragraph, written badly and well

The first draft of Renata's influence section began: "Yara is a natural leader who is always willing
to help other teams and is widely respected across engineering."

Every word of it was true, and none of it could be checked. A panel member reading it learns that
Renata likes Yara. The rewrite:

> **Influence: two teams changed how they build reminders because of Yara's work.** In October,
> Yara wrote up the idempotency pattern she used to stop duplicate reminders (linked). The Payments
> team adopted it for payment notifications in November, and the Clinics team for appointment
> confirmations in December; both leads have written statements below. Separately, Yara mentored
> Thiago through his first design document in May, which the team now uses as its example.

The claim is in the first sentence, in bold, and it is a claim about effect, not character. The
evidence has months, names and links. The statements from the two leads are the part nobody can
dismiss as the manager's opinion.

## Words that weaken a case

Some words recur in cases that fail. They are worth searching for in a draft and replacing:

| weak | why | instead |
|---|---|---|
| "always", "never" | nobody believes it, and one counter-example sinks the sentence | the specific instances |
| "helped with" | hides what the person actually did | "designed", "led", "wrote", "decided" |
| "the team delivered" | gives the panel no way to see the person's part | what she did inside the team's work |
| "has potential" | promotion is for what is already true | evidence of the next level now |

## Your task

Write one row of a promotion case, in your notebook, for yourself or somebody you have worked with,
against a real ladder or Caju's. Then check:

- The first sentence is a claim about the work's effect, not about the person's qualities.
- Every piece of evidence has a date and something a stranger could open or ask about.
- None of the weak words from the table remain.
- You could name the thinnest part, and it is in the gaps section.
