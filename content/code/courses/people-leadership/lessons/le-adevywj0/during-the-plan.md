Writing the plan is the easy part. **The six weeks after it are where it is honest or not**: whether the
check-ins happen, whether the support arrives, whether the written summaries say what is true, and
whether the ending is decided by the plan or by something else.

## The check-ins

Renata met Marcos every Wednesday, for thirty minutes, and wrote a short summary afterwards. Each
summary had the same three lines: what happened against the expectation that week, what support was
given, and what was agreed for the next week.

**The summaries have to say what is true, both ways.** In week two, Marcos revised an estimate within a
day for the first time, and Renata wrote it down in exactly those words. In week four, a ticket went to
twice its estimate without a word until the end, and she wrote that down too. A summary that says
"good progress" in a week when the expectation was not met is the same failure as the vague one-to-one
notes in lesson 5: it removes the evidence the ending will need, and it misleads the person about where
they stand.

## The manager's side

A plan binds the manager too. If Yara had not paired on the first three estimates, because the team was
busy, the plan would have failed on Caju's side, and it could not fairly have been held against Marcos.
**Support promised and not given voids the plan**, and the honest response is to say so and extend it,
or to start again.

Renata kept her own list: the three pairing sessions, the six check-ins, the six summaries. She ticked
each off as it happened, so that at the end she could say, and show, that Caju had done what it said.

## The three endings

At the end of the period, one of three things is true, and each has a correct response.

**The expectation was met.** Say so, in a conversation and in writing, and close the plan. The person
should hear clearly that it is over and that they met it. Managers sometimes leave a met plan hanging,
out of caution, and the person spends months wondering whether they are still on it.

**It was clearly not met.** The next step is a decision about the employment, made with the people team
and the manager's own manager, which lesson 21 is about. It should not be a surprise to anybody,
because the summaries said every week where things stood.

**It was partly met.** This is the hardest, and the most common. An extension is reasonable once, if
there is a specific reason to think more time would close the gap: a part of the expectation that only
came up late, or support that arrived late. An extension because the manager does not want to make the
decision is the dishonest version again, in a different form.

## How Marcos's plan ended

At the end of six weeks, the first half of the expectation had been met completely: every estimate was
written after reading the code, and every overrun after week three was raised within a day. The second
half had not: three of the six tickets finished within one and a half times their estimate, against the
four the plan required, and Renata's summaries recorded each one.

Renata, Sônia and Otávio met. They considered an extension and decided against it: the support had all
been given, nothing had arrived late, and there was no specific reason to believe two more weeks would
produce a different result. They decided to end Marcos's employment. **The decision rested on the
plan's own record, which Marcos had read and corrected every week**, and that is what lesson 21 begins
from.
