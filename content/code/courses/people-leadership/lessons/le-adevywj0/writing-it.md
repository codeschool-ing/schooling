A plan has five parts, and each one has a failure that makes it either unfair or useless. **Written
well, it reads like a careful agreement both people could sign. Written badly, it reads like a list of
complaints with a deadline.**

## The five parts

| part | what it says | the failure |
|---|---|---|
| the gap | what has been happening, as observations, with dates | judgements ("unreliable", "careless") |
| the expectation | what meeting the role looks like, in measurable terms | vague goals ("communicate better") |
| the support | what the manager and the company will provide | none, or support that never arrives |
| the period and check-ins | how long, and when progress is reviewed | too short to change a habit, or no check-ins |
| the consequences | what happens if the expectation is met, and if it is not | softened, or left unsaid |

## Marcos's plan

Renata wrote it with Sônia, Caju's people partner, who checked it against Caju's process and against
what Brazilian labour law requires of the company. In shortened form:

> **The gap.** Of the four tickets you worked on between 6 May and 31 May, two took more than twice
> their estimate (the reschedule cleanup, 3 days estimated, 8 taken; the time-zone migration, 2 days
> estimated, 6 taken). In neither case was the estimate revised, or the delay raised, before the work
> was late. We agreed on 2 May that this was the expectation.
>
> **The expectation, for the six weeks from 10 June.** For each ticket, you write an estimate after
> reading the relevant code. When a ticket is going to exceed its estimate by more than half, you say so
> in the ticket and to me within one working day of knowing. Over the period, at least four of the next
> six tickets finish within one and a half times their estimate, or were revised in time.
>
> **The support.** Yara will pair with you on estimating the first three tickets. We meet weekly, for
> thirty minutes, to review the week against this plan. If something outside work is affecting you
> again, tell me or Sônia, and we will adjust.
>
> **Check-ins.** Every Wednesday; a short written summary from me after each, which you can correct.
>
> **The consequences.** If the expectation is met, the plan closes and is recorded as met. If it is not
> met, the next step may be the end of your employment at Caju. We will talk before any decision.

## What makes it fair

**Every sentence of the gap can be checked.** Dates, ticket names, numbers, and a reference to the
conversation where the expectation was set.

**The expectation is achievable and measurable.** "Four of six" leaves room for one or two estimates
going wrong for reasons nobody could foresee, which happens to everybody. A plan that demands
perfection is a plan designed to fail.

**The support is concrete and has names.** "We'll support you" is not support. Yara, the first three
tickets, every Wednesday, is.

**Six weeks is long enough to see a habit change.** Two weeks would measure luck; six months would
leave the team, and Marcos, in suspense for too long.

**The consequences are said plainly.** Renata found the last sentence the hardest to write and the most
important. Leaving it out would have been kinder for an afternoon and dishonest for six weeks.

## Your task

Write, in your notebook, an improvement plan for a case you invent, in the five parts. Then check each
part against the failure in the table:

- The gap contains no adjective about the person.
- Somebody else could tell, at the end, whether the expectation was met.
- The support has at least one name and one date.
- The consequences are stated in a sentence the person could not misread.
