---
title: The shared document, and whose topics come first
version: 1
---

The single most effective change to a drifted one-to-one is a shared document. **Both people can
add topics during the week, the person's topics come first, and the manager's go at the bottom.**
It takes five minutes to set up and it changes who the meeting belongs to, because it makes the
agenda visible and gives the person a place to put things before they forget them.

## The template

Renata and each person she manages keep one running document, newest meeting at the top. Each
meeting gets a short block:

| part | what goes in it | who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| date | the day of the meeting | either |
| their topics | anything they want to discuss, added during the week | the person |
| my topics | anything the manager wants to raise, added during the week | the manager |
| notes | a line or two per topic, written at the end | the manager, read by both |
| actions | who will do what, by when | both, agreed before leaving |

The order of the first two parts is the point. When the meeting starts, it starts with the person's
first topic. If there is time left, the manager's topics come after. If there is not, the manager's
topics carry over, or move to a message, because the manager has other ways to reach the person and
the person has fewer ways to reach the manager.

## What goes in "their topics"

At the start, the section is often empty, and the manager has to help it fill. Two things help.

**Say what it is for.** Renata told each person, in the first meeting, the four kinds of
conversation from the first section: obstacles, how they are, where they are going, and feedback.
People do not bring growth topics to a meeting they think is for status, and they will not guess
otherwise.

**Model it with your own topics.** In the first weeks, Renata's own topics were things like "I'd
like your view on whether the planning meeting is still useful" and "I'd like to understand what
you want from the next year." Questions about the team and about the person, not about tickets,
teach by example what the document is for.

## What goes in "my topics"

The manager's section is for things the manager needs from the person or needs them to know, that
are not urgent enough for a message and not public enough for a team meeting. Feedback is the
main one. Context from above, such as an upcoming reorganisation the person will be affected by, is
another, when it can be shared.

**What does not go there is status.** If Renata needs to know whether the calendar-sync work will be
ready for the clinics' association meeting, she asks in the ticket or in a message, the day she
needs it. Putting it in the one-to-one document invites the meeting to drift back.

## When the person brings nothing for weeks

Some people never add topics, and it is worth being careful about the reason. It may be that they
are content and private, which is legitimate: not everybody wants to discuss their career with
their manager every week. It may be that they do not trust the meeting yet. It may be that the
manager has, without noticing, reacted badly to something they raised before.

Renata had this with Marcos. For six weeks his section was empty and the meetings were short. She
did not push. In the seventh week she asked directly, as one of her own topics: "Your section has
been empty for a while. Is that because things are fine, or because this meeting isn't useful to
you? Either answer is fine." He said the second, and that his previous manager had used
one-to-ones to go through his mistakes. **The empty section was information, and asking about it
directly was the only way to read it.** Lesson 19 comes back to Marcos.

## Your task

Set up the document for a real one-to-one, if you have one, or for a fictional one with somebody
from the Caju team. Fill in the first meeting's block with at least two topics in each of the two
sections, then check:

- Their section comes first.
- None of your topics could be answered by looking at the tracker.
- At least one of your topics is a question about them, not about their work.
- There is a place for actions, with an owner and a date per line.
