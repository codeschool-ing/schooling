**There is nothing to install for this course.** No program runs in it, and no lesson asks you to
type a command. The subject is people, and the practice is writing, deciding and holding
conversations. What you need is a place to write, a place to count and, now and then, a place to
draw.

## The three tools

| what | used for | free options |
|---|---|---|
| a text editor or word processor | one-to-one notes, feedback drafts, a promotion case, an improvement plan | the editor your system already has (Notepad, TextEdit, gedit), or LibreOffice Writer |
| a spreadsheet | a hiring funnel, an interview scorecard, an attrition rate | LibreOffice Calc, or any spreadsheet you already use |
| something to draw with | a career ladder, an org chart, the dependencies between teams | paper and a pen, or diagrams.net in a browser |

**LibreOffice is the one recommendation**, because it is free, runs on Windows, macOS and Linux,
needs no account, and keeps your files on your own computer. That last point matters more here than
in a technical course, for a reason the end of this section explains. A spreadsheet or document
service you already use at work or at home is fine too. Nothing in the course depends on one
company's product, and every template is written out in the lesson that uses it, so you can copy it
into whatever you have.

If you cannot install anything on the computer you are using, a plain text file is enough for the
writing, and every calculation in the course is small enough to do on paper.

## How the practice works

The questions at the end of each lesson are marked by a machine, and a machine cannot read a
paragraph. So they are built as scenarios: here is what somebody said, here are four replies, which
one does what the lesson describes. The three wrong replies are each wrong for a reason the
lesson names, and the explanation you get after answering says which reason. **Read the
explanations even when you were right**, because the wrong options are the mistakes managers
actually make.

The other half of the practice is writing, and nobody marks it. Several lessons end a section with a
task: draft the feedback, write the job description, plan the first week of a new hire. **Do them
in the notebook**, then compare what you wrote with the checklist the lesson gives. The checklist is
the marking. Being honest with it is your part.

## The notebook

A manager's notebook is the record the long loops of the previous section need. Without it you
cannot learn from a decision whose result arrives in six months, because you will not remember what
you decided or why.

Start it now as one document, or one folder of documents, with four parts:

1. **People.** One page per person you manage, or, if you manage nobody yet, per person in the
   Caju team as the course introduces them. Each page has their goals, the notes of every
   one-to-one, and the feedback you gave with its date.
2. **Decisions.** One line per decision that matters: the date, what you decided, why, and what you
   expect to see if you were right. Lesson 4 adds who decided.
3. **Promises.** Everything you said you would do, for whom and by when. Lesson 7 is built on this
   list.
4. **Drafts.** The written tasks from this course, and later the real ones: a promotion case, a
   job description, a plan.

If you are not managing anybody yet, use the Caju team as your people and your own experience as a
team member as material. Several tasks ask you to remember a manager you had and what they did. You
will find that most people have more examples of what not to do than of what to do, and both kinds
are useful.

## Notes about people are personal data

The notebook holds things people told you in confidence: that somebody is unhappy, looking for
another job, going through a divorce, managing an illness. **Keep it somewhere only you can open**,
not in a shared drive and not in the team's wiki. Brazil's data protection law, the LGPD, treats
health information as sensitive personal data, and most countries have an equivalent rule; your
company's policy may say more.

Two habits follow from that. Write what you need to act on and nothing more: "asked for flexible
hours on Thursdays for the next two months" is a note, the medical reason behind it is not. And
write as if the person might one day read it, because in many places they have the right to ask.
**A note you would be embarrassed to show the person it is about is a note you should not have
written.**
