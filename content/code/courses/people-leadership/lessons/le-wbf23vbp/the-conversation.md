The second conversation with Marcos had a different purpose from the first. Lesson 10's conversation
named the pattern. **This one was for learning why, and everything about it was designed so that
Marcos could say things he would not say to somebody he expected to judge him.**

## How Renata set it up

She told him in advance, as before: "On Thursday I'd like to understand what's been making the last
couple of months hard. It's not a continuation of the serious part of last week's conversation; it's
me trying to work out what would help." She booked an hour, not thirty minutes, and a room rather than a
call.

She opened by saying what she had found herself, the two things from the previous section, before
asking him anything:

> I've been looking at what I could have done differently, and I've found two things. Nobody told you
> that this team wants to hear about being stuck within a day or two, and given how your last manager
> handled that, I can see why you didn't. And the notification library was undocumented, and the only
> person who knew it left last year. I should have known that before I gave it to you.

**Starting with the manager's own part changes what the person is willing to say.** It signals that the
conversation is about finding causes, not assigning blame, and it makes it easier for the person to
name their own part without it feeling like a confession.

## The questions

Then she asked, using the questions with edges from lesson 6, one at a time, with silence after each:

- "What do you think is expected of you in this role? Tell me in your own words."
- "On the two tickets that took longer, where did the time go?"
- "Is there anything about how the team works that's making it harder?"
- "Is there anything outside work affecting things at the moment? You don't need to tell me what. I
  just want to know whether it's a factor."

She did not ask whether he cared about the work. If the answers to the first four left it as the only
explanation, there would be time for that question later.

## What Marcos said

His description of what was expected was close to Renata's on code and far from it on communication:
he thought a good engineer solved problems alone, and that saying "I'm stuck" was admitting he was not
good enough. On the two tickets, he said he had underestimated both because he had not looked at the
code before estimating, and had been embarrassed to revise the estimate once he did. On the page, he
said he had acknowledged it half asleep and fallen asleep again, because his father was in hospital and
he had not slept properly for a week. He had not wanted to swap his on-call week,
because that would have meant explaining why.

**None of the six causes was a single answer.** Expectations were part of it, the environment was part
of it, and his father's illness was part of it. Skill, on the code, was not the problem; asking for help
and estimating honestly were habits, not abilities.

## What happened next

Three responses followed, one per cause, and none of them was a judgement of Marcos's worth:

1. **Expectations:** Renata wrote down, with him, what the team expects about raising problems and
   revising estimates, and they agreed to check it weekly.
2. **Environment:** Yara and Marcos documented the notification library together, which also gave him
   somebody to ask.
3. **Outside work:** with Sônia's help, Marcos took his next two on-call weeks off the rota without
   needing to explain to the team, and learnt about the confidential support Caju offers.

What was left, after those, was whether his performance would change. Lesson 20 picks the story up
there, because a month later it had changed in some places and not in others, and that is when an
improvement plan, written honestly, becomes the right tool.

## Your task

Think of a time somebody's work disappointed you, at work or elsewhere. In your notebook, go through the
six questions from the previous section for that case. Then check:

- How many of the six could you answer without asking the person?
- Which cause did you assume at the time, and where does it fall in the sequence?
- What would you have opened the conversation with, if you had started with your own part?
