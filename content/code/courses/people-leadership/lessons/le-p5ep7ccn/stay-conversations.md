title: Stay conversations, before the exit conversation
version: 1
---

An exit interview asks somebody why they are leaving, at the moment their answer can no longer change
anything and they have every reason to be polite. **A stay conversation asks the same questions of
somebody who has not decided to leave**, while the answer can still change something.

## The questions

After Camila left, Renata added a stay conversation to every person's one-to-ones, once every six
months, taking the place of a normal meeting. It has a handful of questions, asked in the style of
lesson 6:

1. What keeps you here? What would you miss most if you left?
2. What would make you think about leaving?
3. When was the last time you thought about it, and what prompted it?
4. Is there work you would like to be doing here that you are not?
5. What is one thing I could change that would make this a better job for you?

The third question is the one people hesitate over, and the most useful. It assumes that thinking about
leaving is normal, which it is, and asks for a specific moment rather than a general state.

## What Renata heard

In the first round, Yara said what kept her was the scope she had after her promotion, and that what
would make her leave was being pulled back into hands-on work on Agenda alone. Diego said the last time
he had thought about leaving was after his staff case was refused without explanation, under the
previous manager, and that the conversation in lesson 12 had been the reason he stayed. Paula said she
had thought about it during the year when nobody noticed the glue work she was doing.

Thiago said he had not thought about it, and Renata believed him. Lucas, a few months in, said the
question had never occurred to him; the onboarding in lesson 18 had worked.

**Three of five people named a moment, and each moment was something within Renata's control**, which
is what makes the conversation worth having: it surfaces the reasons while they can still be acted on.

## Doing something with it

A stay conversation that changes nothing is worse than none, for the reason lesson 7 gave: asking and
then doing nothing teaches people not to answer. For each person, Renata wrote down one thing to do,
on her promise list, with a date:

- Yara: protect her cross-team scope when the next reorganisation was discussed, and say so to Otávio.
- Diego: keep working on the visibility of his work, towards a second staff case.
- Paula: keep the glue-work rotation going, and keep recording her glue work against the ladder.

## Counter-offers

One more situation belongs here. Sometimes a person resigns and the company considers a counter-offer:
more money to stay. It can work when money was the real reason. It often does not, because the other
reasons, the growth, the manager, the workload, are still there after the raise, and the person now
knows that resigning was the way to get paid properly, as do their colleagues, eventually.

**If the reasons someone is leaving could have been fixed with a counter-offer, they could usually have
been fixed earlier**, in a stay conversation, at less cost and with more trust. Caju made Camila no
counter-offer, because her reasons were not mainly money and Renata knew it.

## Your task

Ask yourself the five questions about your own current or most recent job, and write the answers in
your notebook. Then check:

- Could you name a specific moment for question 3?
- Was the cause of that moment within your manager's control?
- Did anybody ever ask you?
