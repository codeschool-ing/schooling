A load balancer in front of three web servers removes the web servers as single points of failure, and
lesson 19 is about how it shares the work among them. **It also becomes one itself**: every request
passes through it, and when it stops, three healthy servers stand behind a door nobody opens. The answer
is the same as for the gateway in lesson 15, a second machine and an address that can move, and the
question is what the second machine does while nothing is wrong.

There are two answers, and a group of machines arranged either way is called a **cluster**:

In **active-passive**, one balancer holds the public address and carries every request. The other is running, configured identically, and carries nothing until the first fails. It is the gateway pair of
lesson 15 applied to a balancer, and it is easy to reason about: at any moment exactly one machine is in
charge, and its capacity is the site's capacity.

In **active-active**, both balancers carry traffic, each on its own public address, and each is the backup
for the other's. Clients are spread between the two addresses, usually by DNS returning both. Nothing
is idle, which is the attraction.

The common belief is that active-active therefore doubles the capacity and keeps the redundancy. **It
does one or the other, never both at once.** Take two balancers that each run at 70% of what one machine can
handle. On a normal day the site has 140% of one machine's capacity. On the day one of them fails, the
survivor is asked for 140% and falls over, taking the half that was still working with it. Active-active
is only redundant while the two together carry no more than one of them could alone, and at that load it
buys exactly the capacity active-passive has, with more machinery. The last section of this lesson puts
numbers on it.

What active-active does buy is a warm spare that is known to work. A passive machine can sit broken for
months, with an expired certificate or a configuration nobody copied to it, and the failover is the
moment everyone finds out. **A machine that carries real traffic every day is tested every day.** Both
shapes are built in the lab's data centre in this lesson, with HAProxy as the balancer and keepalived, the
VRRP daemon of lesson 15, moving the addresses.
