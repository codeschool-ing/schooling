---
title: Shaping delays, policing drops
version: 1
---

"Limit that link to 5 Mbit/s" sounds like one instruction. It is two, and they behave so differently
that a link configured with the wrong one can look fine on a speed test and fail on a call. **Both hold
traffic to a rate. They differ in what they do with a packet that arrives when the rate has been used
up**: a shaper keeps it and sends it later, a policer throws it away.

| | a shaper | a policer |
|---|---|---|
| a packet over the rate | waits in a queue | is dropped, or re-marked |
| what it costs | delay | loss |
| what TCP sees | a longer round trip | retransmissions |
| what comes out | smooth, at the rate | bursts clipped at the rate |
| needs | a queue, and memory for it | a counter |

The picture is the whole lesson in advance, and the rest measures it. A burst arrives faster than the
rate allows. The shaper sends it at the rate, so the tail of the burst leaves later than it arrived and
nothing is lost. The policer lets through what fits the rate and discards the rest on the spot, so
nothing waits and part of the burst is gone.

**Neither is the better one; they sit on opposite sides of the same link.** A provider polices, at its
edge, because it sells a rate and has no wish to store its customers' excess. A customer shapes, on its
own router, because a queue it owns is a queue it can arrange, which is what lesson 18's classes needed.
The last section of this lesson puts the two together on one link and shows why the customer's number
has to sit a little below the provider's.
