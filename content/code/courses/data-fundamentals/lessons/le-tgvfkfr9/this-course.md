**This course is the map of a profession, drawn before you walk any of it.** The courses after it in
the `data` track each spend forty or seventy hours on one region — SQL, the warehouse, pipelines, the
cloud — and every one of them assumes a vocabulary: a source, an ingestion, a partition, a format
that stores columns, a stream with a late event in it. This is where those words are learnt, and
where each one is seen happening once, on a small scale, on your own machine.

## The ten lessons

| lesson | the question it answers |
|---|---|
| 1 | What does a data engineer do, and how is that different from a data scientist? |
| 2 | What is the job responsible for, and how does one choose a technology? |
| 3 | What happens to a piece of data between the moment it is born and the moment somebody reads it? |
| 4 | Where does data come from, and what does each kind of source cost to read? |
| 5 | What is the difference between a table, a JSON document and a photograph, for the person storing them? |
| 6 | Why are there five file formats, and which one do you pick? |
| 7 | Before collecting anything: how much, how often, how good, and at what price? |
| 8 | Do you process data in batches, or as it arrives? |
| 9 | What changes when the data no longer fits on one machine? |
| 10 | What can a system promise when the network between its machines breaks? |

Lessons 1 and 2 are about the people and the work. Lessons 3 to 7 follow data from where it is made
to where it is stored. Lessons 8 to 10 are about the machinery underneath, which is where the
vocabulary is hardest and where a wrong picture costs the most later.

## Roda Livre

Every example in the course happens at one company, so that the examples add up. **Roda Livre** is a
bicycle-sharing service in Curitiba. It does not exist. It has twelve stations, from Praça Tiradentes
to the Ópera de Arame, ninety bicycles, and an app that unlocks one and charges for the ride.

Four people appear, and the course never needs more:

- **ana** has just joined as the second person on the data team. She is the one typing, and the
  name in every transcript;
- **Davi** is the senior data engineer, who built what exists so far;
- **Marta** runs operations, and asks the questions the data has to answer: where the bicycles are,
  which stations run empty, how long a broken dock waits to be fixed;
- **Caio** is the data scientist, who wants to predict demand per station per hour.

The data is generated by short programs that the lessons print in full, from fixed seeds, so the
numbers you get are the numbers in the text. No file in this course is a download.

## What it does not teach

A map names the regions; it does not walk them. Where a lesson reaches the edge of this course it
says which course takes over, and stops:

- writing SQL is `sql-databases`, and modelling a warehouse is `warehouse-modeling`;
- building and scheduling real pipelines is `pipelines-etl`;
- measuring and repairing data quality in depth is `data-cleaning`;
- the cloud and its storage services are `cloud`, and containers are `docker`.

**The course is short on tools and long on decisions.** The one thing it asks of you that no
other course does is to see the same few ideas — a copy, a delay, a failure, a trade-off — come back
in every lesson under a different name.
