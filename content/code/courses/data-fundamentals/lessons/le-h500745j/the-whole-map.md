**Ten lessons make one picture: data is born in systems built for something else, travels through a
pipeline into a place and a format, and is read by somebody who asked a question.** Underneath all
of it sit a few machines that do not always agree. This section draws that picture once, puts
each lesson where it belongs, and names the course that walks each region from here.

## The regions, and who walks them next

The top band is the people and the decisions. Lesson 1 separated the engineer who makes a question
answerable from the analyst who answers it and the scientist who predicts. Lesson 2 listed what the
job is responsible for, and how to choose a tool without falling for the newest one. The analyst's
half of the work is `analytics-bi`. The law and the protection around personal data, which lessons
2 and 7 touched through the LGPD, are `data-governance`.

The middle row is lesson 3's lifecycle, with the lessons that opened each stage further.

| stage | what this course showed | the course that walks it |
|---|---|---|
| generation | lesson 4's sources, lesson 5's three shapes | `sql-databases` for the databases, `apis` for the services |
| ingestion | full and incremental loads, and checks at the door in lesson 7 | `pipelines-etl` |
| storage | raw kept apart from cleaned, and lesson 6's five formats | `warehouse-modeling`, and `bigdata` for files at scale |
| transformation | cleaning, joining and counting in small programs | `pipelines-etl`, and `data-cleaning` for quality in depth |
| delivery | the morning report | `analytics-bi` |

The bottom band is the machinery every stage runs on. Lesson 8 split processing into batches and
streams, and `streaming` takes the second all the way to a running cluster. Lesson 9 spread data over
many machines, and `bigdata` and `scale` are where that becomes a cluster you operate. This lesson
asked what those machines can promise when they are cut apart; `nosql-operations` opens with the
same question, and `architecture` asks it of whole systems.

## Four ideas that kept coming back

Lesson 1 said the course would show the same few ideas under different names. Here they are,
gathered:

- **A copy.** An ingest is a copy, a replica is a copy, a cache and a report are copies. Every one
  of them can be behind, and every one needs somebody to say by how much.
- **A delay.** Freshness in lesson 2, late events in lesson 8, replication lag in lesson 9, and the
  else of PACELC here. Data is always about a moment that has already passed.
- **A failure.** Loud ones stop and name themselves; quiet ones produce a plausible wrong number.
  From the `KeyError` in lesson 1 to the bicycle last write wins lost in this lesson, **the quiet
  kind is the one this profession exists to prevent.**
- **A trade-off.** Rows or columns, batch or stream, answer or refuse. None of them has a right
  side in general, and every one has a right side for a particular piece of data.

The next course in the `data` track is `statistics`, which teaches what to do with a number once it
can be trusted. Then `sql-databases`, where the first region of this map is walked in full.
