title: The ageing chart
version: 1

`age.py` prints a list. The chart that teams put on the wall, or on the screen at the start of a standup, draws the same information so that the old items cannot be missed: the **ageing chart**, also called the aging work-in-progress chart.

It has the board's columns along the bottom, in the board's order, and age up the side. **Each open item is a dot in its column, at the height of its age.** Behind the dots, horizontal bands show the percentiles of recent cycle times: the 50th, the 70th, the 85th and the 95th. A dot below the first band is young; a dot above the top one is older than almost everything the team has finished lately.

## Reading it

**Look at the top of each column first.** On 15 July the tall dots are all in review; the development column, with more items in it, is young. That is a queue. On 30 September there is one tall dot, alone, in development, far above every band, and nothing else near it. That is a stuck item.

**Look at the right-hand columns before the left.** An old item near the end of the board is closer to done and has more invested in it; finishing it releases more than starting anything new would. The habit of reading right to left comes from this chart, and the next section turns it into a standup.

**Look for dots that cross a band.** A dot that crossed the 50th line yesterday is an item to ask about today, while asking is cheap. Waiting until it crosses the 85th means waiting until it is already among the slowest.

## What the bands are not

The bands are not deadlines and nobody failed when a dot crosses one. They are the team's own history, drawn behind its present, so that **the question "is this item normal?" has an answer before anybody has an opinion about it**. Some items are legitimately large; the chart's job is to make sure that is a decision somebody made, not a surprise everybody finds.

## Drawing your own

A spreadsheet draws it in a few minutes: one row per open item with its column and its age, a scatter chart with the column as the horizontal axis, and four horizontal lines at the percentiles. Every board tool with flow metrics draws it too, under one of its two names. What matters is that **blocked items are on it**. The ageing chart of a board that moves blocked items to a separate list is a chart with its most important dot missing.
