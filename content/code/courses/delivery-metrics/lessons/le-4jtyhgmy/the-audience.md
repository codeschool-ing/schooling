---
title: Who reads the review, and what they decide
version: 1
---

Every quarter, Bia presents the Billing team's numbers to the people above and beside it: the engineering director, the head of product, and, since the double charge, Marta, the director of sales, whose shop owners were the ones charged twice. They have half an hour, and they have a dozen other teams.

**A quarterly review is not a display of what the team measured.** It is a short conversation in which people who do not see the work every day decide something about it: whether to keep investing in a change, whether a date is believable, whether the team needs help. Everything in this lesson follows from treating it that way.

## The three questions they bring

People who manage several teams arrive with the same few questions, whether or not they say them out loud:

- **Is it getting better or worse?** Not "what are the numbers", but which direction they point, and whether the change is real or noise.
- **Can I believe your dates?** The roadmap of lesson 11 depends on it, and so do the commitments the head of product has made to customers.
- **What do you need from me?** A decision, a budget, a person, protection from a request that keeps arriving. If the answer is nothing, that is worth saying too.

A review that answers those three in its first five minutes has done its job, and the rest of the half hour is questions.

## What they do not need

They do not need the team's working charts. The cumulative flow diagram of lesson 3 and the ageing chart are instruments for the people doing the work, read every day; shown once a quarter to people who have never read one, they produce either silence or a question about a colour. They do not need a number for every person, which lesson 8 ruled out. And they do not need fourteen charts, which is what Bia's first draft had, because a dashboard that shows everything says nothing in particular, and half an hour is not enough to find the one thing it says.

## Knowing what will be decided

Before writing anything, Bia asked the engineering director one question: **"What will you decide after this review?"** The answer was two things. Whether the working rules of 3 August should become the department's default for other teams, and whether the Billing team's October, with features frozen under the budget policy of lesson 16, was acceptable to the people waiting for those features.

Both answers changed the review. The first meant the flow numbers had to be honest about what the change did and did not do, because other teams would copy it. The second meant the incident and the budget belonged at the front, not in an appendix.
