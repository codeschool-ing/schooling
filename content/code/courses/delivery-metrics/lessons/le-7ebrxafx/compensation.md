Being on call is work, even in a week when the pager never rings. The person on call cannot travel far, cannot drink much, cannot go to the cinema without checking the phone every ten minutes, and sleeps a little worse because it might ring. **A rota that pays nothing for that is asking people to give up part of their week for free**, and the people who can leave are the first to do so.

## The usual forms

Teams that compensate on-call do it with some combination of three things:

- **a fixed amount for being available**, per week or per day, whether or not anything happens;
- **payment for the time actually worked** when a page comes, often at a higher rate at night and on weekends;
- **time off after a bad night**: a person paged at three in the morning starts late the next day, or takes the morning off, and nobody has to ask.

The third costs least and is the one most often forgotten. A developer who was up from 02:40 to 04:15 fixing card charges and is at their desk at nine is a developer who will release something at eleven with half their judgement, and the error budget of lesson 16 will pay for it.

## In Brazil

Brazilian labour law has a name for being available away from work: **sobreaviso**. The CLT, art. 244, §2, defines it for railway workers, as staying at home awaiting a call, paid at **one third of the normal hourly wage** for each hour, in shifts of at most 24 hours. Courts apply it by analogy to other workers, and the Superior Labour Court's **Súmula 428** says when: carrying a company phone is not, by itself, sobreaviso; it is when the employee, at a distance and under the employer's control by electronic means, is on a duty schedule and can be called at any moment during their rest.

A rota like the Billing team's, with a named person who must answer within minutes all week, fits the second description closely. Hours actually worked after a page are working hours, paid as overtime. **The details depend on the contract and the collective agreement, and this course is not legal advice**: a team introducing a rota in Brazil agrees it with whoever handles the company's labour matters, before the first shift rather than after the first complaint.

## Other countries

Rules differ everywhere, and many countries have nothing as specific. The principle that survives every jurisdiction is the one at the top of this section: availability has a cost to the person carrying it, and **a team that wants its rota to last pays that cost openly** rather than letting it be paid in resignations.

## Paying is not a fix for a bad rota

Money for a rota that pages every night is not compensation; it is a price for burning people out. Paying for on-call makes it fair. Only fixing the alerts and spreading the knowledge, the subjects of the next section and of lesson 18, makes it bearable.
