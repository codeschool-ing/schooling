A histogram throws away time: it says how long items took and not when. The chart teams that measure flow look at most is the **cycle-time scatterplot**: one dot per finished item, placed at the date it finished and at the height of its cycle time, with the percentile lines drawn across. It is the only chart in this lesson that shows a change happening.

## Reading it left to right

**June and July are a wide cloud, from a day to 47**, rising as July goes on. The rise is the review queue growing, which the next section measures; on the scatterplot it shows as dots drifting upwards with no single dramatic outlier, which is how a slowly worsening system looks.

**August is the interesting month, and it is the one a summary would get wrong.** Two kinds of dot sit side by side. Items started after 3 August finished in eight days or fewer. Items started before the change, under the old rules, kept finishing all month: twenty-six of them, with a median of about 20 days and a longest of 55. Those late, tall dots are not a sign that the new rules failed. They are the old system draining out. A tech lead who computed August's 85th percentile and announced that the change had made things worse would be reading the old policy's leftovers as the new policy's results.

**September is a tight band near the bottom**, with a few dots above the 85th-percentile line. Each of those is worth a question, and the question is never "who was slow?" but "what happened to this item?": a dependency, a review that waited, a change bigger than it looked when it was started.

## What the dots are for

The scatterplot answers three questions faster than any table:

- **Has something changed?** A shift in where the dots sit, as in August, is visible before any percentile moves.
- **Is the change real or a leftover?** Dots that belong to the old system can be told apart by their start date, as above.
- **Which items deserve a conversation?** The ones above the 85th-percentile line, while the people who worked on them still remember why.

It has one blind spot, and it is serious: **a dot only appears when an item finishes**. An item that has been open for fifty days and has not finished is not on this chart at all. `BIL-189`, the blocked bug from lesson 1, has no dot. Lesson 3 draws the chart that shows it.
