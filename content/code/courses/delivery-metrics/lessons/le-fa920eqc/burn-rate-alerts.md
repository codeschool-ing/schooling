title: Burn rate: alerting on the budget

The budget gives the team a better alarm than any it had. Instead of alerting on a cause, a server's CPU or a queue's length, it can alert on **the speed at which the budget is being spent**, which is the symptom the shops feel.

That speed is the **burn rate**:

```localised
burn rate = share of bad events in a window ÷ share the objective allows
```

A burn rate of **1** spends the budget exactly over the 30 days: at that pace the month ends at zero, on target. A burn rate of 2 spends it in 15 days; a burn rate of 30 spends it in a day.

## Which rates deserve a person

Google's SRE workbook proposes thresholds that have become the usual starting point. Each one is a share of the 30-day budget spent in a given time, turned into a rate:

| spends | in | burn rate | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% of the budget | 1 hour | 14.4 | pages somebody now |
| 5% of the budget | 6 hours | 6 | pages somebody now |
| 10% of the budget | 3 days | 1 | opens a ticket for working hours |

The arithmetic is short: 30 days are 720 hours, so spending 2% in one hour is a rate of 0.02 × 720 = 14.4. **The fast rate catches the fire, the slow rate catches the leak**, and the ticket catches the slow drift that would otherwise reach the end of the month unnoticed.

Each alert also checks a **short window**, one twelfth of the long one: five minutes for the hour, thirty for the six hours. Both have to be over the threshold. Without the short window, an alert would keep firing for most of an hour after a problem was fixed, because the hour still contains the bad minutes; with it, the alert stops soon after the burning stops.

## The afternoon of 30 September, measured

**Save the program below as `burn.py`.** It writes the charges of the afternoon of 30 September in five-minute buckets and asks when the fast alert would have paged.

Two things in those three lines.

Eight minutes is not a rescue. It is eight minutes of double charges fewer, and a page that names the symptom, card charges going bad, rather than a guess about the cause.

## What it does not replace

A burn-rate alert is a general net, not the fastest one. The alert on duplicate charges for the same invoice, one of the action items from lesson 15, watches the one thing that went wrong on 30 September and would have fired within minutes of the first one. The two do different jobs: the specific alert catches a failure somebody has already imagined, and the burn rate catches the ones nobody has. Lesson 18 is about keeping the set of alerts small enough that both still wake somebody who believes them.
