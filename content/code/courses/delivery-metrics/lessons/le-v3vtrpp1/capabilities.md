If the metrics are symptoms, the research also had to say what they are symptoms **of**. *Accelerate* named 24 **capabilities**, practices that the survey found predicted better delivery performance, and later reports have kept adding to and refining the list. They fall into a few families, and the names are more useful than the exact count.

## The families

| family | examples | what it does to a change |
|---|---|---|
| **technical** | continuous integration, test automation, trunk-based development, deployment automation, monitoring | makes a change cheap to verify and safe to ship |
| **architecture** | loosely coupled services, teams able to deploy without asking other teams | lets a change ship alone instead of in a release train |
| **process and flow** | small batches, limits on work in progress, visible work, fast feedback from users | keeps changes small and moving |
| **culture** | a generative culture, in Ron Westrum's sense: information flows, failure leads to inquiry rather than blame | makes people report problems early instead of hiding them |

The Billing team's August is a process-and-flow change: a limit on work in progress and a rule about reviews. Nothing about its tests, its architecture or its pipeline changed, and the four metrics moved anyway. That is worth noticing, because most teams assume the route to better DORA numbers runs through tooling, and tooling is only one family of the four.

## How to use the list

**As a menu of things to try, chosen by what the numbers show.** A long lead time for changes points at the review and the pipeline: are reviews waiting, is the pipeline slow, is there a release train? A high failure rate points at testing and batch size. A long time to restore points at monitoring and at how easy it is to roll back. Each symptom narrows the search; none of them names the cure.

**Not as a checklist to be completed.** A team that ticks "continuous integration" because it has a build server, while its branches live for two weeks, has the tool and not the capability. The capabilities are habits, and a habit is measured by what happens to a change, not by what is installed.

## Westrum's three cultures

The cultural family deserves a sentence more, because it is the one teams skip. Ron Westrum, a sociologist who studied safety in organisations, described three kinds: **pathological**, where information is a weapon and messengers are punished; **bureaucratic**, where information follows the rules and the org chart; and **generative**, where information goes to whoever needs it and failure is treated as a chance to learn. The DORA research found the generative kind associated with better delivery. Lesson 15 is about the most concrete form it takes, the blameless postmortem.
