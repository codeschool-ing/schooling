Every item in `items.csv` carries the points the team gave it. `billing.py` draws them from what each item really cost, with an error on top, which is a generous model of estimation: the estimates are related to the truth, just not exactly. **Save the program below as `points.py`** and see how well they predict how long an item takes.


## Under the old rules

```
ana@laptop:~/delivery$ python3 points.py 2026-06-01 2026-07-31
78 items started from 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31, and merged
points  items  median days  slowest
     1     20           12       28
     2     28         18.5       55
     3     19           23       47
     5      8         22.5       46
     8      3           29       39
correlation between points and days: 0.38
```

A one-point item started in June or July took a median of **12 days**, and the slowest took 28. A five-pointer took a median of 22.5, slightly *less* than a three-pointer. The correlation between points and cycle time is **0.38**: there is a relationship, and it is weak. The reason is lesson 2's: most of an item's cycle time was waiting, in a review queue and between a developer's three open items, and waiting does not care how big the item is.

## Under the new rules

```
ana@laptop:~/delivery$ python3 points.py 2026-08-03 2026-09-30
45 items started from 2026-08-03 to 2026-09-30, and merged
points  items  median days  slowest
     1     17            2        5
     2      9            4        5
     3     10          5.5       12
     5      5            5       11
     8      4            7       17
correlation between points and days: 0.55
```

The correlation rises to **0.55**. That is the interesting result: **the points did not get better; the system did.** With the waiting removed, the work itself is a larger share of cycle time, and an estimate of the work says more about the time. Even so, the rows overlap: a one-pointer took up to 5 days, a three-pointer as few as two days, and five-pointers had a lower median than three-pointers.

## What that means for a forecast

A team that knows an item is a three can say, from this table, that it will probably take between two and twelve days. A team that knows only that it is **an item** can say, from lesson 2, that 85% of items finish within eight days. The second statement costs nothing to produce and is almost as informative. **Estimating each item buys a small improvement in the forecast for one item, at the price of a meeting per item.** For forecasting many items together, which is what dates are made of, the next section shows the improvement disappears.
