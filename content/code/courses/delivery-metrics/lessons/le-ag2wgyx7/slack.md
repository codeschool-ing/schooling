In 2001 Tom DeMarco, who had spent decades studying software projects, published a short book called *Slack*. Its argument fits in the queue lesson's curve: **an organisation that keeps everybody fully busy loses the ability to respond**, because there is no capacity left to absorb the unexpected, and the unexpected is most of what happens.

## What slack buys

- **Short queues.** The curve: a team at 70% busy has a fraction of the waiting of a team at 95%. Requesters get answers in days instead of weeks.
- **Room for the urgent.** An incident, a security fix or a customer who cannot invoice can be handled without stopping everything else, because somebody has room.
- **Help where it is needed.** A person with slack can review, pair on the oldest item or unblock a colleague; lesson 4's "stop starting" needs people who are not already overcommitted.
- **Improvement.** The time to fix a flaky test, automate a manual step or write the runbook for the next incident comes out of slack, or it does not come at all.

## Where to put it

Slack does not mean idle people. It means **planned work that leaves room**, so that the room can be used when it is needed. Three forms work in practice:

- **Plan to a fraction of capacity.** Commit planned items worth 70 to 80% of what the throughput history says the team finishes, and let the rest absorb what arrives.
- **Keep a limit on work in progress.** Lesson 4's limit is slack in another form: it stops the team filling every gap with a new start.
- **Protect the bottleneck.** Slack matters most at the step everything waits for. In July that was review; giving Bia's queue capacity mattered more than any other change.

## The objection

"We cannot afford people who are not busy." The honest reply is that the team is paying for the busyness already, in waiting: in July the Billing team's requesters waited forty days for work that took a few. **The cost of no slack is real; it is just paid by somebody else, later, and nobody sends an invoice.** Lesson 2 measured that invoice as lead time.
