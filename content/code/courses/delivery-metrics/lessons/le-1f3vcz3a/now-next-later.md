---
title: Roadmaps whose confidence fades with distance
version: 1
---

A roadmap is a forecast drawn as a picture, and most roadmaps draw it wrongly: a row of bars on a timeline, each with a crisp start and end, a year out, with the same sharp edge on the bar for next month and the bar for next autumn. **The picture claims the same certainty about November as about next week**, and everybody who reads it believes the picture.

## Confidence falls with the horizon

What the team knows about work falls off quickly with distance. The next few weeks are items on the board, right-sized, with a history to forecast them from. The next quarter is a set of goals whose items have not been written yet. The quarter after is a direction. A roadmap that tells the truth shows that fading.

## Now, next, later

The simplest honest format drops the dates altogether beyond the first column. It has three columns:

| column | holds | how it is forecast |
|---|---|---|
| **now** | work in progress and the items about to start | Monte Carlo from throughput, with dates and percentiles |
| **next** | the goals for the following one to three months, not yet broken into items | order of magnitude, "about six weeks of work", from lesson 9's reference classes |
| **later** | directions the team expects to take, in priority order | no dates; the order is the commitment |

The format makes two things visible that a timeline hides. Moving something from *later* to *now* is a decision that displaces something else, because *now* is limited by the team's work in progress, lesson 4. And a date only appears on an item once it has been broken down enough to be forecast, so **dates get attached as confidence is earned** rather than promised in advance.

## When people need a timeline anyway

Some audiences will insist on a timeline: a board, a client, a partner planning their own work around yours. Draw one, but draw the uncertainty on it. A bar can have a solid part, up to the median date, and a fading part to the 95th percentile; a bar beyond the first quarter can be a dashed outline with no end at all. **The shape of the bar is a claim about confidence**, and the honest shape costs nothing to draw.
