The AI Act did not switch on in one day. Its articles apply on a schedule, and in 2026 the schedule
itself was changed. **This page is the one in the course most likely to be out of date when you read
it**: it describes the position in October 2026, and a parliament, not a software vendor, decides when
it stops being true.

| from | what applies |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | the Act enters into force; nothing in it binds anybody yet |
| 2 February 2025 | the **prohibited practices** of article 5, and **AI literacy** (article 4) |
| 2 August 2025 | the rules for **general-purpose models**, the governance structure, the penalties |
| 2 August 2026 | most of the rest, including the **transparency obligations** of article 50 |
| 2 December 2026 | the end of a grace period for marking AI-generated content on generative systems already on the market; the new ban on "nudification" tools |
| 2 December 2027 | the obligations for **high-risk systems in Annex III** — employment, credit, education and the rest |
| 2 August 2028 | the obligations for high-risk systems that are **safety components of products** (Annex I) |

## What the Omnibus moved

The last two rows are not the dates the Act was adopted with. The original text applied the Annex III
obligations from 2 August 2026 and the Annex I ones from 2 August 2027. In November 2025 the
Commission proposed a **Digital Omnibus on AI**, arguing that the standards and guidance the
high-risk obligations depend on were not ready. It was adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**,
published in the Official Journal on **24 July 2026** and in force since **27 July 2026**.

Besides the dates, it softened **article 4**: providers and deployers no longer have to *ensure* a
sufficient level of AI literacy among their staff, but to *support* its development, and the text now
says it does not require any specific level to be guaranteed.

What it did **not** move: the prohibitions, which have applied since February 2025, and the rules for
general-purpose models, since August 2025. A team that read "the AI Act has been postponed" in a
headline and stopped there is wrong about both.

## How to keep a page like this honest

A schedule set by law changes by law, and a document that copies it will drift. Two habits help:

- **cite the act, not the article about it**: "Regulation (EU) 2026/1744" can be looked up, and when a
  later regulation amends it, the citation shows the reader where to look;
- **write down the date you checked**. This page was checked in October 2026. A compliance plan that
  says what it was checked against can be rechecked; one that just says "2027" cannot.
