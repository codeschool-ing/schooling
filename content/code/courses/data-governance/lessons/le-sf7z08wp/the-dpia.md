**Article 35** of the GDPR makes a **data protection impact assessment** compulsory when a type of
processing, *in particular using new technologies*, is **likely to result in a high risk** to people's
rights and freedoms. It must be done **before** the processing starts. Three cases always need one
(art. 35(3)):

- a **systematic and extensive evaluation** of personal aspects, based on automated processing
  including profiling, on which decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are based;
- processing **on a large scale of special categories** of data (article 9), or of data about
  criminal convictions;
- **systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area** on a large scale.

Each supervisory authority also publishes a list of processing that requires one; the CNPD has its own.

## What it contains

Article 35(7) sets the minimum, and it is close to LGPD article 38 with one item more:

| GDPR art. 35(7) | LGPD art. 38 |
|---|---|
| a systematic description of the processing and its purposes | the types of data, and how they are collected |
| an assessment of the **necessity and proportionality** of the processing | — |
| an assessment of the risks to people's rights and freedoms | the controller's analysis of the risks… |
| the measures to address the risks, safeguards and security | …and of the measures, safeguards and mitigation adopted |

The extra item is the one that changes outcomes. **Necessity and proportionality** asks whether the
processing should happen at all, in this form — the same question as the second step of lesson 7's
legitimate interest test. An assessment that only lists risks and controls can approve anything, as
long as it lists enough controls.

And where the residual risk stays high, article 36 makes the controller **consult the supervisory
authority before starting**. The LGPD has no equivalent step.

## One document, two laws

Ipê's Lisbon shop sells vitamins and cosmetics, so its processing is not large-scale special
categories in the way prescriptions are. Its candidate for a DPIA is elsewhere — the recruitment
screening tool of section 9, which evaluates people automatically and feeds a decision about a job.

A company under both laws writes **one** assessment per processing, with the facts computed as lesson
7's RIPD computed them, and the sections both laws ask for. Two documents describing the same
processing are two documents that will disagree within a year.
