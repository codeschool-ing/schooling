Brazil has **no AI law in force**. What it has is a bill, and the LGPD.

**PL 2338/2023**, the bill on the development and use of artificial intelligence, was approved by the
**Senate on 10 December 2024**. It went to the **Chamber of Deputies**, where it is still being
discussed. It is not law; its text can change in the Chamber, and if it does, it goes back to the
Senate. Anything said about its final shape is a forecast.

The Senate's text follows the same broad pattern as the European one — a risk-based approach, uses
classed as excessive risk and forbidden, uses classed as high risk with extra duties, rights for
affected people — and gives a central coordinating role in supervision to the ANPD. Whether those
choices survive is exactly what is still open.

## What applies already

Nothing about AI is unregulated while the bill waits:

- **the LGPD** applies to every AI system that processes personal data: a legal basis for training
  and for use, the principles of lesson 7 — purpose, necessity, non-discrimination — and the rights of
  data subjects, article 20 included;
- **the Consumer Protection Code** applies to a chatbot answering a customer as to any other channel;
- **the ANPD** has been publishing studies and guidance on AI and data protection, and acts under the
  LGPD on AI cases like any other processing.

## What Ipê does meanwhile

It treats the inventory of section 9 as the answer to whichever law arrives. The European class is
written down because the Lisbon company needs it now; a column for the Brazilian class will be added
the day there is a Brazilian law to class against. A table that already lists the systems, says what
each is for, who answers for it and what data it uses, is ready for a new law in an afternoon. A
company that has to *discover* its AI systems when a law passes spends the transition period finding
them.
