The **Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados**, Law 13,709 of 2018, is Brazil's general data protection
law. It has 65 articles. A data team needs a handful of them closely and the rest as a map, and this
page is the map.

**What it covers (art. 3).** Any processing of personal data — collecting, storing, using, sharing,
deleting — by a person or company, public or private, when the processing happens in Brazil, when it
aims to offer goods or services to people in Brazil, or when the data was collected in Brazil. A
company abroad selling to Brazilians is inside it.

**What it does not (art. 4).** Processing by a person for purely private purposes; journalism,
art and academic work, with conditions; public security, national defence and criminal
investigation, which have their own rules; and data from abroad that only passes through Brazil
without being shared with Brazilian processors.

**Who answers for what.** Lesson 6 named the roles: the controller decides, the processor acts for
it, the DPO (*encarregado*) is the channel. The controller answers for its processors' handling of
the data it gave them.

**Who enforces it.** The **ANPD**, the national data protection authority. In September 2025 a
provisional measure (MP 1.317) turned it into a regulatory agency, with the autonomy Brazil's other
regulatory agencies have, keeping the acronym. It issues regulations, receives complaints, inspects
and sanctions.

**A legal course this is not.** Everything here is what a data team needs to understand the
obligations it builds for, and to recognise when a question belongs to a lawyer. When it does, the
lawyer will ask the questions this lesson teaches you to answer: what data, about whom, for what,
on which basis, kept how long, protected how.
