Sensitive data — lesson 6's list: health, sex life, ethnicity, religion, politics, union membership,
genetics, biometrics — has its own article and its own, shorter list. **Article 11** allows it to be
processed in two ways.

**With consent** that is *specific and highlighted*, for specific purposes. "Specific" means for this
purpose and not a general one; "highlighted" means it stands out, not buried in terms of use.

**Without consent, only when indispensable** for one of these:

| | basis | at Ipê |
|---|---|---|
| a | a legal or regulatory obligation | keeping prescription records the health authority requires |
| b | shared processing by the public administration for public policies | — |
| c | studies by a research body, anonymised where possible | — |
| d | exercising rights, including in a contract and in proceedings | a dispute about a prescription order |
| e | protecting life or physical safety | — |
| f | **protecting health**, in a procedure by health professionals, health services or the health authority | the pharmacist dispensing a prescribed medicine |
| g | **preventing fraud** and protecting the person in identification and authentication in electronic systems | checking a login is really the customer |

Compare it with article 7 and two things are missing. **Legitimate interest is not on the list.**
Neither is **performing a contract** as such — only "exercising rights" in one. So the convenient bases
that cover most ordinary processing do not reach sensitive data, and every use of it has to name one
of the seven above, or a consent given for exactly that use.

## What that means for Ipê's analysts

Lesson 6 found that a pharmacy's order lines reveal health. An analysis of buying behaviour over
those lines — which customers buy what, how often — is processing sensitive data. Which basis covers
it? Not legitimate interest; not health protection, which the law ties to procedures by health
professionals; not fraud prevention. That leaves specific consent, or **not doing it on identifiable
data**: aggregates with suppression (lesson 5), or analyses that never leave the people who serve the
customer.

**Article 11, §4**, from lesson 6, closes the last door: sharing health data with another controller
for an economic advantage is forbidden, outside health services and the person's own interest.
