(`ex.py` must write into the lesson folder; keep it in the template folder.) Read

## Shape of a lesson

- `lesson.json`: `{"id": <lesson id>, "title": <the topic title from course.json>, "sections": [...]}`.
  First section `intro`, kind `video`, one video `{id: vd-…, version: 1, script, seconds, locales: []}`,
  with an `intro.md` and `intro.pt.md` holding only front matter (`title`, `version: 1`). Then 4–6
  `reading` sections, then `drill`, kind `practice`, with `drill.md`/`drill.pt.md` front matter only
  (`Putting it together` / `Juntando tudo`). **6 to 8 sections in all**; the course budget is ~150
  for 22 lessons and lesson 1 used 9.
- Front matter: exactly `title` and `version: 1`. Titles in sentence case.
- Script: spoken, 60–90 s, median ~13 words per sentence, no bold, marks `{{name}}` where the
  scene changes, says what the lesson is for. `seconds` ≈ words/2.5.
- Readings: ~400–800 words each, reading prose median ~19 words a sentence, bold as signposts
  (house style, ~1 per paragraph or two), British spelling (behaviour, colour, recognise,
  organise), `you`, no "let's", no "we" for the author. Lead with the shape; name the wrong idea
  before the right one; every doubtable claim gets a concrete instance, ideally a capture.
  Forward/backward references name a lesson number ("lesson 13"), never "later". Point at other
  courses by slug and lesson number where the design says the subject lives there:
  `javascript` lesson 11 (DOM), 13 (event loop), 14 (async), 16 (fetch), 21 (npm), 22 (DevTools);
  `manual-testing` 7 (compatibility), 8 (smoke), 10 (regression), 15 (defect report), 20 (test
  data), 21 (environments); `qa-fundamentals` 3 (cost of a defect), 19 (oracle), 20
  (prioritisation); `testing-cicd` 1 (layers), 5 (CI); `non-functional-testing` 10 (front-end
  performance), 12–15 (accessibility); `api-mobile-automation` (API testing, next course).
  Do not teach another lesson's subject; recognise it, point at it.
- Code: consider `schooling-example` (see `docs/TEACHING.md`, "Code that is demonstrated") whenever
  a section walks through a program piece by piece; a whole file the student saves can be an
  example too (the lab joins its parts' `code` with newlines — each part's code is one or more
  whole lines; the joined result is the file). Otherwise plain fences. Fence labels: `javascript`,
  `html`, `css`, `json`, `sh` for commands to type, and NO label for a transcript (prompt on
  its first line) — never `text`, `bash` is fine, `console` is fine.
- Figures: 1–3 per lesson where one EARNS its place (a sequence in time, a nesting, a count, two
  axes) — the race of two responses, a timeline of waits, the WebDriver architecture, a page
  object's layers. Inline SVG in a `schooling-figure` fence, palette tokens only
  (`--amber --ink --panel --paper --paper-dim --phosphor --phosphor-dim --scan --wire`), prose
  labels in `'IBM Plex Sans'`, code labels in `'IBM Plex Mono'`, `role="img"` + `aria-label`,
  a `caption`. The `.pt.md` figure translates every Sans label, `aria-label` and caption (or lists
  an identical word in `"same": [...]`). Keep labels inside their boxes (`figure-fit`).
- Translation: Brazilian Portuguese, natural, not literal. Every fence in `.pt.md` is
  byte-identical to the English one at the same place (render from the same placeholders);
  only `schooling-example` notes and figure prose differ. Same `::: track` groups if any (this
  course is in one track, `qa`; you need none).

## Exercises (~18 per lesson, `ex.py` with `/tmp/lab/bin/exlib.py`)

Spread over sections (each reading section 2–4, drill 2–4), mostly `quiz`, plus at least one each
of `ordering`, `cloze`, `matching` or `numeric`, `multiple-choice` where it fits. Every option has
a `why`, including the correct one, and the `why` teaches. Difficulty per `docs/EXERCISES.md`.
A distractor is a belief somebody holds. The checker (run it!) refuses:

- the correct option at any ONE length rank in more than 45% of the lesson's quiz questions, in
  EN and in PT separately — run `/tmp/lab/bin/ranks <lesson dir>` and aim for an even spread of
  ranks 1–4 in both languages; lengthen/shorten distractors to move it;
- a "read nothing" strategy scoring over 45%;
- hedges (EN usually, often, can, may, tends, generally, sometimes, typically, might, could;
  PT geralmente, normalmente, pode, podem, costuma, às vezes, talvez…) only in the correct option —
  whole words anywhere in the option text;
- the correct option echoing the prompt's long words (≥6 letters) more than any other;
- a cloze hole is exactly `___` with spaces around; its accept list is translated; no blank
  answerable by copying a prompt word;
- ordering of 2 items, matching of 2 pairs; "all/none of the above".
Absolutes (never, always, only, all, every, none, must, cannot; PT nunca, sempre, só, todo,
nada, deve, não pode) should not sit only in wrong options as a habit.

- Numbers in prose come from your captures, or are arithmetic the reader can check, or are
  stated as the vendor's documentation says them (and then say "its documentation says").
