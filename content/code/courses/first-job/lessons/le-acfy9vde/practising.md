---
title: Practising the part practice improves
version: 1
---

Most people practise live exercises by solving many problems alone, in silence, which trains the part that
is already fine and skips the part that fails. **What practice improves most is thinking out loud**, and it
has to be practised out loud:

- **Solve a small problem while speaking**, and record it. Listen to the recording: where did you go
  silent? Those are the places the interviewer lost you.
- **Do it with a person watching**, a friend or a classmate, even one who knows nothing about the subject.
  Being watched is the part that makes people freeze, and it gets easier with repetition like nothing else.
- Use problems at the right level. A junior live exercise is closer to *count the statuses in this log*
  than to a competition puzzle. Practising on puzzles far above the level trains you to feel stuck.

Three or four sessions like this before your first interview change it more than thirty problems solved in
silence.
