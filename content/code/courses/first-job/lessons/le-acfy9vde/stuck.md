---
title: When you are stuck
version: 1
---

Everybody gets stuck in a live exercise at some point, and **what you do in the next thirty seconds is part
of what is being assessed**. Silence is the worst option, because from the outside it looks the same as
having no idea.

Three moves, in this order:

1. Say where you are. *I have the counting working; I'm stuck on how to sort by the count.* This alone
   often unsticks you, and it tells the interviewer exactly what kind of help would be useful.
2. Go back to a smaller case. Work one example by hand, on paper or in a comment, and look at what you
   did.
3. Ask for a hint, plainly. *Could I get a hint on sorting?* Interviewers expect this and usually have
   one ready. Taking a hint and using it well is a good sign, not a failure; refusing to ask and running out
   of time is the failure.

What not to do: pretend you know an API you do not (*I think there's a function for this, let me just…*
followed by guessing), or apologise at length. **Say what you would look up, and carry on**: *in real work
I'd check the documentation for the sort function; here I'll write the comparison by hand.*
