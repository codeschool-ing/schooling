title: Where to knock first
version: 1
---

With five doors, the question is which to try first, and the answer depends less on the salary than on
**who will teach you**.

The strongest first job is the one with **somebody more senior next to you, reviewing your work**. That
can be a product company with a real team, a consultancy that pairs juniors with seniors, an outsourcing
placement in a client team that reviews code, or an IT department with an experienced lead. It is rarely a
freelance project and rarely a role where you would be the only technical person.

So in the screening call (lesson 11), the most useful question about any of the five is the same: **who
would I work with day to day, and who reviews what I do?** An answer with a name and a routine is a good
sign. An answer that says you would be independent from the start is, on a first job, a warning.

The second criterion is **what you would work on**, compared with the verbs you found in the adverts in
lesson 1. Pay comes third, not because it does not matter, but because a first job that teaches you raises
the second salary more than a first job that pays a little more and teaches nothing.
