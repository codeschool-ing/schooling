---
title: Freelance, contract and working abroad
version: 1
---

Working for yourself is possible from the first day, and there is one reason not to start there: **nobody
is teaching you**. A first job is paid training as much as it is work. A freelancer on their first project
has a client who expects a professional, no senior to ask, and no code review to catch the mistake before
the client does.

**In Brazil** most contract work is done as a *PJ*, a *pessoa jurídica*: you open a small company, usually
in the *Simples Nacional* tax regime, and you invoice the client. Some companies
offer "PJ roles" that are full-time jobs in everything but the contract: fixed hours, a manager, a desk.
That arrangement has no paid holiday, no *13º salário* and no *FGTS* unless they are negotiated into the
price, and lesson 17 does the arithmetic of comparing it with a *CLT* salary.

**Working for a company abroad** from Brazil is common in development and increasingly in support, and it
is usually a contract of the same kind: you invoice in dollars or euros as a company, or through an
intermediary that employs you locally. It pays in a stronger currency and it asks for two things a first
job rarely has: **working English**, spoken as well as written, and **the ability to work without
anybody next to you**.

None of this is a reason never to do it. It is a reason to do it second: a year inside a team gives you the
habits (asking, reviewing, estimating) that a contract assumes you already have.

**Elsewhere** the same trade exists under other names (contractor, self-employed, *autónomo*), and the
same advice applies.
