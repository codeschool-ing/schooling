year than the job title does.

**A product company** sells its own software or service. You work on one system for a long time, learn it
in depth, and see the consequence of every change, because the users are the company's customers. The
variety is lower and the pace is set by the product, not by a client.

**An agency or a consultancy** builds or runs things for clients: a site for one, an integration for
another, a migration for a third. You see more technology in a year than most people see in three, and you
learn to deliver on a date. The price is that you rarely live with what you built.

**Outsourcing** (in Brazil often called *alocação* or, less kindly, *body shop*) employs you and places you
inside a client's team. Day to day you work for the client; your contract, your salary and your career are
the outsourcing company's. It is one of the most common first jobs in Brazilian IT, and it is a good one
when the client team teaches, and a lonely one when it does not.

**In-house IT** is the IT department of a company whose product is not software: a school, a hospital, a
factory, a bank's branch network. The work is broad, the users are colleagues, and support and
infrastructure roles are most common here.

**Freelance and contract** work is the fifth door, and the next section explains why it is the hardest
one to start with.
