title: When to follow up

One follow-up, **about a week after applying**, is normal almost everywhere and annoys nobody. More than one,
or one the next day, starts to cost more than it earns.

The drawing is a rule of thumb, not a timetable. Some processes move in two days and some take six weeks;
nothing tells you which from the outside. What the rule gives you is **a decision made in advance**, so that
you are not deciding every morning whether today is the day to write again.

Follow up where you applied, or where you already have a contact: a reply to the recruiter's message, the
e-mail in the advert, or a LinkedIn message to the person who posted the job. Do not look for a manager's
private number; a first contact through a door they did not open reads badly, however good the message.

After an interview the rule is different: **a short thank-you the same day or the next**, and then, if they
gave a date for a decision, **nothing until that date has passed**. If they did not give one, ask at the end
of the interview (lesson 16), so you are not guessing.
