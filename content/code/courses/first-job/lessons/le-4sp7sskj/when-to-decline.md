---
title: When to say no
version: 1
---

Most take-homes are reasonable, and doing them well is one of the best chances a junior gets to show real
work. A few are not, and it is worth recognising them:

- The exercise is the company's real work. *Build a module of our product*, *migrate this client's
  data*. That is unpaid labour dressed as a test, and the fact that you would be building their product
  says something about the rest of the process.
- **The time asked is days, not hours**, for a junior role, with no pay.
- There is no conversation first. A company that sends a large exercise before anybody has spoken to
  you is spending your time to save its own.

Declining is allowed, and it can be polite: *Thank you; I'm not able to commit several days to an unpaid
exercise. I'd be glad to do a shorter one, or to walk you through my portfolio project in a call.* Some
companies accept the alternative; the ones that do not have told you something useful.

In a first job search, when the market is hard, you may decide to do one anyway. That is your call, and it
is a better call made knowingly than by default.
