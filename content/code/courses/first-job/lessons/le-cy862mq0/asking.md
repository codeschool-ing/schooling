---
title: The message that asks
version: 1
---

The message that works makes the favour **small, specific and easy to decline**:

> Hi Rafael, I'm Ana. I studied IT support and I'm applying for the junior support role at Rede Saúde
> (link). I built and documented a small-office lab, with a tested restore (link). Would you be willing to
> refer me, or tell me who to talk to? If it isn't possible, no problem at all — thank you for reading.

Each part is doing something:

- **Who you are, in one line**, because they may not remember or may never have met you.
- **The exact role, with a link**, so they do not have to look for it.
- **One piece of evidence**, so they can see you are serious without reading a CV.
- **A clear request**, and a smaller alternative (*who to talk to*).
- **A way out** that costs them nothing. People say yes more easily to a request they could refuse.

To somebody you have never met, the same message works with one change: a line saying how you found them
(*I saw your post about the service desk team*). Do not ask a stranger for a referral in the first
message to a role you have not researched; ask what the work is like, and let the referral come from the
conversation.

**After**, tell them what happened, whatever it was. People who referred you want to know, and a thank-you
after a rejection is how you get referred again next time.
