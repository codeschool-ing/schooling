---
title: Why a referral works
version: 1
---

A company's hiring problem is not a lack of CVs; it is **too many CVs and no way to know which ones to
trust**. A referral solves part of that: somebody the company already trusts is saying *this person is
worth a look*. That is why many companies ask employees for referrals and some pay a bonus when a referred
candidate is hired.

What a referral does, precisely:

- **It gets the CV read by a person**, often before the advert has closed, and sometimes before it is
  published.
- It adds one sentence of trust. The referrer's name is attached to yours.

What it does not do: **it does not skip the interview**, it does not lower the bar, and it does not make the
referrer responsible if you do badly. A referral opens a door; you still walk through it.

This is also why the person referring you does not need to know your work well. They are not vouching for
your skill, which the interview will test; they are **vouching that you are a real, serious candidate**
who will not waste the company's time. That is a much smaller favour than most people imagine, and it is
the one you are asking for.
