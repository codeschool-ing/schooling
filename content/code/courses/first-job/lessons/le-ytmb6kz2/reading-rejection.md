---
title: Reading a rejection
version: 1
---

A rejection is data about one process, and it is easy to read too much into it. What it usually means, and
what it does not:

- **After the screening call**: most often a practical mismatch (salary, contract, shift, location) or
  another candidate further along. Check your answers to lesson 11's practical questions.
- **After a technical interview**: the exercise, the thinking aloud, or simply a stronger candidate that
  day. If the same stage keeps failing, **that is lesson 8's leaking stage**, and the fix is specific:
  practise lesson 12 out loud with a person watching.
- **After the final interview**: usually close. Somebody else fitted slightly better, often for reasons
  you could not have changed (a referral, internal experience, a timezone).

What a single rejection does not mean is that you are not good enough for the field. **The tracker is the
honest judge**: one no is noise; the same no at the same stage five times is a signal.

Feedback, when it comes, is one person's view. If it names something specific (*you didn't ask any
questions about the problem before starting*), it is gold; if it is vague (*we went with a more experienced
candidate*), thank them and file it.
