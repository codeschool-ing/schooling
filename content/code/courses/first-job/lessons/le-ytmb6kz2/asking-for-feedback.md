---
title: Asking for feedback
version: 1
---

After a rejection that followed an interview, **one short, gracious message asking for feedback** is worth
sending. Many companies will not answer, often because their policy forbids it; some will, and one useful
sentence from an interviewer is worth more than any amount of general advice.

> Hi Mariana, thank you for letting me know, and for the time you and the team gave me. If there is one
> thing I could do better in my next interviews, I'd be very grateful to hear it. Either way, I enjoyed
> the conversation about the service desk, and I'd be glad to be considered for future roles. Ana

What makes it answerable:

- **It thanks them first** and does not argue with the decision.
- **It asks for one thing**, not a full evaluation. *One thing I could do better* is easy to answer in a
  line; *why was I rejected?* is not, and it sounds like a challenge.
- It leaves the door open. Companies do come back to runners-up when the first choice leaves, or when a
  second role opens.

Send it once. If there is no reply, that is the answer, and it says nothing about you.
