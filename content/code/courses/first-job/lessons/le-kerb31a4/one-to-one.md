---
title: The one-to-one
version: 1
---

A **one-to-one** is a short, regular meeting between you and your manager, often weekly or every two weeks,
about **you**: your work, what is blocking you, what you are learning. Many teams have them; many do not,
especially for juniors. **If yours does not, ask for one**: *would it be possible to have twenty minutes
every two weeks to check how I'm doing?* It is a normal request, and the way it is received tells you
something about the team.

It works best when you bring the agenda, which is short:

1. **What I finished** since last time.
2. **What is blocking me**, and what I have tried.
3. **One question about how I'm doing**, against the list from the last section.
4. **Anything coming up** they should know: a course, a day off, a worry.

Take notes, and keep them. Over the trial period they become the record of what you did and what was said,
which is useful when the review comes, and they are the raw material for lesson 22's next step.

A one-to-one is also where **bad news goes early**: a task that is late, a mistake from lesson 20, a
problem with a colleague. Said there, it is a conversation. Discovered later, it is a problem.
