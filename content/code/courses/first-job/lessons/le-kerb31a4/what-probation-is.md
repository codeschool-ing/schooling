---
title: What the trial period is
version: 1
---

**In Brazil**, a CLT job often begins with a *contrato de experiência*: a fixed-term contract of **at most
90 days**, which may be split into two periods within that limit (45 and 45 days is common). While it runs,
ending the contract is simpler for both sides than it is afterwards; if you are kept on past its end, the
contract continues as an ordinary one with no end date. Your rights as an employee (salary, *FGTS*, the
proportional *13º* and holiday pay) apply from the first day.

**Elsewhere**, probation is usually three to six months, with a shorter notice period while it lasts.

In both, the question at the end is the same: **would we hire this person again, knowing what we know
now?** Nobody expects a junior to be fast after ninety days. What is judged is closer to the list from
lesson 1: **do they finish what they are given, do they ask before small problems grow, and are they
getting better?**

Which means the trial period is not passed by working late or by one impressive week. It is passed by
**being predictable**: tasks finished when you said, problems raised early, and questions that get more
interesting over time rather than repeating.
