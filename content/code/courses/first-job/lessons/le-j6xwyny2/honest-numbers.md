---
title: Numbers that are true
version: 1
---

CV advice everywhere says to add numbers, and the result is a genre of invented ones: *improved performance
by 40%*, *reduced tickets by 30%*. An interviewer who reads a number asks where it came from, and **a number
you cannot explain costs more than no number at all**, because it turns the conversation into a check on
your honesty.

A number belongs on the line when you can answer three follow-up questions:

- **Measured how?** *I counted the tickets in the queue for the four weeks before and after.*
- **Compared with what?** A number with no baseline is an adjective.
- **Whose was it?** If the team did it, say *the team* and say your part.

A student project rarely has production numbers, and it does not need them. The numbers it has are real
and small: **seven tests**, **one restart after a crash with the data intact**, **a README a stranger
followed in ten minutes**. Those are more persuasive than a percentage, because the reader can check them.

When there is no honest number, write the line without one. Nobody rejects a clear line for lacking a
percentage.
