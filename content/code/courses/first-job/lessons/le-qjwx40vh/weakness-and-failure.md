---
title: Your weakness, and a failure
version: 1
---

Two questions cause more dread than the rest together, and both have the same answer shape.

**What is your biggest weakness?** The answer that fails is the disguised strength: *I'm a perfectionist*,
*I work too hard*. Interviewers have heard it thousands of times, and it says you either cannot see your
weaknesses or will not say them. Lesson 20 of the portfolio course gave the better shape: **a real
weakness, named with what you do about it.** *I tend to stay quiet when I'm stuck for too long. I've started
setting myself a limit: after thirty minutes on the same problem I ask somebody.*

**Tell me about a time you failed.** The answer that fails is the one with no failure in it, or the one that
blames somebody else. The better one is a STAR story where **the result is honest** and the last sentence is
what changed: *I told the customer the repair would be ready on Friday without checking with the workshop.
It was not. I called her before she came in, apologised, and offered a loan phone. Since then I don't give a
date I haven't confirmed.*

In both, **the fix is the point**. Nobody is hiring a person without weaknesses or failures; they are hiring
one who notices them and does something.
