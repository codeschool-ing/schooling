A behavioural question asks about the past because **the past predicts the future better than a promise
does**. *How would you handle a conflict?* invites a promise; *tell me about a time you had one* asks for
evidence.

The most common structure for the answer is called **STAR**, and it is worth using because interviewers
listen for it:

- **Situation**: where and when, in one or two sentences. *In my last job at a phone shop, on a Saturday.*
- **Task**: what you had to do. *A customer's phone had been sent for repair twice, and it was back broken.*
- **Action**: what *you* did, step by step. This is what the question is really about, so it gets most of
  the time. *I…*, not *we…*.
- **Result**: what happened, and one sentence on what you learned or would do differently.

**Structured is not scripted.** Write the four parts of each story as notes, not as sentences to recite. A
recited answer sounds recited, and the follow-up question (*what did your manager say?*) knocks it over.
Know the story; tell it fresh.
