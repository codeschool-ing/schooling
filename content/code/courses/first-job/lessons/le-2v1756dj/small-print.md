title: The small print
version: 1
---

A handful of clauses surprise people after they have signed. Read for these, and **ask about any you do not
understand before signing**, which is a normal and expected question:

- **Probation** (*contrato de experiência* under CLT). Brazilian law limits it to **90 days**, which may be
  split into two periods within that limit. Lesson 21 is about living through it.
- **Non-compete** (*cláusula de não concorrência*): a promise not to work for competitors for a time after
  leaving. Ask what it covers, for how long, and whether you are paid during that time; a broad, unpaid one
  on a junior contract is worth questioning.
- **Intellectual property**: what you create at work belongs to the employer, which is normal. Check it does
  not also claim what you build **in your own time on your own equipment**, including your portfolio.
- **Exclusivity**, in PJ contracts: a clause forbidding other clients makes the contract look even more like
  a job without the job's protections (lesson 2).
- **Notice**: how much notice either side must give, especially in a PJ contract, where the contract itself
  sets it rather than labour law.

None of this needs a lawyer for a standard junior offer. It needs **reading, and one polite question for
each thing that is unclear.** A company that will not answer questions about its own contract has told you
something.
