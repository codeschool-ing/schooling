LinkedIn is also a feed, and a great deal of advice about it is about the feed: post every day, comment on
influencers, write about what you learned. **For finding a first job, very little of that matters.** A
recruiter finds you by search and reads your profile; neither depends on how often you post.

Three things are worth knowing:

- **The *open to work* setting** can be shown to recruiters only, or to everybody with a green frame on the
  photo. Showing it to recruiters costs nothing. Whether to show it publicly is a matter of taste; nobody
  serious holds it against a junior.
- **Connections** matter for one thing: a referral (lesson 9). Connect with people you studied with, worked
  with or met, and with people at companies you want to work for, with a line saying why.
- **Posting** is worth doing when you have something real: the project is deployed, the retrospective is
  written. One post about a finished thing, with a link, is worth more than thirty about motivation.

Everything else, from endorsements to badges for quizzes, is noise. Spend the time on the next
application.
