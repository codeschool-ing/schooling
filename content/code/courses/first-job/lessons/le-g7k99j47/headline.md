LinkedIn calls it the *headline*: the line under your name. It follows you everywhere (search results,
comments, the list of applicants a recruiter sees) and **in a search result it is the only thing besides
your name and city**.

A recruiter searching for a junior support person types words like *suporte técnico*, *help desk*,
*infraestrutura* or *júnior*. The headline that gets clicked contains **those words, and says what you
do**, in the same way as the line under the name on the CV:

- *IT support, junior · runbooks and small-office labs*
- *Suporte técnico N1 · Windows, redes e atendimento*
- *Junior backend developer · Python and SQL · one deployed project*

What does not work is what most first profiles write: *student*, *passionate about technology*, *seeking
new opportunities*, *future developer*. None of them is a word a recruiter searches for, and all of them
say what you would like to be rather than what you do.

**Write it in the language your adverts use.** If you are applying in Portuguese, write the headline in
Portuguese; if the jobs you want are advertised in English, write it in English. LinkedIn lets you add a
second-language version of the profile, but the headline a search matches is the one in the language of
the search.
