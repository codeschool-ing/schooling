---
title: What the ten seconds look for
version: 1
---

A recruiter screening CVs for a junior role reads the top third of the page and decides whether to read
the rest. They are answering two questions, and **the top of your CV should answer both before they ask**:

1. **What does this person do?** One line under the name: *IT support, junior. Runbooks and small-office
   labs.* Not an objective, not a list of adjectives; the job you are applying for, in the words of the
   advert.
2. **Have they done it?** The evidence, as high on the page as possible: the portfolio project with its
   result, a previous job that involved the same verbs, an internship. Lesson 4 is about writing that part.

Everything else on the page supports those two answers or competes with them. **A CV is not a record of
everything you have done**; it is an argument for one job, and the reader stops at the first line that
does not help it.

That is also why the answer to "one page or two" is one, for a junior. Not because a rule says so, but
because the second page is read by nobody who did not already decide on the first.

**In Brazil**, many companies receive CVs through a tracking system (the application forms on their
careers pages), which extracts the text and lets the recruiter search it. The advice does not change; it
adds one practical point: **send a PDF whose text can be selected**, made from a document rather than a
scanned image, or the system reads nothing at all.
