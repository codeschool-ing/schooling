Cut, in this order:

**What is private.** A photo, age, date of birth, marital status, children, *CPF*, *RG* and a full street
address. None of it tells anybody whether you can do the job, all of it invites a judgement that has
nothing to do with the job, and a document number on a file that will be forwarded around is a document
number you no longer control. **City and state are enough.** Brazilian CVs traditionally carry most of
this, and many still do; leaving it out is not unusual any more, and nobody will ask for it before an
offer.

**What every CV claims.** *Proactive, team player, good communicator, fast learner, Office.* These are
the words everybody writes, so they carry no information. If you are a good communicator, the evidence is a
runbook somebody else followed; put that instead.

**What cannot be read in ten seconds.** Twenty-three online courses in a list. Keep the two or three that
matter for the advert, and group the rest in one line (*also: networking, Linux and Python courses*).

**The objective paragraph.** *To grow professionally in a dynamic company.* Replace it with the one line
from the previous section that says what you do.

Two things stay that first CVs often drop: **a working e-mail and phone, and links**: GitHub, LinkedIn and,
if the project is deployed, its address. A recruiter who wants to call you should not have to look.
