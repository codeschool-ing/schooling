**Tools** change every few years: the ticketing system, the monitoring product, the framework. Learn them
**when the job needs them**, and do not spend your own evenings chasing each new one; the job will teach you
the ones it uses faster than a course can.

**The adjacent skill** is the thing next to what you do: scripting for somebody in support, networking for
somebody in development, SQL for almost everybody. **One at a time, chosen on purpose**, it is what moves
you from junior to the next rung, because it lets you finish a larger piece without help.

**Fundamentals** (how networks, operating systems, data and software actually behave, and how they fail)
change slowly and compound. The track taught mostly this layer, which is why it will outlast the tools it
was taught with. After the track, **this is the layer nobody schedules for you**: the work rarely asks you
to understand why something works, only to make it work.

**Certifications** have a place, especially in support and infrastructure, where some employers filter by
them. Take one when an advert you want asks for it, or when it gives structure to the adjacent skill; not
as a substitute for the work.
