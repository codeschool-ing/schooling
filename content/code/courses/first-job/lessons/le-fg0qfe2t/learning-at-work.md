title: Learning from the work
version: 1
---

The cheapest place to learn is the work you are already paid for, and three habits turn it into learning
rather than just repetition:

1. Keep a work log. A few lines a day: what you did, what surprised you, what you looked up. After a
   month it shows you what you are learning; after a year it is the evidence for a raise, a promotion or a
   CV (lesson 4 wants results, and this is where they are written down).
2. Ask why, once a week. Pick one thing you did because you were told to (*always restart the print
   spooler first*) and find out why it works. That is how the fundamentals layer grows without a course.
3. Read the reviews of your work closely. A code review, a comment on a ticket, a correction from a
   senior: each is a lesson somebody wrote specifically for you. Keep a list of the ones that repeat.

These are the same habits the retrospective of the portfolio course practised: **facts first, then
conclusions**, written down while they are fresh.
