title: The next move
version: 1
---

There is no right number of years to stay in a first job, and anybody who gives you one is guessing. There
are better signals:

- You have stopped learning. The work log has been saying the same thing for months, and there is no
  larger piece anybody will hand you.
- The next rung is not there. Nobody at the company is doing the job you want next, so there is nobody
  to learn it from.
- **The pay has fallen well behind the market** for what you now do, and asking about it (in a one-to-one,
  with lesson 17's research) changed nothing.

When one of those is true, the whole of this course applies again, from a better place: the CV has a job on
it now, the evidence is real work, and the people you worked with are the referrals of lesson 9.

**Move internally first, when you can.** A support person who wants to move into infrastructure, or a
developer who wants to move into data, often gets there fastest by asking to take on that work where they
already are.

And whatever you do next, **keep something you can show**. The portfolio project was the evidence for the
first job. For the second, the evidence is what you did in the first, written down while you still remember
it.
