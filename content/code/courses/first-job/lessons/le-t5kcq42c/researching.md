---
title: Researching a range
version: 1
---

A salary expectation is not a wish; it is **a range you can explain**, and the explanation is where you
found it. Three sources, roughly in order of how much to trust them:

1. **Adverts that state a range**, for the same role, level and city. Increasingly common in Brazil, and
   the most direct evidence there is. Note ten of them in the tracker.
2. **People in the role**, asked plainly: *what does a junior support job pay at a company like yours?*
   Most will give a range, if not their own number.
3. Salary surveys and sites that collect self-reported pay. Useful for the shape of the market, less so
   for one company; the figures are what people chose to report.

From these, write down **a range for this role in this city**, with its lower end the least you would
accept and its upper end the best supported by the evidence. Two things change it: **remote roles** for
companies elsewhere often pay by another city's market, and **the kind of contract** changes what the
number means, which is the next section.

What to leave out of the research: your current salary, if it is from another field. It says nothing about
what this work is worth.
