---
title: Answering the question
version: 1
---

When the question comes, in a form or on the phone, the answer is **the range, where it came from, and what
it depends on**, in one sentence:

> *Based on adverts for junior support roles in São Paulo, between R$ X and R$ Y under CLT, depending on the
> benefits.*

Three details make it work:

- Say the contract. A range with no contract is ambiguous, and the ambiguity rarely resolves in your
  favour.
- Put the lower end where you would really accept it. A range is heard from the bottom, so the bottom
  has to be a number you are content with.
- **A form field that needs one number** gets the middle of your range, not the bottom.

If they ask first for your current salary, you can answer with the expectation instead: *what I'm looking
for in this role is…*. And if you already have their range from the advert or the screening call (lesson
11), you do not need to name a number at all: *the range you mentioned works for me*, or *I'd hoped for the
upper part of it, given the project I've done.*

Never invent a competing offer to push the number up. It is easy to check, and being caught ends the
process.
