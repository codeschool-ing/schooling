---
title: The first mistake
version: 1
---

You will make a mistake in the first ninety days, probably a visible one: a change that breaks something, a
ticket closed that was not fixed, a message sent to the wrong group. **What happens next is what people
remember**, and there is a right order:

1. Say it, early, to the person who needs to know. *I think my change this morning broke the login
   page for the second floor.* An hour of silence while you try to fix it alone turns a small mistake into
   an incident.
2. **Help fix it**, or get out of the way of the person who can, whichever the situation needs.
3. **Afterwards, say what you will do differently**, in one sentence, as lesson 15 taught: *I'll test on
   one machine before rolling out to the floor.*

What not to do: hide it, blame the tool, or apologise at such length that somebody else has to reassure
you. Teams that work well treat an honest mistake by a junior as expected. **The thing they do not forgive
easily is finding out later that you knew.**
