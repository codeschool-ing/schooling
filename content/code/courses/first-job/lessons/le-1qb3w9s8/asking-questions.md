The first month is for **learning how things work here**: who does what, which systems matter, how work
arrives and how it is judged. It feels unproductive. It is the most productive month you will have, because
everything after it depends on it.

Asking is how you learn it, and there is a way to ask that respects other people's time:

- Try first, for a set time. Fifteen or thirty minutes on your own, depending on the task. Lesson 15's
  weakness answer was a real rule: *after thirty minutes stuck, I ask*.
- Ask with what you tried. *I'm trying to reset a user's MFA; I found the admin page but the option is
  greyed out. Is there a permission I'm missing?* That question can be answered in a sentence.
- **Write the answer down**, somewhere you will find it, so you ask each thing once. Many teams value a
  junior who turns their own questions into a page of notes for the next person.
- **Keep a list of the small questions** and ask them together at a set time, rather than interrupting ten
  times a day.

A junior who asks nothing for a month is not independent; they are guessing, and the guesses surface later
as mistakes.
