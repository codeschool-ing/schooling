---
title: The first thing you ship
version: 1
---

The first thing you finish at work (a commit, a closed ticket, a procedure documented) matters more for what
it tells people than for what it is. It should be **small, finished properly and visible**:

- Small. Choose, or ask for, something you can finish in a day or two: a bug with a clear cause, a
  runbook page that is out of date, a script that automates one manual step. A large first task that drags
  for three weeks is the worst start.
- Finished properly. Everything the portfolio course practised applies here, at the team's standard:
  a commit message that says what and why, a self-review before asking for a review, a test if the team
  writes tests, a note in the ticket saying what you checked.
- Visible. Say that it is done, where the team says such things: the stand-up, the ticket, the chat.
  Not as a boast; as the normal end of a task.

For support roles the equivalent is the first ticket you close on your own, and the same three rules apply.
A ticket closed with **what was wrong, what you did and how you confirmed it** is the tech-support course's
method, seen by your new colleagues for the first time.
