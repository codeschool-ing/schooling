---
title: When a letter is worth writing
version: 1
---

A covering letter (in Portuguese *carta de apresentação*) is worth the time in three situations, and a
waste of it in most others:

1. The advert asks for one. Then not sending one is failing the first instruction.
2. Something needs explaining that the CV cannot. A career change (*I worked in a shop for five years
   and have spent the last one studying IT support*), a gap, a move to another city, or why a candidate
   from another field fits this role.
3. You have a real reason for this company. Not *I admire your culture*, but something specific: you
   use their product, you know somebody who works there, their advert names the exact thing your project
   does.

When none of the three applies, **a short, specific message beats a long, generic letter**, and an empty
box beats a pasted one. A reader who meets the same letter in three applications from the same person has
learned something, and it is not good.

**In Brazil** covering letters are uncommon for junior roles, and many application forms do not have a box
for one. **In English-language applications**, especially to companies abroad, they are more expected, and
the three situations above are still the test of whether to write one.
