---
title: Reading the advert
version: 1
---

A job advert is written by several people, and it shows. The team lead wrote what they need this quarter,
HR added what every advert in the company says, and somebody pasted the list of tools from the last one.
The result reads like a single demand and is really **three lists stuck together**.

Read it with three markers:

- What the work is. The verbs in the responsibilities section: *support users*, *maintain*, *write
  tests*, *document*. This is the job, and it is what lesson 3 of the portfolio course told you to
  build around.
- What they need on day one. Usually two or three items, often the first ones in the requirements, and
  often the only ones that also appear in the responsibilities.
- What they would like. Everything else: the fourth framework, the certification, the "desirable".

**Apply when you have the first two and some of the third.** A junior advert asking for three years of
experience with five tools is not describing a person who exists at that salary; it is describing the
ideal, and the company knows it. Nobody checks the list line by line; somebody reads your CV for ten
seconds looking for the work and the day-one needs.

Two lines deserve a second look rather than a pass. **A salary that is not stated** is normal in Brazil and
elsewhere, and lesson 17 deals with it. **A junior role that asks you to be the only person responsible for
everything** (the whole infrastructure, the whole product) is a senior role with a junior salary, and it
is worth asking in the screening call who you would learn from.
