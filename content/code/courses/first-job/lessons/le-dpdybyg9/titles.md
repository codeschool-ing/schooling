---
title: Titles in Brazil and elsewhere
version: 1
---

The same rung has different names depending on where the advert was written.

**In Brazil** the ladder is *estágio*, *trainee*, *júnior*, *pleno*, *sênior*, sometimes followed by
*especialista*. *Estágio* is an internship with its own law: it requires being enrolled in a course,
limits the hours, and is not a job contract. *Trainee* is a programme some large companies run for recent
graduates, with rotations and a selection process of its own. Most first jobs are *júnior*.

**In English-language adverts** the same rungs are *intern*, *graduate* or *entry-level*, *junior*,
*mid-level* and *senior*, and some companies number their levels instead (*Engineer I*, *II*, *III*).
The number means nothing outside the company; read the responsibilities, not the label.

What the first job looks like also depends on the field:

::: track it-support networks-infra
**In support and infrastructure** the first rung is often named by the level of the queue: *N1* (or *L1*)
takes the call, follows the runbook and escalates; *N2* investigates what N1 could not solve; *N3* is the
specialist or the vendor. A junior role is N1 or N2. The advert's "support" and "infrastructure" can mean
the same desk, so the responsibilities section tells you which queue you would sit on.
:::

::: track *
**In development and data roles** the first rung is usually *junior* with no qualifier, and the
responsibilities say which part of the product you would work on. An advert that names a team (payments,
the mobile app, the data platform) tells you more than one that lists languages.
:::

Whichever the title, the question for the rest of this course is the same: **what would they hand you in
the first month, and can you show that you finish that kind of thing?**
