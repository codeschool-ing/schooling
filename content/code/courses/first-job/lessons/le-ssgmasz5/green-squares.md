title: The green squares
version: 1
---

The contribution graph (a year of squares, greener for days with more commits) worries juniors far more
than it interests reviewers. It measures **how often you pushed**, not what you built, and everybody who
reviews profiles knows how easily it is filled.

What it can do is contradict you. A CV that says *six months building this project* next to a graph that
is empty for those six months invites a question, and **the honest answer is usually that the work was
somewhere else**: a private repository, a work account, a lab that never touched GitHub. That is a fine
answer; have it ready.

Two things are worth doing and nothing more:

1. **Make sure your commits count**, by committing with an e-mail GitHub links to your account. Lesson 18 of
   the portfolio course set up the no-reply address for this.
2. Let the graph be whatever it is. Committing daily to keep it green, or with scripts that make empty
   commits, is visible to anybody who clicks on a square, and it reads exactly as it is.

For support and infrastructure roles, where much of the evidence is documentation and a lab, GitHub is one
place among several. A runbook in a repository with a clean history is worth more than a green year of
exercise commits.
