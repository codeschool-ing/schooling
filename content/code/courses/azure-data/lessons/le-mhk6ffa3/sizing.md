**The question is not which way of paying is cheapest, but how much of the reserved capacity would
sit idle.** Manual pays for its peak every hour; autoscale pays for each hour's own peak, at a higher
price per unit; serverless pays only for what was used, at a much higher price per unit again. The
program below puts one day of Quitanda's orders, repeated for thirty days, through all three. It
needs nothing but Python, and **every price and request charge in it is illustrative**, as lesson 1's
price sheet section explains: round numbers to do the sum with, not Azure's. Save it as `sizing.py`
in `~/azure`:

```
peak 2,700 RU/s, 672,624,000 request units in 30 days
  manual at 2,700 RU/s all month          155.52 USD
  autoscale up to 3,000 RU/s               36.00 USD
  serverless                               168.16 USD
autoscale is billed at 14% of its maximum on average
```

**Autoscale costs less than a quarter of manual for this load, and the reason is the last line.**
Manual reserves the promotion's 2,700 RU/s in every one of the 24 hours: 27 hundreds, 720 hours,
155.52 dollars at the illustrative price. Autoscale bills each hour at its own need or at 300, a tenth
of its 3,000 maximum, which comes to 14% of the maximum on average. That is far under the two thirds
where its higher price per unit would catch up, so it comes to 36.00 dollars.

**Serverless comes last, at 168.16 dollars, for a container that is idle most of the day.** Its price
is per unit consumed, and at these illustrative prices an hour of 100 RU/s reserved costs 0.008
dollars, while the 360,000 units that hour could carry cost 0.09 consumed. Serverless is therefore
cheaper than manual only for capacity that would be busy less than 0.008 / 0.09, about 9%, of the time.
Quitanda's manual reservation is busy 672,624,000 units out of 2,700 × 3,600 × 24 × 30, about 9.6%, so
the two come out close and serverless loses. A container read a few hundred times a day is where it
wins.

**Change the shape and the answer flips.** If Quitanda took 300 orders a second around the clock, the
same arithmetic by hand gives manual 155.52 dollars, unchanged; autoscale billed at 2,700 every hour,
at 1.5 times the price, 233.28; and serverless 1,749.60. A load that is busy all day is the case for
manual, and the busier and steadier it is, the further ahead manual gets.

Two things in the program are guesses, and they matter more than the prices. **The 6 request units of
a write and the 1 of a read are assumptions**: on Azure, write one real order and read the header, as
the first section said, and put the measured numbers in. And **the load is a day somebody wrote down**:
the hourly order counts belong in the program from the shop's own records, not from this page.
