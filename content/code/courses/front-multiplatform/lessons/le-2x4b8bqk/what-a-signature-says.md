**A signature on a package says two things: who made it, and that nobody has changed it since.** It does not
say the app is good, or safe, or that anybody read its code. It is a mathematical promise made with a
**private key**, which only you hold, and checked with the matching **public key**, which anybody may have.
Change one byte of the package and the check fails; sign with a different key and the check names somebody
else.

Every system in this course checks one, and each checks it for its own reason:

| system | what it checks | what happens without one |
|---|---|---|
| Android | that every update is signed with the same key as the app already installed | the phone refuses to install it over the old one |
| iOS | that Apple issued the certificate that signed it, for this device or for the store | it does not install at all |
| macOS | a Developer ID signature, and Apple's notarisation | a warning that the app cannot be checked, and an extra step to open it |
| Windows | an Authenticode signature, and the reputation of the certificate behind it | SmartScreen's blue window, "Windows protected your PC" |
| Linux | nothing, by default; the person may check a checksum and a signature | it installs, and the person takes your word for it |

**Android's rule is the one that costs most when it is forgotten.** A phone keeps the app's signature and
accepts an update only if it carries the same one. Lose the key, and no update you build can ever replace the
app on anybody's phone: it has to be published again, under a new identifier, and every person who installed
it has to find the new one.

## Play App Signing: two keys instead of one

Google Play splits that risk in two. **Your upload key** signs the bundle you send, which tells Play the upload
is yours. **The app signing key** is the one phones see, and Play keeps it: it signs the APKs it cuts from your
bundle. If the upload key is lost or stolen, Play's support can register a new one, and phones never notice,
because the key they check did not change. New apps on Play use this arrangement; an APK you hand out
yourself is signed with your own key, and then that key is the one phones check.
