Order totals are skewed: most are small, a few are very large. The mean is pulled up by the large
ones, a histogram in reais is one tall bar and a long empty tail, and every scale in the previous
section suffered from it. **A logarithm turns multiplication into addition**, so R$ 10, R$ 100 and
R$ 1,000 become 1, 2 and 3, evenly spaced, and the tail folds in.

The logarithm has one hard rule: it is defined only for positive numbers. This data set has zeros,
the 137 coupon orders lesson 9 decided were charged nothing:

```
ana@lab:~/clean$ python -c "import numpy as np; from ready import orders; t = orders['total']; print((t == 0).sum()); print(np.log10(t).min())" 2>&1
/home/ana/venv/lib/python3.12/site-packages/pandas/core/arraylike.py:402: RuntimeWarning: divide by zero encountered in log10
  result = getattr(ufunc, method)(*inputs, **kwargs)
137
-inf
```

Python prints a warning once, on standard error, and carries on: **the logarithm of zero is minus infinity**,
and one `-inf` turns every mean, standard deviation and sum computed from the column into `-inf` or
`NaN`. In a notebook the warning scrolls past. In a scheduled job nobody reads it at all.

There are three honest ways out, and each says something different:

- **Leave the zeros out**, as blanks, and state how many. The logged column then describes orders
  that were paid for, which is often the question.
- **Add a constant first**, `log10(total + 1)`, which keeps every row. It is common and it bends
  small values: R$ 0.25 and R$ 0 end up close, and the result is no longer a logarithm of money.
- **Keep the zeros in a separate column**, a flag such as `free_order`, and log the rest. A model
  or a chart can then use both.

This lesson takes the first:

mean 93.87  median 57.60  geometric mean 60.14

The skew of the totals is 65.2; of their logarithms, 0.02, practically symmetrical. And the mean of
the logarithms, turned back with `10 **`, is the **geometric mean**, R$ 60.14: much closer to the
median of R$ 57.60 than the arithmetic mean of R$ 93.87 is. For a skewed amount the geometric mean
is often the "typical order" people are looking for when they reach for an average.
