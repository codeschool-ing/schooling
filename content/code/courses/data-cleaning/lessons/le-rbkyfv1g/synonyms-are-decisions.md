---
title: Synonyms are decisions
version: 1
---

**Some lines of the mapping table are spelling and some are judgement**, and only the second kind
needs anybody's permission. `frutas` and `FRUTAS` are one label by any standard. These are not:

| key | mapped to | the question it hides |
|---|---|---|
| `folhas` | Verduras | are leaves a kind of green, or a separate shelf? |
| `laticinios` | Ovos e laticínios | does a dairy product without eggs belong with eggs? |
| `emporio` | Mercearia | is the delicatessen a category of its own? |
| `graos` | Grãos e cereais | is a bag of beans in the same group as oats? |
| `fruta` | Frutas | none: a singular |

Each of those first four is a choice about how the business wants to see itself, and each one moves
numbers. If `Folhas` is a category of its own, the greens report shows one product fewer; if
`Empório` stands apart, the delicatessen gets a line in every sales table, and somebody will ask
why it is so small.

**The analyst proposes and the owner of the categories decides.** Here that is the buying team:
they made the categories, they know what `Empório` was meant to hold, and they will be asked about
the reports. A mapping table with a column for who confirmed each judgement line — the same idea as
lesson 3's missingness log — makes the decision visible and reversible.

Two signals help separate spelling from meaning before anybody is asked:

- **how many rows use each label.** `Laticínios` on one product and `Ovos e laticínios` on five
  suggests the one is a slip; two labels of similar size are more likely two real categories;
- **what the products under each label have in common.** Listing the products under `Folhas` and
  under `Verduras` side by side answers most of the question by itself: here `Folhas` holds a
  single product, of the same kind as the twelve under `Verduras`.
