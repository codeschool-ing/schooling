**The question is not which mode is cheaper, but how busy the capacity would be.** Provisioned
capacity costs the same every hour; on demand costs in proportion to use. They cost the same at one
utilisation, and the price sheet fixes it: below it on demand is cheaper, above it provisioned is. The
program below puts a month of writes through both, for a load written hour by hour. Save it as
`~/aws/cost.py`, beside `prices.py`.

**The same price at 28.9% utilisation.** That is 0.000975 / (3,600 × 0.0000009375), the hour of a
write unit against the on-demand price of the 3,600 writes it could carry. Capacity that is busy more
than 28.9% of the time, averaged over the hours it is paid for, is cheaper provisioned; capacity that
is idle more than 71.1% of the time is cheaper on demand.

**The steady load is the case for provisioned.** 200 writes a second around the clock is 518,400,000
writes in 30 days, 486.00 dollars on demand. Provisioned at 286 units, 200 at the target of 70%, it is
200.77 dollars, and following the load changes nothing because there is nothing to follow. The units
are busy 70% of the time, far above 28.9%, and provisioned costs 41% of on demand.

**The spiky load is the case for on demand, unless the spike can be planned.** Quitanda's orders are
20 writes a second most of the day and 1,000 in the hour of the evening promotion. On demand it is
157,680,000 writes and 147.83 dollars. Provisioned for the peak all month, 1,429 units, it is 1,003.16
dollars, almost seven times as much, because those units sit at 1.4% of their capacity for 23 hours a
day. Provisioned capacity that followed the load perfectly, 29 units most of the day and 1,429 for one
hour, would be 61.31 dollars, the cheapest of all.

**That last line is the one nobody gets.** It is what autoscaling would cost if it could move 1,400
units up at 18:00 and down at 19:00 exactly. The next section shows why it cannot: it reacts to
load that has already arrived, it takes minutes to do it, and it may only come down a limited number
of times a day. A promotion that starts at a known time can be met with scheduled scaling, raising the
capacity at 17:55 and lowering it after. A spike that arrives when a message goes viral cannot, and
that is the load on demand exists for.
