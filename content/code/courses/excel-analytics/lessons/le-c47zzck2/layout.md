**A dashboard is read in a fixed order, and the layout decides that order before the reader does.**
In a language written left to right the eye starts at the top left and moves right, then down. So
what the reader came for goes first, what explains it goes second, and what they can act on goes
third. A chart placed where it looked tidy is read in whatever order it happened to land.

timeline covers January to June 2026 and the slicer is on every channel.

## Three bands, top to bottom

**The KPI row answers the question.** Four cards, each with the number large and last year's
beneath it in words a person can say aloud: *R$ 15,943, down 10.4% on R$ 17,789*. A reader who
stops here knows whether there is a problem.

**The chart of months explains it.** The KPI row says the half-year is 10.4% down. The chart says
when: January to March 2026 brought R$ 12,139 against R$ 9,303 a year earlier, and April to June
brought R$ 3,804 against R$ 8,486. The fall is all in the second quarter, and no total could show
that, because a total of a good quarter and a bad one looks like a mediocre half-year.

**The breakdowns say where to act.** By channel, `Wholesale` lost R$ 2,264 against the first half
of 2025 while `Online` gained R$ 442. By product, `SUL1K` lost R$ 3,320 and `CER1K` gained
R$ 3,461, so the 1 kg sales shifted from one origin to the other. Those are the two places the
owner can do something on Monday.

**The slicer and the timeline sit above what they filter**, at the top, where the hand looks for a
control. Placed at the bottom, they look like part of the last chart and seem to filter only that.

## Rules that keep it to one screen

- **Everything visible without scrolling**, at 100% zoom, on the screen the reader actually uses. A
  dashboard that scrolls has a second half that most readers never see. If it does not fit, it is
  two dashboards, or it has a chart too many.
- **The cell grid is the layout grid.** Make the columns of the `Dashboard` sheet narrow and equal,
  and size every card and chart to a whole number of them, so edges line up without effort.
- **Gridlines and headings off**, on this sheet only, from the **View** tab. They help somebody
  editing cells and only add noise for somebody reading.
- **One accent colour, and never colour alone.** Neutral for the data, one colour for "worse than
  last year", and always the sign and the word as well, as lesson 9 argued.
- **The chart type follows the question**, as lesson 12 set out: columns for months against months,
  bars sorted by value for channels and products.
- **Numbers formatted for reading**: whole reais with a thousands separator, percentages with one
  decimal. R$ 15,943.00 has two digits nobody needs.

## A title that says what is on screen

The top line names the business, the period and how fresh the data is: *Café Serra · sales ·
data to 23 June 2026*. The date comes from a formula, so it cannot be forgotten when the data
changes, and the next section shows it. Without it, a reader in August cannot tell a dashboard
refreshed yesterday from one nobody has touched since June.
