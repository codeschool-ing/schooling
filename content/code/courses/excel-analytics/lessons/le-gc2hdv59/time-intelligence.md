title: Year to date, last year, and the growth between them
version: 1

**DAX's period functions move the dates of a cell before a measure is worked out.** "Year to date"
is the cell's dates stretched back to 1 January. "Last year" is the cell's dates moved back twelve
months. Both are `CALCULATE` with a filter on `Calendar[Date]`, and DAX has functions that build
those filters, which is why lesson 15 built a calendar with every day of two whole years and marked
it as the date table.

```schooling-example
{"language": "dax", "parts": [
 {"code": "Revenue YTD := TOTALYTD([Total Revenue], Calendar[Date])", "note": "From 1 January of the cell's year to the last date of the cell. In the March row, January to March."},
 {"code": "Revenue LY := CALCULATE([Total Revenue], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(Calendar[Date]))", "note": "The cell's dates, each moved back one year, and Total Revenue over those. In the March 2026 row, March 2025."},
 {"code": "Revenue YoY % := DIVIDE([Total Revenue] - [Revenue LY], [Revenue LY])", "note": "The growth on the same period last year. DIVIDE answers blank when there is no last year to divide by."}
]}
```

**The rows of the pivot table must come from `Calendar`**, not from `Sales[Date]`. The functions
move the calendar's dates, and the filter reaches `Sales` along the relationship; a row label taken
from `Sales[Date]` gives them nothing to move.

## Month by month, 2026

Put `Calendar[Year]` and `Calendar[Month name]` in Rows, filter the year to 2026, and add the four
measures. Computed, like the rest of this lesson, from your rows rather than by Excel:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 5,004 | 4,121 | 21.4% | 5,004 |
| Feb | 2,866 | 2,122 | 35.1% | 7,870 |
| Mar | 4,269 | 3,060 | 39.5% | 12,139 |
| Apr | 971 | 2,839 | −65.8% | 13,110 |

| Dec | | 2,338 | −100.0% | 15,943 |
| **2026** | **15,943** | **35,551** | **−55.2%** | **15,943** |

The first quarter grew on 2025, by 21.4%, 35.1% and 39.5%. The second fell, by between 44% and 66% a
month. That is a finding about Café Serra worth an afternoon of questions, and the kind a dashboard
in lesson 17 is built to show.

The rest of the table is a finding about the model. **July to December say −100.0%**, and nothing
fell by 100%: those months have not happened yet. `Total Revenue` is blank there, because no sale
fell in them, and `Revenue LY` is not, because 2025 had sales in every month. A blank less 1,727,
divided by 1,727, is −100%. **And the year's row says −55.2%**, comparing six months of 2026 with
twelve of 2025. In the year's row the cell's dates are all of 2026, because the calendar runs to
31 December as lesson 15 section 06 decided, and `SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR` moves all of them, so last
year is all of 2025.

## Comparing like with like

The fix is to compare the same months, and the cleanest way is to let the report say which months
those are. Add a slicer on `Calendar[Month]` and select 1 to 6. Now the 2026 cell's dates are
January to June 2026, last year's are January to June 2025, and the year's row says:

| | Total Revenue | Revenue LY | Revenue YoY % |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2026, January to June** | **15,943** | **17,789** | **−10.4%** |

**Revenue in the first half of 2026 was 10.4% below the first half of 2025**, not 55.2%. Both numbers
came out of the same measure on the same rows; the second compared what had happened with what had
not. A timeline from lesson 11, set to January to June 2026, does the same job for that year.

The months of 2025 have nothing to compare with: `Revenue LY` is blank there, since the calendar
and the data start in 2025, and `DIVIDE` leaves `Revenue YoY %` blank rather than showing an error.
That is correct, and it is the reason to write a growth measure with `DIVIDE` rather than `/`.

`Revenue YTD` for June 2026, R$ 15,943, is the same number as the year's total, because nothing was
sold after June. In a workbook that is still being filled, that number is the one a manager asks
for on any given day: how much so far this year.
