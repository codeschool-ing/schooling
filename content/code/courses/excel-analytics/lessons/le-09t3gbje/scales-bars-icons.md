**The rules of the last two sections sort cells into two groups, coloured and not. A colour scale,
a data bar or an icon set grades every cell instead**, by where its value sits between the
smallest and the largest. That is a picture of a whole range at once, and it is only as honest as
the scale it is measured against.

## A grid worth shading

A grade means something when the cells are comparable, and 108 individual sales mostly are not.
A summary is: revenue by month and channel. Add a sheet called `By month` and type `Month`,
`Wholesale`, `Online`, `Shop` and `Total` in A1:E1. In A2 and A3:

```localised
=DATE(2025,1,1)
=EDATE(A2, 1)
```

Fill A3 down to A19, for the eighteen months to June 2026, and format A2:A19 to show a month and a
year. In B2, the total for one month and one channel, built as lesson 5 built its grid:

```localised
=SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Channel], B$1, Sales[Date], ">="&$A2, Sales[Date], "<="&EOMONTH($A2, 0))
```

Fill it right to D2 and down to row 19. In E2, `=SUM(B2:D2)`, filled down. The grand total of
column E is **51,494**, the same as `Sales`, which is the check that no month and no channel was
missed.

## Colour scales: one scale, or one per column

Select B2:D19 and choose **Conditional Formatting › Color Scales**, and any scale of two colours.
The grid now shades from the smallest month to the largest. And it says one thing: the `Wholesale`
column is dark and the `Shop` column is pale all the way down. The largest cell is **4,538**,
wholesale in January 2026, and the best month the shop ever had is 274, so on one scale the shop
is always at the bottom.

That is true, and you knew it before the colours. To see each channel's own months, give each
column its own scale: select B2:B19 and apply the scale, then C2:C19, then D2:D19, as three
separate rules.

## Icon sets: thresholds you did not choose

Icons are where the default settings mislead most. Select `Sales[Revenue]` again and choose
**Icon Sets** and the three coloured arrows. Most of the column turns into red arrows pointing
down.

The arrows are not saying anything about the sales. By default the green arrow goes to values at
or above **67 percent** and the red one to values below **33 percent**, and percent here means a
share of the distance from the smallest value to the largest, not a share of the sales:

```localised
=MIN(Sales[Revenue])+0.67*(MAX(Sales[Revenue])-MIN(Sales[Revenue]))
=MIN(Sales[Revenue])+0.33*(MAX(Sales[Revenue])-MIN(Sales[Revenue]))
```

The revenue runs from 34 to 2,120, so the lines fall at **1,431.62** and **722.38**. A formula
counts the red arrows:

```localised
=COUNTIF(Sales[Revenue], "<"&(MIN(Sales[Revenue])+0.33*(MAX(Sales[Revenue])-MIN(Sales[Revenue]))))
```

**86** of the 108 sales get a red arrow, 11 get the yellow one and 11 the green. A handful of big
wholesale orders stretched the range, and everything else landed at its bottom.

The thresholds can be changed under **Conditional Formatting › Manage Rules › Edit Rule**: each
icon gets a value and a type, which can be **Number**, **Percent**, **Formula** or **Percentile**.
Percentile splits by rank, so about a third of the sales gets each arrow whatever their size. But the
deeper problem is the arrow itself: an arrow pointing down says that something fell, and a sale of
R$ 115 did not fall from anywhere. The next section is about choosing a picture that says what
you mean.
