**A formula does not keep the addresses you typed. It keeps where they are, counted from the cell
it sits in.** Type `=E2*F2` in H2 and Excel stores "the cell three columns to the left, times the
cell two columns to the left, on this row". Copy it to H3 and the same instruction, read from H3,
is `=E3*F3`. That is a **relative reference**. It is the default, and it is the reason one formula
can fill a column of a hundred rows or a million.

## The Revenue column

Lesson 1 left `Sales` without revenue on purpose. Add it now. This column stays for the rest of
the course, and later lessons expect it in column H.

1. On the `Sales` sheet, click **H1** and type `Revenue`.
2. Click **H2**, type this formula and press Enter:

```localised
=E2*F2
```

H2 shows **1456**: sale S1001, 14 bags at R$ 104. Now click H2 again. In the bottom-right corner
of the selection there is a small square, the **fill handle**. Double-click it.

Excel copies the formula down as far as the column beside it has data, which is row 109. Click H3
and the formula bar shows `=E3*F3`. Click H109 and it shows `=E109*F109`, with the value **225**:
five bags at R$ 45. Nobody typed those two formulas. Each is the formula of H2, read from its own
row.

Dragging the fill handle down does the same thing, and so does selecting H2:H109 and pressing
**Ctrl+D**, which is **Home › Fill › Down**. The double-click is the quickest, with one condition:
it stops at the first empty cell of the neighbouring column, so a gap in column G would stop it
halfway without saying so.

## Checking the column

A column of 108 formulas deserves one check. In an empty cell outside the data, J1 for instance:

```localised
=SUM(H2:H109)
```

It answers **51494**: R$ 51,494 of sales in eighteen months. Keep that number. Lesson 5 reaches it
with `SUMIFS`, lesson 10 with a pivot table and lesson 16 with a DAX measure, and the four have to
agree. Delete the check from J1 and save the workbook.

## Copying sideways moves it too

Relative works in both directions. Copy H2 and paste it one column to the right, in I2. The
formula there is `=F2*G2`, the price times the channel, and since `Wholesale` is text the cell
shows `#VALUE!`. Excel did exactly what the formula said: two columns to the left, times one column
to the left.

That is the whole behaviour, and it is worth holding in that form rather than as a rule about
dollar signs. **Every address in a formula moves by the distance the formula moved**, down by the
rows, across by the columns. Delete I2 and carry on. The next section is about the addresses that
must not move.
