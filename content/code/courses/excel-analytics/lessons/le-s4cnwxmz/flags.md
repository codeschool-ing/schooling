**A flag is a helper column that answers one question about each row with 1 or 0.** It is the
plainest use of everything in this lesson, and one of the most useful: once a question is a column,
it can be counted, added, averaged, filtered and checked row by row, by anybody who can read a
spreadsheet.

## Two flags and their combination

On `Sales`, give J, K and L a header each, `Big`, `In 2026` and `Both`, and in row 2:

```localised
=IF(E2>=10,1,0)
=IF(B2>=DATE(2026,1,1),1,0)
=J2*K2
```

The first goes in J2, the second in K2, the third in L2. Fill all three down to row 109. Then, in
cells outside the data:

```localised
=SUM(J2:J109)
=AVERAGE(J2:J109)
=SUM(K2:K109)
=SUM(L2:L109)
```

In order they answer **22**, **0.2037…**, **36** and **5**. Adding a column of ones counts the rows where
the answer is yes: 22 big sales. Averaging it gives the **share** of rows where it is yes, 20.4%,
because an average of ones and zeros is the number of ones over the number of rows. 36 sales
happened in 2026, and 5 of those were big.

The third column is the trick worth keeping. **Multiplying two flags is `AND`**: the product is 1
only where both are 1. Adding them and asking whether the sum is above zero is `OR`. A question with
three parts is three flags and one product, each of them a column you can look at.

A flag also weights other numbers. In M2, `=H2*J2` is the revenue of a big sale and 0 for any
other; filled down and added up, it gives **28925**. Big sales are 20.4% of the sales and R$ 28,925
of the R$ 51,494, more than half the revenue. That sentence is the kind of thing a flag column is
for.

## Why 1 and 0, and not TRUE and FALSE

The obvious flag is the comparison on its own, `=E2>=10`, which shows `TRUE` or `FALSE`. It looks
the same, and it breaks the sum. **`SUM` skips logical values in a range**, so with J holding
`TRUE` and `FALSE`, `=SUM(J2:J109)` answers **0**, not 22. No error, and a count of zero big sales
that is easy to believe.

There are three ways out, and the `IF` above is the clearest of them:

- write the flag as `=IF(E2>=10,1,0)`, so the column holds numbers;
- turn the logical value into a number in the flag itself: `=--(E2>=10)` negates it twice, and
  arithmetic on `TRUE` gives 1. It is shorter and it is a puzzle for the next reader;
- count the `TRUE` values rather than adding them: `=COUNTIF(J2:J109,TRUE)` answers **22**.
  Lesson 5 is about `COUNTIF` and its relatives.

## What a flag is for, and what comes after it

A flag column costs a column. In exchange, every row says why it was counted, and anybody can
