A **trace** is the record of one request's whole journey. Each service it passes through records
one or more **spans**: a named piece of work with a start, a duration, and the id of the span that
caused it. Every span of one request carries the same **trace id**, the one the log lines carried,
and the lab sends them all through the Collector to Jaeger. Asking Jaeger for that id, and printing
each span's service, name, start and duration:

```
ana@obs:~/shop$ curl -s localhost:16686/api/traces/fcc594a0f3c6f9b924cc45718927651c | jq -r '.data[0] as $t | ($t.spans | map(.startTime) | min) as $t0 | $t.spans | sort_by(.startTime) | .[] | [$t.processes[.processID].serviceName, .operationName, "at " + ((.startTime - $t0)/1000|floor|tostring) + " ms", "took " + (.duration/1000|floor|tostring) + " ms"] | @tsv'
storefront	POST /checkout	at 0 ms	took 1533 ms
orders	POST /orders	at 2 ms	took 1530 ms
orders	INSERT	at 12 ms	took 2 ms
orders	POST	at 15 ms	took 1505 ms
payments	POST /charge	at 17 ms	took 1501 ms
payments	wait for the card network	at 18 ms	took 1500 ms
orders	UPDATE	at 1520 ms	took 2 ms
mailer	orders.placed process	at 1532 ms	took 20 ms
mailer	send confirmation	at 1532 ms	took 20 ms
```

**The answer is on one line**: `wait for the card network`, inside `payments`, 1500 of the 1533
milliseconds. Every span above it lasted as long as it did because it was waiting for that one.
`orders` sent `POST` to payments and waited 1505 ms for the answer; `POST /orders` waited for that;
the storefront's `POST /checkout` waited for everything. The database calls, `INSERT` and
`UPDATE`, took two milliseconds each and are not the problem, which is worth knowing before
anyone starts tuning queries.

Three things in this trace are worth noticing now, because later lessons are built on them:

- **Some spans were written by hand and some were not.** `storefront` and `payments` open their
  spans in their own code, and `wait for the card network` exists only because somebody wrapped that
  wait in a span. `orders` has no OpenTelemetry code of its own: its `POST /orders`, `INSERT`, `POST`
  and `UPDATE` come from libraries instrumented automatically. Lessons 2 and 3 are those two ways.
- **The mailer's spans start after the storefront's ended**, at 1532 ms, and still belong to the same
  trace. The confirmation went through a RabbitMQ queue, and the trace id went with the message.
  Lesson 4 is how, and what happens when nobody carries it.
- **This trace was kept because the lab keeps every trace.** At real traffic that is expensive, and
  lesson 12 is about keeping the ones that matter.

What a trace does not do is count. It is one request, and a trace of a fast checkout would look
just as convincing as this one. **You found this trace because the metric said checkouts were slow
and the log line gave you its id.**
