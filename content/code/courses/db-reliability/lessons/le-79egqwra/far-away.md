Everything so far survives the loss of a server. A **region**, a data centre or a cloud provider's
group of them in one area, can be lost whole: a power failure across a city, a fibre cut, a provider's
outage. Surviving that means a copy somewhere else, hundreds or thousands of kilometres away, and
lesson 12 already did the arithmetic for that distance: **a synchronous commit pays the round trip
every time**, a tenth of a second or more between continents.

- **Near the primary**, in the same region, the standbys that a failover uses: synchronous or a
  quorum (lesson 12), managed by Patroni (lesson 16), cheap to wait for because they are close.
- **In the other region**, an **asynchronous** standby. It loses whatever it had not received when
  the first region went down, which lesson 13's lag says, and that loss is the second region's RPO.
  Moving the business to it is a decision people take (lesson 22), not one a cluster manager takes
  across an ocean.
- The far standby is fed **by a standby, not by the primary**: one copy of the log crosses the
  distance, and any other server in the far region feeds from it. That is **cascading replication**,
  and it is the first thing this lesson builds.
