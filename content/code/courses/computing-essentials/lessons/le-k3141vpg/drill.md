This lesson has no facts in it. Every question is about what you would do next, and the answer is
one of four moves: say the symptom precisely, cut the chain in half, change one thing, or swap in
something known to be good.

When a question offers a plausible cause and a test, the test is usually the answer. A cause you
have not tested is a guess, however likely it sounds — and this whole lesson is the argument that
the order of your afternoon matters more than the quality of your guesses.
