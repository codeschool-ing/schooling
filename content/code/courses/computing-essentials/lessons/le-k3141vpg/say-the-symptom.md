title: Say what the symptom actually is
version: 1
---

Almost every wasted afternoon starts here. *The computer is not working* is not a symptom; it is
a mood. A symptom is something specific enough that two people would agree on whether it just
happened.

## The four things a symptom has to say

- **What exactly happens.** Not *it does not start* but *the fan runs, the light comes on, the
  screen stays black.* Those are three different faults and the first sentence covers all of them.
- **When it happens.** Always, or once a day, or only after an hour, or only when the printer is
  on. A fault that happens sometimes is a different investigation from one that happens every
  time, and the difference is the most valuable fact you have.
- **What you did just before.** The single most useful question in this lesson is **what
  changed**, and the answer is nearly always *nothing*, and it is nearly always wrong. An update
  installed itself. A cable was moved to vacuum. Somebody else used it.
- **What still works.** *Everything is broken* is almost never true, and the parts that still
  work are what shrink the search.

## Reproducing it is most of the job

A fault you can make happen on demand is a fault you can test against. One you cannot is a fault
where every change you make appears to have worked, because it has not happened yet.

So before changing anything, find the shortest sequence that produces it. That sequence is what
you will run after each attempt, and without it you have no way of knowing whether anything you
did helped.

**If it cannot be reproduced, the honest next step is to wait and watch**, with a note of when it
happens. A fault that appears every Tuesday morning has already told you something enormous, and
nobody who was busy guessing would have noticed.

## The description, written down, in one line

The habit worth building is to write the symptom as one sentence before touching anything:

> *After about an hour of use, the screen goes black and the fans speed up; the machine has to be
> held off to restart. It has happened four times since Thursday. It does not happen on battery.*

That sentence is a diagnosis half made. Every clause in it is a filter: an hour of use is heat, a
black screen with fans is not a software crash, and on battery it draws less. None of that
required knowing anything about this machine.

## And a symptom is not a cause

*The hard disk is failing* is a conclusion. *Files take thirty seconds to open and the machine
clicks* is a symptom. People report conclusions, including to themselves, and the conclusion is
the thing that stops the investigation before it starts.

Ask what was actually seen. Half of what anybody tells you about a fault is already an attempt to
explain it.
