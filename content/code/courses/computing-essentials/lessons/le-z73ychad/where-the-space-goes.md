almost never the thing people go looking for.

**Google's `15 GB` is shared between Drive, Gmail and Photos.** A full mailbox with fifteen years
of attachments, plus a phone uploading every picture at full resolution, will consume it entirely
while the Drive folder holds a few hundred megabytes of documents.

OneDrive and iCloud count differently in the details — mail on a separate allowance in one case,
device backups on the same one in the other — and the shape is identical: **the thing filling it
is not the thing you think about.**

## Finding it, in the order that works

1. **Sort by size**, in the storage manager each service provides. Google has one at
   `one.google.com/storage`; the others are in settings. Twenty files usually account for most of
   it.
2. **Empty the bin.** Deleted files count for thirty days, so a clear-out that felt like it
   should have freed space and did not is almost always this.
3. **Look at mail with large attachments**, which is one search — `has:attachment larger:10M` —
   and is usually gigabytes.
4. **Check the photo settings.** *Storage saver* against *Original quality* is a decision worth
   making deliberately, once, rather than having made by the default.
5. **Only then look at documents.** They are almost never the cause, and the search above will
   have told you so.

## What to do when it is genuinely full

**A full account stops receiving**, which is worse than it sounds: mail bounces, the phone stops
backing up, and files stop syncing — and the last one is silent on some devices. A machine that
has quietly stopped syncing for three weeks is a machine whose recent work exists in one place.

So the order is: free something today, then decide. Paying for more storage is cheap and is the
right answer for most people; it is a poorer answer if the thing filling it is twelve years of
photographs nobody has looked at, which is the case worth spending an evening on rather than a
monthly fee.

## And the row that catches people out

**Files shared *with* you do not count against your quota. Files *you* share do.** Which means a
colleague putting a 40 GB folder into a folder you own consumes your allowance and not theirs.

On a work account this is somebody else's problem. On a personal one it is worth knowing before
the account stops receiving mail.
