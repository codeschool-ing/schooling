title: Power, and the one that is a safety matter
version: 1
---

Power faults are odd because they produce symptoms all over the machine. A supply that cannot
deliver what is asked of it looks like a crash, a restart, a disk error or a machine that will
not start — depending on what happened to be running.

## A desktop supply that is going

- **It restarts under load** and only under load: a game, a render, anything that draws more.
- **It will not start at all some mornings**, and starts fine on the third try.
- **It has begun to smell**, which is the end of the diagnosis and the start of unplugging it.

The reason the symptoms wander is the same reason as memory: it is not one subsystem failing, it
is the thing every subsystem depends on becoming unreliable at the worst moment.

## A laptop, where there are three things and not one

The charger, the socket in the machine, and the battery. Each fails differently, and they are
worth separating because two of them are cheap:

- **It runs on mains and shows no charge**, which is usually the battery at the end of its life.
- **It charges only when the cable is held at an angle**, which is the socket in the machine or
  the plug on the cable. Held-at-an-angle is a mechanical fault, and it is worth acting on before
  it stops working altogether.
- **It does not run at all without mains**, which is a battery that is finished.
- **The charger is warm and the machine is dead**, which is at least a charger doing something.

**A battery is a consumable.** Four or five hundred full cycles and it holds a fraction of what it
did, and that is the design rather than a defect. A three-year-old laptop with two hours of
battery is a normal three-year-old laptop.

## And the one that is not about computers

**A swollen battery is a safety matter, and everything else stops.**

The signs, and any one of them is enough:

- the case no longer closes flat, or the machine rocks on the table;
- the trackpad has risen and become stiff or unclickable — this is the commonest first sign,
  because the battery sits directly underneath it;
- a phone whose screen is lifting away from its frame at one edge.

What to do: **stop using it, stop charging it, and do not press on it or attempt to remove it with
force.** A swollen lithium cell is a cell whose chemistry has produced gas inside a sealed pouch,
and puncturing it is how it catches fire.

It goes to somebody who handles them, and the old one goes to a proper disposal point rather than
a bin. Nothing else in this lesson is urgent in this way, and this one is not urgent for the
machine's sake.
