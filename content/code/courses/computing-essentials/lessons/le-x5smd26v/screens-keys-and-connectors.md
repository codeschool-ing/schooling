The parts a person touches fail for reasons a person caused, which makes them the easiest group
to diagnose and the most annoying to live with.

## A black screen that is not a black screen

**Shine a torch at it at an angle.** If a faint image is there, the panel is working and the
backlight is not — and that is a different and usually cheaper repair than a new screen.

The rest of the screen list:

- **A crack** is a crack, and the picture around it deteriorates over days.
- **A single dead pixel** is a manufacturing tolerance rather than a fault, and no maker replaces
  a screen for one.
- **Lines across the picture, changing when the lid is moved**, is the ribbon cable through the
  hinge. Extremely common on laptops, and it gets worse.
- **A picture that flickers or changes colour when the cable is wiggled** on a desktop is the
  cable, which is the cheapest swap available.
- **A picture that is there and then not there, with the machine still running**, is the graphics
  side rather than the screen — and a second monitor on the same machine settles it in a minute.

## Keyboards, which are mostly liquid

- **One key that does not work** is usually that key, and on a desktop keyboard it is a cheap
  replacement rather than a repair.
- **Keys near each other, all failing**, is a spill. Even one that dried a month ago.
- **A repeated character** is a key that is not releasing, mechanically.
- **Wrong characters entirely** is not hardware at all: it is the layout setting, and this course
  has already met it.

**On a laptop, a USB keyboard is the swap.** It answers *is it the keyboard or the machine* for
the price of nothing, and it is also the temporary fix that makes an unusable laptop usable for
weeks.

## Ports that need holding

A socket that works only when the cable is pushed, lifted or held is a mechanical failure of the
solder or the socket itself, and it has one property worth knowing: **it always gets worse, and
it never gets better.**

It is worth acting on early for a specific reason. The port is attached to the board, so the
repair is a board-level one, and the thing that turns a small board repair into a new board is
somebody forcing a cable in for another six months.

## The pattern behind this section

Every fault here is **mechanical**, which is why they behave the way they do: they get worse
gradually, they respond to being moved, and they are provoked by exactly the thing you do when
you are frustrated with them.

That gives them the most reliable test in this lesson. **If it changes when you move it, it is
physical.** No software fault has ever depended on the angle of a cable.
