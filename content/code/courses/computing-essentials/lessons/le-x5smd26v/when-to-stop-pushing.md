## Stop immediately, in this order

**A swollen battery.** Not a computer problem. Stop using it, stop charging it, and do not press
on it. This is the only item on the list that is about the room rather than the machine.

**A burning smell, or smoke.** Unplug it at the wall. A smell of hot plastic or of ozone from a
power supply is a component failing in a way that does not need to be investigated further at
home.

**A clicking hard disk.** Every minute it runs is spending reads that may be the last ones. Copy
what matters off, if it will still copy, and then stop. If it has already stopped being found at
all, the next decision is whether the data is worth a recovery service — which is expensive, and
which is made much less likely to work by anything else being tried first.

**Liquid, anywhere near the inside.** Off, unplugged, battery out if it comes out easily, upside
down, and left for days. The damage from liquid is mostly corrosion over time and mostly done by
powering it on again to see.

## Stop for the afternoon, and think

These are not emergencies and they are the point where the next step should be decided rather
than reached for:

- **The repair costs a meaningful fraction of a replacement.** A board or a screen on an old
  machine usually is.
- **The next step cannot be undone**, which is the previous lesson's rule and it applies to
  hardware hardest of all: a cable forced, a connector prised, a clip broken.
- **Nothing you have tried has changed anything at all**, which usually means the chain is being
  cut in the wrong place rather than that the fault is mysterious.
- **You are annoyed.** This sounds like advice about feelings and it is advice about force. Every
  broken clip and every torn ribbon cable in the world was produced by somebody who had been at
  it for two hours.

## What is worth doing yourself, and what is not

Reasonable at home, with no special tools: **dust**, **memory**, **a desktop disk**, **cables**,
**a keyboard**, **a monitor**. Each is a part that unplugs.

Reasonable to take somewhere: **thermal paste**, **a laptop screen**, **a power supply**, **a
socket on the board**, **a laptop disk in a machine that is glued rather than screwed**.

The line is not difficulty. It is **whether a mistake costs the part or costs the machine** —
and a clip on a memory module costs the module, while a ribbon cable in a laptop lid costs the
lid.

## And the sentence the whole lesson is for

**A machine that is failing is not a machine to keep using while you think about it.** Copy the
files off. Everything after that is about money, and money is the half of this that can be
decided slowly.
