---
title: HDMI and DisplayPort, and the two that are still on the back of things
version: 1
---

Four connectors carry a picture out of a machine. Two are current, one is dying, and one is
already dead and still fitted to monitors being sold today.

| | what it is | carries sound | still buy it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HDMI** | the television standard, on everything | yes | yes |
| **DisplayPort** | the computer standard, on graphics cards and better monitors | yes | yes |
| **DVI** | digital, from before HDMI, no sound | no | no |
| **VGA** | the blue trapezoid with screws. Analogue, from 1987 | no | no |

## HDMI or DisplayPort, when a monitor has both

Take **DisplayPort**, and the reason is not brand loyalty:

- it carries more, version for version, so it runs a high resolution at a high refresh rate
  where HDMI of the same age will not;
- it **daisy-chains**, so one cable out of a laptop can drive two monitors that support it;
- it is the one a graphics card is designed around. A card typically has three DisplayPort
  sockets and one HDMI.

**Take HDMI for a television**, always, because that is what a television has.

## The version is where the picture goes wrong

A connector shape has not changed in years while what runs through it has, so the same socket and
the same-looking cable can be any of these:

| | what it manages |
|---|---|
| `HDMI 1.4` | 4K at 30 Hz — visibly stuttery for a pointer |
| `HDMI 2.0` | 4K at 60 Hz |
| `HDMI 2.1` | 4K at 120 Hz, 8K at 60 |
| `DisplayPort 1.2` | 4K at 60 Hz |
| `DisplayPort 1.4` | 4K at 120 Hz |
| `DisplayPort 2.0` | 8K and beyond |

**The 4K-at-30 case is the one to recognise**, because it does not fail. It works, and the
pointer moves in visible steps, and the usual conclusion is that the computer is slow. It is an
old cable or an old port, and both are cheap to change.

## Sound goes down the same cable, which surprises people

HDMI and DisplayPort both carry audio. Plug a computer into a television with one cable and the
sound comes out of the television — **and stops coming out of the speakers you were using**,
because the operating system switched output to the new device.

That is the commonest "my sound has disappeared" on a laptop, and it is fixed in the sound
settings rather than anywhere near the hardware.

## VGA, honestly

`VGA` is analogue: the picture is a varying voltage, so a long or cheap cable produces a soft,
smeared image and no error. It is limited in practice to about `1920 × 1080` and it carries no
sound at all.

It survives because projectors in meeting rooms have it and because it is the one connector every
machine had for twenty years. If you have a choice, take anything else.
