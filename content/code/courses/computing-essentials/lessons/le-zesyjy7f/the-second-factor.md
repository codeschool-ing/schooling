---
title: The second factor, and which kind is worth the trouble
version: 1
---

A second factor means that knowing the password is not enough. It is the single most effective
thing on this list, because it breaks the attack from the previous section: a password from a
breach, tried against your bank, no longer opens anything.

## The four kinds, from weakest to strongest

**A code by SMS.** Better than nothing, and the weakest of the four. A number can be moved to
somebody else's SIM — the attacker convinces a shop or a support line that they are you, and
every code afterwards arrives on their phone. Use it where nothing else is offered, and prefer
anything else where there is a choice.

**A code from an app.** Six digits that change every thirty seconds, generated on the phone from
a secret set up once. There is nothing to intercept, because nothing is sent: the phone and the
service compute the same number from the same secret and the clock.

**A physical key.** A small device that plugs in or taps, and which refuses to answer a site that
is not the right one. Excellent, and the extra step is buying one.

**A passkey**, which is the newest and is replacing the password rather than adding to it. The
phone or the computer holds a key, unlocked by a fingerprint or a face, and the site never learns
a secret at all. Where it is offered it is both the easiest and the strongest.

## The part everybody skips

**The recovery codes.** When a second factor is set up, the service shows a list of one-time
codes, once, and nobody writes them down.

Those codes are the only way back in when the phone is lost, stolen or replaced — and losing the
phone is not an unusual event, it is the most likely thing that will ever happen to your second
factor.

Print them, or write them out, and put them with the papers. A screenshot on the phone is a copy
of the key inside the lock.

## Where to put it first

Not everywhere at once. **The e-mail address, before anything else**, because every other account
resets its password through it — whoever controls the mailbox controls the bank, the marketplace
and the social account by asking each of them politely to send a reset.

Then the bank, then anything holding money, then the rest at leisure.
