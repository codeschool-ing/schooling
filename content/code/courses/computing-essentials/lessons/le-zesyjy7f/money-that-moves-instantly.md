---
title: Money that moves instantly, and what that changes
version: 1
---

An instant transfer is a genuinely good thing and it removes one protection that nobody noticed
they had: **time**. A card payment can be disputed for weeks; a transfer that has left your
account has arrived, and the only route back is the other person agreeing, or a bank's own
fraud process, which is slow and is not a guarantee.

That single fact reshapes every scam that involves money, so they all converge on the same
demand: **transfer it now.**

## The forms it arrives in

- **A payment request that looks like an invoice**, for something plausible — a subscription, a
  delivery fee, a small charge that clears a large problem.
- **A person you know, asking**, from a messaging account that is theirs or that looks like
  theirs. This is the one that works best, because it skips every suspicion aimed at strangers.
- **A seller or a buyer** on a marketplace who needs the transaction to leave the platform. The
  platform's protection is the thing being removed, and that is the entire purpose of the request.
- **A charity or a cause**, at a moment when giving is being talked about.

## Three habits

**Verify the person by a route they did not choose.** A message from a relative asking for money
is answered by calling the number you already have, not by replying. A cloned account has the
photograph and the name and cannot answer the phone.

**Look at who is actually being paid.** Every instant transfer shows the receiving name before it
is confirmed, and that screen is the last place the transaction is still yours. A name that does
not match the story is the answer.

**Keep the transaction where its protection is.** A marketplace that holds the money until
delivery is doing something, and a seller who wants to move off it is asking you to give that up
for their convenience.

## The one about phones

A stolen phone with the messaging account still signed in is the account, and from it the same
request goes to everybody in the contact list, in the owner's name, at two in the morning.

The defences are dull and they work: **a screen lock**, the messaging app's own **PIN for
re-registering the number**, and the second factor on the e-mail address from the earlier section.

## And the sentence for this section

**A request for money that cannot wait an hour is the request to be most suspicious of.** Nothing
real is destroyed by a phone call, and the whole design of this category is to make that phone
call feel unnecessary.
