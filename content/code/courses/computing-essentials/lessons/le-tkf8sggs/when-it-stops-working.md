different causes, and telling them apart is fast, free, and almost nobody does it before phoning
somebody.

## The four places

**One site.** Only one thing fails; everything else loads. There is nothing wrong with your
network, and the two minutes spent restarting the router were spent on somebody else's server.

**One device.** Every other phone and laptop in the house works. The problem is in that machine —
its Wi-Fi, its settings, the software it is running — and the network is fine.

**The network inside.** Nothing in the house reaches the internet, but the devices can see each
other: the printer answers, the router's settings page opens. The line is the suspect, or the
router's connection to it.

**The line.** The modem light says so, and nothing inside the house will change it.

## The questions that place it, in order

1. **Does it fail on a second device?** No, and it is that device. This one question eliminates
   half of everything.
2. **Does another site work?** Yes, and it was that site. A phone on mobile data checking the same
   address settles it completely.
3. **Does the router's settings page open?** Yes, and the Wi-Fi and the router are fine; the
   problem is between the router and the street. No, and the problem is between you and the
   router — the Wi-Fi, or the box.
4. **What do the lights say?** Every box labels them differently and every box has one for the
   line. Red, orange or off means the provider has something to fix, and nothing you do to the
   settings will help.

## Restarting, which works more often than it should

Turn it off, wait thirty seconds, turn it on. It fixes a real and large class of problems: a
router that has been running for two hundred days with a full table of connections, a modem whose
negotiated line state has gone bad, a lease that expired strangely.

**Thirty seconds is not superstition** — some modems need the line to drop properly before they
will renegotiate, and a two-second cycle leaves them in the same state.

Two things it is not. It is not a diagnosis: a router that needs restarting every week is a
router with a fault, and *it works again after I restart it* is the finding, not the fix. And it
is not the reset button, which is a different button that throws away your settings.

## Measuring instead of guessing

A speed test measures the path from the device to a server, and **every part of that path is in
the measurement**. That is why the result is worth so little on its own and so much when it is
repeated in the right two places:

- on Wi-Fi, in the room where it feels slow;
- on a cable into the router, or standing next to it.

If both are slow, the line is slow. If only the first is, the Wi-Fi is the problem, and that is
the section above rather than a phone call to the provider.

**Compare against what you pay for**, which is on the bill and is stated in megabits. A file
manager reports megabytes, which is eight times smaller, and a great deal of *my hundred-megabit
line only downloads at twelve* is that eight.
