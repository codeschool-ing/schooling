title: The case, which is a duct with a side panel
version: 1

A case looks like the least technical purchase in a computer, and mostly it is. Two things about
it are not decoration, and both are about air.

**It is a duct.** Its real job is to make air travel a path: in at the front, across the hot
parts, out at the back and top. A case with no front intake, or with a solid glass front and no
gap, does that badly however good it looks — and looking good is what most of them are sold on.

**It decides what fits.** Three measurements, and each one strands a build that ignored it:

| | the constraint |
|---|---|
| motherboard size | ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX — the case states which it takes |
| graphics card length | in millimetres, and long cards do not fit small cases |
| cooler height | a tall air cooler hits the side panel of a slim case |

## Form factor, in one table

`ATX` is the full-size standard, `Micro-ATX` is shorter with fewer slots, `Mini-ITX` is small and
carries one expansion slot. A smaller board fits a larger case; the reverse never works.

## What none of it changes

A case makes no machine faster. What it can do is make one quieter — a larger case with larger,
slower fans moves the same air with less noise, because noise rises steeply with fan speed and
only gently with fan size.

Which is the last piece of this lesson's chain, and worth saying in one line: **the graphics card
decides the watts, the watts decide the heat, the heat decides the cooling, and the cooling
decides the noise.** Four components, one decision at the front of them.
