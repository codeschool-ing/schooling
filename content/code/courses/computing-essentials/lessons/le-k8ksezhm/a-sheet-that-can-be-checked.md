title: A sheet somebody else can check, which is the whole discipline
version: 1

A spreadsheet is the only thing in this course that produces **a number somebody will act on**,
and the only one where a wrong answer looks exactly like a right one. There is no red underline
under a bad total.

So the discipline is not tidiness. It is making the sheet say what it assumes, loudly enough
that somebody who did not build it can check it.

## The five rules of layout

1. **Headings in row one, data from row two.** No title above them, no blank rows, no merged
   cells. Everything in this lesson — filter, sort, pivot, chart, `XLOOKUP` — reads a range and
   assumes the first row names the columns.
2. **One table per sheet.** Two tables side by side means every range is a guess and every
   *select all* is wrong.
3. **No blank rows or columns inside the data.** A blank row is where `Ctrl+Shift+↓` stops, and
   where a filter decides the table ended.
4. **No total attached to the bottom of the data.** Put it above the headings or on another
   sheet. A total inside the range is sorted with the data, filtered with the data, and charted
   as another category.
5. **One thing per column, one record per row** — the rule from lesson nine, and the one
   everything else rests on.

## Inputs, calculations, output

The arrangement that makes a sheet auditable is three regions, and they can be three areas or
three sheets:

| | holds | nobody should |
|---|---|---|
| **inputs** | every number a person typed. Rates, prices, assumptions, dates | calculate here |
| **calculations** | formulas only | type a number here |
| **output** | what somebody reads: the summary, the chart, the total | edit anything here |

**The test of whether you have done this: can you change one assumption and see every consequence
move?** If the answer is no, the assumption is written in more than one place, and one of those
places is going to be missed.

Colour is the cheap way to make the regions visible — a light fill on every input cell, and
nothing on the rest. It takes two minutes and it tells the next person where they are allowed to
type.

## And the sheet that is worth more than any of it

**A sheet called `notes`, in plain sentences, saying where the data came from, when, what was
excluded and what the sheet assumes.**

Every spreadsheet that survives a year has one, and every spreadsheet that gets thrown away and
rebuilt did not. It is the same argument as naming a file for the person looking in two years,
and that person is still you.
