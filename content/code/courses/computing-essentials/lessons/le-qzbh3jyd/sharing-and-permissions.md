Sharing a document is two separate decisions and the interface presents them as one screen, which
is why people get one of them right and the other by accident.

**Who can open it** is *general access*. **What they may do** is the role beside each name.

## The three roles

| | may |
|---|---|
| **Viewer** | read it. And download it, copy it, print it |
| **Commenter** | the same, plus leave comments and suggestions |
| **Editor** | change it, and by default **change who else can open it** |

The last clause is the one to read twice. An editor can re-share the document unless you turn
that off — *Settings, Editors can change permissions and share*. On anything sensitive, turn it
off, and turn off *Viewers and commenters can see the option to download, print and copy* while
you are there.

That second switch is worth being honest about: **it stops the buttons and not the person.** Text
can be selected, a screen can be photographed, and a determined reader is not inconvenienced for
long. It prevents the accident, not the intent, and that is still worth having.

## The two mistakes that actually happen

**A folder shared once shares everything put into it afterwards.** Including the things somebody
else puts there, and including the things you move there without thinking. A folder shared with
*anyone with the link* two years ago is a folder whose whole contents are public now.

**A link forwarded is a link that works.** *Anyone with the link* means exactly that: the person
you sent it to, the person they forwarded it to, and anybody who finds it in an e-mail archive
later. There is no account to check and nothing to revoke except the setting itself.

## What to actually do

- **Share with named people** for anything that matters. It is two extra clicks and it produces
  a list you can read afterwards.
- **Use an expiry date** on temporary access — available on the role menu for named people. It is
  the only sharing decision that undoes itself.
- **Check `Shared with me` and your own Drive's sharing column** once in a while. The question
  *what have I left open* has an answer and almost nobody asks it.
- **Never share a document to get a file to somebody** who is going to work on it in Word.
  Export and send the file, or invite them properly; the half-measure produces two documents
  nobody can reconcile.

## Suggesting mode, which is Track Changes done better

The pencil menu in the top right switches between **Editing**, **Suggesting** and **Viewing**. In
suggesting mode every change becomes a proposal, coloured by author, that the owner accepts or
rejects.

It is the same idea as Word's tracked changes with one real advantage: **the document has one
copy, so there is no merging.** Three people suggesting at once is three sets of proposals on the
same text rather than three files in an inbox.

The failure mode is the opposite of Word's: rather than sending a document with changes still
tracked, people leave suggestions unresolved and export a PDF that shows them. **Resolve
everything before exporting**, and check the exported file rather than trusting it.
