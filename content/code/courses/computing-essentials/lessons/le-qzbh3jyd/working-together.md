## What several people at once actually looks like

**Presence** — coloured cursors with names on them, moving as people type. You can see who is
reading as well as who is writing, which changes how a meeting goes.

**No locking.** Two people typing in the same paragraph both succeed; the edits interleave. That
sounds like it should produce nonsense and mostly does not, because people spread out
instinctively. Where it does, the version history is the way back.

**Comments, which have a lifecycle.** A comment is opened, replied to, and **resolved** — and
resolving hides it rather than deleting it, so the conversation survives in the history. A
document full of unresolved comments is a document nobody has finished reading.

**Assigning.** A comment with `@` and a name becomes that person's task, appears in their Drive,
and mails them. The difference between a comment and an assigned comment is the difference
between a remark and a request.

## The rule that makes it work

**One document, one address, and no copies.** The moment somebody downloads a copy to work on it
offline and mails it back, everything in the left panel above happens, and the shared document
keeps being edited in the meantime by everybody else.

If somebody genuinely has to work offline, arrange offline access rather than a copy. That is the
whole reason the feature exists.

## Slides, in the four things that are different

- **Q&A** — *Presenter view* has an audience question box with a short link. In a large room it is
  better than hands, and in a small one it is a distraction.
- **Auto-advance and publish to the web** produces a deck that plays itself, which is what a
  screen in a foyer wants.
- **Linked charts from Sheets.** A chart pasted from a sheet keeps a link and has an *Update*
  button when the numbers change. It is the one thing in Workspace that removes an entire class
  of stale-number error.
- **No embedded fonts, ever.** Everything runs in a browser and the fonts come from Google, so
  the failure from last lesson cannot happen — and exporting to `.pptx` brings it straight back,
  because the exported file names fonts the other machine may not have.
