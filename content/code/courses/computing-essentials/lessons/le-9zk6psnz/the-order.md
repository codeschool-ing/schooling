Every step of a build is simple. What is not obvious is that some of them become difficult once
a later one is done, and the whole skill here is doing them in the order that never requires
undoing anything.

## Why each step sits where it does

**The processor, the cooler and the memory go on while the board is out of the case**, flat on
the box it came in, with light on it from above. All three are done at arm's length instead of
inside a dark metal box, and the processor's socket lever needs a firm push that is much easier
with the board on a table.

**The M.2 drive goes in now** because on most boards its slot is directly under where the
graphics card will sit. Afterwards it means removing the card.

**The board goes into the case on standoffs** — the little brass pillars that hold it off the
metal tray. Every mounting hole needs one and **no hole without a standoff may have a screw**: a
screw into the tray with nothing underneath can short the board. A spare standoff left in the
wrong place, under no hole at all, does the same thing and is the classic cause of a build that
never powers on.

**The power supply goes in before the cables** for the obvious reason, and with one non-obvious
detail: if the case has a vent in its floor, the supply's fan faces **down**, drawing cool air
from outside rather than the case's own warm air.

**The graphics card is last** because it is large, it covers the M.2 slot and often the front
panel pins, and it is the part most likely to have to come out again.

**The side panel is last of all, after the test.** Closing a case you have not powered on is
committing to taking it apart again.

## Two things that go wrong in the order rather than in the step

- **The cooler before the memory.** Large air coolers overhang the first memory slot, and the
  module underneath cannot be pressed in with the cooler fitted. Memory first, or check the
  clearance.
- **The board in the case before the rear I/O shield.** On boards without an attached shield, the
  metal plate clips into the case from the inside. Forgetting it means taking the board out
  again, and there is no other way.
