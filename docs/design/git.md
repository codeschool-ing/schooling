**Partly.** Git itself is stable. Lesson 8 is *GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket: issues, pull requests
and code review*, and those three interfaces move — so that lesson's screen is the exception

## Flags

**1 · The 40 hours do not fit; the estimate is ~58.** Nineteen lessons at the minimum shape of a
lesson already exceed what 40 hours buys at this catalogue's density, and lessons are frozen. What
is left is accepting that `git` is a 58-hour course advertised as 40. `course.json` keeps saying
40 until the release that finishes it (`C-27`), and the divergence is above the 20% that makes it
a decision rather than a note.

**2 · The highest-leverage course in the catalogue.** Thirteen tracks, seven dependents, paid in
all of them. Quality here is retention rather than conversion, which is a different argument for
the same investment and a weaker one commercially — worth stating so it is not confused with the
free course's case.

**3 · Half of it is not Git.** Lessons 12 to 19 are process and behaviour — review, estimation,
the definition of done, the retro. That is where video earns most and machine-graded exercise
earns least, so the mix leans to `ordering` and judgement `quiz`, and the video estimate leans
high for a course of this length.

**4 · The strongest single argument for shell being the sandbox's first runtime.** 20% of the
exercises of the course that appears in thirteen tracks. No other course in `foundations` asks for
a runtime at all.
