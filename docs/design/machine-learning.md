## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** `python-data` and `statistics` — pandas and NumPy in the hands, and inference in the head. It is one of only two courses in the category requiring two things, and the pair is exactly right.

**Leaves ready:** the modelling vocabulary and scikit-learn, for `deep-learning`.

## Flags

**1 ·** **Ninety hours — joint largest course in the catalogue**, with `python`, `react-ts`, `ios-apps`, `react-native` and `android-apps`. Twenty-two lessons at 8.8 sections each.

**2 ·** **Its first four lessons are the ones that make it worth doing, and they are the easiest to skip.** Framing the problem, the baseline the model must beat, the split that respects time, and data leakage. Everything after them is algorithms, which is what a reader expects and is the commodity part. **The design instruction is to refuse to compress lessons 1–4**, because a course that opens with linear regression is every other course.

**3 ·** **Lesson 11 is a business decision inside a technical course** — *"The threshold is a business decision, not the default 0.5"* — and lesson 20 is fairness. Those are `management`'s grading problem appearing inside `data`: they are judgement, and judgement needs the scenario-with-four-replies treatment that the rest of this course does not.

**4 ·** **The one course where `numeric` and a blocked runtime meet.** Metrics, thresholds and errors grade today with the machinery that exists; fitting the model that produces them does not. So roughly a third of the practice is publishable now and two thirds waits, which is a more useful split than a single blocked figure and is worth designing to: **write the gradeable third as the spine, not as the leftovers.**
