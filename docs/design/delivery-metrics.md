## Ageing

**Low.** DORA and SPACE are research rather than products, and Little's law is a theorem. The figures quoted from the DORA report move each year, which is a citation problem and not a re-recording one.

## Flags

**1 ·** **The best fit for `numeric` in the entire catalogue outside mathematics.** Little's law, cycle time, throughput, error budgets, Monte Carlo percentiles — the answers are numbers with tolerances, and `numeric` grades those today. Where `people-leadership` needs a grader that does not exist, this course needs one that does.

**2 ·** **Monte Carlo forecasting wants something the platform has no name for.** Lesson 10 is a simulation, and a student who has not run one has not learnt it. That is not a sandbox problem — it is one page of JavaScript in the lesson itself — but the content model has no kind of section for a widget: `reading`, `video` and `practice` are the three, and this is none of them. Either it is a video of somebody else running the simulation, which is the weakest version, or the model gains a fourth kind. **The decision is not this sheet's and it is not free.**

**3 ·** **Lesson 20 is the course arguing with itself, on purpose.** "What a team's numbers may be used for, and what they must not" comes after nineteen lessons teaching how to produce them, and lesson 7 is about gaming the charts. The material has to be written so the last lesson reads as the point rather than as a disclaimer — which is a writing instruction, and the sheet records it because it is the kind of thing that is lost when lessons are written out of order.
