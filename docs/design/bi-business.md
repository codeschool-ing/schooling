# bi-business

**BI for the Business: KPIs and Applications** · `co-e6v57rhz` · 60 h declared · beginner · 21 lessons · `management` · paid

## Reach

In **1 track** — `bi`(2).

**Depends on it:** **nothing**

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** `computing-essentials` — a computer, a spreadsheet, a file. **It assumes no statistics and no SQL**, which is what makes it the second course of `bi` rather than a later one.

**Leaves ready:** the business vocabulary the rest of `bi` reports in: KPI, stakeholder, the four kinds of analysis, the operational/tactical/strategic split. Nothing requires it by name.

## Ageing

**Low as designed, and it depends on a choice not yet made.** Lessons 17 to 21 are applications by industry and they can be taught either from principles, which do not age, or from a vendor's dashboard, which does. **Choose the first.**

## Flags

**1 ·** **Twenty-one lessons and the last five are examples.** Finance, retail, healthcare, industry, compliance — one lesson each, and they are the same lesson four times with the nouns changed unless somebody decides otherwise. This is the `aws`/`azure`/`gcp` shape appearing inside a single course, and the same answer applies: write one of them properly and let the others refer to it rather than restate it.

**2 ·** **A beginner course at 60 hours whose subject is judgement about numbers.** Lesson 12 — averages, vanity metrics, hidden denominators — is the one that makes the course worth doing and it is the hardest to grade: a hidden denominator is spotted, not computed. `numeric` carries part of it (compute the rate two ways and see them disagree) and the rest is a scenario question.

**3 ·** **Reached by one track and it is the track's second course**, which means it is the first thing a `bi` student pays for. Whatever `web-fundamentals` is for the engineering tracks, this is for `bi` — and it has had none of that attention.
