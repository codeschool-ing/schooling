## Ageing

**Severe, and on the shortest clock in the catalogue.** Lessons 8, 9 and 10 are three vendors' agent SDKs and lessons 11 to 16 are one protocol that is barely two years old. **A course can be written and be out of date before it is recorded.**

## Flags

**1 ·** **Eighty hours — the largest course in the category — on the fastest-moving subject in the catalogue.** That combination is the finding. Every other large course the sweep has met is large *because* its subject is settled: `design-patterns` (80 h, GoF is thirty years old), `machine-learning` (90 h). This is 80 hours on SDKs and a protocol whose current version will not be current when the last section is written.

**Lesson 7 is the answer and it is already in the list.** *"Implementing an agent by hand, from scratch"* teaches the loop without any vendor, and it is what makes lessons 8 to 10 readable rather than magic. Weight the course there and treat the SDK lessons as the directory they are.

**2 ·** **Lesson 17 is a decision this platform has already had to make.** Permissions, least privilege and human confirmation — an agent that acts needs a boundary, and `CLAUDE.md` and `internal/audit` are one worked example of drawing one. Teachable from the repository, the way `data-governance` can teach from `internal/privacy`.

**3 ·** **Depth five and the end of its chain.** Nothing requires it, one track reaches it, and it is behind four prerequisites. Highest environment cost and lowest reach in the category — which makes it the last of the `ai` chain worth writing, not the first, whatever the subject's current noise level suggests.
