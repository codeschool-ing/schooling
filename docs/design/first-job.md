## Ageing

**Moderate to severe, and on a schedule nobody controls.** Lesson 5 is the GitHub profile and lesson 6 is LinkedIn — two products that redesign — and the state of the junior market that lesson 1 and lesson 2 describe changes yearly. This is the course most likely to be quietly wrong two years after it is written.

## Flags

**1 ·** **The last thing a student reads, in sixteen tracks.** Whatever impression the school leaves, this course leaves it. Nothing depends on it and it is therefore invisible to every graph-shaped argument about what to build first — which is precisely why the sweep is worth doing: the dependency graph and the student's experience rank the catalogue differently, and here they disagree maximally.

**2 ·** **It is the only course whose subject matter dates on a market's schedule.** Ageing elsewhere in the catalogue means a vendor redrew a console. Here it means the advice is wrong: what a junior role is, where the jobs are, what a screening call checks and what a salary range looks like all moved in the last three years and will move again. **Whatever review cadence the catalogue adopts, this course needs the shortest one**, and `C-24`'s versioning is the mechanism — a fix release per year, not a rewrite.

**3 ·** **Twenty-two lessons in fifty hours — 4.9 sections each, near the floor.** Short lessons are right here: each is one step of a process a reader is walking through, and a reader in the middle of applying for jobs is not reading a nine-section lesson. The shape and the audience agree, which is not true everywhere.

**4 ·** **And it is paid, at the end of sixteen tracks.** A student who has paid for fourteen courses reaches the one about getting hired. There is a version of the free tier — not the one `C-28` describes — where the last course is free because a student who gets a job is the school's best advertisement. **The sheet raises it and does not decide it**; it is a marketing decision with a number attached, and the number is 16 tracks × the abandonment at position n−1.
