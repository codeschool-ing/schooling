**Moderate.** Remote-access tools, ticketing systems and the certification syllabuses of lesson 15 all move. The diagnostic method of lesson 1 does not, and it is the course.

## Flags

**1 ·** **Its prerequisite is the one course the sandbox cannot serve.** The `infra` sweep found `operating-systems` covers three desktop operating systems and has no path from a browser at any price. `tech-support` requires it, is a beginner course, and is the last subject course of `it-support`. So the cheapest track in the catalogue to build — no runtime anywhere in it — **ends behind the most expensive prerequisite in it**. Whatever is decided for `operating-systems` decides this too.

**2 ·** **`ordering` is the course's natural grader and lesson 1 hands it over.** Reproduce, isolate, test, confirm — a method in four steps is an ordering item, and every subsequent lesson can reuse the shape: given this symptom, order these five checks. Cheap to write, right to grade, and it teaches the thing the course is actually about.

**3 ·** **Beginner, 50 hours, and the only course in `management` aimed at somebody who is not an engineer.** It shares a category with technical leadership and enterprise architecture, which is a categorisation artefact rather than a fact about the material. Nothing needs fixing; it is worth knowing before somebody writes "as you already know" into it.
