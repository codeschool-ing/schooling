# enterprise-software

**Enterprise Software and Integration** · `co-5ryatxt9` · 50 h declared · advanced · 15 lessons · `architecture` · paid

## Reach

In **1 track** — `software-architecture`(11).

**Depends on it:** **nothing**

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** no `requires`, and position 11 of `software-architecture` — so the patterns, the modelling and most of the infrastructure of that track are behind it. It is late on purpose: none of it makes sense to somebody who has not seen a system with more than one author.

**Leaves ready:** **nothing.** Position 11 of 13, and nothing requires it.

## Ageing

**Severe, and unusually so.** Lessons 2 to 6 are SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, Salesforce, OpenText and IBM BPM. Five lessons of somebody else's product, and the products are the kind that get renamed at conferences.

## Flags

**1 ·** **The only course in the catalogue that cannot be demonstrated at any price.** The nine vendor courses in `infra` need a paid account, which is a bill somebody can pay. **SAP, Dynamics and Salesforce are not sold to an individual at all** — a licence is an enterprise contract with a sales cycle. So five of fifteen lessons must be taught from screenshots, documentation and description, and a student will finish the course having never touched the systems it names. That is worth writing down as the design rather than discovering it as a disappointment.

**2 ·** **And the half that can be taught properly is the half worth teaching.** Lessons 7 to 15 — BPM, ESB, integration by file, by database, by API and by event, enterprise integration patterns, queues, the strangler, data migration, buy versus build — are vendor-free, durable, and the reason the course is in an architecture track. **The design instruction is to weight the course towards them**, not to balance the two halves because the lesson list looks balanced.

**3 ·** **Buy versus build, in a course written by people who build.** Lesson 15 is the one an architect is actually judged on and the one most likely to be written as a formality. It wants the same treatment `tech-strategy` gives total cost of ownership: numbers, three years out, with a `numeric` grader on them.
