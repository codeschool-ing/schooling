# process-management

**Management, Process and Estimation** · `co-7f7wzrh0` · 60 h declared · intermediate · 15 lessons · `management` · **free**

## Reach

In **2 tracks** — `software-architecture`(12), `tech-lead`(1).

**Depends on it:** `architect-communication`, `delivery-metrics`

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** **nothing.** It is the first course of `tech-lead` and takes no prerequisite, which is deliberate: the audience is somebody who already works and is being asked to answer for a team, so what it may assume is a job rather than a course.

**Leaves ready:** the vocabulary the other four courses of `tech-lead` stand on — sprint, flow, cycle time, estimate, risk, debt. **Two courses require it by name**, `architect-communication` and `delivery-metrics`, and it is the only course in these three categories that anything requires.

## Ageing

**Low.** Frameworks move slowly and the course teaches what each one assumes about the world rather than any tool's screen. Lesson 15, on certifications, is the exception: names, levels and prices change, and it is one lesson.

## Flags

**1 ·** **A free course that is not a shop window.** By `C-28` the first course of a track is the free sample, and this is position 1 of `tech-lead` — 60 hours, given away. But `tech-lead`'s own goal says it "does not start a career, it continues one", so the visitor this free course is meant to convert does not exist: nobody arrives at a technical-leadership track as their first contact with the school. **The rule is keyed on position and the intent is keyed on audience**, and here they disagree. Either `tech-lead` should be reachable only after another track, or this course is 60 free hours serving retention rather than conversion. The sheet does not choose; it records that the free tier was sized for `web-fundamentals` and applied to a course it was not sized for.

**2 ·** **8.6 sections a lesson, and fifteen lessons carrying four framework families.** Waterfall, agile, PMI and ITIL are four vocabularies, and lessons 6 to 8 hold three of them in three lessons. That is the widest ratio in `management` and it is real rather than an artefact: a lesson introducing PMBOK's knowledge areas is closer to a small course than to a lesson.

**3 ·** **The best-graded course of the three categories, and worth using as the proof.** Ordering the ceremonies of Scrum, matching a framework to its artefact, computing a three-point estimate, reading a cycle time off a chart — those are `ordering`, `matching`, `numeric` and `labelling`, four of the eight graders that exist, on their natural material. If judgement cannot be graded here it cannot be graded anywhere in `management`.
