## Ageing

**Low, and unusually so for a technical subject.** Phishing, spoofing, injection and privilege escalation are decades old and still work. Lessons 24 to 26 are frameworks that revise slowly. **Security is the category whose tools age slowest** — nmap and Wireshark are older than most of the catalogue's subjects.

## Flags

**1 ·** **Twenty-six lessons, and the first nine are about people rather than machines.** Social engineering, phishing, shoulder surfing, pretexting, reconnaissance — **the half of the course that needs no environment at all is the half that opens it**, and it is where `quiz` and scenario items work best. Roughly a third of the course is publishable now on that basis alone, which no other course in this category can say at this size.

**2 ·** **Four dependents, two tracks, and the deepest chain in the catalogue runs through it.** `security-fundamentals` → `cryptography` → here → `secure-code` → `threat-modeling` → `secure-pipeline` is **six deep, the longest prerequisite chain in the whole catalogue**, and 360 hours sit in it. A gap here strands more hours than a gap anywhere else in `security`.

**3 ·** **Lessons 16 to 19 are `secure-code`'s subject seen from the attacker's side.** SQL injection, XSS, CSRF, traversal, overflow — taught here as attacks and there as things to prevent, in that order, with `secure-code` requiring this course. **That is the right ordering and it needs saying**, because written independently the two would either repeat each other or both assume the other covered it.
