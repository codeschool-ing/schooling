In **1 track** — `software-architecture`(1).

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** **nothing declared**, and everything in practice. `software-architecture` `continues: backend`, so every student reaching it has finished a fifteen-course track ending in `scale`. The `requires` field is empty and correctly so — the obligation is the track's, not the course's.

**Leaves ready:** the definition of the role that the twelve courses after it argue about. Nothing requires it by name; everything after it assumes it.

## Flags

**1 ·** **Free, and nobody arrives at it free.** It is position 1 of `software-architecture`, so `C-28` makes it the free sample — but that track continues `backend`, and a student who has finished `backend` has paid for fifteen courses. **The free tier is a conversion instrument and this is 40 hours given to somebody already converted.** It is the same defect as `process-management`'s, found independently in the other continuation track, which is what makes it a rule problem rather than two accidents: `C-28` is keyed on position and takes no account of `continues`.

**2 ·** **Seventeen lessons in forty hours — 5.1 sections each, and four of the seventeen are definitions by negation.** Lessons 2, 15, 16 and 17 say what architecture is not, why the architect still writes code, where the role ends and what the antipatterns are. That is a quarter of the course spent on boundaries, and it is the right quarter: the audience arrives with a wrong idea of the job. Worth stating so nobody trims it for length.

**3 ·** **Judgement again, and this time with a way out.** The material is opinion about a role, which no grader reads — but lesson 17's antipatterns are a ready-made item family: given this situation, which antipattern is it, and what would the alternative have been. The first half is a `quiz` and the second is a scenario.
