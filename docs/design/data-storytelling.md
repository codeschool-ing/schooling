# data-storytelling

**Communication and Storytelling with Data** · `co-9sbfhxvk` · 50 h declared · intermediate · 16 lessons · `management` · paid

## Reach

In **2 tracks** — `bi`(15), `data-science`(11).

**Depends on it:** **nothing**

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** `visualization` — chart types, when each one lies, and a tool to make them in. This course does not teach charting; it teaches what to do with a chart once it exists.

**Leaves ready:** **nothing** by name, and in `bi` it is the last course before `ai-dev` and the portfolio, so what it leaves ready is the presentation the portfolio is judged on.

## Flags

**1 ·** **`labelling` fits this course better than any other in the catalogue, and the sweep only sees that from here.** "Mark the three things wrong with this chart" is a labelling item on an image, and labelling has a grader today. A course that looked like pure judgement turns out to be the most machine-checkable of the soft ones — *if* somebody draws the ninety images.

**2 ·** **Ninety images is the bottleneck, and it is the second time the sweep has hit it.** `computing-essentials` needs ~85 diagrams and nobody has been named to draw them; this needs ~90 and here they are not illustration, they are the assessment. **Two courses, ~175 images, no illustrator.** That is now a catalogue-level fact rather than a note on one sheet.

**3 ·** **Lesson 16 is a portfolio, and so is a whole course two positions later.** "An end-to-end analysis project that shows your process" sits sixteen lessons into this course, and `bi` reaches `portfolio-project` at position 17. Either this lesson is the analytical portfolio and the later course is the engineering one, in which case both should say so, or the student builds the same thing twice.
