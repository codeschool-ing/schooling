# excel-analytics

**Excel for Data Analysis** · `co-tcggjfbx` · 60 h declared · beginner · 18 lessons · `data` · paid

## Reach

In **1 track** — `bi`(3).

**Depends on it:** **nothing**

## Assumes, and leaves ready

**Assumes:** `computing-essentials` — a computer and a file. It is the second course of the `bi` track after `bi-business`, and assumes no programming at all.

**Leaves ready:** **nothing** by name, and in practice the spreadsheet literacy every later `bi` course quietly expects.

## Ageing

**Severe.** Microsoft ships Excel continuously; XLOOKUP did not exist a few years ago and lesson 4 teaches it beside VLOOKUP for that reason.

## Flags

**1 ·** **Sixty hours behind a product the student must buy, and the catalogue now has this pattern in four places** — twelve vendor cloud courses, `operating-systems`, `analytics-bi` and this. Unlike a cloud account there is no metered sandbox tier: Excel is a subscription or it is nothing. **LibreOffice is not a substitute** for lessons 13 to 16, which are Power Query, Power Pivot and DAX — Microsoft-only features that are half the course.

**2 ·** **And it is position 3 of `bi`, early enough to be the course that loses people.** A student four hours into a track discovering they need a paid Microsoft licence is a conversion problem, not just a content one. Whatever is decided, **it should be decided before the `bi` track is sold**, and it is the kind of thing a sheet exists to surface.

**3 ·** **The last lesson is the course being honest about itself** — *"The limits of Excel and when to move to a database or BI platform"* — and it points at `sql-databases`, which the same track reaches two positions later. That is a good sequence and worth writing to explicitly.
